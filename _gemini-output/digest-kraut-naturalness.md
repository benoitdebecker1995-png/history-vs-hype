## 4. Attributing sources aloud (exact spoken formulas for citing authors/documents/studies: name-first? credential-first? how much detail aloud?)

*   **Name-first, concise:** The author's name is given first, often as part of a short, direct statement. Example: "This phrase is from Tolstoy."
*   **Credential-first, then name:** The author's profession or role is stated before their name. Example: "As the historian John LeDonne described it,..."
*   **Author + book title:** For significant literary works, both the author and the book title are provided. Example: "This series of events is commented on by George Orwell in his famous book Animal Farm..."
*   **General group attribution:** Attributing to broad categories like "historians" or "medieval accounts" when specific sources are less critical for the point. Examples: "Medieval accounts tell us...", "There is debate among historians..."
*   **Media type + title:** For non-book media, the type is mentioned before the title. Example: "The recent HBO series Chernobyl did an excellent job at hinting to its audience..."
*   **Recommendation format (Title + Author):** When suggesting further reading, the title is given, followed by the author. Example: "I recommend to you to read "Drink: A Cultural History of Alcohol" by Ian Gately."
