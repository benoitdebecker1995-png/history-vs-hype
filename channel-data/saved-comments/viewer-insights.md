> Glad you enjoyed it, and thanks again for the kind of comment that's actually worth writing back to.
>
> On the King David Hotel, you're right, and Deir Yassin doesn't work either, since that came in April 1948, after the vote. Neither was a good counterexample. But there are genuine ones from before 1947. Jabotinsky, who led the Revisionist wing, wrote openly in 1923 that the Arabs would never consent to Zionism and that it would have to be pushed through behind an "iron wall" of force. During the Arab revolt of the late 1930s the Irgun set off bombs in crowded Arab markets, killing twenty-one people in Haifa in July 1938 and thirty-nine there a few weeks later. And "transfer," moving the Arab population out to secure a Jewish majority, was discussed at the very top: the Peel Commission recommended it in writing in 1937, and Ben-Gurion privately wrote that he was "for compulsory transfer" and saw "nothing immoral in it." So an Arab in 1947 wasn't reacting to nothing, any more than a Jew looking at Husseini was.
>
> Where I'd still push back a little is the identity question... [1834 revolt vs Egyptian rule; Filastin newspaper 1911; King Abdullah shot 1951 by a Husseini-circle Palestinian over the West Bank annexation; PLO founded 1964, before the Six-Day War. Mass nationalism hardens after 1967 but builds on something older.]
>
> By that same logic, though, there wasn't much of an "Israeli" identity before 1948 either — it had to be built after the fact. [Pre-state Jewry as divided as Arab society: Ashkenazi/Sephardi/Mizrahi, Yiddish/Ladino/Arabic, religious vs secular; Zionism a modern minority movement opposed by ultra-Orthodox (heresy) and assimilationist Western Jews. Anita Shapira: pre-WWII Zionism was a minority, "never consensual." "Israeli" made after 1948 via Hebrew revival + melting pot — the Golda Myerson→Meir (1956) Hebraization is a piece of exactly that. Ancient element is peoplehood, not nationhood; Palestinian rootedness is the mirror.]
>
> On "non-Jewish communities," you're basically right about the Declaration itself... the phrase carrying into the 1922 League of Nations Mandate — the actual governing document for 25 years — is the part that matters, defining 90% of the country in law only by the religion they weren't.
