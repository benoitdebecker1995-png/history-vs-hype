# Who is actually watching

Day 1b. 24 August 2026. First time the channel's comments have been read. Roughly 600–700 comments
across the ten videos with real engagement; I read the top threads on each, which is where challenges
and requests concentrate. Corrections are in `CORRECTIONS.md`. This document is the audience.

---

## There is no channel audience. There are two, and they don't overlap.

**One: the Belize–Guatemala audience.** 36,476 views across two videos, 455 comments — more than the
other 56 videos combined. Belizean and Guatemalan nationals, plus diaspora in the US and Britain,
plus Salvadorans and Hondurans with a stake. They are not watching a history video. They are watching
a live case about their own country's borders.

The tone is unmistakable:

> *"I live in the middle of the area Guatemala is claiming."*
> *"I remember that Guatemala's map had Belize in it when I was in primary school. That's around 40
> years ago."*
> *"thank you very much for that wonderful analysis, you are appreciated! Linda from Belize"*

They arrive knowing things. One of them is the son of a former president of Guatemala and corrected
you on four points in a single comment, correctly. Another recommended a Spanish-language monograph
on the ex aequo et bono procedure. Another cited the 1931 Exchange of Notes, which your video did not
mention and which is stronger evidence than what it did mention.

**Two: everyone else.** Fifty-six videos, mostly under 300 views, comments in the single digits,
almost no cross-video repeat commenters. Somaliland is the one exception and it is instructive: 466
views, 15 comments, but two of those commenters brought Contini, Cotran, the Hazelwood ruling of
1963, Article 10 of the Law of Union and ILC Articles 40–41. Fifteen comments from people who could
teach the subject.

The pattern across both: **the people who comment on this channel are not a general audience.** They
are participants in the dispute, or specialists in it. Almost nobody comments as a casual viewer who
found something interesting.

---

## What they engage with — and what they walk straight past

They argue about **the legal mechanism**. Estoppel. Article 60 material breach and the Vienna
Convention. *Uti possidetis juris*. Article 62 intervention. Whether the 1859 agreement was a
boundary treaty or a treaty of cession. Whether self-determination is *jus cogens*. Gabčíkovo-
Nagymaros. Cameroon v Nigeria. They came for the argument and they use its vocabulary.

They argue, second, about **who the land morally belongs to** — which is the fight you keep having to
referee, and which the ICJ explicitly does not decide.

**Nobody, in any of these comments, engages with source criticism as a method.** Not one person
praised the channel for showing provenance, for reading the document rather than the claim about the
document, for distinguishing what a source establishes from what a historian infers. That is the
channel's whole identity and it produced zero comments. What it produced instead is the generic
"excellent research," which people say about anything.

That is one data point, not a verdict — comments are a biased sample and the people who like the
method may be the ones who watch and leave. But it belongs on the record before the thumbnail
decision on project 62 gets made, because the retired `FACT CHECKED` concept was betting on exactly
the thing the comments show no appetite for.

**What nobody ever says:** that a video was boring, slow, too long, or that they stopped watching.
Every criticism is about content — *you simplified, you left out X, you're missing context.* People
who comment finish. Whatever is capping this channel, the comment section says it is not the script.

## The finding you will like least: your replies are better than your videos

This is the clearest thing in the whole sweep.

Your reply to @emilianosintarias7337 on the 1947 partition — Transjordan carved off in 1921, Syria
settled at San Remo, the difference between refusing to negotiate a partition and having no
counter-offer, Benny Morris cited against interest, an explicit concession that the regional
asymmetry point is real and a precise statement of why it doesn't settle the question asked — is
better history than the video it is attached to. It ends by conceding the split was not fair to the
Arabs and not crazy for the Jews, which is a harder and more honest landing than most channels reach.

Your reply to @goodali5519 on Somaliland walks through ILC Articles 40 and 41, notes that the ICJ
declined to confirm self-determination as *jus cogens* in Chagos 2019, lays out the paradox this
creates for the AU, and then lists four complicating factors against your own position.

Your reply to @FeenaV: *"Unfortunately simplifying things is what happens when you try to make an
engaging video on a limited budget and still trying to grow as a creator... I do take feedback at
heart and will try to do better next time."*

And to @AndreyBrusinenko: *"I'm not a historian. I'm just someone passionate about these topics who
tries to rely on academic sources and evidence-based research."*

That last pair is the voice. It is direct, it concedes, it does not perform authority it doesn't
have, and it is completely absent from the packaging. The channel presents as a verdict machine.
The person in the comments is someone working it out in public and saying so.

@AkitaMix, on the Somaliland video: *"I'm just glad you're not an AI pretending to be a real human.
They're all over youtube."* You replied that this was your motivation for starting the channel. That
exchange is worth more than most of the thumbnail work in the repo, and it exists nowhere a viewer
can find it.

**Three consequences worth acting on:**

1. **Some of this is publishable material.** The Somaliland Article 10 thread, the 1947 Jordan
   thread, and the Belize estoppel threads are drafted arguments you already wrote for free. They
   are not video scripts, but they are the hard part of one.
2. **You are spending real, uncounted hours here.** Several replies run 400+ words with citations.
   That is a production cost nobody has been tracking, in a week you're describing as 35+ hours.
3. **Whatever you did to your voice in the replies, the videos don't have it.** This is the same
   finding that produced `repairing-script-voice` — but this is the first evidence for it from
   outside your own head.

## The comment sections are a fight, and you are refereeing it

On the Belize videos, viewers tell each other to *"go back to Jamaica or Africa or England."* On the
Greek islands video the argument is Greek versus Turkish nationalism with an antisemitic comment
sitting in it, liked and agreed with. On Kashmir a commenter accuses you of pushing "a left
narrative" for describing Pakistani forces as "invaders" in a UN resolution's own words.

You handle it by arguing rather than deleting — sourced, patient, and sometimes very good:

> *"If Guatemala claims to represent the Maya, how does that square with its own record?"*

But two structural costs come with it, both in `CORRECTIONS.md` §E. Your moderation filter has three
times produced public accusations that you delete dissent, and you have an antisemitic comment
standing under a video about nationalist distortion. Both are fixable today.

## What brought them

Every one of the videos with real engagement is attached to something live:

| Video | Views | What was happening |
|---|---|---|
| Belize | 30,758 | ICJ case; Guatemalan claim in the news |
| Belize ICJ follow-up | 5,718 | Same, four weeks later |
| Essequibo | 1,967 | Venezuela/Guyana oil crisis |
| JD Vance | 1,167 | The speech, days old |
| Greek islands | 979 | Ongoing Aegean dispute |
| KGB/Palestine | 640 | Post-Oct-7 argument about Palestinian identity |

The dead videos are the ones with no live event attached. **This is not the same claim as
"territorial disputes work" — that lane was tested to destruction across 21 videos with a median of
127 views, and it is closed.** The variable that separates 30,758 from 127 is not the subject
category. It is whether a proceeding is live and whether the people affected by it are online
looking for someone to explain it. Belize had both. The other twenty territorial videos had neither.

That distinction is for Day 3 to test properly, with named competitors and real numbers. It is
written here as an observation from the comments, not a recommendation.

## Five things to take away

1. **Your audience is Belizean and Guatemalan.** Not "history viewers." One dispute, two nationalities
   and their diasporas, 12% of everything the channel has done. You have never spoken to them as a
   group.
2. **They know the law and want the mechanism**, not the moral story — and they will correct you at
   page level when you get it wrong.
3. **Nobody engaged with source criticism as a method.** The thing the channel is built on generated
   no comment in 600. Worth knowing before the next packaging decision.
4. **Your comment voice is better than your script voice**, and several viewers noticed. The
   concession, the "I'm not a historian," the admission about budget — none of it is in the videos.
5. **The one thing they asked for, you have not delivered.** They asked what happens with the cayes.
   It happened on 19 March 2026 and nobody told them. See `CORRECTIONS.md` §C.
