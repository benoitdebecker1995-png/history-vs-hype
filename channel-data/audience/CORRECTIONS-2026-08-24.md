## A. Belize — *The Country That Might Disappear* (`Y21EjQ0v9W4`, 30,758 views, 381 comments)

All four originate in one thread from **@amaldonadolutomirsky** (+17 likes), who was then identified
by another commenter as the son of a former Guatemalan president. You replied conceding three of the
four. The fourth came from a different commenter and you did not concede it.

### A1. "Guatemala claims the entire country" — **WRONG. You conceded it. Not corrected on the video.**

Guatemala's claim runs from the **Sibun River south to the Sarstoon**, plus the cayes — roughly half
of Belize. Your own follow-up video states 53%, so the channel already contradicts itself across two
uploads. Raised independently by at least two commenters (@magic-one8: *"do your research brother"*).

Your reply: *"I simplified for a 10-minute video, but you're right that the distinction matters."*

**Verdict: confirmed error.** Your defence in the reply to @magic-one8 — that losing half your
territory would make the country "disappear" — is a fair reading of the *title*, but it is not what
the *video* says, and you have already conceded that elsewhere. Fix the video, not the argument.

### A2. "Guatemala refuses to recognize Belize's independence" — **WRONG since 1991. You conceded it. Not corrected.**

Guatemala recognised Belize on **5 September 1991**. Guatemala did vote against Belize's UN admission
in 1981 and does still dispute the territory — those are different things from non-recognition.

Your reply: *"My statement that 'Guatemala refuses to recognize it' was accurate for 1981–1991 but
outdated afterward."*

**Verdict: confirmed error.** This is the most serious of the four, because it is the one a viewer
would carry away as the headline fact.

### A3. "Guatemala has no highway to its Caribbean coast" — **WRONG. You conceded it. Not corrected.**

**Checked and confirmed against Guatemalan press archive:** the *Carretera al Atlántico* (CA-9),
Guatemala City to Puerto Barrios, was **inaugurated on 29 November 1959**. Construction ran through
the 1950s. Guatemala also had rail to Puerto Barrios from 1908.

Your reply: *"You're right."*

**Verdict: confirmed error, and it matters more than the other two.** The emotional spine of the
video is that Britain never built the road and Guatemala never got its route to the Caribbean.
Guatemala built its own, sixty-seven years ago. That does not weaken the *legal* argument — the 1859
treaty road was Guatemala City to Belize City, through the disputed zone, and Article 7 is about
that specific undertaking — but a viewer who knows Guatemala will hear the video claim something
visibly false and stop trusting the rest.

### A4. "Guatemala became independent in 1839" — **WRONG, and you never conceded this one.**

@pdpc1013: *"Guatemala's independence from Spain was in 1821 not 1839."* Correct. Independence from
Spain: 15 September 1821. Secession from the Central American Federation: 1839. Proclamation of the
sovereign Republic of Guatemala: 21 March 1847.

Your reply said Guatemala *"became a separate republic in 1839."*

**Verdict: the commenter is right about the video; your reply is defensible but imprecise.** 1839 is
the secession, not the founding of the republic. Two different dates, both non-1821. Use "1821 from
Spain; left the Federation in 1839" and leave 1847 out of it unless you have room.

### A5. Islands and cayes not covered — **not an error. It is a demand signal. See §D.**

@amaldonadolutomirsky was right that the cayes were a separate matter you didn't cover. You replied
that there was *"an ongoing case between Belize and Honduras over the Sapodilla Cayes, with Guatemala
seeking to intervene."* That case has since moved. See §C — this is the most valuable thing in the
whole comment sweep.

### A6. "Q'eqchi' were never Maya until communist guerrillas invented it in 1982" (@TheBeltra95)

**Verdict: your reply is correct and well sourced.** Kaufman 1974, Campbell 1977, Guatemalan
Constitution Art. 66 (1985). The commenter conceded. **No action.**

### A7. Subtitle errors: "Eisasina" for **Aycinena**; road cost "£145" for **£145,000** (@TheBeltra95)

You replied *"I have corrected them."* **Verify this actually happened** — subtitle edits are easy to

### A8. The 1931 Exchange of Notes / boundary-vs-cession distinction (@elstonwade3479)

## C. The thing that is not a correction — it is the biggest finding of the sweep

Your December 2025 replies on the Belize videos tell viewers:

> *"the ICJ case has oral hearings in November 2025, ruling expected 2027"*

**That is wrong, and it conflates two different ICJ cases.**

**Checked against the ICJ's own docket:**

| | Case 177 — *Guatemala's Territorial, Insular and Maritime Claim (Guatemala/Belize)* | Case 185 — *Sovereignty over the Sapodilla Cayes (Belize v. Honduras: Guatemala intervening)* |
|---|---|---|
| The case your videos are about | ✔ | ✘ |
| Last procedural step | **Order of 24 June 2022**, fixing Reply and Rejoinder | Guatemala filed its written intervention statement **19 June 2026** |
| Oral hearings | **None held. None scheduled.** | Held **24–26 November 2025** |
| Ruling | **Nothing supports a 2027 date** | Judgment on intervention **19 March 2026** |

The November 2025 hearings you told viewers about were the intervention hearings in the *Sapodilla
Cayes* case, not hearings in Guatemala's claim against Belize. Case 177 has not had a hearing and
has not had a date set. **Verdict: confirmed error, in a reply, on the channel's biggest video, about
the exact procedural detail the channel's authority rests on.**

**And the answer to the question your audience actually asked has been public for five months.** On
**19 March 2026** the ICJ ruled — **unanimously** — that Guatemala has a "real and concrete" legal
interest and granted it permission to intervene as a **non-party**. Honduras had argued the
application was an abuse of process and lost. Guatemala filed its written statement on **19 June
2026**.

You told @amaldonadolutomirsky the intervention was pending. It resolved. You never came back.

**Who has covered it:** only news outlets. Greater Belize Media (78.8K subs) has the field to itself —
a 6-minute preview at 3,201 views, a 3-minute ruling clip at 1,025, a 2-minute government reaction at
904, and, tellingly, an **unedited one-hour hearing feed at 6,462 views**. Guatemala's TN23 (712K
subs) did 958 on a 1:29 clip. Every one of them is a news bulletin. Nobody has explained it.

That last number is the interesting one: 6,462 people sat with a raw hour of ICJ proceedings because
