## 3. The recommendation: Treaty of Wuchale, Article 17 (1889)

**The spine.** Italy and Ethiopia signed one treaty in two languages. Article 17 in Amharic says the
emperor **may** use Italian good offices for foreign affairs. The Italian says he **must**. Italy
declared a protectorate on the strength of its own version, Menelik repudiated it, and the dispute
ran to Adwa in 1896 — where an African state destroyed a European army and stayed independent.

**Why it's the pick:**
- **The inversion is as clean as it gets.** 4,268/mo on the anchor, competition 11.3 on the document
  term, and the best English video *about the document* has **1,619 views**. The SERP's top two
  results aren't even about the treaty — they're Haile Selassie speech footage. Positions 3–12 are
  Amharic-language uploads and sub-400-view scraps.
- **Cold-parse passes outright.** *"Italy and Ethiopia signed the same treaty in two languages. The
  two versions said different things."* No prior knowledge required — the property the handoff calls
  the only structural thing the channel's one breakout had.
- **H6 both terms:** pocket named (Ethiopia) ✓ · stranger-legible ✓.
- **It is the format at its purest.** One word, two texts, one war. Not a legal-status explainer.

**The verification path — this is the part that makes an unreadable language affordable.**
Sven Rubenson, *Wichale XVII: The Attempt to Establish a Protectorate over Ethiopia* (1964),
**collected the original treaties and their drafts in Italian and Amharic** — a published bilingual
critical edition. Plus his *Journal of African History* article "The Protectorate Paragraph of the
Wichalē Treaty" and the Giglio exchange in the same journal. Rubenson also establishes the damning
point: **Antonelli was not ignorant of Amharic and personally worded the Italian draft** that turned
an offer into an obligation. So the discrepancy is documented as deliberate, by name, in peer-reviewed
scholarship — the channel quotes Rubenson with page numbers rather than claiming to read Amharic.

**Cost, stated honestly:** lower than Volhynia. Volhynia needed outside verification of living-language
sources with no single critical edition; Wuchale has one scholar who did the bilingual collation and
a published journal controversy around it. Budget an Amharic reader for the facsimile line only.

## 4. Three things that argue against it — decide with these in view

1. **It is a treaty, and that is exactly the drift the owner called out.** The defence is that the
   subject is the *translation*, not the border: Article 17 as a language artifact, Antonelli as the
   actor, Adwa as the consequence. **The discipline this requires is real** — the moment the script
   starts explaining protectorate law or colonial boundaries, it has become the thing he rejected.
   If that can't be held, pick something else.
2. **Topicality scores 0.** No live hook, and the evergreen cohort is the one that historically never
   cleared 5.5K impressions (H1). **The lever is the Adwa anniversary, 1 March** — which argues for a
   **February 2027 slot**, not an immediate one, and that fits the queue anyway.
3. **This will not solve discovery.** Barrier demand is a niche phenomenon (nothing above 2.9/1,000
   on mass-reach channels), and serve is the binding constraint. This is the best-evidenced way to
   deepen the audience. It is not a breakout mechanism, and nothing measured supports calling it one.
