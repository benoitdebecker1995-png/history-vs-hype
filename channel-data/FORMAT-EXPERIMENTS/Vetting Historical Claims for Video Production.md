### **1\. General Sherman’s "40 Acres and a Mule"**

* **Verification:** The document **does** say what the summary claims. **Section 4** of Special Field Order No. 15 (Jan 16, 1865\) states: "...each family shall have a plot of not more than (forty) acres of tillable ground... in the possession of which land the military authorities will afford them protection, **until such time as they can protect themselves, or until Congress shall regulate their title**". It notably **omits** any mention of "mules".

* **Myth Origin:** **DOCUMENTED.** The specific phrase "40 acres and a mule" is traced to a separate administrative action later in 1865 where Sherman authorized the army to *loan* mules to settlers. The "permanent promise" myth was popularized by Freedmen's Bureau rhetoric and later used as the legal basis for modern reparations claims.

* **Steelman:** Radical Republicans *did* intend for this to be permanent. The Freedmen’s Bureau Act (March 1865\) authorized the bureau to rent 40-acre plots with a **right to purchase** after three years. Academic consensus: It was a radical redistribution of 400,000 acres that was legally undermined by Andrew Johnson's 1865 amnesty proclamation.  
* **5-Minute Tellability:** **YES.** (30s version: Sherman was followed by 40,000 refugees he couldn't feed. He gave them "possessory title" to coastline land as a military logistics fix. The document shows it was temporary, which is how the next President was able to legally snatch it all back 10 months later.)  
* **Competition:** **HIGH.** Multiple recent deep-dives (e.g., *Reveal's* "40 Acres and a Lie"). **GAP:** Most focus on the "lie"; few visually contrast the specific "until Congress shall regulate title" clause on screen.  
* **Red Flags:** Reparations is a high-heat topic. Preempt by clarifying Sherman’s racist motivations—he did this to clear Black people away from his army, not for social equality.

### **2\. The Māori Cession of Sovereignty (Te Tiriti)**

* **Verification:** The document **does** say this. Article 1 (Māori) uses **"Kawanatanga"** (governorship). Article 2 (Māori) guarantees **"tino rangatiratanga"** (unqualified exercise of chieftainship).

* **Myth Origin:** **DOCUMENTED.** Traced to the English-language version of the treaty, which used "Sovereignty" for Article 1 and "Possession" for Article 2\. This version was likely a fraudulent translation by missionary Henry Williams to secure signatures.

* **Steelman:** Some historians argue that by 1840, Māori concepts of authority were shifting and some chiefs may have understood "governance" as an extension of the Queen's power over them.

* **5-Minute Tellability:** **YES.** (30s version: Britain claims they own New Zealand because the chiefs signed away "sovereignty." They didn't sign a surrender; they signed a partnership.)  
* **Competition:** **MODERATE.** Popular in NZ, but low saturation in US/UK/DE/CA markets.  
* **Red Flags:** Active 2026 political debate in NZ regarding the "Treaty Principles Bill". Avoid "co-governance" buzzwords; stick to the 1840 ink.

### **3\. The Treaty of Tripoli (Article 11\)**

* **Competition:** **HIGH.** A staple of atheist/secularist YouTube. **GAP:** Rarely do videos show the *missing* Arabic page or explain why Joel Barlow (a former chaplain) was the one who wrote it.  
* **Red Flags:** Magnet for "culture war" comments. Preempt by noting that while the *people* were Christian, the *government* was legally secular.

### **4\. Captain Cook’s "Terra Nullius" Mandate**

* **Myth Origin:** **DOCUMENTED.** *Terra Nullius* was a 19th-century legal fiction developed by colonial courts to ignore land rights; Cook himself never used the term.

* **Steelman:** British law at the time allowed possession of land that was "not being used" (cultivated) by local populations, which served as the loophole for ignoring presence.  
* **5-Minute Tellability:** **YES.** (30s version: The myth is that Cook was sent to find an empty continent. His actual orders told him to get "consent." He landed, saw fires, recorded inhabitants in his journal, and then claimed the land anyway—violating his own secret mission.)  
* **Competition:** **LOW/MODERATE.** High academic awareness, low popular "forensic" coverage.

* **Red Flags:** High sensitivity regarding Indigenous "Invasion Day". Avoid taking a side on modern land rights; focus on the "Captain violates orders" angle.
