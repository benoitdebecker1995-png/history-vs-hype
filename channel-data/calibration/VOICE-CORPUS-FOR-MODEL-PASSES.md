## 3. HOW HE ACTUALLY TALKS — two unscripted samples

Read these before touching a single sentence. This is the target register.

**Sample A — opening a topic, unscripted, one take:**

> "Why did Ukraine decide to honor the UPA… when they are in the middle of a war against Russia that appears to be, at least from their side, them fighting against evil. Especially because Poland's reaction… was before very supportive of Ukraine and was helping them militarily, financially, also by taking in refugees, then decided to strip Zelensky of its highest military honor… Whether this was a genocide or ethnic cleansing, I would leave in the middle, but we are going to have a deeper look at it. **Sample B — closing a topic, unscripted:**

> "So at the end, we can see that, like so many times in history, this black and white story is not really black or white. It is actually a mix of both. The massacre was real. The same men who committed this massacre did also fight Moscow… Whether this is a good thing to do or an ethical thing to do or a moral thing to do, these are all claims that I cannot answer. What I can answer is that Ukraine is definitely not the first country to have done this… These are all people that are well respected and glorified in most countries' histories, but that were not saints, and we should, I think, try to look at these people in their historical context, without justifying everything that they did."

**What to notice, because these are the markers no writing process has ever reproduced:**
- **`so` is his engine, not `but`.** "So right now…", "So of course…", "So at the end…" He reaches for *so* to move forward and *but* only to genuinely turn.
- **"very" is his intensifier** — "very interesting," "very supportive," "very afraid." He never says "really" as an intensifier.
- **Hedges are clause-final and land on judgments, never on evidence** — "I would leave in the middle," "these are all claims that I cannot answer," "we should, I think, try to…", "in my personal opinion."
- **"kind of"** as an approximator — "kind of figure out," "kind of showing you."
- **He chains with `and`** across sentence boundaries when extending a case. `and` runs at **34/1,000** in his natural speech.
- **Long, flowing, complete sentences.** He is not a short-sentence speaker. Median 13 words with a real tail of 21+.
- **He states his own limits out loud** — "I cannot answer," "I would leave in the middle," "this is not an attack." That is the channel's whole posture and it is native to him.

## 4. THE NUMBERS — and the trap in them

| marker | target | note |
|---|---|---|
| `so` per 1,000 | **≥ 12** | this is the one that matters |
| `but` per 1,000 | **8–10** | do not exceed |
| **`so` : `but` ratio** | **≥ 1.2 — `so` must OUTNUMBER `but`** | ⚠ **the trap** |
| `and` per 1,000 | **32–34** | never trade `and` away to buy connectors |
| `very` | 2–3 in a full script | his intensifier |
| `really` as an intensifier | **0** | not his word |
| clause-final hedges | 2–3 max, **judgment beats only** | see the ban below |
| median sentence | 11–16 words | he is not clipped |
| nominalizations per 1,000 | ≤ 25 | people and verbs, not abstract nouns |

**⚠ THE TRAP, stated plainly because a previous pass fell straight into it.** A model asked to raise `so`+`but` will stuff `but`, because `but` can be bolted to the front of almost any sentence and `so` requires an actual causal relationship. That pass reached 20.6 per 1,000 with **30 `but` against 23 `so`** — ratio 0.77, when his real ratio is 1.29 — and it paid for them by dropping `and` from 28.5 to 25.6. It hit the number and moved *away* from him.

**Report `so` and `but` separately, with the ratio. A combined number is not evidence of anything.**

**⚠ WHERE HEDGES MAY NOT GO.** Never on: the killing, the death tolls, the registry, the documents, the archives, what historians have found, or any beat where the sentence's authority comes from evidence. A hedge there converts an attributed finding into his personal opinion, which is the exact opposite of what this channel does. Hedges belong only where he is openly giving an opinion — and he will usually have already marked those himself ("in my opinion," "I would leave in the middle").

## 5. HIS LOGIC LAW — in his own words

> **"I feel like we can condense a lot of what we say just by adding some logic back into the script."** — T1
>
> **"You have no idea how to structure things… something needs to happen with how things flow inside of each other."** — T2

**The viewer runs an open-question ledger. A paragraph is legal only if it answers the question currently open and raises the next one.** Five violations, in his own examples:

- **V1 — new before known.** *"Who the fuck is the UPA."* A term, name, or claim used before it is introduced.
- **V2 — answering a question nobody asked.** *"Nobody gives a fuck if it's legal."*
- **V3 — raising a question and dropping it.** *"Why is Lebed not enough? If I don't understand, viewers won't."*
- **V4 — re-answering a closed question.** This is what repetition actually is. ⚠ **But see §6 — not every restatement is V4.**
- **V5 — breaking a promise the script made.** *"We promised documents… quoting Snyder."*

**Also his, and load-bearing:**
- **Transitions are his single most persistent complaint.** A transition must name the next beat's actual subject or state the causal link to it. He rejects content-free announcements ("step back," "the deeper point is") and vague referents ("the popular story gets the very first one wrong" → *"the very first what?"*).
- **Show, don't announce.** Never narrate the act of display. "It survives. It reads:" — not "this is it, on your screen." He cut every instance of display-narration on the ear.
- **Weigh sources in voice, point with the screen.** What a source *is*, what class of evidence it is, and how much weight it carries gets spoken. Where it's shown does not.
- **He audits argument-state continuously.** Every "this doesn't connect" from him is a missing causal or referential link, never a style note.
- **He humanizes victims and rescuers, never perpetrators.** *"I don't think we need to humanize them."*

## 6. GUARDS — things that look like defects and are not

**A model pass broke four of these. Check every one before you cut.**

1. **A restated fact is not automatically V4 repetition.** #62 states the death toll in the overview *and* in the chronology, deliberately: the scale must be stated **before** the honoring is explained, or the video reads as pre-emptive minimisation. A previous pass cut the first instance as repetition and broke the guard. **Before cutting any restatement, ask what its position is doing.**
2. **A sentence that repeats a word may be doing it on purpose.** "buries the part that doesn't" and "burying what they did" three sentences apart is a deliberate echo he picked.
3. **A hedge word may be evidence language.** "really," "by the best account," "some historians call it," "he doubted that order had *really* come from the top," "we don't know if" — these carry precise epistemic weight. Deleting one changes a claim.
4. **An unanswered question at the very end of a script is allowed.** Everywhere else, a question must be answered by the next sentence. The closing question is the exception and it is his deliberate choice.
5. **Never remove an attribution to tighten a sentence.** "As X puts it," "Poland says," "for him it's," "Ukraine's official historians dispute" — the entire channel rests on judgments belonging to whoever made them. If a quoted phrase has no owner, either name the owner or drop the quotation marks. Never leave a quoted verdict floating.
6. **Never change anything inside quotation marks.** Every quote is character-exact from a primary source and appears on screen beside the audio.
7. **Never add a fact, a number, a date, an actor, or a causal link that is not on the page.** A previous pass wrote "Soviet interrogators captured Stelmashchuk" — they interrogated him; someone else captured him. That is an invented fact produced by a flow edit.
