# CRAFT-RULES-IMPORTED.md — published craft guidance on writing for spoken delivery

> **UPGRADE-PLAN S7, swept 2026-06-12.** Sources: broadcast-writing pedagogy (NPR Training, journalism course texts), documentary-VO practice, speechwriting research (Atkinson's claptrap studies), and spoken-comprehension literature. **Curated HARD against YouTube-guru content** — nothing here comes from creator-economy advice.
>
> **Tier: ALL rules are IDEA** (rules-hedge: imported rules are ideas to test, never mandates). Selection bias is deliberate: rules that CONFLICT with or SHARPEN current practice were preferred over rules that merely confirm it (⚡CONFLICT / 🔧SHARPENS / ✅CONFIRMS tags). 12 rules, under the 15 cap.

---

## R1 — One thought per sentence (OTPS) — IDEA ⚡CONFLICT
**Source:** broadcast-journalism canon (taught as "OTPS"; COM252 *Writing for the Mass Media*; Knight Center "Writing for the ear" module).
**Rationale:** the listener gets one pass; a second idea in the same sentence is the one that's lost.
**Testable formulation:** every sentence carrying a LOAD-BEARING fact contains exactly one new idea; color sentences are exempt.
**Fit:** conflicts with the flowing register (GS-01: 26% of gold-standard sentences run 21+ words) — but S3 found delivery sheds mid-sentence asides (56-24/RP-7), which is OTPS asserting itself at the delivery layer. Test as a load-bearing-only constraint, not a global one.

## R2 — Relative clauses are toxic to the ear — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** radio-writing pedagogy (NPR Training "Campfire tales"; Journalism University radio-vs-print guides): "who/which/where" additions normal in print lose listeners, who can't see where the main clause resumes.
**Rationale:** the ear can't bracket; it hears one linear stream.
**Testable formulation:** no load-bearing fact may live inside a relative clause or em-dash aside; promote it to its own main clause.
**Fit:** sharpens 56-24 (he drops em-dash asides on camera — the royal-fifth fact vanished). The fix isn't shorter sentences; it's main-clause placement for facts that must survive.

## R3 — Average 15–20 words, subject-verb-object, active — IDEA ✅CONFIRMS
**Source:** broadcast newswriting texts (*Writing for the Ear: Principles of Broadcast News Writing*, Kendall Hunt; The Process of Writing News ch. 5).
**Rationale:** listenability research identifies sentence length as the single best grammatical-complexity predictor of comprehension.
**Testable formulation:** per-beat sentence-length median in the 12–20w band (matches FINGERPRINT §1: his median 13, mean 19 — he's already in spec).
**Fit:** confirmation with a number — useful as the voice_lint band (S5 proposed median 10–22; broadcast's 15–20 average is the tighter prior to test against).

## R4 — Attribution BEFORE the quote, aloud — IDEA ✅CONFIRMS
**Source:** broadcast style (TV/radio newswriting guides: listeners can't hear quotation marks, so the source must come first).
**Rationale:** a tail-attributed quote is heard as the narrator's own claim until the attribution arrives — too late.
**Testable formulation:** zero tail attributions in VO; every spoken quote opens with name/credential ("As X put it, …").
**Fit:** confirms the validated credential+name+"put it" pattern and 57-24 (he ad-libs credentials back in when the script omits them). Worth encoding as a lint check precisely because it's already his instinct.

## R5 — Repeat the word; don't elegant-vary — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** writing-for-listening practice (Podcastwerkstatt "Writing for listening"; The Media Mentor on repetition in radio): synonyms are a print habit; re-using the exact word strengthens focus, synonym-switching creates phantom new referents.
**Rationale:** the listener treats a new word as a new thing.
**Testable formulation:** each key entity/concept keeps ONE name for the whole script; synonym swaps only when the swap itself is the point.
**Fit:** sharpens callback-word threading (P2) and 57-26 (he normalizes a quote's archaic spelling to the established term mid-quote — same instinct, stronger than the imported rule).

## R6 — Strategic redundancy: say the critical thing twice — IDEA ⚡CONFLICT
**Source:** radio practice (broadcast news repetition doctrine; The Media Mentor): listening is partly passive, listeners can't re-read, so key information is deliberately restated.
**Rationale:** one-pass medium → planned redundancy at different positions.
**Testable formulation:** the thesis line (only) is stated twice — once early, once in the close — in the same words (per R5).
**Fit:** direct conflict with observed behavior — recaps die at his read-aloud (57-05) AND at delivery (#56 Equiano recap, #57 enumeration recap, RP-7). Counter-consideration: YouTube viewers can rewind (the radio rationale half-fails) and his retention curves don't show late confusion exits. Informative test: does a same-words thesis restatement survive his read-aloud where paraphrase recaps didn't?

## R7 — Round numbers in the voice, exact numbers on the card — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** broadcast numbers convention (radio/TV newswriting style: round aloud, listeners can't retain precision; print carries exactness).
**Rationale:** spoken precision is lost in seconds and costs comprehension while it's being parsed.
**Testable formulation:** VO uses round/spoken forms ("about eighty thousand a year"); the on-screen card carries the exact figure + source.
**Fit:** sharpens 56-21 (he already spells numbers as spoken) into a two-layer rule matching the verbatim-on-card split (56-11b/57-25).

## R8 — "Say cow, see cow" — never narrate what the viewer can see — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** documentary editing craft (New Doc Editing, "Ten Tips for Writing Documentary Narration").
**Rationale:** narration that describes the visible wastes the channel's scarcest resource (VO seconds) and reads as padding.
**Testable formulation:** at every document reveal, the VO line must add something the card can't show — provenance, stakes, or meaning — never a description of the image.
**Fit:** sharpens the document-on-screen mandate: pointer language ("look at the numbers") survives because it directs attention; description would violate this rule. Audit #58's B-roll beats against it.

## R9 — Leave silence; not every moment needs narration — IDEA ⚡CONFLICT
**Source:** documentary craft (New Doc Editing; doc-VO practice guides): the strongest beats can be image-only; the narrator steps aside.
**Rationale:** silence after a reveal lets the viewer process and marks importance.
**Testable formulation:** one engineered no-VO hold (1–3s) per video, placed after the single strongest document reveal.
**Fit:** conflicts with the wall-to-wall talking-head+VO format — there is currently no scripted silence anywhere. Cheap A/B at the edit layer; pairs with the existing stay-on-face quote-performance pattern (56-08).

## R10 — Script the pauses — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** documentary-VO practice (narration-writing guides: mark PAUSE/beat in the script to drive pacing rather than leaving it to the read).
**Rationale:** pacing decisions made at the keyboard survive; decisions left to the booth are accidents.
**Testable formulation:** the teleprompter render (derived from locked SCRIPT.md, per teleprompter-after-lock) carries explicit beat-pause marks after each evidence land.
**Fit:** sharpens 56-25 — he flattens written cliffhanger seams in delivery; if a pause matters, it must be MARKED, because he won't perform unmarked drama.

## R11 — Don't be fast AND complex: rate × syntax multiply — IDEA 🔧SHARPENS
**Source:** spoken-comprehension research (Speech, Language and Hearing, 2023: speech rate and syntactic complexity act as multiplicative, not additive, factors in sentence comprehension; aging-comprehension literature agrees).
**Rationale:** a dense clause at speed fails disproportionately — comprehension collapses at the product, not the sum.
**Testable formulation:** the script's densest beats (mechanism explanations, treaty clauses) are written in the SIMPLEST syntax; complexity and delivery pace never peak in the same sentence. Audit: flag any 25+ word sentence inside a fast-paced evidence run.
**Fit:** sharpens the 205wpm delivery reality (#58) — at that rate, syntax is the only free variable left.

## R12 — Claptrap devices: contrast pairs and three-part lists — IDEA ⚡CONFLICT (scoped)
**Source:** Max Atkinson, *Our Masters' Voices* / *Lend Me Your Ears* (empirical applause-trigger research: contrast pairs and three-part lists reliably mark completion and emphasis; third item longest lands hardest).
**Rationale:** the two devices give listeners structural closure cues — the best-evidenced rhetoric findings that exist.
**Testable formulation:** at most one contrast pair per script, placed at the TURN (never the close); triples for color only, pairs for load-bearing proof.
**Fit:** double conflict — he cut a mirrored contrast pair at #56 lock (56-12, "the denial contains the confession") and 57-29 shows spoken 3-lists truncate to 2 in delivery. But his natural speech produces triples ("cool and edgy and flashy", GS-09). Hypothesis to test: the devices work for him in COLOR positions and die in VERDICT positions.
