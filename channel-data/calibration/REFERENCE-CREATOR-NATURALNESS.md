## 2. Transition phrases (spoken inventory)

- **Kraut:** But (~10+), However (~5-7), And so / And (~10+), Consequently (~3-4), In the end, Throughout the centuries, Additionally, What's more is that. Notably: Kraut uses formal connectors (However/Consequently) FREELY in speech — ⚡TENSION with the gold-standard fingerprint (his own unscripted stack is so ≫ because; "consequently/thereby" absent, style guide says ration them). Kraut gets away with essayistic connectors; whether HE does on camera is an S8/A-B question.
- **O'Connor:** Okay (10+/video — section pivot), But (20+), I mean (5-10 — elaboration opener), Now, So, Well, Next. "Okay" as a spoken paragraph break and "I mean" as an elaboration opener are cheap to test and close to his natural register.

## 5. Cross-creator convergences (both refs do it → strongest IDEAs)

1. **Concession spoken in the same breath as the pivot** (Kraut "However…" / O'Connor "yes… however…") — not a separate steelman *section*, a per-evidence-beat reflex.
2. **Wry interpretation immediately after evidence, one sentence, then move on** (Kraut's "the name kind of gives away…"; O'Connor's "right off the bat this isn't really an argument for atheism") — evidence never lands cold (matches 56-18) and never gets a paragraph of milking.
3. **Hedged register over certainty** (Kraut: somewhat/largely/pretty much; O'Connor: I suppose/perhaps/seems/I think) — both refs hedge MORE than typical YouTube; consistent with the gold-standard hedge family (GS-05). The genre norm of absolute confidence is what all three voices reject.
