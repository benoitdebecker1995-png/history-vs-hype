## 2. Recurring objections & what they reveal

### Pattern A — the losing side's best advocate shows up in the comments (the biggest lesson)
On every referee/territorial video, the calmest, most-liked critical comment is the *steelman we didn't include*. These are gold — free R&D for a stronger script:

- 🔎 @amaldonadolutomirsky (Guatemala, 16 likes): a detailed, courteous list — cayes weren't in the treaties, *"Guatemala is not claiming the entire territory of Belize, just the area south of the Sibun,"* Guatemala recognized Belize in 1991, the highway *was* built by Guatemala. - 🔎 @Argonautica8 (Serbia/Kosovo): angry but substantive — Serbian churches destroyed, *"you are irresponsibly using the word genocide over and over,"* Tito's border changes. Accuses the video of one-sidedness + loose terminology.

**Reveals:** our territorial/referee videos systematically **under-steelman the losing side.** The fix is upstream, not in the comments.

### Pattern B — concrete factual/production flags worth a verify pass
Specific, checkable, and the kind of thing that quietly erodes trust:

- 🔎 **Guatemala independence date.** Three commenters (@Jose7Lavinge, @Mariosilvagt3, @pdpc1013) insist independence was **1821, not 1839**. (Both dates are real — 1821 from Spain, 1839 from the Central American Federation — so this is a *precision* issue: which "independence" did the video mean, and did it say it cleanly?)
- 🔎 **Name + number error.** @TheBeltra95 (Guatemala): the plenipotentiary was **"AYCINENA," not "Eisasina,"** and the road cost was **"145 THOUSAND pounds… not just 145 pounds."** A misread name + an order-of-magnitude number error, if true.
- 🔎 **B-roll anachronism.** @edgalvani6483 (Guatemala): *"you show pages typing with a type writing machine 1800 — the typewriter was not invented yet."* (Typewriters are ~1860s+.)
- 🔎 **"French principalities."** Two viewers (@Jelena-eo8zv, @andresamplonius315) flag the *same* line at ~1:46 in the Ukraine video — likely "Frankish"/"Rus" mis-said or mistranslated.

### Pattern C — a misconception the audience *holds* (teachable moment)
Across the Guatemala comments, several viewers believe the **ICJ is "in England" and therefore British-biased** (@georgemaldonado8654, @pdpc1013, @vicentesolis6116). It sits in **The Hague**, with judges elected by UN vote. This recurs enough to be worth a single on-screen clarifier + a pinned comment — it directly answers a trust objection.

## 6. Apply to future videos (prioritized, each traced to real feedback)

1. **Pre-empt the losing side's best argument, in-script.** For every territorial/referee video, add a 20–30s "strongest case for the other side" beat built on their best *documentary* point — because that exact argument always arrives in the comments (§2A). Routes to the `referee-steelman-sourcing` / document-led-referee discipline. **Highest leverage.**
2. **Run a tighter accuracy + B-roll-provenance pass before lock.** The audience catches dates, names, magnitudes, and anachronistic B-roll (§2B). Fold the specific catch-types into `/verify` number-grounding and add a B-roll-era sanity check (no typewriters pre-1860, etc.).
3. **Answer predictable misconceptions on screen.** One clarifier line kills a recurring trust objection (the ICJ-in-The-Hague point, §2C). Cheap, repeatable across legal-dispute videos.
4. **Weight the pipeline toward the analytical viewer, not the partisan.** The comments that signal a subscriber ("calm," "sourced," "not AI," "balanced") come from mechanism/ideology-analysis videos, not identity brawls. Territorial-identity topics generate *heat* (and a one-video traffic spike) but not the "intellectual competence" subscriber trigger. Favor HOW>WHY / systems framings.
5. **Make "real human, not AI" a packaging lever.** It's praised unprompted (§1) and is a genuine moat in a slop-filled niche. Signal visible humanity in voice, presence, and title/thumbnail cues.
6. **Treat the top calm correction on each video as next-video R&D.** e.g. @brodonnellYT on the medieval-literacy video: *"Is literacy … really most people's barometer for what made the Middle Ages 'dark'? … My understanding of what made medieval Europe so 'dark' is lack of economic mobility for the average person."* — that's arguably a *better thesis frame*. Harvest these deliberately.
7. **Add a pinned "sources" comment as standard practice.** Satisfies the recurring source-request questions and reinforces the primary-source brand at zero cost.
