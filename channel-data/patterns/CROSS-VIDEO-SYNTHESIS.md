## TOP 5 by Views: What They Share

**Videos:** Essequibo (1,962), Operation Ajax (1,424), JD Vance/Maya (1,127), Turkey/Greece (923), Crusades (669)

### Common Traits

1. **High impressions volume.** The top 4 all received 9,700+ impressions. YouTube showed them to people. The bottom half averages under 3,000 impressions. The primary bottleneck for most videos is not retention or content quality -- it is that YouTube never shows them.

2. **News hooks or cultural flashpoints.** Every top performer connects to something people are already searching for or arguing about: Venezuela-Guyana oil dispute (active news), JD Vance (political figure searches), Turkey-Greece tensions (perennial), Crusades (culture war topic). These are not obscure topics.

3. **CTR above 3% minimum.** Top performers that have CTR data all hit at least 3.1%. The JD Vance video hit 9.46% -- the highest CTR on the channel by a wide margin.

4. **Territorial disputes dominate views.** 3 of the top 5 are territorial/border disputes. These get the most impressions from YouTube's algorithm, likely because they overlap with geography/geopolitics audiences (RealLifeLore, Wendover).

5. **Subscriber conversion is strong.** Top videos average 13.2 subs gained. Essequibo alone brought 19 subscribers.

## BOTTOM 5 by Views: What They Share

**Videos:** Bermeja (11), Gibraltar (24), Thailand/Cambodia (27), French Africa (27), Iran 1953 (34)

### Common Traits

1. **Catastrophically low impressions.** Bermeja got 109 impressions. Gibraltar got 2,299 impressions but 0.70% CTR. YouTube either did not understand the topic or did not find an audience for it.

2. **Obscure topics with no search demand.** "Bermeja Island" and "Treaty of Utrecht" and "Francafrique coups" have no existing search audience. Nobody is searching for these. The content may be excellent, but there is no demand funnel.

3. **CTR under 2%.** Gibraltar (0.70%), South China Sea (1.83%), Bermeja (1.83%), French Africa (1.87%). The titles and thumbnails failed to convert even the small audience YouTube showed them to.

4. **Multiple drop-off points throughout.** Bottom performers tend to have 6-12+ drop-off points scattered across the entire video, not just intro. This suggests pacing problems beyond the hook.

5. **Zero subscribers gained.** 4 of the bottom 5 gained 0 subscribers. The audience that did watch was not compelled to subscribe.

## FINDING 1: The Intro Is Killing Every Video

**Pattern:** Across ALL 31 videos with retention data, the biggest drop-off is always at the 2-4% mark (first 10-30 seconds). The median drop at position 2% is 16.7%.

**The numbers:**
- 29 out of 31 videos have their #1 drop-off in the "intro" zone (0-6%)
- Average first-position drop: 17.1% of viewers lost
- Videos with 20%+ intro drops: Armenia (29.4%), Iran 1953 (26.5%), Flat Earth (25.6%), Thailand/Cambodia (25.9%), Peru (22.2%), Viking (21.7%)

**What to DO:**
- The first sentence must contain a specific number, a name, or a shocking contradiction. Never start with context.
- Front-load the payoff. "This document proves X" beats "In 1916, during World War I..."
- Test cold opens (straight to B-roll/document) vs. talking head. The Israel-Palestine video lost 13.6% on a cold open with no talking head -- the evidence is mixed on whether cold opens help.

## FINDING 2: CTR Is the Single Biggest Lever

**Pattern:** There is a direct relationship between CTR and views that dwarfs all other factors.

| CTR Range | Avg Views | Videos |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| 5%+ | 649 | 5 videos (KGB, Crusades, Kosovo, JD Vance, Ajax) |
| 3-5% | 555 | 10 videos |
| 2-3% | 93 | 10 videos |
| Under 2% | 69 | 6 videos |

**The outlier:** JD Vance at 9.46% CTR got 1,127 views despite being a relatively niche topic. The title format -- "[Person] Claims [X]. Here's the Evidence." -- is the highest-performing title pattern on the channel.

**What to DO:**
- Never publish with a CTR prediction below 3%. If the title does not pass the "would I click this?" test, rewrite it.
- The "versus" title pattern (Turkey vs Greece, Venezuela vs Guyana) consistently delivers 3%+ CTR.
- Avoid abstract/academic titles. "The 1922 Treaty Loophole" (0.70% CTR) vs. "Why TURKEY and GREECE Can't Agree" (3.10% CTR). The human conflict framing wins.
- Dark Ages got 7,237 impressions but only 1.11% CTR -- YouTube tried to show it, but the title/thumbnail failed. That is 7,000 wasted impressions.

## FINDING 3: Impressions Are the Gate, Not Retention

**Pattern:** Good retention does NOT guarantee views. Bad impressions guarantee failure.

**Proof:**
- South China Sea: 50.5% retention (best on channel), 91 views, 3,007 impressions
- NATO Expansion: 48.3% retention, 46 views, 1,214 impressions
- Middle East British Betrayal: 48.3% retention, 327 views
- JD Vance: 27.5% retention (below average), 1,127 views, 9,705 impressions

YouTube gave the JD Vance video 9,705 impressions because people were searching for JD Vance content. It gave South China Sea 3,007 despite flawless retention because nobody was searching for "1947 nine-dash line map."

**What to DO:**
- Before committing to a topic, verify that search demand exists. Use VidIQ keyword research.
- Attach every video to an existing search trend, news event, or cultural debate. "This 1908 map" means nothing to the algorithm. "Thailand vs Cambodia temple dispute" has a search audience.
- Retention optimization matters, but only AFTER you have solved the impressions problem. Perfecting a video nobody sees is wasted effort.

## FINDING 5: Subscriber Conversion Correlates With Depth, Not Views

**Pattern:** The videos that convert viewers to subscribers at the highest rate are NOT the most-viewed videos.

| Video | Views | Sub Rate | What It Does Differently |
|-------|-------|----------|--------------------------|
| NATO Expansion | 46 | 6.52% | Document-first, proves a claim wrong |
| KGB Palestine | 275 | 5.09% | Deep causal chain, unexpected angle |
| Channel Intro | 40 | 5.00% | Self-selection (people seeking the channel) |
| Stalin | 120 | 4.17% | Comprehensive evidence stacking |
| Bermeja | 11 | 0% | Insufficient views to measure |
| Essequibo | 1,962 | 0.97% | Broad audience, lower commitment |

**What to DO:**
- Videos with high sub conversion share a trait: they prove a specific, falsifiable claim wrong with documents. This is the channel's competitive advantage.
- Broad territorial explainers get views but lower conversion. Deep, document-driven debunks get fewer views but stronger conversion.
- The ideal video does BOTH: a searchable territorial/political topic (for impressions) + document-first argumentation (for conversion). Essequibo partially achieved this.

## FINDING 7: The 2% Drop Is Not the Hook -- It Is the "Thumbnail Mismatch" Point

**Pattern:** The massive 2% position drop (first 5-10 seconds) is not about hook quality. It is about viewers who clicked the thumbnail, saw the video, and immediately decided "this is not what I expected."

**Evidence:**
- Armenia: 29.4% drop at 2%. Title says "Hidden Pattern" -- viewer expects a revelation, gets a history lecture setup.
- Flat Earth: 25.6% drop at 3%. Title promises "who invented it" -- first 30 seconds are background context.
- Peru: 22.2% drop at 2%. Title says "500-Year Lie" -- opening does not immediately deliver the lie.

**What to DO:**
- The first 5 seconds must deliver on the thumbnail/title promise. If the title says "The Document That...", show the document in the first 5 seconds.
- Never open with background context. The viewer already decided to watch based on the title's promise. Fulfill it immediately, then backfill context.
- Match the energy: if the thumbnail is dramatic, the first seconds must match. Academic calm after a dramatic thumbnail = instant bounce.

## FINDING 8: Videos With 6+ Drop-off Points Have Structural Problems

**Pattern:** Videos with scattered drops throughout (not just intro) consistently underperform.

| Drop Points | Avg Views | Avg Retention | Examples |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|----------|
| 2-3 drops | 516 | 40.2% | Essequibo, Turkey/Greece, South China Sea |
| 4-5 drops | 197 | 29.7% | Somaliland, Crusades, Dark Ages |
| 6+ drops | 43 | 27.3% | Georgia, Kosovo, Iran 1953, Thailand, French Africa |

**What to DO:**
- If a video has 6+ drop-off points in the retention curve, the script has pacing problems -- tangents, unnecessary historical detail, or sections without modern relevance anchors.
- Apply the 90-second rule: every 90 seconds must either (a) reveal new evidence, (b) connect to a modern consequence, or (c) deliver a pattern interrupt.
- Before filming, read the script aloud and mark any section longer than 90 seconds without a payoff. Cut or restructure.

## FINDING 9: Engagement Rate Does Not Predict Views

**Pattern:** Some of the lowest-viewed videos have the highest engagement rates.

- Bermeja: 18.2% engagement, 11 views
- Peru: 17.4% engagement, 46 views
- Channel Intro: 20.0% engagement, 40 views

Meanwhile:
- Essequibo: 1.5% engagement, 1,962 views

**What this means:** High engagement on low-view videos indicates passionate niche audiences who YouTube never connected to. The content resonates with those who find it, but YouTube's algorithm cannot find more of them.

**What to DO:**
- Do not optimize for engagement rate. Optimize for impressions and CTR first. Engagement follows naturally with good content.
- High engagement + low views = the topic has a dedicated audience but no search funnel. Consider making these topics as sections within larger, more searchable videos rather than standalone.

## Summary: The 5 Rules That Would Have Changed Outcomes

1. **Verify search demand before production.** 60% of videos got under 3,000 impressions because the topic had no search audience. Use VidIQ keyword research before committing to any topic.

2. **Title must contain human conflict or a falsifiable claim.** "[Country] vs [Country]" or "[Person] Claims [X]" outperforms academic/descriptive titles by 3-10x in CTR.

3. **First 5 seconds must fulfill the title's promise.** The 2% drop (15-30% of viewers lost) is a thumbnail/title mismatch problem, not a hook quality problem. Show the document, map, or contradiction immediately.

4. **Retitle high-retention / low-view videos.** At least 5 videos have 35%+ retention but sub-100 views due to bad titles. These are recoverable with title/thumbnail swaps.

5. **90-second payoff rule.** Videos with 6+ drop-off points have structural pacing problems. Every 90 seconds needs a revelation, a modern connection, or a pattern interrupt. No exceptions.
