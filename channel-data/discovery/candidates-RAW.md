### CAT-01 — Treaty of Waitangi (1840)
**Document date:** 1840
**Document:** Treaty of Waitangi, English and Māori (te Tiriti) versions
**Popular narrative:** Britain and Māori chiefs signed an agreement establishing British sovereignty over New Zealand.
**What document actually shows:** The English version granted Britain "sovereignty"; the Māori version (te Tiriti) granted only "kawanatanga" (governance), while explicitly preserving Māori "tino rangatiratanga" (full chieftainship) over land, villages, and people. The two documents are not translations of each other — they make different legal promises. The Waitangi Tribunal has been litigating this discrepancy since 1975 and is still active in 2026.
**Visual:** Gorgeous handwritten document in English and Māori columns side by side, with ornate Māori chief signatures.
**Source:** Published in full by NZ Archives, academic editions by Claudia Orange ("The Treaty of Waitangi," Bridget Williams Books), Vincent O'Malley.
**Series fit:** Untranslated Evidence #3 or standalone
**Origin:** Prompt B (bilingual discrepancy)

### CAT-02 — Treaty of Nerchinsk (1689)
**Document date:** 1689
**Document:** Treaty of Nerchinsk — written in Latin (neutral language), Manchu, and Russian
**Popular narrative:** Russia and China have disputed their border for centuries — it's an eternal territorial conflict.
**What document actually shows:** The Treaty of Nerchinsk in 1689 definitively settled the Sino-Russian border in China's favor, in a document drafted in Latin (neutral) as the official version. Russia later claimed the Latin text and Manchu text differed on key boundaries. When Russia took the Amur region in 1860 (Treaty of Peking), it used these discrepancies to argue Nerchinsk had been superseded. Almost nobody in the English-speaking world knows this treaty exists.
**Visual:** Three-language document (Latin, Manchu, Russian), 17th-century diplomatic manuscript. Manchu script is visually striking.
**Source:** John Maddox (Cambridge), Mark Mancall, "Russia and China: Their Diplomatic Relations to 1728."
**Origin:** Prompt A + Plan seed list

### CAT-03 — Sublimis Deus (1537)
**Document date:** 1537
**Document:** Papal bull "Sublimis Deus" issued by Pope Paul III
**Popular narrative:** The Catholic Church actively supported and enabled the colonization and enslavement of Indigenous Americans.
**What document actually shows:** Pope Paul III officially declared that Indigenous Americans have souls, are rational human beings, and CANNOT be enslaved or deprived of their property — "even if they are outside the faith of Jesus Christ." The Bull was suppressed within two years by the Spanish Crown, which pressured Paul III to issue a retraction ("Non Indecens Videtur"). The suppression of this document was itself an act of state. The Church said no; Spain overruled the Pope.
**Visual:** Ornate papal bull with official seals, Latin text.
**Source:** Lewis Hanke, "The Spanish Struggle for Justice in the Conquest of America" (University of Pennsylvania Press); full text in Church archives.
**Series fit:** Untranslated Evidence angle — Latin original + the Spanish suppression document

### CAT-04 — Fort Laramie Treaty (1868)
**Document date:** 1868
**Document:** Treaty of Fort Laramie between US and Lakota/Dakota/Arapaho nations
**Popular narrative:** The US broke its treaty with the Sioux — a vague betrayal of a vague promise.
**What document actually shows:** Article 2 of the treaty explicitly guaranteed "the absolute and undisturbed use and occupation" of the Great Sioux Reservation, including the Black Hills, FOREVER. Article 12 required approval of three-quarters of adult males for any land cession. When gold was discovered in 1874, the US sent Custer in, then bought the Black Hills in 1877 from a council of ~10% of adult males — explicitly violating Article 12. The Supreme Court ruled in 1980 (US v. Sioux Nation) that the taking was an "unabashed taking of tribal property" — and awarded $102 million (now $2.1 billion with interest). The Sioux refuse the money, still claiming the Black Hills.
**Visual:** Handwritten treaty with Lakota pictograph signatures, ornate headers.
**Source:** Vine Deloria Jr., "Behind the Trail of Broken Treaties" (University of Texas Press); Jeffrey Ostler, "The Lakotas and the Black Hills" (Viking/Penguin).
**Modern relevance:** Sioux Nation is STILL refusing the $2.1 billion payment in 2026.
**Origin:** Prompt B + Phase 1C winner pattern

### CAT-05 — Haitian Constitution of 1805
**Document date:** 1805
**Document:** Constitution of Haiti, Article 14 (and surrounding provisions)
**Popular narrative:** Haiti declared independence from France in 1804, the first Black republic.
**What document actually shows:** Article 14 of the 1805 Constitution declared that ALL citizens of Haiti — regardless of skin color, including white women who married Haitian men, Polish soldiers who defected to the Haitian side — would be legally classified as "Black" ("Noirs"). This was a radical reversal of the colonial racial hierarchy — not abolition of racial classification, but inversion of it. Most English coverage of Haitian independence focuses on 1804 and never reads the 1805 constitution.
**Visual:** Original French manuscript, ornate header.
**Source:** Laurent Dubois, "Haiti: The Aftershocks of History" (Metropolitan Books/Picador); original at Haitian National Archives (facsimile widely published).
**Origin:** Prompt A + Prompt C

### CAT-06 — Royal Proclamation of 1763
**Document date:** 1763
**Document:** Royal Proclamation of 1763, issued by King George III
**Popular narrative:** After the French and Indian War, Britain reorganized North American territory. American colonists then rebelled because of taxation.
**What document actually shows:** The Proclamation explicitly drew a line at the Appalachian mountains and declared all land west of it "Indian Country" — reserved for Indigenous peoples, off-limits to colonial settlement, and under direct Crown protection. It recognized Indigenous peoples as "Nations or Tribes with whom We are connected" with sovereignty over their lands. Colonists routinely violated it. This document is STILL cited as the legal basis for First Nations land claims in Canada today — in active 2026 Supreme Court cases.
**Visual:** Ornate royal proclamation with George III's seal, dramatically large format.
**Source:** Colin Calloway, "The Scratch of a Pen: 1763 and the Transformation of North America" (Oxford); full text at National Archives UK.
**Modern relevance:** Canadian courts cite it in current Indigenous land cases.
**Origin:** Prompt A + Phase 1C winner pattern

### CAT-07 — Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (1774)
**Document date:** 1774
**Document:** Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca, Article 7 and Article 14
**Popular narrative:** Russia has always had ambitions to "protect" Orthodox Christians in the Ottoman Empire. The Crimean War was about Russian expansion.
**What document actually shows:** Article 7 gave Russia the right to "make representations" to the Ottoman government on behalf of a new church in Constantinople. Article 14 gave Russia protections for specific monasteries. But over the next 80 years, Russian diplomats progressively REINTERPRETED these articles to claim a general right to protect ALL Orthodox Christians throughout the Empire — a right that does not appear anywhere in the treaty text. This manufactured justification was the legal basis Russia used for the Crimean War in 1853. The gap between text and interpretation is deliberate and documented.
**Visual:** Ottoman-Turkish and Russian versions, ornate 18th-century diplomatic documents.
**Source:** Norman Itzkowitz & Max Mote (translators), "Mubadele: An Ottoman-Russian Exchange of Ambassadors" (Chicago); Orlando Figes, "The Crimean War."
**Modern relevance:** Russia's "protecting Russians abroad" doctrine in Ukraine 2014/2022 uses the same interpretive structure.
**Origin:** Prompt A + Prompt B

### CAT-08 — Treaty of Hünkâr İskelesi (1833) — SECRET ARTICLE
**Document date:** 1833
**Document:** Treaty of Hünkâr İskelesi between Russia and Ottoman Empire, public + secret article
**Popular narrative:** Russia dominated the Ottoman Empire diplomatically in the early 19th century.
**What document actually shows:** The public treaty was a mutual defense pact. But a SECRET article — known to almost nobody outside the signing parties — stated that instead of military assistance, the Ottomans would only need to close the Bosphorus to foreign warships "in favor of Russia." This effectively gave Russia control of the strategic waterway. The secret article was leaked to Britain within weeks and caused a major international crisis. The concept: a treaty that says one thing publicly and another in a secret annex.
**Visual:** The public treaty vs the leaked secret article — a two-document reveal structure perfect for on-screen display.
**Source:** Matthew Anderson, "The Eastern Question 1774-1923" (Macmillan); original documents in Russian State Archive.
**Origin:** Prompt B

### CAT-11 — Twelve Articles of the German Peasants (1525)
**Document date:** 1525
**Document:** "The Twelve Articles" (Die Zwölf Artikel) — first printed demand list of the German Peasants' War
**Popular narrative:** Martin Luther championed freedom of conscience and individual rights in the Reformation.
**What document actually shows:** The German Peasants in 1525 wrote a list of 12 demands based explicitly on Luther's own writings — the right to choose pastors, end serfdom, reduce tithes, restore commons. Luther initially said the demands were reasonable. Then when the peasants revolted, Luther published "Against the Robbing and Murdering Hordes of Peasants," urging the nobility to "stab, smite, slay" the peasants without mercy. Thousands were massacred. The gap between the document's reasonable, Biblically-grounded demands and Luther's response is documented in Luther's own published text.
**Visual:** Original German woodblock printed pamphlet — one of the first mass-printed political documents.
**Source:** Peter Blickle, "The Revolution of 1525" (Johns Hopkins); full text in multiple editions.
**Origin:** Prompt A + Prompt C

### CAT-12 — Treaty of Tianjin (1858) — Bilingual Clause War
**Document date:** 1858
**Document:** Treaty of Tianjin, English and Chinese versions, Article 6
**Popular narrative:** The Second Opium War was about trade. China was forced to open its ports.
**What document actually shows:** Article 6 of the English version clearly stated foreign envoys would have the right of audience with the Emperor. The Chinese version (Manchu/Chinese) was deliberately ambiguous on this point. When Britain's envoy Lord Elgin tried to ratify the treaty in 1859, Chinese officials interpreted the "audience" clause differently and opened fire on the British fleet. This triggered the Second Opium War's second phase, the burning of the Summer Palace, and an expanded treaty. The bilingual discrepancy was the direct cause of a war.
**Visual:** Chinese-English bilingual treaty text, ornate official seals.
**Source:** Julia Lovell, "The Opium War" (Picador); Harry Gelber, "Opium, Soldiers and Evangelicals."

### CAT-13 — Treaty of Ghent (1814) — Article 9 Native Rights
**Document date:** 1814
**Document:** Treaty of Ghent (War of 1812), Article 9
**Popular narrative:** The War of 1812 ended in a draw — nobody won, everything returned to status quo.

This clause was agreed, signed, and immediately violated by both sides. The US government treated Article 9 as a dead letter within months. Britain never enforced it. This is one of the earliest and clearest cases of a treaty provision being written, signed, and ignored simultaneously.

**Document date:** 1878
**Document:** Treaty of Berlin, Article 61 (Armenian reforms)
**Popular narrative:** European powers tried to protect Armenian Christians in the Ottoman Empire. The Ottoman government refused.

### CAT-18 — Peace of Westphalia (1648) — The Sovereignty Myth
**Document date:** 1648
**Document:** Peace of Westphalia (Treaty of Osnabrück + Treaty of Münster)
**Popular narrative:** The 1648 Peace of Westphalia established the modern nation-state system and the principle of state sovereignty. Every IR textbook says this.
**What document actually shows:** The actual treaties contain almost nothing about sovereignty as modern IR theory defines it. They deal with religious tolerance, the restitution of confiscated church properties, and specific territorial settlements. The "Westphalian sovereignty" concept was largely invented by IR scholars in the late 19th and early 20th centuries reading what they wanted into the texts. Historians and legal scholars have extensively documented this as a myth — Andreas Osiander's 2001 International Organization article "Sovereignty, International Relations, and the Westphalian Myth" is the most cited debunking.
**Visual:** Two ornate 17th-century treaties, portraits of the diplomats.
**Source:** Andreas Osiander, "The States System of Europe, 1640-1990" (Oxford); Derek Croxton.
**Origin:** Prompt A

### CAT-21 — Code Noir (1685) — IN PIPELINE #49
**Document date:** 1685
**Document:** Code Noir, Louis XIV's edict governing slavery in French colonies
**Popular narrative:** Colonial slavery was an informal system of violence and exploitation.
**What document actually shows:** France codified slavery in 60 detailed articles — setting rules for baptism, marriage, punishment, manumission, property rights of enslaved people, inheritance. Article 44 defined enslaved people as "meubles" (movable property). Articles 32-38 specified permitted punishments. But Article 9 gave enslaved people the right to legal marriage recognized by the Church. Article 59 allowed self-purchase (manumission). The document simultaneously reduces people to property AND grants them specific legal rights — a contradiction at the heart of colonial law, never discussed in most accounts.
**Visual:** Original French edict, ornate Louis XIV-era document.

### CAT-22 — Merer Diary (~2560 BCE) — IN PIPELINE #16
**Document date:** ~2560 BCE
**Document:** Merer's Logbook (Wadi al-Jarf Papyri) — oldest known papyrus document
**Popular narrative:** We don't know how the pyramids were built, and probably never will. Slaves built the pyramids.

### CAT-23 — Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848) — Protocol of Querétaro
**Document date:** 1848
**Document:** Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, Articles VIII-IX + Protocol of Querétaro
**Popular narrative:** Mexico was forced to cede half its territory to the US after the Mexican-American War.
**What document actually shows:** Article VIII protected property rights of Mexicans who stayed in US territory. The US Senate deleted Article X (which explicitly guaranteed Mexican land grants) before ratification. Mexico refused to ratify without a guarantee, so the US negotiated the "Protocol of Querétaro" — a written promise that Articles VIII and IX provided the same protections as the deleted Article X. Mexico accepted. The Protocol was NEVER submitted to the US Senate, making its legal standing doubtful. Subsequent US courts largely ignored it. Mexican land grant holders in California, Texas, New Mexico lost their properties anyway.
**Visual:** The treaty + the Protocol side by side; the deleted Article X text visible with strikethroughs.
**Source:** Richard Griswold del Castillo, "The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo: A Legacy of Conflict" (University of Oklahoma); full text at Yale Avalon Project.
**Note:** Already in pipeline as #20 (idea stage)
**Origin:** Prompt B + Plan seed

### CAT-24 — Monroe Doctrine (1823) — What the Document Actually Said
**Document date:** 1823
**Document:** Monroe's 7th Annual Message to Congress, paragraphs on European non-colonization
**Popular narrative:** The Monroe Doctrine declared the Western Hemisphere off-limits to European interference — a bold American defense of hemispheric independence.
**What document actually shows:** The actual paragraphs are brief, vague, and explicitly stated the US would NOT interfere in existing European colonies or in European affairs. The US had no military capacity to enforce it in 1823 — it was an aspiration, not a policy. Britain, who actually had the navy, supported the same position. The "Monroe Doctrine" as an interventionist tool was invented 80 years later by Teddy Roosevelt (the Roosevelt Corollary, 1904) — which REVERSED the original by claiming the US could intervene in Latin America to prevent European debt collection. The document says one thing; the doctrine became the opposite.
**Visual:** Original presidential message (newspaper and government printing), side-by-side with Roosevelt's Corollary.
**Source:** Jay Sexton, "The Monroe Doctrine: Empire and Nation in Nineteenth-Century America" (Hill and Wang); Ernest May.
**Note:** Already flagged as #3 (idea stage); high VidIQ volume (~1,245/mo parent term)
**Origin:** Plan seed
