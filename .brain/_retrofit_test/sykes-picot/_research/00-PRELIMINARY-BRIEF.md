## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Sykes-Picot Agreement was a secret wartime deal signed May 9-16, 1916, by British diplomat Mark Sykes and French diplomat François Georges-Picot, dividing anticipated Ottoman territories into British and French spheres of influence. It allocated what is now Iraq and Jordan to Britain, Syria and Lebanon to France, and designated Palestine for "international administration." Crucially, it was negotiated in parallel with — and directly contradicted — the McMahon-Hussein Correspondence, in which Britain had promised Arab independence in exchange for the Arab Revolt against the Ottomans. The Bolsheviks exposed the secret deal in November 1917, producing what the Wikipedia article calls "great embarrassment to the allies and growing distrust between them and the Arabs."

The standard narrative treats Sykes-Picot as the origin of all Middle East problems — a lazy cartoon of two diplomats drawing lines on a map. The underexplored angle for this channel: **the agreement was never meant to create permanent borders, was partially superseded before WWI ended, and yet the "betrayal" narrative became so politically powerful that it now drives decisions by actors who invoke it — including ISIS, Tom Barrack in August 2025, and Israeli commentators predicting "Sykes-Picot 2026."** The mechanism story is not the map-drawing — it is why the myth of the map proved more durable than the map itself.

## STANDARD NARRATIVE (What competitors will say)

Every competitor video on Sykes-Picot tells the same story:

1. Two European diplomats sat down with a map of the Middle East they barely knew
2. They drew lines ignoring tribes, sects, and ethnicities — purely for colonial convenience
3. Britain promised the Arabs independence (McMahon-Hussein) and simultaneously carved up their lands (Sykes-Picot)
4. The Balfour Declaration added a Zionist promise on top of an already broken Arab promise
5. The agreement created the modern Middle East borders that persist today
6. Every conflict since — Israeli-Palestinian, Iraq, Syria, Kurdistan — traces back to these lines

**The implicit frame:** European arrogance + Arab betrayal = century of instability. The lines on the map are the root cause.

**What every competitor misses:**
- Sykes-Picot was never actually implemented as written — it was substantially renegotiated before the peace conference (Dec 1918 Lloyd George-Clemenceau private meeting) and replaced by the mandate system (San Remo 1920)
- The lines that actually matter are NOT the 1916 Sykes-Picot lines but the 1920 mandate borders — which shifted significantly (Mosul moved from French to British; Palestine removed from international administration)
- The McMahon ambiguity was deliberate, not incompetent — the Foreign Office later acknowledged this
- The King-Crane Commission documented what Arabs actually wanted; its findings were suppressed — the closest thing to a "democratic verdict" on these borders was actively buried
- The agreement's political afterlife (ISIS invoking it in 2014; US officials dismissing it in 2025) tells us more about how colonial-era frames function than about the actual borders
