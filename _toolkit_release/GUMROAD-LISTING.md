## Long Description (Gumroad description field)

### The bottleneck for your history channel isn't content. It's packaging.

If you make well-researched history, geopolitics, or fact-checking videos and your view counts are flat, you already know this. The video that took 30 hours to research gets 200 views. The competitor's hot-take video gets 100K. It's not because their content is better. It's because their title and thumbnail won the 8-second decision on the YouTube home page.

This is a **47-video reverse-engineered methodology** for fixing that.

### What you get

A 40-page PDF documenting the exact packaging system that took a niche history channel from 169 subs (Nov 2025) to 462+ subs (Feb 2026) — **173% growth in 90 days** — in a niche where most channels plateau under 1,000.

Built from:
- 47 long-form videos with measured CTR
- 33 videos with paired CTR + impression data
- 388 competitor titles in the same niche
- 650 competitor thumbnails analyzed
- 4 retitle experiments with before/after CTR delta

### Inside the PDF

**Section 2** — The six title patterns ranked by measured CTR, with sample sizes and confidence levels for every claim. (versus, declarative, how_why, question, colon, the_x_that)

**Section 3** — The three hard-reject patterns that cost 28-46% of CTR every time you use them. With detection rules and the exact rewrites that fix them.

**Section 4** — The six bonus signals that the top-CTR videos all share. Including the strongest single signal in the calibration set: the evidence promise (+9.5% CTR ceiling).

**Section 5** — The 48-hour swap protocol. Three thresholds, three actions. The exact data-driven rule for when to swap title, swap both, or hold steady.

**Section 6** — The retitle decision tree. Five steps, including when *not* to retitle (which is most of the time).

**Section 7** — Niche-specific thumbnail rules from 650 competitor thumbnails analyzed. Why faces underperform 0% in this niche. Why text overlay is mandatory. Why maps work for territorial topics.

**Section 8** — How to calibrate the system to your own channel after 30 days of data.

**Appendix A** — A working Python script (`title_scorer_standalone.py`) that runs every rule out of the box. No installs, no dependencies, no setup. Just `python title_scorer_standalone.py "Your Title Here"` and you get a 0-100 score with full breakdown.

**Appendix B** — 12 worked examples from a real 47-video back catalog. The 28K-view outlier kept as-is. The 9.5% CTR champion (highest in calibration set). The self-inflicted wound (0/100 score) and its 80/100 rewrite.

**Appendix C** — 50-title pre-flight checklist you run before publishing any title.

### Sample finding from the PDF

> *Pattern detection order: versus → the_x_that → colon → question → how_why → declarative. First match wins. "How Spain vs Portugal Divided the World" detects as **versus**, not how_why. This is correct because the versus framing dominates the click decision.*

> *Top-CTR title in the calibration set: "JD Vance Claims Christians Found Child Sacrifice. Here's the Evidence." → 9.5% CTR. Pattern: declarative. Bonuses: evidence promise (+10), named entity (+5), controversy frame (+5), two-sentence (+5). Final score: 90/100.*

### Methodology behind the methodology

Every CTR claim in this PDF has a sample size in parentheses. When n is small, the guide says so. When n is large, the guide says so. There are no unbacked claims. There are no "trust me" assertions.

The Python script ships with hardcoded `PATTERN_SCORES` calibrated to one specific channel. Section 8 of the PDF walks you through replacing those scores with your own data after 30 days of measurement.
