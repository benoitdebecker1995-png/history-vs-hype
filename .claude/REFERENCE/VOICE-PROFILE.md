## Voice signature (one paragraph)

**The governing image, in his words: "I want it to be like me talking to people in a bar."** A smart friend explaining history to you over a drink — plain, simple, and genuinely interesting, *without sacrificing historical integrity.* He sounds like **Kraut crossed with Alex O'Connor**: an analytic mind reasoning out loud through a causal chain, conversational and intellectually honest, with a dry, deadpan wit. He is **NOT** History Matters (clipped), **NOT** Wendover (ice-cold systems-engineer), **NOT** Johnny Harris (breathless), and his explicit *anti-voice* is **RealLifeLore** (the rapid scale-comparison atlas). "Calm Prosecutor" still fits the stance — evidence-based referee who goes to the document — but the register is **plain bar-talk explanation, not the gavel-punch and not literary prose.**

**The two enemies (he rejects both extremes):** (1) **staccato/gimmick** — clipped fragments, "guess what," 8-word gavels; and (2) **purple/writerly prose** — over-explaining, padding, literary flourish, stacked credentials. The target is the lean middle: **plain, tight, clear bar-talk.** Explain the *mechanism* simply; then stop.

## THE HEADLINE CORRECTION (read this first)

**He explains the mechanism plainly — but tight.** The channel's cult of the clipped 8-word verdict / staccato gavel is *over-applied* to him; his default is a **plain, complete, explaining sentence**, not a fragment. The short punch is rare and reserved for a clean *landing*. His instinct is **consequence-driven**: never just state the fact, land *what it meant / why it matters.*

**BUT the explanatory lean has a hard ceiling: bar-talk, not prose.** He flags "too much prose," over-citing, and clunky transitions as standing problems, and on the paragraph test he chose the **tighter** version. So "explanatory" ≠ wordy. Explain the mechanism *simply and clearly, then stop.* Don't pad, don't go literary, don't stack credentials.

When in doubt: **explain the mechanism plainly, like you're telling a smart friend in a bar — then cut everything that isn't that.**

## Standing dislikes (perennial — he named these unprompted, 2026-06-05)
1. **Clunky transitions** — beat-to-beat handoffs that don't truly bridge. (His single most persistent complaint — see the transition rules below.)
2. **Credential-heavy / over-cited** — too many "as historian X writes" stacked close together. Use credentials sparingly; the bar-talk register doesn't name-drop a scholar every beat.
3. **Too much prose** — writerly padding and flourish. Plainer and tighter, always.

## ⭐ THE BAR-TALK LOCK TEST (run on EVERY beat BEFORE it reaches him — 2026-06-07)

His real lock-test, in his words: *"I'd never talk to someone like this"* / *"not how you explain it to a person interested in understanding the topic."* Operationalized into a checklist Claude runs **before** showing options, to stop burning drill rounds. Nine checks. **#1 (say-it-out-loud) and #6 (smart-but-plain) override the rest when they conflict; on a #3-vs-#5 clash, completeness of the WHY wins.**

1. **Say it out loud in one breath.** Flowing, connected sentences — not staccato, not chopped fragments, not a list-then-stab. (The #1 "too-AI" tell — see §Sentence rhythm.)
2. **Start on the substance.** No runway, no announcement ("step back," "here's why," "the deeper point is"). The zoom-out is invisible. (See §Synthesis.)
3. **Walk every leap.** Explain the *why* on both halves of a claim; never assert a conclusion without crossing the viewer to it. **When this fights "keep it tight," completeness of the why WINS — cut everything else to make room.**
4. **Plain words a person actually uses.** No academic/corporate register, no decorative metaphor, no forced figure.
5. **Tight.** No restating, no second kicker, trust the listener. (Yields to #3 when they clash.)
6. **Respects their intelligence.** Real substance, not dumbed down — the viewer is *interested in understanding*, so give the full mechanism, just in plain language.
7. **Concrete anchor.** Every explanatory beat carries a named agent / specific detail / lived example, not abstraction. (Named-agent atrocity; geometry-via-lived-consequence.)
8. **Names the HOW / mechanism**, not just the outcome — the HOW>WHY subscriber trigger. (Distinct from #7: concreteness is the detail, this is the causal engine.)
9. **Honest (auditor's edge).** Concede what the other side gets right, attribute claims to the right party, never overclaim. (Folded into the test 2026-06-07 — it's part of "sounding like him," not a separate gate.)

> If a beat fails any check, fix it before showing it. The #58 session ran ~14 rounds largely because options reached him failing #1/#2/#3 — this checklist is the fix for the round-count.

## The fingerprint (each rule = an approved line + a rejected counter-example)

### Sentence rhythm
- **Default to full, complete, FLOWING sentences — the way you'd explain it out loud to a friend. Never staccato.** This is the **#1 "too-AI" tell** (2026-06-07): chopped fragments and clipped list-then-stab verdicts are *not how you talk to someone.* When a line reads generated, the fix is almost always **stop chopping — connect it into flowing sentences.**
- **His signature sentence-shape: flowing parallel clauses → em-dash → a verdict that carries the catch.** The em-dash is a *within-sentence pivot*; the verdict rides the same breath — it is NOT chopped off into a separate stab.
  - ✅ "They wrote their own histories, minted their own coins, fielded their own armies — everything a state does except the one thing they never managed: unite."
  - ✅ (flowing landing, his pick 2026-06-07) "So you've got a people running their own courts, minting their own coins, raising their own armies. At that point, whatever you want to call it, this isn't a tribe answering to an empire anymore — it's a state running itself."
- **The staccato STAB-VERDICT is DEMOTED to rare-exception (was ✅, now dispreferred, 2026-06-07).** A verdict chopped into its own short sentence after a period reads as machine.
  - ⚠️ (dispreferred) "…by any working definition, that's not a tribe. It's a state." — the "It's a state." period-stab is the staccato; prefer the flowing landing above.
  - ✅ (OK — one connected sentence, no stab) "That's not a tribe — that's a functioning state."
  - ❌ "That is not a tribe. That is a state." / "Their own courts. Their own coins. Their own armies." (chopped — rejected live)
- **Fragments** only for a proper-noun reveal landing ("…Saddam Hussein.") or a single genuinely rare beat. Never triplets, never chopped lists, never a period-stab verdict as the default.
- **⭐ THE GOVERNING DISTINCTION for short-sentence rhythm (2026-06-08, TWO live MISSES — #4 Crusades + #51 Tripoli — read this before "fixing" any chopped-looking beat).** The choice is NOT "flowing vs chopped" globally; it's **what the units ARE:**
  - **Distinct sequential STEPS, each a different point** → clean short COMPLETE declarative sentences, separate, are right (and build momentum). ✅ #4 turn: "The video makes five major claims. Every one leaves out critical evidence. I went to the primary sources… And it's not what this video tells you." (count → omission → action → reveal = four different steps). [I predicted the flowing rewrites; he kept the clean-declarative build — MISS.]
  - **PARALLEL list-items of ONE idea** (esp. a negation series "not X, not Y, not Z") → **FLOW them into one connected sentence that lands on the resolution.** ✅ #51 reveal: "It wasn't mistranslated, or paraphrased, or softened — it simply isn't there." (three facets of one "it's not-X" point). ❌ chopping that into "It isn't mistranslated. It isn't paraphrased. It just isn't there." (my prediction — MISS) AND ❌ the fragments "Not mistranslated. Not paraphrased. Missing." (both lost live).
  - **Fragments** (no verb) lose either way, except the rare proper-noun reveal landing.
  This is the §Sentence-rhythm face of §Enumeration ("flowing parallel prose for short list-items; separate beats only when each carries a full explanatory clause"). Anti-staccato bans FRAGMENTS + period-stab VERDICTS as the *default rhythm* ("Their own courts. Their own coins." / "That is not a tribe. That is a state.") — it does NOT ban clean declaratives for distinct steps.

### Transitions (HIS #1 STANDING COMPLAINT — get this right)
**The rule: a transition must be THESIS-FORWARD and hand directly into the next beat's subject.** Name where we're going — tightly — and/or state the causal link to it. Never just sit adjacent. Three validated forms, all thesis-forward:
- **Thesis-forward (state the through-line of what's coming):** ✅ "What comes next is one pattern, on repeat: the Kurds get armed when they're useful, and abandoned the moment they're not." (chosen over the vaguer "you get the part you've probably half-heard")
- **Plain causal bridge that names the next subject:** ✅ "So those states didn't just fade out. Someone took them apart — and it was the empire they'd served for three centuries." (chosen over the abstract question-bridge — it hands straight into Act 2's subject)
- **Thesis-pointed question, then flow into a plain statement** (one tool among several): ✅ "So if the Kurds had states, the real question is who took the ones they had." → then plainly into the next beat.

**Where the bridge LIVES (2026-06-07):** **Default = a forward-pointing TAIL at the END of the outgoing beat/act**, so the next act can open *cold* on its own subject. (Act 2→3 does this: Act 2's tail reaches forward — "…forty years before the great powers would sit down to carve up the region" — so Act 3 opens straight on WWI, no back-reference needed.) A bridge **may also** open the next paragraph — but **only when it's a genuine content hand-off** (names the next subject / states the causal link), **never a meta-runway** ("step back, here's the meaning" = the §Synthesis cringe). Consequence for the linter: its transition check should look BACKWARD (did the prior beat's tail hand off?), not flag a clean cold-open act-start as a "bare jump." (L75/L104 flags = likely false positives — Act 2's tail already bridges.)

**Two specific tics he flags:**
- ❌ **Vague referents in the bridge.** "the popular story gets the very first one wrong" → he asked "the very first *what*?" Name it or restructure.
- ❌ **Calling the common story flatly "wrong."** Prefer **"oversimplified / tells only one part of the story"** (intellectually honest); reserve "wrong" for a specific factual error.

### The turn (myth → evidence)
- **Analytical signpost, not a gimmick.**
  - ✅ "And here's the part almost everyone gets wrong."
  - ✅ "Except none of that is the real story."
  - ✅ (document turn, second-person) "But read the treaty and you realize this promise was conditional…" then unpack.
- ❌ **"But guess what?" / "And guess who…" are RETIRED.** They're in his old cuts, but given a plain/analytical alternative he drops them every time. A gimmick always loses to a good plain alternative.
- **Turn/verdict button: throw the TARGET'S OWN WORD back, deadpan (2026-06-08 #4 Crusades, live pick).** When the target claim has a memorable word ("awesome"), the sharpest button is a deadpan understatement that turns it against them — callback to the hook + auditor's edge in one. ✅ (after laying out what the Crusaders actually wrote) "And 'awesome' is about the last word you'd use for it." ❌ the generic button "And it's not what this video tells you." (flat, no callback, no teeth).

### Connectors
- His: **"So"** (the workhorse), **"as a matter of fact"** (typed in unprompted), **"which is why"**, **"and that meant"**, **"because"**, **"actually"** (frequent emphasizer).

### Causal explanation (the core of his voice)
- **Consequence-driven: after the fact, land what it MEANT / WHY it matters.** Don't state "X gave authorities discretion" — explain *how the discretion worked in practice.*
- **Second-person walk-throughs for mechanisms.**
  - ✅ "When you kill the princes, you don't get a modern province. You get a power vacuum."
  - ✅ "If your government helped mark the boundary points, you can't turn around decades later and say the border was never valid." (chosen over the jargon "estopped")
- **Two-part mechanisms: name BOTH halves** (the gain *and* the cost) — never compress to one.

### Numbers / scale

### Tense
- **Past tense is the default.** BUT historical present **is** his for a **single sharp dramatic moment** (a confrontation, a signing, an action beat).
  - ✅ "It's June 1923. In a hall in Lausanne, the Turkish delegate stands up and says the Kurds and the Turks are one people." (single moment — chosen over past)
- ❌ Present tense for **decade/era scene-setting** is NOT his. "Go back to the 1970s. The Kurds are in open revolt…" — rejected (period summary, not a moment). The distinction is **moment (present OK) vs. era-framing (past only).**

### Enumeration
- **Flowing parallel prose for short list-items**, not chopped date-beats.
  - ✅ "…armed by the Shah and abandoned in 1975, gassed by Saddam in 1988, urged to revolt by Bush in 1991 and then left to be crushed."
- Break into separate named beats *only* when each item carries a full explanatory clause.
- **An EVIDENCE list is the full-clause case, not the short-item case (2026-06-08 #19 flat-earth, confirms completeness on a 3rd topic).** When the list is *proof* of a claim, each item must carry its *why-it-proves-the-point*, not just a label — naming the proofs ("geometry, observations, the orb") and stopping is the under-explain trap. ✅ "…they could watch a ship vanish hull-first, the mast disappearing last — something that only happens if the surface is curved." ❌ "They had geometric proofs. They had sensory observations." (labels, no why — also chopped).

### Steelman / concede
- **"To be fair, some people might say…" — and attribute it honestly** (name the party, e.g. "this is Turkey's narrative"). Don't invoke a "standard story" you never laid out.
- **Neutrality is a HARD CONSTRAINT on charged/political topics — it overrides craft (promoted to body 2026-06-08: now 2 topics).** The calm-prosecutor stance both (a) KEEPS honest framing that'd otherwise be cut — the explicit "this isn't me arguing for either side" declaration earns its place because the audience is pre-primed to assume bias (#59 I/P); and (b) VETOES any move that reads as partisan — e.g. ending on or dunking a living political figure, even when it's a tidy bookend (#4 Crusades: he dropped the "Deus Vult tattoo" callback for the plain idea/mission landing, "ending on a political figure feels too pointed"). When craft and neutrality collide on a charged topic, neutrality wins; land on the idea/mission instead.
- **When a primary DOCUMENT carries the concession, lead with the document — don't narrate a verbal concede/meta-frame (2026-06-07 #58 Sèvres reveal).** He picked "In 1920, the Kurds really were promised a state — in writing. [SHOW Sèvres KURDISTAN section]…" over BOTH the verbal concede "The famous part is true, as far as it goes" AND the meta-frame "the part everyone gets half-right." The real treaty on screen concedes the famous point for you; then "but read the fine print" lands the catch. **Show the concession, don't announce it.** (The lean-on-devices law applied to concede: the verbal concede-frame is a device → cut it when the primary source does the work. Extends "primary sources on screen.")

  - ❌ (rejected — abstract moralizing) "It was the system working as designed — human beings counted as cargo because the law said they were." (he took the plain death toll instead)

- So: lead with the plain fact; add heat ONLY when you can name *who* and *why* concretely; never sensory, never abstract sermon.

### Rhetorical questions
- **One payoff question is fine** — but the **question-bridge to the present is now DISPREFERRED vs flat continuity (2026-06-07 #58).** He picked flat "And it isn't only history. In January 2026…" over "So has any of this actually changed? Look at January 2026." The signpost question *telegraphs* the pivot and makes the modern beat feel MORE tacked-on, not less. To hand into the present organically, narrate it as the next instance of the same pattern, flat — don't announce the jump. No pile-ups.
- ❌ **"One word: Mosul." / "Two words: X."** — compressed clever reveal, cringe. Use a full causal sentence: ✅ "…Because there was something it wanted more than Kurdish autonomy: the province of Mosul."

### Figuration
- **Default plain.** Figures only when *literally true* and earning their place; never decorative; never anachronistic. (He rejected even a mild figure when it risked overclaiming.)

### Closing
- **Land on a scholar/primary quote, scoped for accuracy** — plain bookend, not dramatic, not a recap.
- **A CONCRETE bookend-callback at the open of the close is fine — but flowing, never staccato (2026-06-07 #58).** The close may echo concrete beats that bookend the cold open (✅ "They gave Islam its greatest hero, they built kingdoms, they minted their own coins —" callbacks Saladin + the on-screen coin), as long as it's connected prose, not a chopped triplet (❌ "They gave… They built… They minted." rejected). This is DISTINCT from the banned abstract verdict-recap (#59: re-listing findings). Rule: **concrete callbacks that bookend = OK flowing; abstract verdict re-listing = cut.** ⚠️ **CONSTRAINT (2026-06-08 #4 Crusades): the callback target must be neutral/historical, NOT a living political figure** — that reads as a partisan jab and the neutrality stance vetoes it (he dropped the "Deus Vult tattoo" bookend for the idea/mission landing). See §Steelman — neutrality overrides the bookend craft on charged topics.
  - ✅ "…as the historian David McDowall writes, 'nations are built in the imagination before they are built on the ground.' The Kurds built theirs in the imagination. Every empire around them made sure it never reached the ground." (time-scoped to the 1800s-on national idea)

### Synthesis / throughline beat (the recurring failure spot)
- **It is NOT a special rhetorical mode and gets NO runway.** Don't write a narrated "here's the big meaning" paragraph. The throughline is **one plain sentence said cold + a sourced card on screen.** (His call, in the grill: "B with A inside it.")
- **Open straight on the claim, walk ONE concrete mechanism, land once.** ✅ "Nobody around them ever actually wanted the Kurds to have a country. A Kurdish people with no state of their own was useful: you could arm them, send them to make trouble for a rival, and walk away the day it suited you. Give them a real country and you lose all that…" → then [SHOW: scholar card].
- ❌ Any opener whose only job is to announce the move: "so step back…," "underneath the whole story…," "here's why it keeps happening," "the deeper pattern is this." (see no-list — six #58 rewrites died here.)
- **Mine the source for the scholar's OWN crisp summation; don't generate thesis-prose.** "Just ask NotebookLM for a spoken version" surfaced McDowall's verbatim "nuisance, not resolution" — tighter and truer than anything generated. Auditor's edge applied to PHRASING, not just evidence.

### CTA
- **Plain personal mission.** ✅ "That's what I'm trying to do with this channel — go to the documents and show you what they actually say. If you like that, subscribe."

### Casualness ceiling
- Contractions throughout, yes. But **clean and plain, not folksy.** ❌ "little states", "a bunch of", "about as much" — dead-weight padding he cuts. "basically"/"actually" OK as natural softeners.

### Humor
- **LITERAL-FIRST (confirmed 2026-06-07). The plain concrete fact wins whenever it's already vivid or self-evidently absurd — the modern analogy is NOT the default humor move.** On #58 Sherif Pasha he took the literal "wrote essays in a café" (now SCRIPT.md L75) over even the *clean* analogy "the 1919 version of a blogger." The literal fact — a man in a Paris café claiming to speak for a nation — already carries the absurdity, so the analogy is decoration, and decoration gets cut.
  - ✅ "A British official waved him away as 'a Parisian pamphleteer' — a man who wrote essays in a café." (literal + the real quote; deadpan by understatement)
  - ❌ "…the 1919 version of a blogger." (analogy as decoration over an already-absurd fact — rejected live)
  - ❌ "…basically a café blogger. You can guess how that went." (winking — rejected)
- **The modern analogy fires ONLY as a comprehension aid** — when a historical role is opaque and "think of it as the modern X" makes the function legible. Reached for to *clarify*, never as a joke; the humor is a byproduct of the straight, deadpan delivery. If the literal fact already lands, don't add it. (No confirmed analogy-win exists yet — when one is picked live, log it here as the positive example.)
- The deadpan lives in **understatement** ("waved him away as," "not someone who could deliver a country"), not in a punchline.
- **Frequency: only when it arises naturally — never a quota (2026-06-07).** Don't seed humor beats for pacing. If a fact is dryly absurd, deliver it deadpan; otherwise stay plain. Count varies by topic; zero is fine.
- **Grim irony from the powerful: play it FLAT (no wink) but ANCHOR it to the consequence (confirmed 2026-06-07).** A cynical quote from someone with power is NOT left to sit cold — deliver it straight, then bolt it to what it cost. ✅ (#58 L104) "…Kissinger's only defense was that 'covert action should not be confused with missionary work' — about an operation that had just left his allies to be crushed." (Calibration: Claude predicted the cold-sit; missed toward the leaner option — the standing drift.)
- Aimed at absurdity / self-seriousness, never at people who suffered.

The genuine open question IS the label. (2026-06-06 #59 drill.)
- **synthesis-runway / meta-signpost opener on a LANDING beat** — "so step back, and the same logic runs under all of it," "underneath the whole story is one hard rule," "here's why it keeps happening," "the deeper pattern is this," "step back, and the pattern that emerges is this." Same family as the significance-telegraph openers, but the bug is treating the SYNTHESIS beat as a special mode that earns a runway. It doesn't. **Generalize the ban from the cold-open to EVERY beat: start cold on the substance; the zoom-out is invisible.** A throughline = one plain sentence said cold + a sourced card on screen, NOT a narrated "here's the meaning" paragraph. (2026-06-06 #58 grill — six rewrites died on this; see the new §Synthesis fingerprint.)

A name earns a natural spoken clause ("the government passed a decree…") or it goes on screen only. (NOT a ban on the em-dash itself — his signature dash-verdict stands; the ban is the rammed proper-noun tag.) (2026-06-06 #58.)
- (prior) clever-clever wordplay, meta-framing openers ("here's what almost no video will tell you"), essay-prose ("this is what the whole rest of the story comes down to / keep both in your head")

## Worked exemplar (imitate this, don't just obey the rules)

**From-scratch cold open (flat-Earth, the accepted from-scratch model — famous-then-puncture + concede-first + two medium sentences):**

> Everyone knows the story. Columbus sailed west to prove the Earth was round, while everyone back home was sure he'd drop off the edge. *Why it works:* famous thing set up, **then** punctured (not a bare one-liner); concede-first ("It's a great story —" before the denial); two medium sentences carry the mechanism, not a run-on; the heat is **specific + attributed** (named *who*: Victorian writers, and *why*: needed the Middle Ages to look stupid), never an abstract sermon; plain dates ("the 1800s", "over a hundred years"), no scale comparison.

When showing exemplar/option text, run the historian gate (9-check #9) on it, not just the voice gate. The from-scratch, Claude-fabricated exemplars are the highest risk; the ones derived from verified research carry their own sourcing.
