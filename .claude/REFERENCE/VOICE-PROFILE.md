## Voice signature (one paragraph)

**The governing image, in his words: "I want it to be like me talking to people in a bar."** A smart friend explaining history to you over a drink — plain, simple, and genuinely interesting, *without sacrificing historical integrity.* He sounds like **Kraut crossed with Alex O'Connor**: an analytic mind reasoning out loud through a causal chain, conversational and intellectually honest, with a dry, deadpan wit. He is **NOT** History Matters (clipped), **NOT** Wendover (ice-cold systems-engineer), **NOT** Johnny Harris (breathless), and his explicit *anti-voice* is **RealLifeLore** (the rapid scale-comparison atlas). "Calm Prosecutor" still fits the stance — evidence-based referee who goes to the document — but the register is **plain bar-talk explanation, not the gavel-punch and not literary prose.**

**The two enemies (he rejects both extremes):** (1) **staccato/gimmick** — clipped fragments, "guess what," 8-word gavels; and (2) **purple/writerly prose** — over-explaining, padding, literary flourish, stacked credentials. The target is the lean middle: **plain, tight, clear bar-talk.** Explain the *mechanism* simply; then stop.

## THE HEADLINE CORRECTION (read this first)

**He explains the mechanism plainly — but tight.** The channel's cult of the clipped 8-word verdict / staccato gavel is *over-applied* to him; his default is a **plain, complete, explaining sentence**, not a fragment. The short punch is rare and reserved for a clean *landing*. His instinct is **consequence-driven**: never just state the fact, land *what it meant / why it matters.*

**BUT the explanatory lean has a hard ceiling: bar-talk, not prose.** He flags "too much prose," over-citing, and clunky transitions as standing problems, and on the paragraph test he chose the **tighter** version. So "explanatory" ≠ wordy. Explain the mechanism *simply and clearly, then stop.* Don't pad, don't go literary, don't stack credentials.

When in doubt: **explain the mechanism plainly, like you're telling a smart friend in a bar — then cut everything that isn't that.**

**⭐ CALIBRATION — "clearer" means TIGHTER, never MORE (2026-06-13, #60 Guadalupe Hidalgo — a severe live miss).** Across four "explain this better" notes the script ballooned **5.6 min → 12 min**, because each clarity fix *added* words instead of replacing them. His rule, enforced hard: clarity for a lay viewer is NOT length. A "make it clearer" ask is answered by re-writing the same point in plainer, shorter words and then *stopping* — never by adding explanation. **Decide the runtime / what-video-this-is up front and treat it as a hard constraint; a clarity pass is a rewrite at roughly constant length, not an expansion.** (Same engine as §Quote-handoff "clearer = precision, not more words" — now promoted to the whole-script/runtime level.) The fix landed at ~8.5 min, plainer AND clearer than the bloated 12-min cut. His verdict: "a lot better."
- **The north star is the lay viewer:** *"common people just wanting to learn a bit of history, while staying academically honest and showing the sources."* Plain words, short sentences, every term glossed the moment it appears, the law/mechanism rendered in everyday terms. Cut proper-noun bookkeeping a layperson can't track — name only the ONE load-bearing item (e.g. "Article Ten," not all three articles); case names ride the citation card, not the VO.
- **Carry every referent FOR THE EAR — a listener can't scroll back (his single most-repeated #60 pushback).** Every "it / one / that / built one / it started / moved it / this question / the commons" must have its noun present and unmistakable. Walk every leap; *state the question before you answer it*; say exactly what happened ("the Court threw out the confirmation"), never a vague verb the viewer has to decode ("reversed it / took it back / the land it moved"). No appositive-fragment lists for the ear ("five judges, actual trials" → "it had five judges and held real trials").
- **Every asserted fact that has a WHY gets the why** — don't drop a fact in cold ("a Court that had grown less sympathetic" → say *why*: a more conservative bench than the one that set the precedent). And use precise, un-loaded, concrete actors: "the Santa Fe Ring," not "Anglo lawyers… cheap"; "Californians kept three-quarters," not "California kept" (don't anthropomorphize the state).
- **Keep the objective, source-driven EDGE by pushing the verdict onto the DOCUMENT, not the narration.** When tempted to narrate a judgment ("the wrong books," "rigged"), instead put the primary verbatim on screen (*Las Siete Partidas* "belong to the common of each town"; Fuller's "property of the king") and keep the VO plain and precise. Show deliberateness through concrete FACT (the govt's own lawyer compiled the biased rulebook the judges relied on), never asserted malice — the actors' *intent* stays the open fork (auditor's edge). Cut loaded narrator words ("quietly," "rigged," "paid to take the land"); the sourced fact is damning on its own. See memory [[feedback-clarity-first-lay-audience]].

## ⭐⭐ HOW HIS LINES GET LOST — they are eaten by tidying, not by bad writing (n=9, 2026-07-22)

**The single most useful rule for anyone — model or human — editing a draft that contains his own lines.** Nine violations of the locked-line corpus were found in one #62 draft. Every one moved in the same direction, and **not one was bad writing**: each was grammatical, most were shorter, several read better in isolation, and **none would be caught by `voice_lint` or any style rule in this file.**

The three recurring moves:
1. **Merging two of his sentences into one** ("The east of Ukraine fell to the Soviets. In the west, Volhynia…" → one sentence; "That's what I try to do on this channel. I go to the sources…" → em-dash merge).
2. **Replacing a concrete noun with a pronoun** ("This state lasted for about three years" → "It lasted about three years") — the same failure as the carry-every-referent-for-the-ear rule, seen from the editing side.
3. **Converting a plain landing into an abstract summary of itself** ("Now the idea had men to carry it out." → "the movement had both the idea of removing Poles and trained men capable of doing it"; "So that's why honoring these men is useful now." → "gives Ukraine a history of resistance to Moscow").

> **A locked line cannot be protected by any rule about quality. It can only be protected by the diff.** Any pass touching a chapter containing his lines diffs against `channel-data/calibration/VOICE-CORPUS-FOR-MODEL-PASSES.md` §1 **before returning**, never after. **"I only tightened it" is the signature of the failure, not a defence.**

⚠ Discovery note: the ninth violation survived seven read-alouds *and* two model audits, and surfaced only when a costed cut-proposal happened to put that line under a diff. Assume undetected ones exist in any script that has been through more than two passes. Full table: corpus **62-20**.

## Standing dislikes (perennial — he named these unprompted, 2026-06-05)
1. **Clunky transitions** — beat-to-beat handoffs that don't truly bridge. (His single most persistent complaint — see the transition rules below.)
2. **Credential-heavy / over-cited** — too many "as historian X writes" stacked close together. Use credentials sparingly; the bar-talk register doesn't name-drop a scholar every beat. **Sub-tic flagged 2026-06-09 (#58 read-aloud): the anonymous "one historian writes / one historian puts it" formula becomes a CRUTCH when repeated across many beats** ("he flagged it's been repeated a lot in this video"). Fixes: (a) NAME the scholar when he's load-bearing/recurring (David McDowall is the spine source — name him), (b) drop the attribution from the VO and let the [SHOW] card carry the quote + name, (c) vary the verb/frame. Never run the same "one historian ___" frame two beats in a row.
3. **Too much prose** — writerly padding and flourish. Plainer and tighter, always.

## ⭐ THE BAR-TALK LOCK TEST (run on EVERY beat BEFORE it reaches him — 2026-06-07)

His real lock-test, in his words: *"I'd never talk to someone like this"* / *"not how you explain it to a person interested in understanding the topic."* Operationalized into a checklist Claude runs **before** showing options, to stop burning drill rounds. Nine checks. **#1 (say-it-out-loud) and #6 (smart-but-plain) override the rest when they conflict; on a #3-vs-#5 clash, completeness of the WHY wins.**

1. **Say it out loud in one breath.** Flowing, connected sentences — not staccato, not chopped fragments, not a list-then-stab. (The #1 "too-AI" tell — see §Sentence rhythm.)
2. **Start on the substance.** No runway, no announcement ("step back," "here's why," "the deeper point is"). The zoom-out is invisible. (See §Synthesis.)
3. **Walk every leap.** Explain the *why* on both halves of a claim; never assert a conclusion without crossing the viewer to it. **When this fights "keep it tight," completeness of the why WINS — cut everything else to make room.**
4. **Plain words a person actually uses.** No academic/corporate register, no decorative metaphor, no forced figure.
5. **Tight.** No restating, no second kicker, trust the listener. (Yields to #3 when they clash.)
6. **Respects their intelligence.** Real substance, not dumbed down — the viewer is *interested in understanding*, so give the full mechanism, just in plain language.
7. **Concrete anchor.** Every explanatory beat carries a named agent / specific detail / lived example, not abstraction. (Named-agent atrocity; geometry-via-lived-consequence.)
8. **Names the HOW / mechanism**, not just the outcome — the HOW>WHY subscriber trigger. (Distinct from #7: concreteness is the detail, this is the causal engine.)
9. **Honest (auditor's edge).** Concede what the other side gets right, attribute claims to the right party, never overclaim. (Folded into the test 2026-06-07 — it's part of "sounding like him," not a separate gate.)

> If a beat fails any check, fix it before showing it. The #58 session ran ~14 rounds largely because options reached him failing #1/#2/#3 — this checklist is the fix for the round-count.

## The fingerprint (each rule = an approved line + a rejected counter-example)

### Sentence rhythm
- **Default to full, complete, FLOWING sentences — the way you'd explain it out loud to a friend. Never staccato.** This is the **#1 "too-AI" tell** (2026-06-07): chopped fragments and clipped list-then-stab verdicts are *not how you talk to someone.* When a line reads generated, the fix is almost always **stop chopping — connect it into flowing sentences.**
- **His signature sentence-shape: flowing parallel clauses → em-dash → a verdict that carries the catch.** The em-dash is a *within-sentence pivot*; the verdict rides the same breath — it is NOT chopped off into a separate stab.
  - ✅ "They wrote their own histories, minted their own coins, fielded their own armies — everything a state does except the one thing they never managed: unite."
  - ✅ (flowing landing, his pick 2026-06-07; **"answering to an empire anymore" CUT 2026-06-08 read-aloud — it overclaimed independence and contradicted the next beat's "owed allegiance to a sultan or a shah"; the honest frame is self-GOVERNANCE, tribe→state, not independence-from-empire. Read-aloud catches cross-beat contradictions a clean-looking line hides.**) "So you've got a people running their own courts, minting their own coins, raising their own armies. At that point, whatever you want to call it, this isn't a tribe — it's a state running itself."
- **The staccato STAB-VERDICT is DEMOTED to rare-exception (was ✅, now dispreferred, 2026-06-07).** A verdict chopped into its own short sentence after a period reads as machine.
  - ⚠️ (dispreferred) "…by any working definition, that's not a tribe. It's a state." — the "It's a state." period-stab is the staccato; prefer the flowing landing above.
  - ✅ (OK — one connected sentence, no stab) "That's not a tribe — that's a functioning state."
  - ❌ "That is not a tribe. That is a state." / "Their own courts. Their own coins. Their own armies." (chopped — rejected live)
  - **THE ONE EARNED period-stab — DEADPAN DEFLATION (#57 Piri Reis, live pick HIT 2026-06-08).** When the curt stab's function is to *puncture a mystique you just built up* (deadpan-by-understatement; see §Humor) — NOT to assert a verdict — the flat two-word stab IS the joke, and it's earned. ✅ "It sounds like it should mean something: the title of some old, mysterious class of map, old enough to carry lost knowledge. **It isn't.**" (the buildup→curt-puncture is the comic timing). ❌ flowing it kills the deadpan: "…old enough to carry lost knowledge — except it doesn't mean anything at all." (lost live). **Discriminator: a deflation-of-a-buildup earns the stab; a verdict-assertion ("It's a state.") does not — flow that one.** This is the only standing exception to the period-stab demotion.
- **Fragments** only for a proper-noun reveal landing ("…Saddam Hussein.") or a single genuinely rare beat. Never triplets, never chopped lists, never a period-stab verdict as the default.
- **Don't tack a key payoff onto a dash as an afterthought — lead with it or give it a clean landing (2026-06-08 #58 read-aloud).** ✅ "And for three hundred years, they did." ❌ "And they did — for three hundred years." (the duration dangling off the dash deflates the payoff; front it or land it clean).
- **An evocative DETAIL/fragment must carry a clear PURPOSE or referent, not be dropped for atmosphere (2026-06-08 #58 read-aloud).** ❌ "A dynasty writing down its own kings, four centuries ago." failed read-aloud — evocative but the viewer can't tell *why* it's there → rewritten to state the point: "Four hundred years ago, the Kurds already had dynasties worth writing the history of." Discriminator: clear referent/purpose = keep the fragment; atmosphere-only = state the point.
- **TWO conditions for an earned standalone fragment — clear referent AND it must BE the landing, not follow one (2026-06-10 #58, REVERSAL).** The close "A country." was held up here as the canonical earned-fragment (referent explicit = the one thing they never got) — but after the Act-4 trim he **cut it**, flagging "doesn't fit at all." The reason: the rewritten close already lands hard on "…made sure that idea never became reality," so "A country." became a **second kicker after a clean landing** (fails BAR-TALK check #5, no second kicker). So a clear referent is necessary but NOT sufficient: if the preceding sentence already lands the beat, the trailing fragment is redundant and gets cut. **The fragment must carry the landing itself, not echo a landing that already happened.** Calibration: a previously-"earned" fragment can stop fitting when the surrounding beat is rewritten — re-test it on read-aloud, never assume it still earns its place.
- **⭐ THE GOVERNING DISTINCTION for short-sentence rhythm (2026-06-08, TWO live MISSES — #4 Crusades + #51 Tripoli — read this before "fixing" any chopped-looking beat).** The choice is NOT "flowing vs chopped" globally; it's **what the units ARE:**
  - **Distinct sequential STEPS, each a different point** → clean short COMPLETE declarative sentences, separate, are right (and build momentum). ✅ #4 turn: "The video makes five major claims. Every one leaves out critical evidence. I went to the primary sources… And it's not what this video tells you." (count → omission → action → reveal = four different steps). [I predicted the flowing rewrites; he kept the clean-declarative build — MISS.] **Also covers distinct ESCALATING CLAIMS, not just narrative steps (#57 Piri Reis cold open, live pick HIT 2026-06-08):** the wild-claim pile (accurate map → lost-civ proof → alien proof) builds best as complete-sentence anaphora with a verb each time — ✅ "People have called it the most accurate map… They've called it proof of a seafaring civilization… They've even called it proof the Earth was visited by aliens." The escalation/momentum is the point, so flow drowns it (❌ the em-dash "…most accurate map — proof of…, even proof the Earth was visited by aliens" lost). **Discriminator within this case: COMPLETE-sentence anaphora is the vehicle; the FRAGMENT anaphora is the banned form** (❌ "Proof of a seafaring civilization… Proof the Earth was visited by aliens." — verbless, the cold-open fragment-punch). Anaphora is never the problem; fragments are.
  - **PARALLEL list-items of ONE idea** (esp. a negation series "not X, not Y, not Z") → **FLOW them into one connected sentence that lands on the resolution.** ✅ #51 reveal: "It wasn't mistranslated, or paraphrased, or softened — it simply isn't there." (three facets of one "it's not-X" point). ❌ chopping that into "It isn't mistranslated. It isn't paraphrased. It just isn't there." (my prediction — MISS) AND ❌ the fragments "Not mistranslated. Not paraphrased. Missing." (both lost live).
  - **ASYMMETRIC reveal + verdict pair** (a concrete REVEAL bolted to a VERDICT-negation) → **flow the reveal INTO the connected clause; give the verdict ONE clean short-sentence landing** — not two stabs, not full-flow. ✅ #57 Piri Reis (live pick 2026-06-08): "…copied, name for name, from a map by Christopher Columbus, a man who died in 1506. Not the relic of a lost civilization." (the date-reveal "1506" rides the clause; the verdict "Not the relic…" lands clean and ALONE). ❌ the double-stab "Not twelve thousand years old. The work of a man who died in 1506." (two fragments, reveal buried last). ❌ the full-flow "…a man who died in 1506, not a relic twelve thousand years old." (verdict loses its earned punch — **my prediction, MISS toward flow: I over-applied the #51 symmetric-facets rule to an asymmetric pair**). **Discriminator:** symmetric facets of one kind → all flow (#51); a reveal-then-verdict pair → flow the reveal, LAND the verdict.
  - **Fragments** (no verb) lose either way, except the rare proper-noun reveal landing.
  - **Confirmed on a second topic (2026-07-20 grill, #62 Volhynia): the MID-PARAGRAPH register-drop pivot fragment ("…and it went on for years. Then came Sachsenhausen.") loses to the flowing version too** — it's not just close/landing fragments that die, a pivot-into-the-next-beat fragment dies the same way. Anti-staccato has no pivot-position carve-out; the only standing exception anywhere in this rule is the rare proper-noun reveal landing.
  This is the §Sentence-rhythm face of §Enumeration ("flowing parallel prose for short list-items; separate beats only when each carries a full explanatory clause"). Anti-staccato bans FRAGMENTS + period-stab VERDICTS as the *default rhythm* ("Their own courts. Their own coins." / "That is not a tribe. That is a state.") — it does NOT ban clean declaratives for distinct steps.

- **THE CEILING on "flowing" — flow is NOT a long inverted/periodic sentence (2026-06-10 #58, read-aloud "too long of a sentence").** The anti-staccato rule pushes toward flow, but flow has an upper bound. A sentence that piles subordinate clauses BEFORE the main verb, or inverts into a periodic build, reads as written prose, not speech. ❌ "And only then, with their independence slipping away, did the strongest still standing, Bedirxan of Bohtan, finally try to unite them." (inverted "did the strongest… finally try" + stacked clauses, verb arrives late). ✅ split it and lead subject-verb: "By now the princes could see what was coming. With their independence slipping away, the strongest still standing — Bedirxan of Bohtan — finally tried to unite them." Tell: the main verb arrives late, or the sentence inverts ("only then did X…"). Break it; lead with who-did-what. (This is the §Sentence-rhythm face of "no purple/writerly prose" — the OTHER end of the dial from staccato.)

  - ❌ "Almost nobody knows he was a Kurd." (RETIRED — bare over-explain; the corpus opener-craft anti-pattern "most people have never heard of him." Replaced by the framed "not X but Y" above.)
  - ❌ "The most famous Muslim hero of the Crusades was a Kurd." (bare one-liner — rejected; too compressed to open on)

### Transitions (HIS #1 STANDING COMPLAINT — get this right)
**The rule: a transition must be THESIS-FORWARD and hand directly into the next beat's subject.** Name where we're going — tightly — and/or state the causal link to it. Never just sit adjacent. Three validated forms, all thesis-forward:
- **Thesis-forward (state the through-line of what's coming):** ✅ "What comes next is one pattern, on repeat: the Kurds get armed when they're useful, and abandoned the moment they're not." (chosen over the vaguer "you get the part you've probably half-heard")
- **Plain causal bridge that names the next subject:** ✅ "So those states didn't just fade out. Someone took them apart — and it was the empire they'd served for three centuries." (chosen over the abstract question-bridge — it hands straight into Act 2's subject)
- **Thesis-pointed question, then flow into a plain statement** (one tool among several): ✅ "So if the Kurds had states, the real question is who took the ones they had." → then plainly into the next beat.

| "I guess" / "it will not be perfect" humility hedges | **Drop them** — that was first-video nerves. Confident-but-plain. | "That's what I'm trying to do with this channel." |
| Vivid casual nouns ("cram dates", "turn you into a parrot") | **Keep vivid CONCRETE imagery; cut the cutesy metaphor.** | "cramming dates into your head until you can repeat them back" (kept "cram", cut "parrot") |
| Two-pole "either…or" framing | **Genuinely his — keep it, tight.** | "Every history teacher is one of two things: the one everyone remembers, or the one who put you to sleep." |
| Forward-tease to next video | **Warm, first-person, understated.** | "Next time, I'm looking at the claim — pushed by Putin himself — that Ukraine never really existed as a state. There's a lot to say about that one." |

**The bar-talk register is CALM bar-talk** — a smart, even-tempered friend explaining and conceding, not a passionate rant. **Concede-first is built into his natural speech** ("interesting to watch… *but* little basis") and is the single most reliable scripted move.

## Worked exemplar (imitate this, don't just obey the rules)

**From-scratch cold open (flat-Earth, the accepted from-scratch model — famous-then-puncture + concede-first + two medium sentences):**

> Everyone knows the story. Columbus sailed west to prove the Earth was round, while everyone back home was sure he'd drop off the edge. It's a great story — and historians have known it's false for over a hundred years. Nobody in the Middle Ages actually thought the world was flat. The idea that they did was invented in the 1800s, by Victorian writers who needed the Middle Ages to look stupid.

*Why it works:* famous thing set up, **then** punctured (not a bare one-liner); concede-first ("It's a great story —" before the denial); two medium sentences carry the mechanism, not a run-on; the heat is **specific + attributed** (named *who*: Victorian writers, and *why*: needed the Middle Ages to look stupid), never an abstract sermon; plain dates ("the 1800s", "over a hundred years"), no scale comparison.

> **⚠️ Accuracy note (corrected 2026-06-08, live pick).** An earlier draft of this exemplar read "the people who actually believed in a flat medieval Earth … were Victorian writers" — which is FALSE: the Victorians didn't believe the Earth was flat, they *invented the myth* that medieval people did. **A voice exemplar gets the same accuracy/logic gate as a script** — the creator catches logic errors even inside a voice drill ([[feedback-read-aloud-catches-logic]]), and a garbled exemplar is *worse* than a garbled draft line because it propagates into every future script that imitates it. When showing exemplar/option text, run the historian gate (9-check #9) on it, not just the voice gate. The from-scratch, Claude-fabricated exemplars are the highest risk; the ones derived from verified research carry their own sourcing.

**Before → after (generic-explainer draft → bar-talk rewrite; raw material = the #58 Sèvres beat):**

> ❌ **Generic explainer:** "It is often claimed that the Kurds were betrayed at the Paris Peace Conference, and there is some truth to this narrative. The Treaty of Sèvres, signed in 1920, did contain provisions promising the Kurdish people an independent state, creating expectations that were ultimately never fulfilled by the great powers."
>
> ✅ **Bar-talk (his voice):** "The famous part is true, as far as it goes. In 1920, a treaty really did promise the Kurds a state — the Treaty of Sèvres. But read the fine print: that independence was conditional, it ran on a one-year clock, and it never happened at all. Sèvres was void before the ink was dry."

*Rules that fired:* **concede-first** ("The famous part is true, as far as it goes" before the catch); **tighten** (cut "It is often claimed… narrative… creating expectations… ultimately not fulfilled" — Latinate padding → plain Anglo-Saxon); **two medium sentences** for the mechanism (conditional · one-year clock · never happened), not one dense compound; **plain numbers** ("1920", "one-year clock" stated flat, no comparison); **thesis-forward landing** (the verdict "void before the ink was dry" — a scoped primary/scholar quote, not a recap).
