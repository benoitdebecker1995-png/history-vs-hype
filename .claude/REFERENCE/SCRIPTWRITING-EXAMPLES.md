## §2: Logic Bridge Examples

```
WRONG: "The treaty gave control. They challenged it in 1945."
RIGHT: "The treaty gave control. For 86 years they accepted this. That's why their 1945 challenge looks suspicious—why cooperate for a century if invalid?"
```

Bridge phrases: "BECAUSE X, THEREFORE Y", "This matters because...", "Translation:"

## §4: Spoken Delivery Patterns

### Informational Fragment Fixes

| Written (choppy) | Spoken (flows) |
|---|---|
| "Ambassadors. Embassies." | "...including the exchange of ambassadors and the opening of embassies." |
| "Population: 4.5M. GDP: $2.1B." | "Somaliland has a population of 4.5 million and a GDP of $2.1 billion." |
| "World War I. Foreign occupation. Famine." | "World War I, foreign occupation, famine." |

### Colon → Period for Spoken Pauses

| Written | Spoken |
|---|---|
| "They had a problem: they were broke." | "They had a problem. They were broke." |
| "The result: Khomeini won." | "The result was that Khomeini won." |

Exception: Colons for introducing direct quotes: "The treaty states: 'All territories...'"

### Natural Delivery Patterns (from A-roll analysis)
- Expand abbreviations: "African Union" not "AU"
- Drop parenthetical asides: "Ethiopia is landlocked" not "Ethiopia—120 million people—is landlocked"
- Uncontract for emphasis: "That is true." (emphatic) vs "That's true." (casual)
- No quote markers: Don't say "quote" — document on screen provides signal
- Explicit transitions: "The deal was this." not "The deal:"
- Personal ownership: "Here's what I found" not "they found"
- Polite CTAs: "please subscribe"

## §5: Debunking Framework — The Big Seven

| Principle | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Fact-First Headlines** | Lead with CORRECT FACT, not myth | "Medieval scholars knew earth was spherical" |
| **2. Alternative Explanations** | Fill mental gaps, don't just negate | "Columbus argued about SIZE: 20,400 vs 24,901 miles" |
| **3. KISS Principle** | 3 key points MAX per section | Too many counter-arguments make simple myth more attractive |
| **4. Self-Affirmation** | Acknowledge shared values FIRST (high-stake only) | "National heritage matters—that's why getting the record right is crucial..." |
| **5. Toulmin Model** | Make logical bridges EXPLICIT | "The key principle here is..." or "That's why..." |
| **6. Source Credibility** | Expose WHY myth was created | "Irving needed a villain. Draper needed anti-Catholic ammunition." |
| **7. Seixas Big Six** | Teach historical thinking invisibly | Significance, Evidence, Cause/Consequence, Perspective |

## §7: Transition Phrase Libraries

### Micro-Transitions (Hook-and-Eye)

| Technique | Last Sentence | First Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Question → Answer** | End with rhetorical question | Open with answer |
| **Name drop → Explain** | End by naming something unexplained | Open with explanation |
| **Pattern plant → Callback** | "Keep that in mind." | "Remember that [X]?" |
| **Consequence tease** | End with ominous foreshadow | Open with consequence |
| **Evidence → Implication** | End with a quote | "Basically, what that means is..." |

### Evidence-to-Narration Transitions (CRITICAL)

- **"Basically" decode:** "Basically, what he just said is..." (after legal/technical jargon)
- **Critical evaluation:** "Now I don't know any way of reading this other than..." (after self-incriminating quote)
- **Modern reframe:** "To me, [archaic phrase] just sounds like..." (after archaic language)
- **"So what" pivot:** "That's from a Christian source. Not Muslim propaganda." (after evidence that contradicts a narrative)

### Linking Phrase Upgrades

| Generic (avoid) | Specific (use instead) |
|---|---|
| "However," | "But that wasn't the case." |
| "Additionally," | "And to top it all off..." |
| "Furthermore," | "And here's the real kicker..." |
| "In conclusion," | "So where does that leave us?" |
| "Moving on," | "Which brings us to..." |

### Macro-Transitions (Section-to-Section)

| Type | Template | HvH Version |
|---|---|---|
| **Prerequisite Pivot** | "To understand why [X]... we need to look at..." | "To understand why this clause matters, we need to look at..." |
| **Contextual Flashback** | "Before we can get to [X], it's important to..." | "Before we get to the ruling, there's a clause we need to see first." |
| **Analytical Pivot** | "So what's going on here then?" | "So what does the treaty actually say?" |
| **Geographic/Faction Handoff** | "Things were different in [Place]." | "That's the British position. Now let's look at what [other party] was arguing." |
| **Acknowledged Tangent** | "I need to go off on a tangent that will seem unrelated..." | "There's a legal concept we need to understand here..." |

### Mid-Video Escalation Phrases

HvH-adapted (Calm Prosecutor): "But here's where the evidence gets damning." / "And that's not even the strongest piece." / "Look at what happens next."

### Re-Centering After Tangents

- "Which brings us back to [main topic]."
- "Okay, so let's get back to our timeline."
- "Here we are again! I told you this will be a recurring theme."

## §9: Rebuttal Technique Examples

### 1. Source-Flip

| Signal Phrase | Example |
|---|---|
| "So the stats come from [Source X]. Let's take a look..." | Shaun reads opponent's cited source — it contradicts their claim |
| "This is taken from the abstract of the cited paper:" | Three Arrows reads Crowder's cited paper — contradicts Crowder |

Sub-variant — Chain Audit: Trace citation → their citation → original data. Shaun: Lynn → Wober 1969 → data called "virtually unusable."

### 2. Hypothetical Concession

- "Even if we accept..." / "Even if we assume..."
- "Let's imagine that..." / "Let's say that..."
- System Trap: Walk through every proposed solution and show each one is blocked.

### 3. Forensic Detail Accumulation

Sweet spot: 4-7 items. Save most damning for last. Shaun variant: Stop at 4 and say "I trust you get the point."

### 4. Omission Exposure

"They quote Article III. Here's Article III in full — notice what they left out."

### 5. Contradiction Catalogue

"In 1884, they argued X. But in 1919, they argued the opposite."

## §10: Data Delivery Connector Phrases

### Equivalency Bridge (abstract → physical picture)

- "or as much [X] as..." / "or enough to [verb]..."
- "which is comparable to..." / "that's about the same as..."
- "roughly the size of Chicago" / "the population of Pittsburgh"

### Modern Translation (historical → present-day stakes)

- "which is [amount] in today's money"
- "$75 million. That's over a trillion today."

### Perspective Shift (violent reference frame pivot)

- "about the same number died on this single beach as the entire 13 years of Afghanistan"
- "Just for perspective: an NBA basketball has a circumference of only 29.5 inches."

## §16: Voice Examples (Few-Shot — From Published Scripts)

### Causal Chain + Source Attribution (Iran Part 1 — 30%+ retention)

> Government bankruptcy. Spiraling inflation. The Shah couldn't pay his debts. So for decades, the Qajars had been selling off pieces of Iran to foreign powers—telegraph rights, banking rights, tobacco rights, oil rights. Each sale made the Shah more dependent on foreigners and less responsive to Iranians.
>
> So when the governor beat those merchants, he wasn't just enforcing price controls. He was defending a system that had sold the country piece by piece while ordinary people starved.
>
> And that's when something unprecedented happened.

### Document Reveal + Legal Analysis (Vichy)

> Article 2 is the ban list. And when you read it, you understand the scale.
>
> Head of State. Every court, from the supreme court down to the local magistrate. Every elected assembly. All prefects. All police. All diplomats. All colonial governors. All military officers. All teachers. Any director of a company receiving public money.
>
> That's not a ban on a few senior positions. That's a systematic removal from every institution that exercises authority, shapes culture, or controls information.

### Legal Precedent Made Accessible (Belize — 23K views)

> Britain never built it. Guatemala filed their ICJ memorial in December 2020 arguing: no road means the treaty's void. Belize's counter-memorial in June 2022: the road clause was separate from the boundary. Breach of Article 7 doesn't void Article 1.
>
> The legal test comes from the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, Article 60.
>
> So: Was that road essential to the treaty's purpose? Three ICJ cases give us the answer.

### Both Extremes Wrong (Iran Part 1 — opening)

> If you ask online about the situation in Iran, you'll get two answers.
>
> One side says it's America's fault. The 1953 coup destroyed Iranian democracy. Everything since is blowback.
>
> The other side says Iran is just culturally authoritarian. Nothing will change until they have their own Enlightenment.
>
> So I did some digging. Read books about the topic. Then went to the primary sources.
>
> The truth is, both narratives are wrong. And they're wrong because they start in the wrong place.

## §20: Topic-Type Structural Blueprints

### TERRITORIAL/BORDER
- **Opening:** Cold Fact + Question. Map on screen. Add document tease.
- **Turn timing:** Late (45-50%) or early IF myth exists.
- **Source density:** Low verbal, high visual (maps + on-screen data).
- **Energy arc:** Steady build → geographic revelation → geopolitical warning.
- **Closing:** Geopolitical Warning. Loop back to opening map.
- **Model:** WonderWhy.

### IDEOLOGICAL/MYTH-BUSTING
- **Opening:** Standard Myth Subversion. Tell wrong version → shatter.
- **Turn timing:** EARLY (15-25%). Cognitive dissonance from sources.
- **Source density:** Very high (20+ citations). Full credential chains.
- **Energy arc:** HIGH → low steelman → HIGH turn → oscillating → HIGHEST climax → quiet verdict.
- **Closing:** Philosophical Synthesis. Loop back to opening myth.
- **Model:** Shaun / Knowing Better.

### COLONIAL/PATTERN
- **Opening:** Mechanism hook: "Here's the pattern..." + Scale beat.
- **Turn timing:** Mid (35-45%). Reveal mechanism repeats.
- **Source density:** Moderate. Legal documents + firsthand accounts.
- **Energy arc:** Scale hook → context → pattern reveal → escalating repetitions.
- **Closing:** Pattern synthesis. "The playbook hasn't changed."
- **Model:** Knowing Better + Kraut.

### MECHANISM/HOW
- **Opening:** Definitional Correction or Systemic Paradox.
- **Structure:** Macro → Micro → Stress-Test.
- **Source density:** Moderate verbal. Authority through data density + proper nouns.
- **Energy arc:** Correction → system build → stress variable → consequences → quiet close.
- **Closing:** Systemic Verdict or Unresolved Status Quo.
- **Model:** Wendover + RealLifeLore.

### Cross-Type FAILURE MODES (never do)
- Narrative opening on myth-busting → massive drop-off
- Minimal sourcing on myth-busting → no credibility
- Point-by-point rebuttal on territorial → breaks chronological flow
- Myth-busting structure on mechanism topic → no lie to bust
