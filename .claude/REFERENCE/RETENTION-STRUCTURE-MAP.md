# Retention Structure Map — sustaining the gap past the opener (8–12 min explainer)

**Scope:** This is CRAFT RESEARCH (behind camera) — how to keep curiosity alive *after* the 30-second opener so a strong hook doesn't bleed out in the body. Distilled from the Opener-Craft notebook (McKee *Story*, Yorke *Into the Woods*, Made to Stick / Cialdini). Companion to `OPENER-CRAFT-BRIEF.md` (first 30s) and `OPENER-PLAYBOOK.md`. Source: `OPENER-CRAFT-BRIEF-2.md` §2.

**Why it matters:** The opener wins the first 30s. Everything after is sustained by *systematic gap management*, not repeated surprise. Made to Stick: "Surprise gets our attention. Interest keeps our attention." A great opener with no sustain mechanism still bleeds between 0:30 and 2:00.

---

## The time map (scale to runtime; figures shown for a 10-min video)

```
0:00–0:30   MACRO-GAP OPENS
            State the unresolved question / contradicted claim. Do NOT resolve it.
            Name the document/evidence that eventually will.

0:30–2:30   BRIDGE / SUB-PLOT
            Deploy a SECOND open question or a competing claim to hold interest
            until the primary evidence lands. Introduce context in clue-form
            (Made to Stick "treasure map, not MapQuest" — landmarks, not directions).
            End the segment on a NEW question (Yorke "top-spin").

~1:30–2:30  FIRST MAJOR TURN  (land at 15-25% of runtime — land early; McKee's 25% = OUTER bound, not the target; 25-35% = documented dead zone)
            Introduce the inciting piece of evidence. It partially resolves the
            sub-gap but DEEPENS the macro-gap: "Now you know X. But that raises Y."

~4:00       MID-VIDEO CALLBACK TO THE MACRO-GAP
            Explicitly reference the original unresolved question (Cialdini's
            "return to the mystery in the middle"). Re-anchors anyone drifting.

~6:00–7:00  PENULTIMATE TURN
            Complicate the macro-gap — a counter-claim, a scholar who disagrees,
            a document that cuts the other way (Yorke's "dying sun": the segment
            resolves but opens the FINAL question).

8:30–10:00  MACRO-GAP RESOLUTION
            The primary evidence resolves the opened question — the "Aha!"
            (Cialdini's students stayed past the bell for exactly this).
```

---

## The three engines

1. **Top-spin (Yorke):** every segment ends on a question — "answer, then new question, then (delayed) answer." Chain it: each answered question opens the next. The Calm-Prosecutor version: withhold the *resolution/meaning* of the evidence, never the evidence itself. Show the document; don't say what it proves yet. (Yorke admits raw withholding "feels cheap" — showing-but-not-interpreting is the honest form.)

2. **The turn (McKee):** land the first major event at **15-25% of runtime** (~1:30–2:30 of a 10-min video) — land early. McKee's 25% is the OUTER bound, not the target; 25-35% is a documented dead zone. A sub-plot/second question must bridge 0:30→turn. Every turn delivers four effects: surprise, increased curiosity, insight, new direction.

3. **Three-touch macro-gap (Cialdini, via Made to Stick):** state the mystery at the start (0:30), **return to it in the middle** (~4:00), resolve it at the end (8:30+). The middle touch is the one creators skip — it's what re-hooks drifters.

---

## Hard don'ts

- **No new gaps in the final ~2 minutes.** Viewers who invested 8+ min have earned a clean resolution. Yorke: a story's only fault is "making the audience not want to know what happens next."
- **No MapQuest context.** Don't front-load full background; drop one clue, then the next. Context is the reward, not the hook.
- **No un-bridged 0:30–2:30 dead zone.** If nothing pulls between the opener and the first turn, the opener's gains leak here.
