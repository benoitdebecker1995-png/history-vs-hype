# PART 5 — TECHNIQUES TOOLKIT (cross-medium)

*Techniques are tools, not defaults. Overuse kills the effect — use judgment about when each one serves the specific piece. The user's principle: "these are just methods, some work in some instances."*

Copy-paste patterns for rapid script improvement during writing and revision.

## 5.1 Creator phrase library

### Kraut patterns (deep causal chains)

```
"And once every few centuries they [verb]..."
"However, as [adjective] as these [things] were, a curious thing about them was that..."
"There is however one big exception..."
"This resulted in the geopolitical shift..."
"Throughout the rest of Europe, [X]. But Russia remained untouched by these developments."
"The [institution] was derived from [Y], and [characteristic] meant that..."
"Meanwhile, in the rest of Europe, [contrast]."
"You can see [historical pattern] almost reflected in [modern parallel]."
"While in Europe [X happened], in Russia [opposite happened]."
"In Europe, [institution] had [function]. In Russia, there was no [equivalent]."
"A key difference to other parts of Europe was that..."
"One can only imagine what [place] would look like if [alternative history]."
"This is what makes it so unique."
"A mistake often made though is to assume that these developments were unavoidable."
"As the historian [name] described it, [quote]"
"Medieval accounts tell us of [detail]."
```

### Knowing Better patterns (source verification)

```
"Let's take a look at the facts and see if we can't figure out which ones have been misunderstood, misattributed, exaggerated, and sometimes straight-up fabricated."
"What do you notice about this quote? How about the fact that it's neither the beginning nor the end of the sentence."
"In full context the word [X] could mean [A] or [B] or [C]."
"So we're gonna have to look it up. And here it is."
"Let's just shove it into [tool] and see what we get."
"These aren't his own words because his real name wasn't [English name] it was [original name]."
"Now we have to translate it."
"It takes some linguistic gymnastics to get from [original] to [translation]."
"They picked the absolute worst most biased translation to quote."
"See what he did there? He took something that [person] [did once] and made it look like..."
"This is something that you've heard everywhere and it's probably already down in the comments."
"That's quite the contrast. Well let's take a look at the facts."
"You might think [assumption]. You'd be wrong."
"50 years is a long time. That's two or three generations."
"He's attributing an entire 50-year span to one person."
```

### Shaun patterns (document analysis, dry precision)

```
"So that's quite a simple story there. Presented like this, [action] was pretty unambiguously the right thing to do. When compared to the worst alternative anyway."
"This is [title] who was [role] and [credential]."
"And after the war, with regards to [topic], [person] wrote the following:"
"So what's going on here then?"
"Now I don't know any way of reading this other than..."
"They want to [do X]. They have decided to [do X]. And they know it. But they're too cowardly to admit it."
"What a load of rubbish honestly."
"More like Joseph Stalling." (wordplay)
"...because some ministers weren't available sooner. What were they doing? Oh they had something more important to do clearly."
"There are three possible explanations that I can imagine."
"All these options are just speculation on my part. However if I was forced to guess I'd say..."
"This is particularly concerning in light of the fact that..."
"By no stretch of the imagination can [X] be considered [Y]."
"It is in fact the exact place you would [verb] if you were trying to [opposite of claimed intent]."
"More than a full day after [event], [person] received the following message..."
"On the [date], just prior to [event]..."
"This was [timeframe] before [other event], meaning that..."
```

### Alex O'Connor patterns (intellectual humility)

```
"I might just be missing something here."
"Church patristics is really not my area. I don't know very much here."
"I'm genuinely interested in seeing where [person] has gotten this evidence."
"That's not to say that I can prove [X] or that [person] was mistaken or lying..."
"Sure [valid point]. But [important caveat]."
"Right off the bat this isn't really an argument for [X], just for the idea that [Y]."
"I agree that this is how a theist should respond to [objection]."
"Yeah I agree with this. It's a bad argument if it is an argument."
"Look you're right. I wouldn't claim to have discovered some new [thing] that nobody has noticed before."
"Okay [dramatic claim]. Sounds awesome. I would love to take a look at that [documentation]. I get the feeling you're not going to provide any, are you?"
"That hardly fulfills my evidential expectations."
"This is in a different category of evidence."
"That's a far cry from making any commentary about [claimed point]."
"So it seems like when an atheist is arguing [X], [response A]. But when it comes to [Y], hey look at [opposite response]."
"Didn't you literally just say that [contradicting statement]?"
"Look I don't know what [detail] really was."
"Some people claim [A]. Some people think [B]. I don't know."
"For what it's worth..."
"I mean to use a crude example..."
"I'm just going to be blunt here. I think that [direct assessment]."
```

### Universal phrases (introducing surprise, presenting documents, etc.)

**Introducing surprising info:**

```
"This might surprise you but..."
"The reality is more complicated — and more interesting."
"However, this is [title] who was [credential]. And [their view] was that..."
"Here's a question for you: [question]. You don't have to answer right now but just tuck that away."
```

**Presenting documents:**

```
"Let's take a look at the facts."
"Here's what we're looking for from [date/source]."
"So we're gonna have to look it up. And here it is."
"In full context..."
```

**Acknowledging complexity:**

```
"There are [number] possible explanations I can imagine."
"All these options are just speculation on my part."
"I don't know the actual reason. I only know that [observable fact]."
"If I was forced to guess I'd say..."
```

**Calling out bad arguments:**

```
"What a load of rubbish honestly."
"By no stretch of the imagination can [X] be considered [Y]."
"They're too cowardly to admit it so they're pretending..."
"This is what they really mean: [actual meaning]."
```

**Sarcasm / dry humor (use 1-2 per script max):**

```
"I'm joking. [Person] was ignored obviously."
"[Sarcastic aside]. Just kidding."
"More like [wordplay]."
"What were they doing? Oh they had something more important to do clearly."
```

**Ending sections:**

```
"And this is important for us here because..."
"Which brings us to..."
"So that's [summary]. Let's talk about [next topic]."
"You can see [pattern] almost reflected in [modern example]."
```

## 5.2 Hook formulas

High-level — for full templates with retention data, see `OPENING-HOOK-TEMPLATES.md`.

### Hook Type 1: Data Comparison (Kraut)

Development divergence, inequality, border effects.

```
[City/Region A]: Population: [X]. Median income: $[Y]. Education rate: [Z]%.
Just [distance] away, across the [border]...
[City/Region B]: Median income: $[much lower]. Education: [lower]%.
Same geography. Same resources. Same people, divided.
What created this gap?
```

### Hook Type 2: Common Knowledge Trap (Knowing Better)

Myth-busting, "everyone knows" topics.

```
[Rhetorical question that seems to have obvious answer]?
[Obvious answer]. Everyone knows that.
Still wrong.
```

Examples:

- "When did slavery end in America? 1865? Everyone knows that. Still wrong."
- "What caused the fall of Rome? Barbarian invasions? Everyone knows that. Still wrong."

### Hook Type 3: Visual-First Map (Johnny Harris / RealLifeLore)

Territorial disputes, border anomalies.

```
I want to show you [number] [borders/territories] that are very peculiar.
[SHOW MAP]
Not just because they look weird on a map — but because of what they mean in real life.
```

### Hook Type 4: Pattern + Exception (Kraut)

Comparative history.

```
[Pattern with 3-4 examples]:
The [Group A] conquered [Place] and became [Identity A].
The [Group B] conquered [Place] and became [Identity B].
The [Group C] conquered [Place] and became [Identity C].

There is, however, one big exception: [Your subject].
```

### Hook Type 5: Both Extremes Wrong (Channel Signature)

```
There are two narratives you'll hear about [topic].
One side says: [Extreme A — present fairly].
The other says: [Extreme B — present fairly].
When you examine the evidence, both oversimplify what actually happened.
```

### Hook Type 6: Visual Contrast (Belize, 23K views)

Territorial disputes with map contradictions.

```
Open a map of [region], you see [entity], [brief description].
Now open a [opposing party]'s map. [Entity] disappears, instead you see [their claim name].
[Opposing party] claims [territory description] as their own.
Both sides point to [document/year] to prove who's right.
```

### Hook Type 7: Current Event Hook with Precise Details (Essequibo, 35.6% retention)

Active disputes with military dimension.

```
[Specific date], [time], [military/diplomatic action with exact numbers].
[Quote or dialogue].
But [contradiction — what's actually true].
To understand what's really happening, I went to [primary source type].
```

### Hook Type 8: Fact-Check Declaration (Vance, 42.6% retention)

Political figure fact-checks.

```
[Quote from political figure making historical claim].
I fact-checked [name]'s claims with [academic sources/primary documents].
Here's what [historians/archaeologists/documents] actually say.
```

### Hook Type 9: Personal Research Authority (Belize variant)

Treaty disputes.

```
Both sides cite [document/treaty] to prove their case.
So, I read that [document].
[Side A] says [claim].
But the [document] actually [what it says].
[Current stakes: ongoing case/modern consequence].
```

### Hook Type 10: Mystery Hook (Almada)

Stories about discovered documents.

```
For [timeframe] it was dismissed as [how it was framed].
[Extreme claim].
Anyone who said it out loud was [how they were treated].
Then [unlikely person with personal stakes] [action] and found [evidence type].
```

### Hook Type 11: "Zoom In" (RealLifeLore)

```
[Subject] looks pretty normal when you look at it on a map,
but things begin to get very strange when you zoom in just a little closer.
```

### Hook Type 12: "In X Words" Summary (Shaun)

Conspiracy debunking, widely-believed myths.

```
[Complete summary of topic in one paragraph].
In [number] words, I just gave you the basic story.
However, [large percentage] of people watching this will think some or most
of what I just said is wrong.
I'm about to prove why [thesis statement].
```

### Hook Type 13: Paradox Opening (Wendover)

Logistics, systems, counterintuitive topics.

```
This is one of the most [extreme descriptor] [things] on Earth.
But it doesn't look like it.
It's as far from [A] as [B]. As far from [C] as [D]. As far from [E] as [F].
That's all to say: it's weird how [observation].
```

### Hook Type 14: Double Assumption Subversion (PolyMatter)

Topics where viewers think they know the answer.

```
[Describe situation that seems to have obvious interpretation]
You might assume [logical conclusion A]. But you'd be wrong. [Why not A]
Knowing this, you might instead conclude [logical conclusion B]. Again, wrong. [Why not B]
So, what is it?
```

## 5.3 Bridge phrases and connectors

### Author's natural connectors (use these first)

- "On top of that..."
- "At the same time..."
- "This brings us to..."

### Causal connectors (full set in 2.4)

`consequently`, `thereby`, `which meant that`, `because... and because`, `this resulted in`, `as a result`.

### Past-to-present

- "This structure would remain in place throughout [region] and, to some extent, still to this very day." (Kraut)
- "You can see the echoes of this today in [modern example]."
- "Centuries later, the same pattern repeated when [modern example]."
- News clip injection: `[Historical/geographic explanation] [CUT TO NEWS: "[Relevant headline quote]"] That's not ancient history. That's [year].`

### Pivots

- "In fact..." (myth → truth)
- "The upshot is..." (data → verdict)
- "Consider for example..." (abstract → visceral)
- "To be fair..." (steelman / counter)
- "What was the secret of success?" (question as bridge)

### "But here's where it gets interesting" (1x per script)

Major pivot only. Don't burn it.

## 5.4 Decoder phrases and source-flip patterns

### Decoder phrases (translate jargon/claim into plain meaning)

- "In simpler terms..."
- "Translation:"
- "The Court is saying:"
- "In other words..." — allowed when quote needs translation (legalese, archaic prose) OR explication (meaning is technically plain but significance isn't obvious). Forbidden as filler before a paraphrase the reader could have already grasped.
- "What that actually means is..."

### Source-flip introduction phrases (Rule 55)

Use when reading an opponent's own cited source against them:

| Template | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| "So the [stats/claims] come from [Source X]. Let's take a look at those..." | When opponent cites a specific report/study |
| "This is taken from the abstract of the cited paper:" | When opponent cites academic research |
| "And here it is, [paper/treaty title], and let's read a little of that..." | When tracing a citation chain deeper |
| "I found [this source] in [opponent's] own [description/footnotes/bibliography], by the way..." | When opponent's own supplementary materials contradict them |
| "Luckily, we can check what it says in the original [language/document]." | When opponent relies on a translation or summary |

**HvH-specific:** "Both sides point to [treaty/ruling]. So, I read [treaty/ruling]." / "Here's what [document] actually says. Article [X]."

### Unanswered question (Almada)

```

So the question isn't just '[historical question].' It's '[present-tense question that applies today].'

```

### Modern relevance (Vance)

```
[X claims checked, Y held up].
Why does anyone in [current year] care about [historical myth]?
Because [politician/movement] isn't just talking about history. [Show recent policy/rhetoric using same logic].
This is the same [pattern].
When [group] rehabilitate [historical narrative], they're not [innocent explanation]. They're [actual purpose].
```


## 5.6 Data connectors and scale anchors

Every statistic needs at least one connector. Three categories:

### Equivalency Bridge (abstract unit → physical picture)

- "or as much [X] as [familiar thing]" — "or as much water as a thousand Amazon rivers"
- "or enough to [verb] [familiar area]" — "or enough to cover North America"
- "which is comparable to [familiar equivalent]" — "comparable to the entire trade volume of Italy"
- "that's about the same as [familiar reference]" — "about the same as Pennsylvania"
- "or [fraction] the [size/area] of [place]" — "or 3/4 the area of Hawaii's Oahu"

### Modern Translation (historical → present-day stakes)

- "which is [amount] in today's money" — "just over a hundred in today's money"
- "If this were to happen in the present-day, it would cost..." — "$75 million. That's over a trillion today."
- "would you [modern action] if it cost you that much?" — "would you vote if it cost you a hundred dollars?"

### Perspective Shift (violent reference-frame pivot)

- "so about the same [X] as [known modern event]" — "same number as the entire 13 years of Afghanistan"
- "which is [multiplier] the number who [suffered in known event]" — "three times the 2004 tsunami deaths"
- "Just for some perspective, [familiar thing] is only [smaller number]." — "an NBA basketball is only 29.5 inches"

### Numerical precision for impact (script-side)

```
[Event/claim]. [Precise number with units: meters, barrels, dollars, years].
[Context: comparison to modern equivalent, what this number meant at the time].
```

**Example (Essequibo):** "March 1st, 2025, 7am, a Venezuelan warship sails within 700 meters of an American oil platform, producing over 200,000 barrels daily."

**Example (Belize):** "A British surveyor estimated 157 miles through dense jungle, costing around 145 pounds, around 18 million in today's money."

## 5.7 Forensic accumulation, source-flip, hypothetical concession

### Forensic accumulation escalation markers

Use between items when listing anomalies/problems sequentially:

- "complicating things further still..."
- "and then to add on even more complexity..."
- "and it gets worse."
- "but that's not the worst part."
- "oh yeah, and [final devastating item]." (HC's deadpan capper)

**Sweet spot:** 4-7 items. Save most damning for last. The Shaun Variant: stop at 4 and say "I trust you get the point" — the sarcastic early stop IS the move.

### Hypothetical concession (Rule 55)

Granting the opponent's strongest premise before showing the conclusion fails:

| Template | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| "Even if we accept the most generous reading of [treaty/source]..." | Grants interpretation, pivots to "the clause still says..." |
| "Let's grant that [opponent's strongest claim]. Even then..." | Concedes premise, shows conclusion doesn't follow |
| "Imagine [opponent's ideal scenario]. What happens next is..." | Builds best case for opponent, shows it still fails |
| "Let's say you'd rather [proposed solution]... Good luck with that." | System Trap variant — walk through escape routes, show each blocked |

### Timeline acceleration (suspicious timing)

```
[Comparable process] took [normal timeframe]. This one took [abbreviated timeframe].
[What the speed reveals: desperation, suspicious timing, predetermined outcome].
```

OR:

```
[Date of trigger event]. [What happened].
[X days/weeks later], [date], [response].
The timing is not a coincidence.
```

### Bureaucratic detail as horror (Almada pattern)

```
They found [specific quantity] of [document type].
The [characterization: bureaucratic memory, filing system] of [regime/organization] that believed [they'd never face consequences].
Mixed in with [routine documents], they found [evidence of crimes].
```

### Post-war lie forensics (Shaun)

Trace how justifications were invented AFTER the event. Compare contemporaneous documents to later claims. Show what they said AT THE TIME vs LATER.

### Stealth debunk (Shaun)

Build entire video as apparent neutral analysis, then reveal at end that you were responding to a specific source all along. Build the case first, reveal the target last.

### "Let me be clear" framing (Shaun)

Anticipate bad-faith objections by clarifying your position before critics can strawman it.

> "I think it's very important to make clear here that nothing we've talked about today should generate any sympathy whatsoever for [X]. The crimes of [X] were dreadful and there is no punishment that perpetrators would not deserve."

## 5.8 Mechanism forensics (HOW > WHY)

The channel's subscriber trigger is "intellectual competence" — showing you understand SYSTEMS, not narratives.

**Instead of:** "[System] is authoritarian"
**Use:** "[System] maintains control through [specific mechanism 1], [mechanism 2], and [mechanism 3]"

### Example (PolyMatter, Singapore elections)

```
The PAP employs a wide range of tools to disadvantage the opposition:
For starters, preparation can begin only after an election is called.
Even then, politicians can't actually campaign.
Since 1968, the legal "campaign period" has lasted no more than 11 days —
giving the opposition less than two weeks to present their case.
In other words, Singaporeans have only been exposed to the alternative
109 days in the past half-century.
It gets far worse.
The Prime Minister has the unchecked right to draw electoral boundaries —
leading to intense gerrymandering.
In 2020, some districts had just 19,000 voters while others had nearly twice that.
One neighborhood has suspiciously been moved between four different districts
from 1988 to 2011.
```

### Application to History vs Hype

- **Colonial extraction:** Don't just say "France exploited Haiti." Show the MECHANISM: forced loans, debt service requirements, trade monopolies.
- **Treaty violations:** Don't just say "X violated the treaty." Show HOW: which article, what loophole, what legal fiction.
- **Border disputes:** Don't just say "The border is contested." Show the MECHANISM of why it's unclear: surveyor error, ambiguous wording, rivers that moved.

### Key phrases

- "It gets far worse."
- "[Specific mechanism] had the effect of [outcome]."
- "In other words, [summary of cumulative effect]."
- "See if you can find what they had in common."

### Step-by-step social hierarchy (Kraut)

For social systems, colonial structures, bureaucracies:

```
[System] worked like this:
At the top: [Group A] — [description/privileges].
Followed by: [Group B] — [description].
Followed by: [Group C] — [description].
At the bottom: [Group Z] — [description/restrictions].
```

The "followed by" rhythm creates momentum.

### Elite vs mass belief distinction

When academic consensus differs from popular understanding:

```
"Among historians, [X] is settled. Among the public, [Y] persists."
"Scholars moved on from this debate in [year]. But the textbooks — and the myths — haven't caught up."
"The academic consensus shifted decades ago. The popular narrative never did."
```

### Scale shift

**Zoom out:** "This one treaty clause → regional instability → global precedent."
**Zoom in:** "This global pattern → but in this one village → here's what it looked like."

```
"One British surveyor drew this line in 1859.
That line became Belize's border.
That border is now before the ICJ.
That ruling will set precedent for every colonial border dispute worldwide.
One line. One surveyor. Global consequences."
```
