# Cromer named, colonial bridge capped at two sentences

The colonial period (post-caliphate to modern day) is covered in two sentences: Lord Cromer named explicitly (Ahmed p. 153), secular and Islamist state responses gestured at collectively. Angle B — a fuller colonial mechanism showing how Western states weaponized the veil debate — was considered and rejected.

The reason: Angle B is a second mechanism. Adding it would require sourcing the colonial administrative logic (Cromer's Egypt reports, Qasim Amin, the nationalist response) at the same depth as the Islamic codification mechanism. That doubles the video's scope, breaks the single-mechanism ADR (0001), and shifts the thesis from "how the ruling was made" to "how it was exploited." Those are different arguments.

Cromer is named because the irony is self-contained in one sentence and does not require setup: the man lecturing Egypt about women's rights was simultaneously president of the Men's League for Opposing Women's Suffrage. No additional sourcing needed. Naming him lands the "same move, different uniforms" point without opening Angle B.

**Considered alternatives:** Full Angle B treatment (Cromer + Atatürk + Reza Shah + nationalist response + 1970s Islamist revival). Rejected: scope doubles, second mechanism introduced, single-mechanism ADR violated.

**Constraint to preserve:** Colonial bridge must read as "new players in an already-political game," not as a new argument. If it reads like a pivot, cut it further.
