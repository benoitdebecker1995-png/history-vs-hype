When a topic already has an existing competitor video that performs primary-source close-reading (Format C / citation archaeology methodology), and that competitor scatters across multiple exhibits/anachronisms/data points — our 5-min compression should NOT be "faster version of all their points." It should be **single-exhibit forensic focus** that goes deeper than they did on one piece of evidence.

**Why:** During 2026-05-08 weekend-test planning for Donation of Constantine video. Top philological competitor (Center for Process Studies, 40K views, 2017) already shows Valla's Latin and performs anachronism checks across multiple words (`satrapas`, tense shifts, geographical errors, "fief"). My initial framing was "do what they do, but in 5 min instead of 37." User stress-tested: "make sure we can make a video about it that is genuinely different." Honest reckoning: a faster CFP would be commercially viable but intellectually empty.

The differentiation came from sharpening to ONE word — *satrapas* — and structuring the entire video around tracing that single word. CFP scatters; we focus. Same evidence base, different rhetorical strategy. The viewer leaves remembering exactly one piece of evidence (which is what Valla himself wanted — he led with satrapas).

**How to apply:**
- Before locking ANY Format C / citation archaeology video, run a competitor positioning audit: identify whether an existing video already does the close-reading methodology
- If yes and they scatter across exhibits → our angle is single-exhibit depth (one word, one date, one paragraph)
- If yes and they focus on one exhibit already → pick a DIFFERENT exhibit they didn't cover, OR add a meta-layer (the document's afterlife after exposure, the forger's identity, etc.)
- If no → standard Format C structure works
- The "Walk-Away Test" applies: the viewer should be able to repeat ONE concrete piece of evidence after watching. Multiple-exhibit videos tend to leave viewers with vague "the Latin is wrong" — single-exhibit videos leave them with "the forger used a Persian word."

**When NOT to apply:** Topics where multiple-exhibit accumulation IS the point (e.g., synchronic interpretive contests, three-case braids per FORMAT-TEMPLATES.md #9-#10). Those formats earn the scatter; Format C compression doesn't.

**Generalizable rule:** Compression edge ≠ summary edge. Compressing 37 min to 5 min is not "the same content, faster." It's "different content, with deeper focus on a smaller subject."
