# Research depth ≠ video heaviness

**Rule:** A well-researched video and a heavy video are not the same thing. The channel goal is to bring the heavy *discipline* of historiography to a broader public — but the OUTPUT is accessible, not heavy.

**Why:** When evaluating whether research is "enough," I have to stop benchmarking against video runtime, claim density, or how-many-sources-look-impressive. The user explicitly does NOT want videos to feel as dense as Manhattan-level when the topic doesn't need it. Source thoroughness is the foundation; accessibility is the surface. Conflating them produces over-stuffed scripts and generates pointless additional research requests.

**How to apply:**
- When asked "is the research enough?" — test against accuracy, honesty, source verification, nuance preservation. NOT against "is this as heavy as project X?"
- Soft spots in research are worth flagging only if they affect *honesty* or *accuracy*. Biographical color or extra quotes that just add density are NOT soft spots — they're optional flavor.
- A short well-researched video is the goal, not a long one. 5-6 min with verbatim primary sources can be more rigorous than 12 min with summary sources.
- The user's mental model: scholarly discipline behind the camera (NLM verification, primary sources, page numbers, Hurgronje not Miller, 23-of-35 not "unanimous"); accessible voice in front of it (clear language, period quotes that land, no academic hedging).
- When in doubt, the question is: "is this claim defensible against an academic challenge?" If yes, ship. Don't add more.
