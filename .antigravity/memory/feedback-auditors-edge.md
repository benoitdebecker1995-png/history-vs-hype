# Auditor's Edge — Primary Documents On Screen

## Hard rule

**When evidence appears on screen as a quote, it must be a PRIMARY document.** Scholar's conclusions, paraphrases, or interpretive readings never go in the on-screen quote slot — they go in the citation tag or get rephrased into our voice as interpretive bridges.

**Why:** The channel's competitive advantage (defined in `D:\History vs Hype\CONTEXT.md`) is the **auditor's edge** — showing the primary source document with page numbers and exact quotes. No competitor does this systematically. The auditor's edge is *what* goes on screen, not just *that* something is sourced. A well-cited scholar-conclusion still loses to a primary fragment.

**How to apply:**

1. **First instinct check.** When NotebookLM or the research file gives you a clean scholar quote that "works," ask: *what primary document is the scholar reasoning from?* That document is the on-screen quote. The scholar gets the citation tag.

2. **What counts as primary:**
   - Quranic verses, hadith narrations (with chain), Talmud / Mishnah passages
   - Court records, fatwas, treaties, decrees, legal codes
   - Archaeological inscriptions, manuscript pages
   - Period poetry, letters, diaries, sermons
   - Period chronicles (al-Tabari, al-Maqrīzī, Ibn Saʿd, Bede, Eusebius — historians of their own moment counting as primary witnesses)
   - Internal critics from inside the tradition / period (e.g., a 16th-c jurist criticizing 12th-c jurisprudence — primary because contemporaneous to the legal corpus he's reading)

3. **What does NOT count as primary (and stays off-screen):**
   - Modern academic conclusions ("Ahmed argues X")
   - Scholar's reading of a primary text we don't quote ourselves ("Katz characterizes Ibn al-Jawzi's tone as Y")
   - Survey claims about "the consensus of scholars" without naming the texts
   - Paraphrases attributed to the scholar rather than to the document

4. **When the primary document is inaccessible:** If we cannot get the verbatim primary text in our notebook, we have three choices:
   - Add the source to the notebook and re-query
   - Reframe the beat around primary evidence we DO have
   - Cut the beat
   
   We do NOT use the scholar's conclusion as a substitute for primary text on screen.

5. **Citation tag format:** `[SOURCE: Author, Title, p. X]` or `— *Primary Author, Document, location* [SOURCE: scholar conduit, p. X]`. The primary document and its location come first; the scholar/conduit gets the page number bracket.

6. **Interpretive bridges in our voice are fine.** If you need a sentence that does interpretive work — "this is the same mechanism we just traced," "the verse fine-tunes the drape, not the invention" — write it in our voice as the host. Don't outsource the interpretive sentence to a scholar quote. The scholar's reading informs *our* sentence; it doesn't replace it.

## Anti-patterns to watch for

- **Scholar-as-truth:** "Ahmed says veiling pre-existed Islam" on screen instead of an Assyrian law tablet, a pre-Islamic poem, or Ibn Manẓūr's etymology entry on screen.
- **Scholar's interpretation as evidence:** "Katz reads Ibn al-Jawzi's tone as 'a noteworthy aspect of male piety'" on screen instead of Ibn al-Jawzi's verbatim line, OR — if his line is inaccessible — a different primary document that establishes the same point.
- **Survey paraphrase:** "Most jurists held that…" on screen instead of one named jurist's specific text.
- **Modern feminist or revisionist conclusion as on-screen evidence:** even when correct, this puts the channel in the same epistemic register as the very competitors we differentiate from.

## Extension: Title mechanism words (added 2026-05-10)

The Auditor's Edge applies to **title mechanism words** as well as on-screen quotes.

The mechanism word locked under ADR-0003 must be technically defensible against the primary documents in the project notebook — not just rhetorically punchy. The same first-instinct check applies: when a sharp word feels right, ask whether the primary evidence actually supports it.

**Examples:**
- "Forged" implies document fabrication. If the actual mechanism is *deliberate translation discrepancy + a false-equivalence clause*, "forged" is technically wrong even if it's a stronger hook.
- "Rigged" implies intentional manipulation. If sources say "unclear cause," "rigged" overshoots what the evidence supports.
- "Caught" as protagonist-agency implies a named figure discovered the discrepancy. If sources don't credit that figure with discovery, "caught" is fabrication at the title level.

**How to apply:** Before locking a mechanism word in the title, run it through the project notebook (part of the title gate stack per `feedback-topic-vs-angle-ordering.md`). LOW confidence from the notebook = soften the language to what sources support OR close the source-acquisition gap. A title that ships a technically inaccurate mechanism word is an Auditor's Edge failure even if no on-screen quote is wrong.

**Origin:** Adwa #39 (2026-05-10) — initial title candidate "Italy Forged the Ethiopia Treaty" caught in grill. "Forged" implies document fabrication; the actual mechanism is a translation discrepancy. McLachlan (Phase 1 source) documents the discrepancy but does not characterize it as forgery. Title word requires Phase 2 sourcing before it can lock.

## Extension: Source substitution discipline (added 2026-05-11)

The "primary document inaccessible" rule (How to apply point #4) lists three choices: add to notebook, reframe, cut. A **fourth option** is acceptable when a procedurally-equivalent primary source exists:

**Substitute the text from the equivalent source AND relabel the citation tag honestly.**

The key word is *honestly*. Substituted text must never be presented as the original. The citation tag must reflect what we actually have, not what we wanted.

**How to apply:**

1. **Confirm procedural equivalence.** The substitute source must use near-identical scribe formula, legal language, or genre convention. Example: a Spanish Inquisition torture transcript from Trial 100 (same tribunal, same era, same scribe formula) is procedurally equivalent to Trial 91. A modern paraphrase is NOT equivalent.
2. **Relabel the citation tag.** Strip away the specific case identifier. Use a generic, accurate descriptor: *"AHN Inquisición, Ciudad Real, c. 1494 — procedural transcript"* not *"Trial 91, Marina González."*
3. **Document the substitution.** In project files (research, B-roll plan, asset prompts), note that the text is from the equivalent source, why, and what the citation tag claims.
4. **Never inherit the original's specificity.** If Trial 91's narrative beats included "she screamed," but Trial 100's text doesn't say that, don't graft it on. The substitution copies what the substitute actually contains, nothing more.

**When this fails:** if the substitute introduces a meaningfully different procedural element (e.g., a charge type, an outcome, a specific tortured body part), it's not equivalent — fall back to options 1-3.

**Origin:** Inquisition #54 (2026-05-11) — Beinart's *Records of the Trials* Vol. II Trial 91 (Marina González torture session, April 29, 1494) original Spanish was inaccessible. Trial 100 (María González, same Ciudad Real tribunal, identical scribe formula for disrobing/rack/cord sequence) was available verbatim in NotebookLM. Substituted Trial 100's Spanish for the nanobana facsimile; relabeled citation as procedural transcript not Trial 91-specific. On-screen viewer sees authentic period Spanish legal language with an honest citation; channel's auditor's edge integrity preserved.
