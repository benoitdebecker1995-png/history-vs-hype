## Prompt 28: Wendover Number Landing Techniques

### Setup → Number → Landing Structure
Wendover almost never leaves a number "naked." He wraps data in a distinct Setup → Number → Landing structure.

**Setup (Estimation Hedge):** Before dropping a number, he uses hedging words ("nearly," "estimated," "some," "about") to build credibility.

**Landing (Rank or Translation):** Immediately after the number, he tells the audience what it means by translating into a rank, consequence, or superlative.

**Instances:**
- "Kadina High School with **717 kids enrolled** [Number] **it's the largest American high school in Japan** [Landing]" (Wendover)
- "by personnel at **around 20,000** [Number] **Kadina is the largest American air base in the Pacific** [Landing]" (Wendover)
- "With **a nearly 98% evacuation rate** [Number] **and ultimately only three deaths... this strategy worked** [Landing]" (Wendover)
- "each year the UK spends **about $115 million** on this military posture [Number] **breaking down to a perperson cost of about $31,000** [Landing]" (Wendover)
- "...**a meager 120 evacuees** [emotional adjective as setup] **headed towards Houston** [Landing]" (Wendover)
- "**eight crashes** in and around the island in 2016 [Number] **alone** [Landing — single word amplifier]" (Wendover)

### Lists of Numbers (Rapid-Fire Inventory)
- **Intro phrase:** "An armada of..." / "FEMA staged nearly..."
- **Closing phrase:** "...along with other supplies positioned elsewhere..."
- **Summary sentence after list:** Ties data to narrative: "The day before landfall the map of supplies and personnel deployed at the federal level looked like this..."

### Scale Comparisons
- **Geographic mapping:** "an island of only 463 square miles... an island **3/4 the area of Hawaii's Oahu**" (Wendover)
- **Population mapping:** "a permanent population of 1.5 million **50% larger than Oahu's**" (Wendover)
- **Time mapping:** "an island that takes **only 2 hours to drive across from tip to tail**" (Wendover)
- **Ratio mapping:** "**For every two to three permanent residents** there's one person there on military payroll" (Wendover)
- **Fractional mapping:** "**1/5 of the entire island** is taken up by the US military" (Wendover)

### Percentages vs Absolute Numbers
- **Absolute numbers** = physical logistics, assets, human presence (heavy, literal feel): "70,000 people still in the city" / "475 buses deployed" / "30,000 active military members"
- **Percentages** = judging system success/failure or highlighting disparities: "98% evacuation rate... this strategy worked" / "prosecution rate... was 13% compared to the 44% national rate"

### Signaling Shocking Numbers
Wendover does NOT pause or say "billion with a B." He maintains emotionless delivery and signals shock via **Mathematical Multiplier or Rate**:
- "$33 million → about a billion **a 32-fold increase**" (Wendover)
- "rescuing **750 people an hour** by boat and **100 people an hour** by air" (Wendover)
- "$115 million... **breaking down to a per-person cost of about $31,000**" (Wendover)

### Data-to-Narrative Bridge Sentences
- **"Meanwhile" Bridge:** "All the while as federal state and local emergency operators worked to scramble goods into the city FEMA slowly began to mobilize to get people out" (Wendover)
- **"Contrast" Bridge:** "And all this chaos and suffering was playing out at the designated shelter points. Things were far worse at the convention center..." (Wendover)
- **"Philosophical Synthesis" Bridge:** After listing populations, salaries, school sizes → "these bases don't remind of America they simulate it" (Wendover)
