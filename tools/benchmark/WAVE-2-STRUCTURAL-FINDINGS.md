## 6. MODERN RELEVANCE BRIDGES (Prompt 6) → Rule 45

### New Bridge Type Added: Type 4 — "Just Like" Analogy

- "in a similar way to football today" (FoC on Byzantine chariot racing)
- "just like in a modern city" (FoC on Byzantine tenements)
- "just like our modern sports teams today" (FoC on racing team rivalries)

**Mechanic:** Compare ancient institution to modern equivalent the viewer already knows. Makes unfamiliar feel immediately familiar. Best for ancient history where direct legal/political bridges don't exist.

- "Now, why does this even matter?" (Three Arrows) — explicit justification pivot

- "It's basically a continuum of oppression" (Knowing Better)

- **Distinction from "still in effect":** Type 1 bridges say the TREATY/LAW persists. Systemic continuum says the SYSTEM OF HARM persists — the audience is implicated as participants, not observers.

## 7. GAP ANALYSIS — Patterns NOT Covered by Rules 41-46 (Prompt 7)

### 7A. "Everything is a System" Conceptual Frame
**What:** Start with an abstract, non-historical principle (car manufacturing, keys to power, supply chains) and reveal that history is an instance of this system.
**Who:** Kraut (all videos), CGP Grey ("Rules for Rulers")
**Why it works:** Transforms "history lesson" into "manual for how the world works" — increases perceived value.
**Status:** NOT encoded. Relates to opening hooks (1B Relatable Analogy in Wave 1 findings) but is broader than a hook — it's a full-video framing strategy. HvH's "HOW > WHY" subscriber trigger aligns with this. Consider encoding if HvH adopts systems-first framing for future videos.

### 7B. Ethos-Based "Professional Vulnerability"
**What:** Position self as a reformed believer in the myth being dismantled ("I was taught the Standard American History Myth as a teacher"). Or frame the video as a reluctant burden ("I'm neither an expert... nor a social psychologist").
**Who:** Knowing Better (reformed teacher), Three Arrows (reluctant debunker), Shaun (weary expertise)
**Why it works:** Builds trust bridge with viewers who still believe the myth. Makes debunking feel like shared discovery, not attack.
**Status:** NOT encoded. HvH's "Calm Prosecutor" voice doesn't use vulnerability — it uses authority. This is a valid alternative ethos strategy but would require voice profile change. Documented for reference.

### 7C. Narrative Inversion (Switching the Protagonist)
**What:** Shift focus of a well-known era from "Great Men" to marginalized or systemic forces.
**Who:** Atun-Shei Films (Civil War as slave revolt, not generals' war), Kraut (Mongol Empire as extractive institution, not Pax Mongolica)
**Why it works:** Gives viewer a genuinely new perspective on a story they think they know.
**Status:** NOT encoded as a rule but IMPLICITLY used by HvH (myth-busting format inherently inverts the protagonist). No action needed.

### 7D. Hook-and-Eye Micro-Transition
**What:** End paragraph with a cliffhanger entity (name, question, date) that the first word of the next paragraph resolves.
**Who:** FoC ("...what had happened to the great legacy of Byzantium?" → "That's the thousand-year epic..."), Shaun ("So what's going on here then?" → immediate answer), Johnny Harris ("one of Mohammed's sons is named Osama" → "I told you this was going to be relevant")
**Why it works:** Creates seamless flow between paragraphs. Viewer is pulled forward by the unresolved entity.
**Status:** NOT encoded. Partially overlaps with Rule 35 (micro-transitions) and Rule 43 (causal connectors). This is a paragraph-level technique, not sentence-level. Consider encoding as Rule 47 if script audits show paragraph-transition weakness.

### 7E. Loop-Back (Long-Form Reward)
**What:** Plant a minor name/concept in the first 10% of a video, "harvest" it in the final 10%.
**Who:** Knowing Better ("Crow Dog" name planted early, returned hours later: "Does that last name sound familiar to you? It should.")
**Why it works:** Creates narrative satisfaction and rewards attention — key for long-form retention.
**Status:** NOT encoded. Primarily a long-form (30+ min) technique. HvH's 8-12 min hard cap limits the payoff distance. However, a shortened version (plant in Act 1, harvest in Act 3 of a 10-min video) could work. Low priority — document for future reference.

## 10. WHITE SPACE ANALYSIS — What the Entire Niche NEVER Does (Prompt 10)

**Source:** 51 sources (44 YouTube videos + 3 analysis docs + processing)

### Niche Blind Spots (things NO competitor does)

**1. Show the messy research process**
Every creator plays "Omniscient Narrator" — thesis is perfectly formed before the video starts. Even "So what's going on here then?" is a rhetorical device, not genuine discovery. Nobody shows dead ends, translation struggles, or changing their mind mid-video.
- **White space:** "Live Investigation" format — bring the viewer along for the actual messy process of finding truth
- **HvH fit:** HIGH. The Untranslated Evidence series is already closest to this. Could lean harder into "I read this treaty, and here's what I found" discovery narrative

**2. Engage opponents in live dialogue**
All debunking is asynchronous clip-and-rebut. Nobody interviews the person spreading the myth, or a historian who disagrees with their premise.
- **White space:** Integrated debate or adversarial interview format
- **HvH fit:** MEDIUM. Would require format change but aligns with "evidence-based referee" identity

**3. Accept the "Great Man" theory**
The entire niche is obsessed with systems, geography, supply chains. Nobody argues that a single irrational individual bent history through sheer personality.
- **White space:** Biographical history that argues FOR individual impact, subverting the expected systemic explanation
- **HvH fit:** LOW for main format. Could be a special series

**4. Leave radical ambiguity**
Every video closes with a neat synthesis — Philosophical, Geopolitical Warning, etc. Nobody ends on an open, unsolvable historical mystery.
- **White space:** "We don't know, and here's why not knowing matters"
- **HvH fit:** HIGH. Aligns with uncertainty hierarchy (Rule 41) and intellectual honesty. The "Calm Prosecutor" can present all evidence and say "the jury is still out"

**5. Use non-vlogger preambles (already broken by all)**
This isn't actually white space — ALL creators already do this. But the data shows that compressing thesis to FIRST 10 seconds (WonderWhy model) is still underexplored.

## 11. HvH-SPECIFIC COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGES — What Documents On Screen Unlock (Prompt 11)

**Source:** Same 51 sources, query focused on what HvH's document-first approach uniquely enables

### Structural Moves Only HvH Can Execute

**1. The "Redaction Reveal"**
Show an opponent's quote on screen → bring up original source → physically highlight the words they cut. Visual proof of the lie of omission. No narration-only channel can do this.
- **Pattern:** "The problem isn't the facts the video presents. It's the facts it leaves out." → SHOW what was left out

**2. The "Draft Comparison"**
Show two document drafts side-by-side to trace how intent shifted. Example: Shaun takes minutes to verbally explain Potsdam Declaration edits. HvH can physically show original draft → red-line the removed clause → show final draft. The visual edit does the argumentative work.

**3. The "Exhibit A" Jury Effect**
Using "Notice this specific phrase" / "Open a map" cue system to turn the screen into a courtroom exhibit. Transforms the viewer from passive listener into a jury examining hard evidence.

**4. Self-Condemnation Without Editorializing**
When the actual document is on screen with a page number, the viewer can't doubt authenticity. The "Calm Prosecutor" never has to editorialize — the visual shock of the undeniable primary source text provides the emotional impact. Competitors must spend time establishing source credibility verbally; HvH does it visually.

**5. The "Semantic Trap"**
Show how definitions, accounting ledgers, or legal codes manipulate reality. Saying "the government classified enslaved people as livestock" is a strong claim; zooming in on a 1863 Confederate inventory ledger that lists nine men under "livestock" is an unassailable conviction.

**6. Pacing Advantage**
Competitors spend 5-10% of runtime building credential chains ("Fleet Admiral William D. Leahy, who was the senior most..."). HvH's on-screen page numbers and academic text act as IMPLICIT credentials — the pacing stays tight because the visual does the authority work.

### Bottom Line
HvH's competitive moat isn't just "better sources" — it's that on-screen documents unlock **structural moves that narration-only channels physically cannot execute**. The Redaction Reveal, Draft Comparison, and Semantic Trap are format-locked advantages.

## 12. SHAUN DEEP DIVE — Document-Presentation Techniques (Prompt 12)

**Source:** 5 Shaun transcripts (expanded corpus, 51 sources)

### 5 Core Techniques

**1. The "Credential Chain" Introduction**
Before reading a quote, Shaun builds authority by providing full name + official title + exact relevance. Never drops a quote cold.
- **Phrase:** "Published in [Year], this [Document]..." / "[Full Name], who was [Title], wrote the following:"
- **Example:** "This is General Dwight Eisenhower, who was Supreme Commander of the Allied Expeditionary Force in Europe during World War II and later President of the United States..."
- **HvH adaptation:** On-screen documents handle credential work visually — HvH can compress this. Show the document header + page number; speak only the most impactful credential detail.

**2. The Investigative Contradiction & Steelmanning**
Builds strongest version of myth first (5-10% of runtime), then shatters with primary sources.
- **Key phrase:** "So that's quite a simple story there and presented like this... pretty unambiguously the right thing to do"
- **Pivot phrase:** **"So what's going on here then?"** (his signature turn)
- **HvH note:** Already covered by Rule 42. Shaun's specific value: the DURATION of the steelman (full 90s in Hiroshima) — he lets it breathe.

**3. "Self-Condemnation" — Let the Villain Speak**
Quotes antagonist's own words, then points out gap between text and reality. Never editorializes upfront.
- **Post-quote phrase:** "Now I don't know any way of reading this other than..."
- **Example:** Truman's diary: "The target will be a purely military one" → Shaun: "a city center is the exact place you would drop the bomb if you were trying to target women and children"
- **HvH application:** PERFECT fit for Calm Prosecutor. The physical document on screen makes the self-condemnation even more devastating.

**4. Weaponizing Opponents' Own Sources**
Pulls up exact sources opponents cited, reads the parts they left out.
- **Example:** Lauren Southern cites Oxford Migration Observatory → Shaun reads the same studies, proving Polish (white) immigrants top statistics. "It's because they're white, obviously."
- **HvH application:** The "Redaction Reveal" (Section 11) is HvH's version of this, but with the visual advantage.

**5. Sarcastic Understatement as Micro-Transition**
Dry wit between evidence blocks. "More like Joseph *Stalling*, anyway." "More like... crock of shit! Thank you."
- **HvH note:** Use sparingly. Calm Prosecutor allows dry understatement but not broad comedy. Best at section boundaries, not mid-evidence.

## 13. WONDERWHY DEEP DIVE — Territorial Narration Techniques (Prompt 13)

**Source:** 5+ WonderWhy transcripts (expanded corpus)

### 4 Core Techniques

**1. "Cold Fact + Question" Geographic Hook**
States modern geographic anomaly in first sentence, asks "why?" by sentence 3.
- **Example:** "This is the island of Ireland. The island is politically split... But why exactly is the island split? And when did it happen?"
- **HvH application:** STRONG fit for territorial topics. Topic keyword in first 10 seconds. No philosophical preamble.

**2. Clean Chronological Progression**
After establishing the puzzle, jumps to historical origin and traces strictly forward. No detours, no tangents.
- **Ireland:** 12th-century Norman invasion → Union of Crowns → Plantation of Ulster → Glorious Revolution → 1920 partition → Good Friday Agreement
- **HvH note:** HvH's myth-busting format inherently requires non-linear structure (myth first, then evidence). But for territorial topics, WonderWhy's clean chronology may be more effective.

**3. De Jure vs De Facto Separation**
Explicitly separates legal frameworks from on-the-ground reality.
- **Example:** Croatia-Serbia border: legal border = original river path (Croatia's claim), de facto control = current river position (Serbia's claim). Creates *terra nullius* parcels claimed by neither.
- **Example:** Nagorno-Karabakh: controls land it never claimed, doesn't control land it does claim.
- **HvH application:** HIGH value. Treaties on screen + map showing actual control = visual de jure/de facto comparison. Structural move competitors can't replicate.

**4. Treaties as Mechanical Pivots**
Treats treaties/legal frameworks purely as mechanical map-changers, not political drama.
- **Example:** Carrington plan: 5 votes needed, Serbia blackmailed Montenegro to flip. Plan dies. No personality analysis — pure mechanics.
- **Example:** Vance-Owen plan: 10 ethnic provinces, Bosnian Serbs would lose 25% of conquered territory. They refuse. Map unchanged.
- **HvH application:** PERFECT for "HOW > WHY" subscriber trigger. Mechanism over politics.

## 16. TOP 5 ACTIONABLE TECHNIQUES FOR HvH (Prompt 16)

Ranked by likely retention impact for a 500-sub channel making 8-12 min document-based myth-busting:

**1. Standard Myth Subversion (0:00-1:00)**
Tell the wrong version first, in full, for 20-60 seconds. "You've probably heard..." Then shatter it. Proven 2.31% sub conversion on ideological topics.

**2. Early Investigative Contradiction Turn (1:30-2:30)**
Place the turn at 15-25% of runtime (3.2x normalized views). For 10 min = 1:30-2:30. Use "Redaction Reveal" — show claim, then show original source highlighting the omission.

**3. Credential Chain Before Every Major Quote**
Full name + title + relevance → Quote → "So what?" analysis. HvH's visual advantage: on-screen page numbers compress the verbal credential chain. Speak the most impactful detail, show the rest.

**4. Modern Relevance Bridges Every 90 Seconds**
"This [treaty/ruling] is still in effect right now." / "This narrative is being used right now by..." / Pattern bridge across territories. Rotate all three types.

**5. Hook-and-Eye Micro-Transitions**
End every paragraph with a cliffhanger entity (name, question, consequence). Start next paragraph by immediately resolving it. "...if these were actually his words, which they're not." → "The actual author was..."

## 17. COMPETITOR FAILURE PATTERNS — What to AVOID (Prompt 17)

### 5 Anti-Patterns Identified

**1. The "Disclaimer Dump"**
Spending minutes apologizing for runtime, justifying the topic, or listing disclaimers before a single historical fact.
- Three Arrows: "But first I have to get a bunch of disclaimers out of the way"
- Shaun: "As you can see this video is rather long..."
- **HvH rule:** The Calm Prosecutor never justifies why court is in session. Jump straight to the myth.

**2. The "Russian Nesting Doll" Tangent**
Needing to explain a concept, then explaining the concept's concept, spiraling into definitions of definitions.
- Shaun: "If we want to understand G we first need to understand factor analysis and if we want to understand factor analysis..."
- Knowing Better: "I need to go off on the first of many tangents that will seem unrelated..."
- **HvH rule:** Weave mechanisms into the causal chain. Never announce you're going on a tangent.

**3. Meta-Framing Gimmicks**
Skits, props, fictional interruptions that break immersion.
- Knowing Better: "This video is going to take forever if you keep cutting to stuff..."
- **HvH rule:** Use clean "Evidence Hand-off" phrases. No props, no fourth-wall breaks.

**4. Broadcasting Ignorance Mid-Script**
Admitting on camera that you haven't researched the topic you're presenting.
- TikHistory: "I genuinely don't know anything about [Freemasonry]. If you guys could let me know below the names of books..."
- **HvH rule:** FATAL for Calm Prosecutor. If outside research scope, omit entirely. Never confess ignorance to the jury.

**5. Burying the Lede (Late Topic Reveal)**
Delaying the actual topic for 2-4 minutes with unrelated conceptual setups.
- Kraut: Ramen noodles → prison economies → Portuguese trade → China → Trump trade policy (~4 min)
- **HvH rule:** Topic keyword spoken within first 30 seconds. WonderWhy model, not Kraut model, at current channel size.

## 18. TOPIC-TYPE STRUCTURAL BLUEPRINTS (Prompt 18)

HvH makes three types of content. Each requires a fundamentally different script structure.

### Territorial/Border Topics
| Element | Optimal Approach |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Opening** | Cold Fact + Question. Topic in sentence 1, question by sentence 3. Map on screen. |
| **Turn timing** | Late (45-50%). Continuous revelation, not sudden shock. |
| **Source density** | MINIMAL verbal citations (0.2/video). Authority built visually through maps + specific data. |
| **Closing** | Geopolitical Warning / Continuing Story. End on unresolved present. |
| **Model channel** | WonderWhy |

### Ideological/Myth-Busting Topics
| Element | Optimal Approach |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Opening** | Standard Myth Subversion. Tell wrong version for 20-60s, then shatter. |
| **Turn timing** | EARLY (15-25%). Cognitive dissonance from primary sources contradicting myth. |
| **Source density** | EXTREMELY HIGH (20+ citations). Full credential chains. Word-for-word quotes. |
| **Closing** | Philosophical Synthesis. One universal insight that reframes everything. |
| **Model channel** | Shaun / Knowing Better |

### Cross-Type Failure Modes
- Narrative opening on myth-busting = massive drop-off (need claim in first 30s)
- Minimal sourcing on myth-busting = no credibility (need credential chains)
- Point-by-point rebuttal on territorial = breaks chronological flow (need clean timeline)
- Geopolitical Warning closing on narrative = destroys emotional catharsis

## 19. FIRST 30 SECONDS — What Separates Top Performers (Prompt 19)

### The 5-Point Top-Performer Opening Pattern
1. Open with Specificity Bomb (5.4x) or Cold Fact (3.7x) — never "Welcome back"
2. Drop a hard date or number immediately (within first 101s, ideally first sentence)
3. State topic keyword in under 15 seconds
4. Pose a direct question ("you" format) or historical paradox (third-person format)
5. Promise to reveal HOW a mechanism works, not just WHAT happened

### Opening Word Signals
- **Top performers use:** "going" (6.1x), "our" (4.1x), "world" (2.7x), "are" (2.4x), "history" (2.4x)
- **Bottom performers use:** "tour", "took", "dead", "occurred" — passive/distant language

### "You" vs Third Person
- **"You" = myth-busting/systems:** "Somewhere close to *you* there's a prison..." (Kraut) | "I want *you* to keep this question in mind..." (Knowing Better)
- **Third person = narrative immersion:** "In the year 1852 the French writer..." (FoC) — ignores viewer, creates historical distance

### HOW > WHAT Promise
- "The odd thing is that we barely understand *why* this happened" (HC) — mechanism of mystery
- "So *why* exactly did Yugoslavia split up?" (WonderWhy) — mechanism of geography
- "Have you ever wondered what is the *difference* between..." (WonderWhy) — mechanism of language

## 20. NUMBERS & DATA PRESENTATION (Prompt 20)

### 4 Introduction Techniques
| Technique | Phrase | When to Use |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Scale Anchor | "To put things in perspective..." | Before large abstract numbers |
| Estimation Hedge | "It's estimated that..." | Historical uncertainty |
| Translating Pivot | "What this means in English is..." | After technical/jargon data |
| Emphasis Check | "Just to reiterate that, that's..." | After shocking statistic |

### Making Statistics Land Emotionally
- **Asymmetry highlighting:** "Of tens of thousands killed, only 150 were soldiers" (Shaun) — tiny number makes big number devastating
- **Individualizing:** Tell one person's story before dropping the aggregate stat (Knowing Better: John Clarke → 30% death rate)
- **Tragedy juxtaposition:** "About the same number died on this beach as the entire 13 years of Afghanistan" (Fallen of WWII)

### Contextualizing Comparisons
- Geographic overlays: "Enough to cover North America from California to Virginia" (FoC)
- Physical objects: "An NBA basketball is only 29.5 inches" (Knowing Better)
- Country equivalents: "Roughly the electricity consumption of Belgium" (CaspianReport)
- Budget multiples: "Three times larger than the current defense budget" (CaspianReport)

### Key Data Finding
- Number frequency (0.6/min) has NO correlation with views (+0.114)
- **TIMING matters, not frequency.** First number at 203s (top half) vs 304s (bottom half)
- Rule: Specific date or number within first 101 seconds

### Data Dump Prevention
- **Implicit sourcing (WonderWhy model):** Pack narration with specific dates/percentages without naming scholars
- **Statistical mystery (Shaun model):** Turn the data itself into a mystery — investigate methodology rather than listing results

## 21. MID-VIDEO RETENTION — Preventing the Valley of Death (Prompt 21)

### 6 Techniques for the 30-70% Mark

**1. The Narrative Turn (34-42% of runtime)**
Place the major assumption-shattering moment exactly at 34-42%. This is the single most powerful mid-video retention mechanic.

**2. Hook-and-Eye Micro-Transitions**
End every paragraph with a cliffhanger entity. Start next paragraph by resolving it.
- Spoiler hook: "...spoiler warning this man makes very poor decisions" → character intro
- Contradiction hook: "...if these were actually his words, which they're not" → reveal real author
- Recurring theme: "This will be a recurring theme" → prove it again later

**3. Mechanical Pacing Shifts**
- Fast-forward stable eras: "I'm going to fast forward the story now..."
- Slow down for primary sources: drop pace, shift music
- Never maintain the same speed for more than 2-3 minutes

**4. Ominous Shift Escalation**
Describe peak/beauty → "But this was all about to change in the most dramatic way imaginable" → plunge into chaos

**5. Recurring Theme Anchors**
"This will be a recurring theme" on first instance → "Here we are again!" on later instances. Creates "aha!" satisfaction when pattern repeats across different territories/decades.

**6. Modern Relevance Pivots (every 90s)**
Break from history → "This narrative is being used right now by..." / "This treaty is still in effect right now." Rotate three bridge types:
- Treaty/ruling still in effect + pending case
- Narrative being used by specific modern actor
- Pattern: "It happened in [X]. It happened in [Y]. It's happening in [Z]."

### HvH-Specific Mid-Video Advantage
Document-on-screen creates VISUAL pattern interrupts that narration-only channels can't match. Every time a new document appears on screen, it resets attention — HvH should treat document reveals as deliberate mid-video hooks, not just evidentiary support.

## 22. CLOSING MECHANICS (Prompt 22, Wave 4)

### 5 Closing Types

| Type | Register | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Philosophical Synthesis** | Declarative verdict | Shaun: "...no monopoly held by any nation or race on a disregard for the lives of the powerless." |
| **Geopolitical Warning** | Ominous, unresolved | WonderWhy: "...another war in the Balkans is always a possibility." |
| **Pattern Synthesis** | Analytical, ongoing | KB: "This isn't something that happened 150 years ago, it's something we are still doing to them right now." |
| **Atmospheric Lament** | Quiet, tragic | FoC: "If what you were built to protect is no more, for what purpose do you still stand?" |
| **Cynical Maxim** | Wry, sharp | CaspianReport: "Diplomacy after all is the art of telling people to go to hell in such a way that they look forward to the trip." |

### Loop-Back Structure
Most satisfying closings return to a visual/phrase from the opening. FoC opens Byzantium with a French writer at overgrown walls → closes at those same walls. KB opens with "Standard American History Myth" → closes "Because now, you know better."

### CTA Placement
**Rigid boundary:** CTAs go AFTER final narrative point. Shaun: verdict → pause → casual CTA. Kraut: essay ends → "If you wish to have a say..." KB: thematic pun CTAs after conclusion ("colonize that subscribe button").

### Closing Signal Phrases
- "So where does that leave us?" (analytical pivot)
- "And that brings us back to..." (loop-back signal)
- Three Arrows: "I'll have to tag in historian Timothy Snyder" (hand-off to authority for final word)

### Key Insight
Top creators end on RESOLVED notes. The thesis is delivered with authority. Open questions appear only about the geopolitical future, never about the argument itself.

## 23. EMOTIONAL ARC — Oscillating Intensity (Prompt 23, Wave 4)

### The Pattern
Emotional arcs are OSCILLATING, not linear. Three intensity zones:

| Zone | Runtime | Energy |
|------|---------|--------|
| Hook + Turn | 0-25% | HIGH → low → HIGH |
| Evidence Oscillation | 25-75% | Alternating |
| Climax + Close | 75-100% | HIGHEST → low |

### Valley-Before-Peak Pattern
Creators deliberately LULL the audience before their most devastating evidence:
- FoC: 20% runtime on peaceful Constantinople (markets, food, church bells) → Ottoman cannon bombardment
- Three Arrows: calm Rorschach test discussion → Auschwitz commander's memoir
- Shaun: dry Russo-Japanese War history → Target Committee meeting notes

### Post-Turn Energy Management
After the turn, intensity OSCILLATES (not stays maxed). Pattern interrupts every ~90 seconds. Breathing room techniques: modern relevance bridges, mechanism explanations, document reveal setups.

### Per-Channel Energy Maps (approximate)
- **Knowing Better (Neoslavery):** HIGH (0-5%) → low (10-15%) → HIGH turn (20-30%) → oscillating (30-80%)
- **Three Arrows (Cruelty):** low (0-15%) → HIGH gut punch (15-20%) → low (30-50%) → HIGHEST climax (60-75%)
- **FoC (Byzantium):** HIGH (0-5%) → low valley (40-60%) → HIGHEST siege (75-85%) → quiet lament (95-100%)
- **Shaun (Hiroshima):** low (0-5%) → HIGH turn (5-10%) → low (15-30%) → HIGHEST outrage (60-80%)

### Key Finding for 8-12 min Format
