# [VidIQ] 9 YouTube Hooks That Keep Viewers Watching

Source: https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-intros/

## Overview

The initial 10 seconds of a video function as an audition. Creators lose approximately 20% of viewers in the first 15 seconds when intros fail to communicate value clearly.

## The Nine Hook Techniques

### 1. The Curiosity Hook: Ask a Question That Demands an Answer
Humans resist unresolved questions. Instead of stating "Today I'm going to show you how to grow on YouTube," try: "One video changed everything for me, but it wasn't the one I expected."
**Template:** "I thought I knew [topic], until I found out [surprising twist]."

### 2. Drop Into the Action: Cut the Buildup
Begin mid-moment rather than with lengthy setup. Example: "It's 2am. I just lost 43 subscribers in one hour. I made a huge mistake."
**Template:** "It's [time]. I'm [in the middle of problem]. Here's what I did next."

### 3. The Audience-Centric Hook: It's About Them, Not You
Position viewers as the protagonist. Highlight their problems with precision: "Your YouTube intros are putting people to sleep. Here's why."
**Template:** "You're doing [common mistake], and it's costing you [pain]. Let's fix it."

### 4. The Time-Promise Hook: Deliver Fast, Deliver Real
Establish clear expectations about speed and outcomes. Example: "In the next 60 seconds, I'll show you the thumbnail strategy that doubled my click-through rate."
**Template:** "In the next [X] minutes, you'll learn [specific outcome] so you can [benefit]."

### 5. Raise the Stakes: Make It Matter
Create investment by establishing consequences. Example: "This is the advice I wish I had before wasting two years on YouTube."
**Template:** "If you don't fix [problem], you'll keep [bad outcome]. Here's the workaround."

### 6. The Contrarian Hook: Say What No One Else Will
Challenge prevailing assumptions: "Stop asking people to subscribe." This disrupts expectations and demands explanation.
**Template:** "Stop doing [popular advice]. Do this instead, and here's why."

### 7. The Visual Hook: Show Something They Can't Ignore
Unexpected visuals generate questions before words are spoken. Critical rule: visuals must connect to the message, not serve as random elements.
**Template:** "Show [unexpected visual] first, then say: 'This happened because [reason].'"

### 8. Hit With a Shocking Stat
Numbers carry authority and weight. Example: "Only 6.6% of YouTube channels ever reach 1,000 subscribers."
**Template:** "[Surprising stat]. That's why most people fail at [goal]. Here's the fix."

### 9. The Unanswered Question: Spark Self-Reflection
Plant thoughts that encourage internal consideration: "What if everything you know about success is wrong?"
**Template:** "What if [assumption] is wrong, and the real reason is [new idea]?"

## Core Principle

The strongest intros frequently blend multiple techniques simultaneously—statistics, narrative elements, visuals, and questions applied in rapid succession. Effective openings earn viewer investment rather than merely capturing momentary attention.
