# Title → Overlay Operation Map

**Date:** 2026-04-26
**Purpose:** Replace generic "add a 2-4 word text overlay" advice with a specific operation the overlay must perform. The current notebook's outputs all default to one operation (compression) because the source data never named the others. This file names all 5 operations seen in 3x+ outliers across close-match channels, with worked examples and the topic shapes each operation fits.

**Why this matters:** Among 16 outliers in the close-match corpus, the overlay operation distribution is:

| Operation | Outliers using it | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| TITLE REPETITION | 6 (38%) | Title is itself the click — cultural touchstone or curiosity-complete phrasing |
| COMPRESSION | 3 (19%) | Title is long/abstract — strip to 1-3 charged words |
| MECHANISM REFRAME | 2 (12%) | Topic ≠ thesis — overlay names the actual argument |
| VISUAL ANSWER (low/no text) | 2 (12%) | Visual itself answers the title (maps, venn diagrams) |
| NO OVERLAY (visual-only) | 3 (19%) | Iconic visual; overlay would clutter |

The current notebook only does COMPRESSION. Adding the other four is the highest-leverage source improvement.

---

## Operation 1: COMPRESSION

**Definition:** The overlay strips the title to 1-3 charged words. Used when the title is long, narrative, or has multiple beats — the overlay must surface the *single most charged word* and let the visual carry the rest.

**Mechanic:**
1. Identify the *charged term* — the word that does emotional work (the noun or verb that carries the click)
2. Strip everything else
3. The visual must do the contextual work the dropped words used to do

**Outlier examples:**

| Channel | Title | Charged term | Overlay | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knowing Better | "The Part of History You've Always Skipped \| Neoslavery" | Neoslavery | "Neoslavery" | Coined word does all the work — the charged term IS the click |
| Knowing Better | "They Were Just in the Way \| Indian Removal" | Indian Removal | "Indian Removal" | Right of pipe is the topic; emotional title goes in voiceover |
| WonderWhy | "Why Ireland Split into the Republic of Ireland & Northern Ireland" | "WHY IRELAND SPLIT" | "WHY IRELAND SPLIT" | 13 words → 3; satellite map carries the geography |
| WonderWhy | "The Most Complex International Borders in the World - Part 2" | "PAKISTAN, INDIA, CHINA" | "PAKISTAN, INDIA, CHINA" | Vague title → specific geographies; map color-codes them |

**When NOT to use:**
- When the title isn't long (already compressed) → use TITLE REPETITION instead
- When the topic word is abstract ("Cultures and Institutions" — Kraut loser) → COMPRESSION fails because the word itself isn't charged
- When the visual is iconic enough alone → use NO OVERLAY

**HvH application:**
- Long descriptive titles ("The 1825 Indemnity France Forced Haiti to Pay" → "THE INDEMNITY")
- Pipe-separated titles ("Why Bakassi Belongs to Cameroon | The 1913 Treaty" → "1913 Treaty")
- Multi-clause myth-busting ("The Vance Speech Got the Slave Trade Backwards" → "Got It Backwards")

## Operation 2: TITLE REPETITION

**Definition:** The overlay literally is the title (or its left-of-pipe segment). Used when the title is already curiosity-complete or a cultural touchstone — adding compression would lose the click magnetism.

**Mechanic:**
1. Test: would a hypothetical viewer click on the title alone, even without context? If yes — the title is already doing the work.
2. Repeat the title (or its core phrase) on the thumbnail in bold, high-contrast text.
3. Visual = direct illustration of the title (no metaphor needed).

**Outlier examples:**

| Channel | Title | Overlay | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaun | "Harry Potter" | "HARRY POTTER" | Cultural touchstone — the name IS the click |
| Shaun | "The Bell Curve" | "THE BELL CURVE" | Famous controversial book — title alone is curiosity-complete |
| Knowing Better | "American Exceptionalism but as a Religion \| Mormons" | "American Exceptionalism but as a Religion" | Left of pipe is the angle — repeating it primes the thesis |
| WonderWhy | "Fun Fact About Every Country in the World - Part 1" | "Fun Fact About Every Country in the World Part 1" | Title formula is the curiosity payload — repeating it amplifies |
| WonderWhy | "The Breakup of Yugoslavia" | "Breakup of Yugoslavia" | Already 3 words; map carries the visual answer |
| Kraut | "The Turkish Century \| From Hittites to Atatürk" | "THE TURKISH CENTURY FROM HITTITE TO ATATURK" | Sweeping scope is the click |

**When NOT to use:**
- When the title is long and isn't already a cultural touchstone → use COMPRESSION
- When the topic is *not* recognizable to the cold audience (most HvH topics aren't) — repeating "Bakassi Peninsula" doesn't help
- When the title has high emotional charge that compression would amplify (e.g., "They Were Just in the Way" — Knowing Better — uses COMPRESSION on the right of pipe instead)

**HvH application:**
- Rare for HvH because most HvH topics aren't household names
- Use for the rare case when HvH covers a famous touchstone: "The Treaty of Versailles", "The Crusades", "Operation Condor"
- Use when HvH titles are short and emotive: "Spain vs Portugal" — repeating it works because "vs" is its own click engine

## Operation 3: MECHANISM REFRAME

**Definition:** The overlay names the *actual thesis* of the video, which is different from the *topic* the title names. Used when the title is bait (the topic) and the overlay is the truth (the mechanism). This is the most underused operation in HvH's current thumbnail thinking.

**Mechanic:**
1. Identify the title's surface topic (what it claims to be about)
2. Identify the script's actual thesis (the mechanism, system, or causal chain the video reveals)
3. The overlay names the thesis. The title bait stays in voiceover.

**Outlier examples:**

| Channel | Title (topic) | Overlay (thesis) | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kraut | "How Vodka ruined Russia" | "A HISTORY OF SYSTEMIC ADDICTION" | Topic = vodka. Thesis = systemic addiction (state policy, alcohol monopolies, control mechanism). The overlay reveals the *real argument* |
| Kraut | "Trump's Biggest Failure" | "Ni Hao" | Topic = Trump's failure. Thesis = the China policy collapse. Two-word Chinese greeting compresses an entire mechanism |

**Why it works:** The title gets the click. The overlay confirms there IS a thesis (not just a topic recital). Curiosity isn't satisfied — it's escalated. "I clicked because of vodka, but now I want to understand systemic addiction."

**When NOT to use:**
- When the title already names the mechanism (no reframe possible)
- When you don't have a sharp thesis — if the script is a topic recital, mechanism reframe will land empty and viewers bounce
- When the thesis can't be compressed to ≤4 words (too dense → use COMPRESSION on the topic instead)

**HvH application — high leverage:**
- "France Bankrupted Haiti. The Documents Prove It." → MECHANISM REFRAME could be "ENFORCED BY GUNBOATS" (the actual mechanism: military coercion as financial extraction) or "5x THE NATIONAL BUDGET" (the actual scale)
- "The Bakassi Peninsula Belongs to Cameroon" → MECHANISM REFRAME: "THE 1913 TREATY" (the document is the thesis, not the territory)
- "The Manhattan Purchase Myth" → MECHANISM REFRAME: "TWO LEGAL SYSTEMS" (the mechanism: Lenape and Dutch property law incompatibility)
- "Operation Condor" → MECHANISM REFRAME: "STATE-LEVEL COOPERATION" (the mechanism: not rogue actors, but coordinated state policy)

**Test for HvH topics:** if the script's thesis fits in ≤4 words and is *different* from the title, MECHANISM REFRAME is the strongest operation. This matches HvH's subscriber trigger (mechanisms over narratives) better than any other operation.

## Operation 4: VISUAL ANSWER (low or no text)

**Definition:** The visual *itself* answers the title. The overlay (if any) labels the visual elements, not restates the title. Used when the topic has a strong visual primitive — comparison, geography, or formal structure — that the viewer can read in 2 seconds.

**Mechanic:**
1. Ask: "Can I show the answer to the title's question or claim in a single image?"
2. If yes — design the visual to BE the answer. The overlay labels the parts.
3. If no — fall back to a different operation.

**Outlier examples:**

| Channel | Title | Visual answer | Overlay |
|---|---|---|---|
| WonderWhy | "What's the Difference Between Latino and Hispanic?" | Two-flag venn diagram | "LATINO / HISPANIC" (labels the circles) |
| WonderWhy | "Why Ireland Split into the Republic of Ireland & Northern Ireland" | Satellite map with flag fills on each side | "WHY IRELAND SPLIT" (anchors the operation) |
| WonderWhy | "The Breakup of Yugoslavia" | Political map of post-Yugoslav states with flag fills | "Breakup of Yugoslavia" |

**Why it works:** The viewer reads the visual faster than the title. By the time they finish the title, the visual has confirmed there's a real answer ahead. The overlay isn't selling — it's labelling.

**When NOT to use:**
- When the topic doesn't have a clean visual primitive (most ideological topics)
- When the answer is too complex to show in 2 seconds (multi-step mechanisms)
- When the visual would spoil the click (don't show the *whole* answer — show the visual *frame* of the answer)

**HvH application:**
- Strong fit: territorial/border topics with two claimants (political_map_flag_fill is the visual answer)
- Strong fit: comparison topics ("Two Treaties That Contradict", "The Two Versions of the 1913 Anglo-German Agreement")
- Weak fit: mechanism topics, narrative topics, ideological reframes

## Operation 5: NO OVERLAY (visual-only)

**Definition:** No text overlay. Used only when the visual is iconic enough that text would clutter the click. 3 of 16 outliers use this — it's a real option, not always wrong.

**Mechanic:**
1. Ask: "Does the visual alone tell me what this video is about, in 2 seconds, on a phone?"
2. If yes — drop the overlay. Add only a tiny channel logo if needed.
3. Pair with a strong, self-explaining title.

**Outlier examples:**

| Channel | Title | Visual | Why no overlay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shaun | "Dropping the Bomb: Hiroshima & Nagasaki" | Iconic Hiroshima mushroom cloud / aftermath photo | The image is universally recognized — overlay would dilute |
| Kraut | "India & Pakistan - A continuing Story" | Distinctive countryball animation in geographic context | Channel brand carries it; title clarifies |
| WonderWhy | "A Geopolitical Tour of the World" | Globe with flag pins | Visual primitive is self-explaining |

**When NOT to use:**
- When the visual isn't iconic — most stock images, even most historical photos, aren't strong enough
- When the channel is small and viewers don't recognize the brand (HvH at 515 subs is in this bucket — be cautious)
- When the title is long or abstract — pairing a long title with no overlay leaves no anchor

**HvH application:**
- Limited use at current channel size — viewers don't have brand recognition to fall back on
- Possible for Untranslated Evidence series when the document image itself is iconic (e.g., the original 1825 Charles X ordinance, the Treaty of Tordesillas as a famous artifact)
- Low priority until subscriber base grows

## Decision tree (how to pick the operation for any HvH topic)

```
1. Is the title a cultural touchstone (recognizable name/phrase)?
   YES → TITLE REPETITION
   NO  → continue

2. Does the script have a sharp thesis (mechanism) that's DIFFERENT from the title topic?
   YES → MECHANISM REFRAME
   NO  → continue

3. Can the visual itself answer the title in 2 seconds (map, venn, comparison)?
   YES → VISUAL ANSWER
   NO  → continue

4. Is the title long or narrative? Does it contain a single charged term?
   YES → COMPRESSION on the charged term
   NO  → continue

5. Is the visual genuinely iconic (universally recognized image)?
   YES → NO OVERLAY (only at scale; risky for sub-1K channels)
   NO  → fall back to COMPRESSION
```

## Anti-patterns (operations that LOSE in the corpus)

From the 37 content losers, 3 patterns repeatedly fail:

1. **Question→answer in overlay** (kills curiosity): "Do We Really Need Time Zones? / yes."
2. **Title repetition on abstract topics** (no charge): "CULTURES AND INSTITUTIONS" (Kraut loser)
3. **Title repetition on niche topics** (no recognition): "Why the President of France is Prince of Andorra" (full title, no compression — 0.17x)

**Rule:** TITLE REPETITION only works when the title is curiosity-complete (Harry Potter) or formula-amplifying (Fun Fact About Every Country). Otherwise, use COMPRESSION or MECHANISM REFRAME.
