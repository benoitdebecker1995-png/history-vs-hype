# Competitor Script Pattern Analysis

**Date:** 2026-03-20
**Source:** 15 transcripts from 5 competitor channels (top-3 videos per channel by views)
**Purpose:** Identify structural patterns to inform History vs Hype script-writing improvements

---

## 1. Per-Channel Summaries

### Kraut (3 videos: India/Pakistan, Trump's Biggest Failure, Vodka Russia)

**Opening Pattern:**
- **India/Pakistan:** Opens with seemingly unrelated topic (car manufacturing, assembly lines, just-in-time production) and spends ~2 minutes building a conceptual framework (supply chains, internal integration) before pivoting to the actual subject. No thesis until ~3:30 ("there is no geographical region that is as badly integrated internally as the Indian subcontinent").
- **Trump's Biggest Failure:** Opens with prison economy anecdote (~9s in), builds to abstract principle (wanting stuff, trade vs. theft), then pivots to China's 2,000-year trade dominance before arriving at trade policy. Topic doesn't land until ~4 min.
- **Vodka Russia:** Opens with historical anecdotes about alcohol across civilizations (Pharaohs, Greek wine, British opium) for ~60s, then narrows: "but no historic relationship between a society and an addictive substance is as fascinating, sinister and barely talked about as the relationship between Russia and vodka."

**Hook type:** Contextual opening (all 3). Kraut NEVER opens with the topic directly. He always builds a conceptual frame first.

**Structure:**
- **Deeply chronological with causal chains.** Every event is connected to the next with "which meant that," "consequently," "this was so profitable that."
- No numbered chapters or explicit transitions. Structure is one continuous argument.
- The "turn" comes from showing how a seemingly benign system (vodka production, car manufacturing) is actually a mechanism of control or collapse.
- Average video: 30-45 min. No arbitrary breaks.

**Sourcing:**
- Rarely names specific scholars or books in the script itself.
- Sources are implied through density of specific facts (dates, percentages, mechanisms).
- No "according to Professor X" verbal citations.
- Paraphrases heavily rather than quoting word-for-word.
- ~0-2 explicit source mentions per video in the spoken script.

**Tone:**
- Third-person narration predominantly. Occasional "you" to address viewer.
- Academic but accessible. Long, complex sentences with multiple clauses.
- Dry humor through understatement ("the English and Dutch tried sailing around Russia where they only found more Russia").
- No explicit engagement with opposing views -- presents his interpretation as the obvious conclusion from evidence.
- Very confident, almost lecturing.

**Pacing:**
- Long sentences (20-40 words average).
- Few rhetorical questions (maybe 1-2 per video).
- Pattern interrupts are rare -- relies on content density to maintain attention.
- Modern relevance is structural (the whole video connects past to present).

**Closing:**
- Returns to the conceptual frame from the opening.
- Synthesis: here is what this historical pattern means for today.
- No call to action. No emotional plea. Just the conclusion of the argument.

### Shaun (3 videos: PragerU Lies, Harry Potter, Hiroshima/Nagasaki)

**Opening Pattern:**
- **PragerU Lies:** "Hello everyone. Now then, what would you say if I told you, that I knew of a very generous Nigerian prince..." Satirical comparison between internet scams and PragerU's "five-minute university courses." Topic clear by ~20s.
- **Harry Potter:** "Hello everyone, today I'd like to talk about Harry Potter." Then meta-commentary about video essay introductions ("I could do the standard introduction but we all know who Harry Potter is"). Topic immediately stated, context built afterward.
- **Hiroshima/Nagasaki:** "Hello everyone, today we're going to be talking about the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki." Then presents the conventional narrative for ~90s before the turn: quotes from Fleet Admiral Leahy, Admiral Nimitz, and Eisenhower contradicting the standard story.

**Hook type:**
- PragerU: myth_contradiction (satirical frame)
- Harry Potter: contextual_opening (meta, self-aware)
- Hiroshima: story_hook followed by myth_contradiction (tells the standard story, then quotes primary sources contradicting it)

**Structure:**
- **Argument-rebuttal.** Plays clip of opponent, pauses, dismantles point by point.
- PragerU and Harry Potter follow a serial structure: moves through the target's claims sequentially.
- Hiroshima/Nagasaki is more thematic: presents conventional narrative, then systematically demolishes each pillar with primary source quotes.
- Clear "sections" but not explicitly labeled. Transitions are conversational ("so anyway," "now then").

**Sourcing:**
- **HEAVY direct quotation.** This is Shaun's defining feature.
- Names specific people with full titles: "Fleet Admiral William D. Leahy, who was the senior most United States military officer on active duty during World War II."
- Quotes word-for-word from memoirs, speeches, official documents.
- Hiroshima video: ~15+ named sources with direct quotes (Leahy, Nimitz, Eisenhower, Stimson, etc.)
- Shows source text on screen alongside verbal quotation.
- Source density: approximately one named source every 2-3 minutes in the Hiroshima video.

**Tone:**
- First person ("I"). Very conversational.
- Dry, understated British humor throughout ("which, you know, doesn't even bear thinking about really, does it").
- Addresses viewer with "you" frequently.
- **Steelmans before demolishing:** Hiroshima video spends 90 seconds building the strongest version of the pro-bomb argument before dismantling it.
- Sardonic but not aggressive. Lets evidence speak.

**Pacing:**
- Short-to-medium sentences. Punchy.
- Frequent rhetorical questions ("Is that right, there? Have I got that?").
- Pattern interrupts through humor, sarcasm, or playing opponent clips.
- Modern relevance: PragerU video is inherently modern; Hiroshima connects to ongoing debates about nuclear weapons.

**Closing:**
- Synthesis of the argument. Returns to the thesis.
- Often ends with a broader point about why the myth matters.
- No aggressive CTA. Sometimes mentions Patreon.
- Emotional note: measured, intellectual satisfaction rather than outrage.

### Knowing Better (3 videos: Indian Removal, Mormons, Neoslavery)

**Opening Pattern:**
- **Indian Removal:** Personal anecdote (reading in backyard, neighbor says "they were just in the way") combined with meta-commentary ("I wasn't fed a false story, I just... wasn't fed at all"). Hook lands within ~40s.
- **Mormons:** Parallel structure -- tells the Pilgrim story, then reveals "Fewer people learn about the second time this happened" with the Mormons. Surprise/recognition hook. Topic clear by ~30s.
- **Neoslavery:** Dramatic question: "When was the last slave freed in America?" Then a dialogue sequence where assumed answers are progressively rejected ("1865" / "Well..." / "Juneteenth" / "Still wrong"). Very effective tension-builder. Topic clear by ~15s but mystery sustains for 60s.

**Hook type:**
- Indian Removal: story_hook (personal anecdote leading to thesis)
- Mormons: myth_contradiction (parallel structure revealing hidden history)
- Neoslavery: question + myth_contradiction (asks a question everyone thinks they know the answer to, then contradicts it)

**Structure:**
- **Chronological with character devices.** Uses recurring characters (a Mormon dialogue partner, a Civil War character) as plot devices.
- Mormons video literally has a Mormon character who provides the believer's perspective as a foil.
- Neoslavery has a meta-frame: "the Standard American History Myth" which he tells in full (as a PragerU parody), then systematically deconstructs.
- Explicit scope-setting: "I should start by clarifying what this video is and what it is not."
- Clear chapters within long videos (Indian Removal is ~90 min).

**Sourcing:**
- **Moderate verbal citation.** Names books and historical figures but rarely gives page numbers.
- More focused on correcting mainstream narratives than citing specific scholars.
- Uses on-screen visuals (maps, documents, historical photos) alongside narration.
- Neoslavery video references specific laws, amendments, court cases by name.
- ~5-10 explicit source references per video.

**Tone:**
- First person throughout. Very personal.
- "I'm an American, always have been. I went through elementary, middle, and high school..."
- Conversational with frequent humor (character bits, visual gags, self-deprecation).
- **Positions himself as having been fooled too:** "I was taught this story -- and the story I then taught to others."
- Handles opposing views by presenting them first (through characters or the "Standard Myth"), then dismantling.

**Pacing:**
- Medium sentences. Conversational rhythm.
- Frequent rhetorical questions ("Were you expecting me to say more?").
- Character bits serve as pattern interrupts every 5-10 minutes.
- Modern relevance woven throughout (these myths are still being taught).

**Closing:**
- Brings the narrative to the present day.
- Often returns to the personal angle from the opening.
- Emotional note: a mix of outrage and empowerment ("now you know better").
- CTA tied to the content ("If you want to learn more...").

### Three Arrows (3 videos: Anti-SJW Rabbit Hole, Crusades Defensive, Hitler Socialist)

**Opening Pattern:**
- **Anti-SJW Rabbit Hole:** "Hey everyone, today's video is going to be a little different." Disclaimer-heavy opening (~30s of caveats). Personal framing: "I get a lot of people coming forward to me telling me how they used to watch various anti-SJW YouTubers."
- **Crusades Defensive:** "Hello history lovers, it's your favorite mispronouncer of words here." Self-deprecating humor, then immediately identifies the target: Steven Crowder's Crusades video (9 million views). Topic clear by ~40s.
- **Hitler Socialist:** "*sigh* Well, here we are. At the question that is apparently occupying the great minds of our times. Was Adolf Hitler... a socialist?" Weary, sardonic opening. Immediately frames the video as reluctant debunking.

**Hook type:**
- Anti-SJW: contextual_opening (personal/meta)
- Crusades: myth_contradiction (identifies specific myth-maker to debunk)
- Hitler Socialist: myth_contradiction (exasperated setup)

**Structure:**
- **Point-by-point rebuttal.** All three videos follow the same core pattern: play opponent's claim, respond with historical evidence.
- Hitler Socialist and Crusades follow the opponent's argument sequentially.
- Anti-SJW is narrative/experiential -- walks through the radicalization pipeline step by step.
- Clear "opponent clips" serve as structural markers.
- Crusades video explicitly promises a structure: "let's go over this and at the end we'll talk a bit more about the Crusades in the context of more recent news."

**Sourcing:**
- **Document-first, especially for Hitler Socialist.** Quotes the actual Strasser pamphlet, identifies misattributed quotes, cites the Night of the Long Knives.
- Names specific historical figures, dates, events.
- Corrects opponent's sources: "The actual author of this quote was Gregor Strasser... who wrote a pamphlet called 'Thoughts about the tasks of the future' in 1926."
- Crusades video uses specific dates (638 AD, 846 AD, 711 AD) from primary historical chronology.
- ~8-12 explicit source references per video.

**Tone:**
- First person. Personal and conversational.
- German accent adds scholarly credibility (unintentionally).
- Self-deprecating humor ("your favorite mispronouncer of words").
- **Weary expertise:** presents debunking as tiresome but necessary ("I didn't want to do it for quite some time").
- Steelmans somewhat: presents opponent's full argument before responding.
- More emotionally engaged than Shaun -- occasional exasperation and sarcasm ("I'm sorry, everyone. Bear with me. It will be over in a second.").

**Pacing:**
- Medium sentences. Uses opponent clips as natural pattern interrupts.
- Rhetorical questions are opponent-directed ("Is that right?").
- Anti-SJW video uses second-person narrative ("you click the video and find out...") to walk the viewer through the experience -- very effective pacing technique.

**Closing:**
- Returns to broader implications (why this myth matters politically).
- Hitler Socialist: ends with why the "Hitler was a socialist" claim is dangerous for historical understanding.
- Anti-SJW: ends with empathy for people who got sucked in, not condemnation.

### WonderWhy (3 videos: Latino vs Hispanic, Ireland Split, Yugoslavia Breakup)

**Opening Pattern:**
- **Latino vs Hispanic:** "Have you ever wondered... what is the difference between the terms 'Latino' and 'Hispanic'?" Direct question. Topic stated in first sentence.
- **Ireland Split:** "This is the island of Ireland. The island is politically split between the Republic of Ireland... and Northern Ireland. But why exactly is the island split? And when did it happen?" Map-first, geography-first. Question posed by ~10s.
- **Yugoslavia Breakup:** "For most of the 20th century, there existed a country in Southeastern Europe called Yugoslavia. Today, however, what used to be Yugoslavia is now 6 fully independent countries..." Sets up the puzzle immediately. Question by ~15s.

**Hook type:**
- Latino/Hispanic: question (direct, conversational)
- Ireland: cold_fact + question (geographic fact + "but why?")
- Yugoslavia: cold_fact + question (historical fact + implied "how did this happen?")

**Structure:**
- **Clean chronological.** WonderWhy is the most straightforward chronological narrator.
- Ireland video: 12th century to present, no detours.
- Yugoslavia: creation (1918) to breakup (1990s), linear.
- Latino/Hispanic: definitional/thematic rather than chronological.
- Very clear transitions: "So moving onto 'Latino'..." / "This led to..."
- No character devices, no opponent clips. Pure narration.

**Sourcing:**
- **Minimal explicit citation.** Almost no named scholars or books.
- Facts are presented authoritatively without attribution ("it's estimated that...").
- Uses specific numbers and dates as implicit sourcing.
- Latino video: names the US Census Bureau, dictionary definitions (Oxford, Merriam-Webster, Dictionary.com).
- ~2-5 explicit source references per video.
- No word-for-word academic quotes.

**Tone:**
- Third person with "you" for viewer address.
- Clean, neutral, educational. Not argumentative.
- Very little humor (occasional dry observation).
- Does not engage with opposing views -- presents a single authoritative narrative.
- Most "textbook-like" of all five channels.

**Pacing:**
- Short-to-medium sentences. Clear, simple language.
- Frequent "But..." and "However..." for pivots.
- Map animations serve as visual pattern interrupts (can't detect from transcript alone).
- Modern relevance is implicit in topic choice (these are ongoing political situations).

**Closing:**
- Summary/synthesis. "So like many things... the definition often comes down to convention."
- Ireland: brings to present-day situation, mentions Good Friday Agreement.
- Yugoslavia: ends with Kosovo's declaration of independence and ongoing disputes.
- Emotional note: measured, informational. No emotional crescendo.
- Brief CTA or none.

## 2. Cross-Channel Patterns

### What the best-performing myth-busting channels have in common:

**A. Opening: The first 60 seconds determine everything.**

| Channel | Time to Topic | Hook Strategy |
|---------|--------------|---------------|
| WonderWhy | 1-15s | State the question immediately |
| Three Arrows | 5-15s | Name the specific myth/opponent immediately |
| Shaun | 5-30s | "Hello everyone" + topic in first sentence, OR satirical frame |
| Knowing Better | 15-60s | Personal anecdote or dramatic question |
| Kraut | 120-240s | Build conceptual framework before revealing topic |

**Key finding:** The debunking channels (Shaun, Three Arrows, Knowing Better) name their target within 30 seconds. WonderWhy states the question immediately. Only Kraut delays the topic, and he compensates with intrinsically interesting conceptual frames (car manufacturing, prison economies).

**B. The "Everyone Knows Wrong" move.**

This appears in 11 of 15 transcripts. The pattern:
1. State what "everyone knows" or "everyone was taught"
2. Pause for recognition
3. Reveal it's wrong/incomplete
4. Explain what actually happened

Examples:
- Knowing Better: "When was the last slave freed? 1865... Still wrong."
- Shaun (Hiroshima): Presents the "simple story" of bombs-saved-lives, then: "However, this is Fleet Admiral William D. Leahy..."
- Three Arrows (Hitler): "*sigh* Was Adolf Hitler... a socialist?"
- Knowing Better (Mormons): "Everyone learns about the Pilgrims. Fewer people learn about the second time this happened."

**This is the single most common opening move in myth-busting content.**

**C. Source integration follows a spectrum:**

| Level | Channel | Style | Frequency |
|-------|---------|-------|-----------|
| Heavy | Shaun | Full titles + word-for-word quotes | Every 2-3 min |
| Heavy | Three Arrows | Document quotes + misattribution corrections | Every 3-5 min |
| Moderate | Knowing Better | Laws, amendments, court cases by name | Every 5-8 min |
| Light | Kraut | Specific facts imply sourcing | Rarely explicit |
| Minimal | WonderWhy | Dictionary/census definitions | 2-5 per video |

**Key finding:** The channels with the strongest "intellectual authority" brand (Shaun, Three Arrows) cite the most. But Kraut achieves authority through sheer density of specific facts without explicit citation. WonderWhy compensates with visual authority (maps, graphics).

**D. Structure follows content type:**

| Content Type | Common Structure | Used By |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| Debunking a specific claim/video | Point-by-point rebuttal | Shaun, Three Arrows |
| Correcting mainstream narrative | Chronological with "Standard Myth" frame | Knowing Better |
| Explaining why X happened | Chronological with causal chains | Kraut, WonderWhy |
| Defining terms/concepts | Thematic/definitional | WonderWhy |

**E. Humor correlates with subscriber loyalty, not view count.**

- Knowing Better: Most humor (character bits, visual gags) -- strong subscriber engagement
- Shaun: Dry British sarcasm -- loyal audience
- Three Arrows: Self-deprecating -- loyal audience
- WonderWhy: Almost none -- high views but less personality-driven
- Kraut: Occasional dry wit -- high views, conceptual authority

**F. Every successful myth-busting video has a "turn" moment.**

The point where the viewer realizes the common understanding is wrong:
- Shaun (Hiroshima): When 3 senior military officials all contradict the standard narrative (~2:30)
- Three Arrows (Hitler): When the misattributed Strasser quote is revealed (~6:00)
- Knowing Better (Neoslavery): "Still wrong" after multiple guesses (~1:00)
- Kraut (India/Pakistan): "There is no geographical region as badly integrated as the Indian subcontinent" (~3:30)
- WonderWhy (Ireland): Less of a "turn" -- more continuous revelation

**The earlier the turn, the higher the retention potential.** Shaun and Knowing Better front-load their turns.

## 3. Differences from History vs Hype's Current Approach

### What History vs Hype does similarly:
- Document-first argumentation (aligned with Shaun)
- Word-for-word quotes with page numbers (exceeds Shaun's standard -- competitive advantage)
- "Calm Prosecutor" tone (between Shaun's dry sarcasm and Kraut's lecture style)
- Deep causal chains (aligned with Kraut)
- Steelmanning opposing views (aligned with Three Arrows / Alex O'Connor)

### What History vs Hype does differently (potential gaps):

**1. Opening speed.**
History vs Hype's STYLE-GUIDE.md says "Stakes/payoff first" and warns against "1:11 dropout." But the competitor data shows even more urgency:
- WonderWhy states the question in the FIRST SENTENCE
- Three Arrows names the specific myth within 15 seconds
- Only Kraut delays, and he has 1M+ subscribers to sustain audience patience

**Gap:** HvH's hooks may still spend too long on context before the "Everyone Knows Wrong" move.

**2. The "Standard Story" technique.**
Knowing Better and Shaun both tell the wrong version of history FIRST, in full, as if it were correct -- then dismantle it. This is hugely effective because:
- It hooks viewers who believe the standard version (recognition)
- It creates a clear before/after contrast
- It gives structure to the dismantling

**Gap:** HvH tends to present evidence chronologically rather than starting with the myth. The myth-first approach creates stronger cognitive dissonance and retention.

**3. Named opponents.**
Three Arrows and Shaun always debunk a SPECIFIC PERSON or INSTITUTION (Crowder, PragerU, mainstream textbooks). This:
- Creates search demand (people search for "Steven Crowder Crusades debunked")
- Gives the video a clear adversarial structure
- Makes the stakes concrete rather than abstract

**Gap:** HvH debunks myths but rarely names specific myth-makers. The channel's style guide explicitly avoids this ("evidence-based referee between opposing camps"). This is a strategic choice, but the data shows named-target videos consistently outperform general-topic videos in this niche.

**4. Sourcing verbosity.**
Shaun reads quotes ALOUD in full, with full titles of the people being quoted. This is slower but builds enormous credibility:
- "Fleet Admiral William D. Leahy, who was the senior most United States military officer on active duty during World War II, and the personal chief of staff to Presidents Franklin D. Roosevelt and Harry S. Truman..."
- Then the quote itself.

**Gap:** HvH cites well in text (page numbers, exact quotes) but may not always PERFORM the citations with enough verbal gravitas. The title + credential + quote pattern is a Shaun signature that builds authority.

**5. Personal positioning.**
Knowing Better and Three Arrows position themselves as having once believed the wrong thing:
- "I was taught what I'm going to be calling the Standard American History Myth. Throughout all my years of education, the story had barely changed."
- "I, and I'm sure many others, got introduced to Political Youtube without even intending to."

**Gap:** HvH's "Calm Prosecutor" voice is effective but lacks this "I was wrong too" vulnerability. The data shows this technique is extremely effective for building trust with viewers who currently hold the myth.

**6. Sentence length and complexity.**
- WonderWhy: Short, clear, subject-verb-object. Most accessible.
- Shaun: Short with humor. Punchy.
- Knowing Better: Medium, conversational.
- Kraut: Long, complex, multi-clause. Most demanding.
- HvH style guide targets: "Calm Prosecutor" with contractions and natural phrasing.

**Gap:** HvH may skew closer to Kraut's complexity than to Shaun/WonderWhy's accessibility. For a 475-subscriber channel trying to grow, simpler sentences with evidence density may outperform complex ones.

## 4. Actionable Recommendations

### HIGH PRIORITY (strong evidence, directly applicable):

**R1: Front-load the "Everyone Knows Wrong" move.**
- Every myth-busting video should state the common belief within the first 20 seconds.
- Use the Knowing Better pattern: state the wrong answer, pause, then "Actually..."
- This is the #1 structural pattern across all 5 channels for this niche.
- **Implementation:** Add a "Standard Story" beat to the opening hook template. The first 20s should be what the viewer currently believes. The next 20s should be the first crack.

**R2: Tell the wrong version first, in full.**
- Before presenting the real history, spend 60-120 seconds telling the commonly believed version as if it were true.
- This creates a clear "before" mental model that makes the "after" more impactful.
- Knowing Better's Neoslavery PragerU parody and Shaun's Hiroshima conventional narrative are the gold standard.
- **Implementation:** Add a "Standard Myth Narration" section to the script template, BEFORE the chronological evidence. Mark it clearly so viewers know it's being presented for contrast.

**R3: Perform citations with full credential chains.**
- When quoting a source, give the person's full title and relevance BEFORE the quote.
- Shaun pattern: [Full name] + [Title/position] + [Why they're relevant] + [Direct quote].
- This is where HvH's competitive advantage (page numbers, academic sources) can be maximized -- but only if the credentials are SPOKEN, not just shown on screen.
- **Implementation:** In script-writer-v2, add a rule: every major quote gets a 1-sentence credential introduction. "According to Chris Wickham, Professor of Medieval History at Oxford and author of *The Inheritance of Rome*: '[quote]'"

**R4: Cut opening context to under 30 seconds before the topic.**
- Only Kraut (1M+ subscribers) can sustain 2-3 minutes of conceptual framing before the topic.
- At 475 subscribers, every second of delayed topic introduction costs retention.
- WonderWhy's model: topic in sentence 1, question by sentence 3.
- **Implementation:** Enforce in structure-checker-v2: flag any script where the topic keyword doesn't appear in the first 30 seconds of spoken text.

### MEDIUM PRIORITY (moderate evidence, worth testing):

**R5: Add an explicit "turn" moment and place it early.**
- The moment when the viewer's assumption is proven wrong should happen within the first 3 minutes.
- Shaun's Hiroshima "turn" (military leaders contradict the standard narrative) happens at ~2:30.
- Knowing Better's Neoslavery "turn" happens at ~1:00.
- **Implementation:** Identify the single most surprising fact in each video. Move it to within the first 3 minutes. Use it as the thesis setup.

**R6: Consider the "named opponent" approach for some videos.**
- Not every video, but for ideological myth-busting videos (the 2.31% subscriber conversion type), naming who is spreading the myth creates:
  - Search demand ("X debunked")
  - Clearer structure (point-by-point rebuttal)
  - Higher emotional stakes
- This doesn't mean attacking individuals -- Three Arrows is respectful even while debunking.
- **Implementation:** For ideological topics, identify the most-viewed YouTube video or public figure spreading the myth. Reference them by name and let the evidence do the work.

**R7: Use WonderWhy's map-first opening for territorial videos.**
- WonderWhy's Ireland and Yugoslavia videos both open with a geographic fact and a simple question ("But why?").
- This matches HvH's strongest content type (territorial disputes).
- **Implementation:** For territorial dispute videos, the first frame should be a map and the first sentence should state the geographic anomaly. "This is [territory]. It belongs to [country]. But [surprising fact about why]."

### LOWER PRIORITY (suggestive evidence, personality-dependent):

**R8: Test personal vulnerability framing.**
- "I used to believe this too" or "I was taught this wrong" builds trust.
- Works for Knowing Better and Three Arrows because it's authentic.
- Only adopt if it fits HvH's voice naturally.
- **Implementation:** Where the creator genuinely held the common misconception, say so. Don't fabricate vulnerability.

**R9: Experiment with shorter sentences for acquisition content.**
- For videos targeting new viewers (high-search-demand topics), use WonderWhy-level sentence simplicity.
- Reserve Kraut-level complexity for topics where the existing audience expects depth.
- **Implementation:** In script-writer-v2, add a "target audience" flag. New-viewer scripts get a max sentence length guideline of 20 words average. Loyal-audience scripts can go to 30+.

## 6. Key Takeaways for Script-Writing Tool Updates

### For `script-writer-v2`:
1. Add Rule: "Standard Myth Narration" beat must appear before evidence if the video is myth-busting type

4. Add Rule: The "turn" (moment the myth is contradicted) must occur within the first 3 minutes

1. Flag if topic introduction takes longer than 30 seconds

3. Check for at least one full credential-chain citation per major section

2. This is distinct from existing `myth_contradiction` -- it tells the full wrong version first, THEN contradicts
3. Based on this data, it should be the DEFAULT for all myth-busting/ideological content
