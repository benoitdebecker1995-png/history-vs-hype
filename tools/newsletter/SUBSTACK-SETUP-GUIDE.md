# Substack Setup Guide — History vs Hype

**One-time setup. Copy-paste into Substack when creating your account.**

---

## 1. About Page

**Name:** History vs Hype

**Tagline** (shows everywhere — under your name, in recommendations, in search):

> Primary sources. Real quotes. No myths.

**About page body:**

> History vs Hype uses primary documents and academic sources to challenge the historical myths that shape modern politics, borders, and beliefs.
>
> Every claim is sourced. Every quote is real. When a document hasn't been translated into English, I translate it myself — from French, German, Latin, Greek, Spanish, and Dutch.
>
> I've produced 47 evidence-based videos doing this on YouTube. The newsletter goes deeper: full annotated translations, academic citations with page numbers, and the data that connects 500-year-old decisions to consequences you can still measure today.
>
> Free to read, always.

**Photo:** Use one from your YouTube channel (consistency across platforms).

**Don't add:** Links to YouTube, Twitter, or anywhere else. Keep visitors on Substack.

---

## 2. Publication Settings

Once your account is created, go to **Settings** and configure:

| Setting | Value | Where |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Allow search engine indexing of paywalled content | **ON** | Settings > Publication details |
| Recommendations | **ON** | Settings > Recommendations |
| Custom domain | Optional for now — revisit at 500 subs | Settings > Publication details |
| Comments | **ON** (all subscribers) | Settings > Comments |
| Community | **ON** | Settings > Community |

---

## 3. Welcome Email

Go to **Settings > Emails > Welcome email** and paste:

> **Subject:** What you'll get from History vs Hype
>
> Thanks for subscribing.
>
> Every article here does one thing: takes a historical claim people repeat, checks it against the actual documents, and shows you what the evidence says.
>
> I use academic sources from Cambridge, Oxford, and Chicago university presses — with page numbers. When the original document is in French, German, or Latin, I translate it myself.
>
> Here's a good place to start: [link your first published article]
>
> Free to read, always. If you find this useful, share it with someone who cares about getting history right.

## 4. Recommendation Network

Responsible for 50% of all new free subscriptions on Substack.

### A. Newsletters to recommend from your page (add immediately)

These are newsletters your readers would genuinely like. Adding them costs you nothing and signals to the algorithm what niche you're in:

- **Age of Invention** (Anton Howes) — innovation history, academic tone, 46K subs
- **Chartbook** (Adam Tooze) — economic history with data, 180K subs
- **Experimental History** (Adam Mastroianni) — myth-busting with evidence, 92K subs
- **History Can't Hide** (Kahlil Greene) — hidden/buried histories, fast-growing

### B. Cross-recommendation targets (approach after 2-3 weeks of engaging)

Don't cold-pitch. Comment on their posts, restack their Notes, engage genuinely. Then reach out. Target similar-size writers first.

- **Dead Language Society** (Colin Gorrie) — academic expertise, language angle, your translation edge is a natural fit
- Small history Substacks in the 1K-10K range — search Substack's history category, sort by "new & noteworthy"

### Pitch template (when ready, not day 1):

> Hi [name], I run History vs Hype on Substack — primary source myth-busting with academic citations. I translate documents from French, German, Latin, Greek, Spanish, and Dutch that haven't been available in English. I've been reading your work on [specific thing you liked] and think our audiences overlap. Would you be open to a mutual recommendation?

## 5. YouTube > Substack Pipeline

**YouTube channel About section:**
> I also write a free newsletter that goes deeper than the videos: historyvshype.substack.com

**Every video description** (after sources, before tags):
> Read more with full academic citations: historyvshype.substack.com

**Pinned comment** on videos that have a corresponding article:
> I wrote about this in more depth with full academic citations: [article link]

---

## After Setup

1. Publish your first article (run `/newsletter --score` on the 3 existing drafts to pick the best one)
2. Start posting 2-5 Notes per day (short research insights, quotes, questions)
3. Engage with other history writers on Notes for 2-3 weeks before pitching cross-recommendations
4. Update welcome email with link to your first published article
