### 2.2 Thesis verb — SOFTEN: harmonize on ONE verb

This is the strongest pre-film red flag the gate surfaced.

**Current state:**
- `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` line 19: thesis verb is `BUILT`
- `02-SCRIPT-DRAFT.md` line 54: `read the receipts from the people who BUILT the Atlantic system... To understand how it was ENGINEERED, you need to read the four documents`
- Script line 205: `the smoking gun. The document that proves this entire system was ENGINEERED top-down`
- Script line 253: `a specific, ENGINEERED catastrophe`
- Script line 279 (closer): `Buyers didn't find the Atlantic slave system. They ENGINEERED it.`

Five usages, two verbs, inconsistent.

**NLM grounding (full P11.1b queries A and B):**

| Verb | NLM-confidence cross-corpus | Notebook scholar support |
|---|---|---|
| **BUILT** | HIGH | "Africa Today" transcript [direct]; Howard French via Postlethwayt "magnificent superstructure built on an African foundation"; ports/fortunes "built" in Eltis & Richardson |
| **DESIGNED** | HIGH | Eltis & Richardson "slave ships were especially designed"; "Africa Today" "designed the legal systems"; Davis "designed to bring back the lucre" |
| **ENGINEERED** | MEDIUM/HIGH | Edward Baptist verbatim ("massive and cruel engineering"); conceptually mirrored by Smallwood ("rationalized science," "technology of power"). **Baptist is in the notebook but NOT in script's scholar-tag list.** |

**Scholar-by-scholar reverse check on "engineered":**
- HIGH endorsement: Smallwood (uses "construct/fashioned"), Davis (uses "designed/created"), French (uses "linchpin of the machine")
- MEDIUM/HIGH: Lovejoy (prefers "transformations" — would endorse the conceptual claim but soften the verb), Green (uses "reconfigurations," "constructing")
- MEDIUM: Manning (uses "construction" structurally — not top-down), Eltis & Richardson (uses "designed" for ships only)
- LOW (**direct contradiction**): John Thornton — `Europeans simply TAPPED this existing market, and Africans RESPONDED to the increased demand`. Thornton is currently in the script's scholar tag list (Beat 3 mechanism support; line 285 of research file). **If "engineered" stays in the closer while Thornton stays in the tag pool, the script asserts a frame that the cited scholar explicitly rejects.**

**Recommendations (pick one):**

- **Option A — Harmonize on BUILT.** Replace all "engineered" with "built" in script. Pro: matches research-file thesis verb, broadest scholar grounding, no contradiction with Thornton. Con: less rhetorical jolt; "built" doesn't carry "deliberate top-down design" the way "engineered" does.

- **Option B — Harmonize on ENGINEERED + add Baptist to scholar tags + acknowledge Thornton as minority view.** Replace "built" at script line 54 with "engineered" for consistency. Add Edward Baptist (*The Half Has Never Been Told*) to the scholarly source list (he literally uses "engineering" in the verbatim sentence — currently the strongest grounding for the closing verb is uncredited). At Beat 3 or in a tag, briefly acknowledge Thornton represents a minority "tapped existing market" view. Pro: keeps rhetorical force; adds Baptist who is already in the notebook. Con: requires script tweaks and a Thornton concede-line.

- **Option C — Compound: "they built and engineered it"** (or similar). Pro: covers both verbs. Con: rhythmically clunky; closing lines are typically single-verb for punch.

**Recommendation: Option B.** Highest defensibility + keeps the rhetorical force of the closer. Requires adding Baptist (~5 min), one script edit at line 54 (~5 min), and either dropping Thornton from tag list OR adding a one-line concede ("Some scholars like Thornton emphasize African agency — but even Thornton concedes the legal architecture was European..." — would need its own NLM check).
