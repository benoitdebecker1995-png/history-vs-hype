## Q4 — João III 1529 Reply Audit

### Verbatim text (the actual gut-punch)

> *"If I say now that I desire, as you request, that there shall not be any trade in slaves in your kingdom, I will still want to provide wheat and wine for use at Mass, and for this only one caravel a year will be necessary. If this seems good to you, it shall be so. **However, it does not seem to me to be to the honour of you or your kingdom because it would be more praiseworthy to draw each year from the Kongo 10,000 slaves** and 10,000 manilhas and as many tusks of ivory... If you do not want anyone to bring merchandise to Kongo, this would be against the custom of every country... **and if a fidalgo of yours rebels against you and receives merchandise from Portugal, where will be your power and greatness because I well understand what constitutes your military strength and the fear that all have of you.**"*

### Source

- Toby Green, *A Fistful of Shells* — Document 36 ("The Slave Trade is Good for the Kongo")
- Originally: António Brásio, *Monumenta Missionaria Africana* Vol 1, pp. 521-7 (Lisbon 1952)
- English translation: Malyn Newitt
- Page: Green's Document 36, pp. 152-153
- **Confidence: ✅ verbatim verified**

### Resolution of the earlier "nothing of interest" line

That phrasing was **Howard French's interpretive paraphrase** (⏳), not João III's verbatim. The real verbatim is sharper anyway — *"more praiseworthy to draw each year from the Kongo 10,000 slaves."*

### Script-utility recommendation

**Slot 1 — MID-AFONSO-TURN GUT PUNCH (1:30-2:30)** ← NOTEBOOK STRONGLY RECOMMENDS

The notebook's reasoning:
1. **Systemic exposure** — "where will be your power and greatness" exposes the **protection-racket logic** of the trade. João III is explicitly telling Afonso that without the slave trade to buy Portuguese weapons, his own vassals will use that trade to overthrow him.
2. **Emotional jolt** — Afonso's distress ("steal nobles and the sons of nobles") → João III's clinical counter-offer that 10,000 people/year would be "more praiseworthy." Hard interpersonal collision establishes the "Europe made it worse" thesis.
3. **Mechanism > rhetoric** — captures the mechanism of dependency. Even kings who wanted to stop the trade couldn't, because the trade itself was the only counterweight to internal revolt funded by the same trade.

He didn't just say no; he told Afonso that it was 'more praiseworthy' to export 10,000 people a year, and then he delivered a warning: if Afonso stopped the trade, Portugal would simply sell to his rebels instead."*
