### D.2 CONCEED-BEAT (1:00 – 2:15) — MAJOR EXPANSION

**Current frame:** Trans-Saharan + Ahmed Baba (Muslim Africa) + 80x scale comparison

**Issue:** Ahmed Baba framework is Muslim-Africa-specific. Doesn't address right-wing claim that sub-Saharan polities sold their own people to Europeans. Steelman is for the wrong target.

**Restructure:**

**Part 1 — Acknowledge BOTH forms of pre-Atlantic African slavery:**

Open with "there is a kernel of truth here" framing (drop Sowell name).

Two-part steelman:
1. **Trans-Saharan / Muslim framework** (existing Ahmed Baba beat, 1614, p. 130 quote): "Non-belief is that which makes enslavement permissible..." → "bound enslavement to religion, not race. In the *Muslim* parts of Africa, any Muslim of any color could not be enslaved." (Note: scope correction — "Muslim parts" not "African internal framework.")
2. **Sub-Saharan inter-polity slavery** (NEW): warfare captives, debt slavery, kin-vs-outsider distinction. Source: Davis, *Inhuman Bondage* p. 221 — Wolof *jaam sayor* (saleable captive) vs *jaam juddu* (born into household, NOT saleable); Bamana *jonfin* (war captive, saleable) vs *jonba* (non-saleable). African polities had legal frameworks distinguishing saleable from non-saleable.

**Part 2 — The pivot:**

But there's a massive difference between (a) regional slavery bounded by faith or kinship — and (b) an engineered catastrophe bounded by skin.

**Two transformations**, not one:
- **Scale** — Trans-Saharan ~1,000/year per route. Atlantic peak ~80,000/year. 80x multiplier.
- **Kind** — Lovejoy: pre-1450, slavery in Africa was a marginal social institution; Atlantic demand transformed it into a "mode of production." Sub-Saharan rulers were pressured to find ways around their own kinship strictures (Davis p. 221).
- **Pricing mechanism** — Manning ch 4: two-tier price structure. Europeans were buying *newly captured* people at "capture cost" prices, NOT pre-existing domestic slaves. European demand created the incentive to capture.

**Synthesis line:** "African polities had slavery. But the Atlantic system didn't tap into it — it overran it. The kind of slavery the talking point invokes — mass commodity, racially coded, demographically catastrophic — was created by Atlantic demand, not pre-existing it."

### D.3 NEW BRIDGE BEAT — between Evidence 1 (Zurara raid) and Mid-Pivot (João III) — ~45-60s

**Purpose:** Fill the 80-year gap. Show what happened between the 1444 raids and Afonso's 1526 desperate letter.

**Narrative arc (use scholar citation tags; no new on-screen primary doc unless we promote the asiento):**

1. **The raids didn't stop because Europeans had a moral awakening.** African military resistance shut them down — Zurara himself records that in 1445, Africans wielding poisoned arrows killed 20 of 22 Portuguese on Nuno Tristão's expedition. By 1448 the raids ended. (Already in current GUIDE; promote to spoken VO.)

2. **Portugal pivoted to a commercial substitute.** By 1450, Cadamosto records the Arguim factory "supplying eight hundred to a thousand slaves a year to a burgeoning Portuguese market." [CITATION: French, *Born in Blackness*, p. 66]

3. **The labor system was prototyped on the Atlantic sugar islands.** Madeira, the Canaries, the Cape Verdes, São Tomé — Iberian sugar plantations using African slave labor were operational decades before Brazil scaled. [CITATION: Davis, foreword to Eltis & Richardson *Atlas*, p. xiv; French, *Born in Blackness*, p. 116]

4. **Volume ramped during exactly the period the papal-bull legal architecture would have been operationalized.** Lovejoy's data: 81,000 enslaved Africans crossed the Atlantic 1450-1500; 338,000 in the next century. [CITATION: Lovejoy, *Transformations in Slavery* 3rd ed., p. 45, Table 3.1]

5. **And operators explicitly cited the papal bulls as authority.** Russell: "Henry... had much more powerful authority than tradition or the *Siete partidas* to justify his engagement in the slave trade. Various earlier papal bulls could be interpreted as giving him the right to take slaves in Guinea." [CITATION: Russell, *Prince Henry "The Navigator": A Life* (Yale UP, 2000), p. 250]

6. **By 1518, the system formalized.** The Spanish crown issued the first Atlantic asiento — a formal license to import 4,000 enslaved Africans to the Americas, "as many as possible being taken from Cape Verde." This is the moment the Atlantic mass-extraction system goes operational. [CITATION: Donnan, *Documents Illustrative*, Vol I, pp. 41-42, Doc 2]

**THE BRIDGE LANDS HERE:** "By the time Afonso of Kongo wrote to João III in 1526, he wasn't writing about a few raids — he was writing about an operational system that had been ramping for 75 years on the papal license."

**On-screen treatment:**
- B-roll: Lovejoy Table 3.1 (volume column visible), Madeira/São Tomé period maps, asiento page from Donnan
- NO new "primary document" branding — the asiento is presented as ramp evidence, not the 5th pillar

**This beat does FOUR things at once:**
- Fills the 80-year gap
- Lowers the RP overreach (because RP itself doesn't have to claim "engineered top-down before it scaled" — this beat proves it)
- Pre-stages Romanus Pontifex (closes the loop: bulls weren't just paper)
- Smooths the de Marees → RP transition
