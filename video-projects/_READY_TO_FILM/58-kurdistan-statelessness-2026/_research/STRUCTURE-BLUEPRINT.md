## ACT 1 — THEY HAD STATES (0:55–3:25, ~2.5 min)  [C23, C12, C30, C1, C21, C33]
Beat goal: detonate the "never existed" denialism with documents.
- **Kurdistan was a named place for ~900 years:** al-Kashgari's 11th-c. map ("the land of the Kurds"); the Seljuks named a "province of Kurdistan" (1150). In **1597 a Kurdish prince, Sharaf Khan, wrote the *Sharafnama*** — a history of the Kurdish dynasties. [C21/C33]
- **Saladin = the Ayyubids, "the most powerful Kurdish dynasty in the history of Islam."** [C1]
- **The emirates:** after the Ottomans beat Persia at Chaldiran (1514), the diplomat **Idris Bitlisi** cut a deal — the Kurdish princes got **~300 years of self-rule.** The top tier (*hükûmet*) paid **no tax, took no inspectors, owed no conscription.** Bitlis had its own courts and a 12,000-strong cavalry. [C23]
- **Specificity bomb:** **Muhammad Kor of Soran minted his own coins, had the Friday prayer read in his name, and built workshops casting his own cannons.** [C30]
- **HONESTY GUARD (the auditor's edge):** but this was never one country. Rival statelets that fought each other and bowed to sultan or shah. *"The Kurds were never united under one political unit named 'Kurdistan'"* (Posch). **They had states. They never had a nation-state.** [C33]
On-screen: al-Kashgari map · Sharafnama page · a Soran/Baban coin · emirates map c.1800.

## ACT 2 — THE DISMANTLING (3:25–5:25, ~2 min)  [C23, C30, C17, C32]
Beat goal: the hidden cause. *How do you lose states? Someone takes them.*
- **The "second Ottoman conquest" (1830s–50s):** modernizing, centralizing empires could no longer tolerate autonomous princes. [C23]
- **Muhammad Kor's fall (1836):** lured to surrender on a **Quran bearing the Ottoman commander's seal**, promised reinstatement — sailed for Istanbul, and on the way home "**disappeared under mysterious circumstances.** With him disappeared also the emirate of Soran." [C30]
- **Bedirxan, the last emir (1847):** the **near-miss** — his league of princes "**dreamt of establishing an independent state**" — destroyed after his own **cousin Ézdin Sher betrayed him** and guided the troops in. [C33/C23]
- **The mechanism (the payoff):** van Bruinessen — "**the last emirates were deliberately, by military force, destroyed.**" Result: "**re-clanization**," the Sufi sheikhs fill the vacuum, and Eppel's verdict — it "**wiped out any potential nuclei … for Kurdish statehood after the First World War.**" [C23/C17]
- **Bridge to the 20th c.:** the first man to try to *rebuild* — **Sheikh Ubeydullah, 1880:** "**We are a nation apart. We want our affairs to be in our own hands … like other nations.**" [C32]
On-screen: Muhammad Kor · the sealed Quran · the shifting-borders map (emirates → empires).

## ACT 3 — THE FATAL WINDOW (5:25–8:25, ~3 min)  [C22, C6, C7, C15, C8, C19, C9, C27]
Beat goal: the moment the map was redrawn — and the Kurds had no hand on the pen.
- **The controlled comparison (why the Arabs, not the Kurds):** the Arabs had Sharif Hussein — descendant of the Prophet, guardian of Mecca — who **revolted** against the Ottomans and won a written British promise + Faisal's seat at Paris. The Kurds? Most **fought *for* the Ottomans** (hundreds of thousands died in Ottoman uniform), and their Paris man, Sherif Pasha, was dismissed as a "**Parisian pamphleteer**" with "no constituency." **Lloyd George literally forgot to put Kurdistan on the mandate list.** [C22]
- **The promise — Sèvres, 1920:** an actual treaty section headed "**KURDISTAN**," Article 64 dangling independence. But conditional, one-year, gated on a League "capable of independence" test — and **never ratified. "Void before the ink was dry."** Not a promise betrayed; a promise that was never real. [C6/C7/C19]
- **The erasure — Lausanne, 1923 (the keystone):** Britain's Curzon actually argued the Kurds "**ought to be an autonomous race**" — İnönü's Turkey insisted Kurds and Turks were "**a single unit in race, religion and manners.**" The treaty protects "**non-Moslem minorities**" — so the Kurds, as **Muslims, fall outside the category entirely.** The erasure is in the wording. [C15/C8]
- **The price — Mosul:** Britain accepted it for a defensible, viable Iraq — and the oil. **Honesty clause:** the oil wasn't even struck until 1927, *after* the deal — a strategic gamble, not a simple "sold for oil." [C9/C27]
On-screen: Sèvres "Section III — Kurdistan" facsimile · Lausanne Art. 39 · Curzon & İnönü.

## ACT 4 — THE COST + THE RHYME (8:25–10:50, ~2.5 min)  [C36, C35, C31, M1–M3, C25, G1]
Beat goal: subvert the "betrayed pawn" story, land the thesis, close on the rhyme.
- **The pattern everyone knows (SETUP):** used and dropped — Mahabad (1946), the 1975 Algiers sell-out (**Kissinger: "covert action should not be confused with missionary work"**), 1991 ("a nasty bout of amnesia"). *"No friends but the mountains."* [C36]
- **The peak — Halabja & Anfal, 1988:** ~4,000 villages, 100,000+ dead; at Halabja the survivors smelled "**apple and garlic**"; a filmmaker found "**a dead woman holding her son, her arm outstretched.**" And the world's "**deafening silence**" — the US blocking sanctions (Iowa wheat), Britain *doubling* its credit to Iraq, both having supplied the chemicals "**knowingly.**" [C35] *(callback: one year after Saddam's "Saladin II" conference.)*
- **THE TURN / THESIS (subvert the setup):** they were always the pawn and never the player — *not because they wouldn't fight, but because they had no state to fight from.* The empires had made sure of that in 1850. **"More useful to everyone divided and in place — as buffer, as auxiliary, as oil-territory — than as a state."** [C31]
- **THE RHYME (modern, brief):** January 2026 — the last Kurdish self-rule, in Syria, dismantled; the Kurds got **citizenship and a language law — but no state**, and the **oil went back to the capital.** A hundred years after Lausanne: **rights from above, not statehood.** [M1–M3, C25]
- **CLOSE (callback):** "The Kurds gave Islam its greatest king. They built kingdoms that minted their own coins. The one thing they were never allowed to keep was the one thing every empire around them needed them *not* to have — a country of their own." [G1]
