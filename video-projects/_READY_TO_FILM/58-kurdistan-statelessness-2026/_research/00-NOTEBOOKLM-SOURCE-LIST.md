## ACT 1 — Who are the Kurds (identity / deep-origins)
*Job: kill the "Kurds never existed" denialism. Establish a documented people with medieval dynasties and a self-written history.*

### CORE
**[P1] Sharaf Khan Bidlisi. *The Sharafnama / History of the Kurdish Nation* (1597)** — [P] T1
- Izady English trans. (Mazda, 2005). **Also acquire the fuller Charmoy French edition** (*Chèref-nâmeh*, 4 vols, St. Petersburg 1868–75) — more complete than the English; FR is readable for us, and it carries detail the English abridges. The foundational primary: a Kurdish prince's own dynastic history of the emirates.
- Access: English on Internet Archive; Charmoy FR on archive.org/Gallica.

**[P2] Ibn Shaddad, Baha al-Din. *The Rare and Excellent History of Saladin*** — [P] T1
- Trans. D.S. Richards, Ashgate (Crusade Texts in Translation), 2001. Saladin's own biographer on his Kurdish (Hadhbani) origin. The "Saladin was a Kurd" detonator, from a named medieval primary.

**[S1] Eppel, Michael. *A People Without a State: The Kurds from the Rise of Islam to the Dawn of Nationalism*** — [S] T1
- University of Texas Press, 2016. The deep-origins scholarly synthesis spanning exactly this act.

**[S3] van Bruinessen, Martin. *Agha, Shaikh and State: The Social and Political Structures of Kurdistan*** — [S] T1
- Zed Books, 1992. The structural analysis of tribe/religion — the mechanism behind the internal-coherence problem recurring across the arc.

### BREADTH / DISCOVERY
**[P7] Ibn al-Athir. *The Chronicle / al-Kamil fi'l-Ta'rikh*** — [P] T1
- Trans. D.S. Richards, 3 vols, Routledge (Crusade Texts). Independent medieval corroboration of the Marwanid, Shaddadid and Ayyubid Kurdish dynasties — so Saladin isn't a one-source claim.

**[P8] Evliya Çelebi. *Seyahatname* (Book of Travels) — esp. *An Ottoman Traveller*** — [P] T1
- Sel./trans. Robert Dankoff & Sooyong Kim, Eland, 2010. A 17th-c. Ottoman eyewitness who travelled the Kurdish emirates and described them in vivid detail. On-screen primary color almost nobody uses. Doubles into Act 2 (the emirates between two empires).

**[S10] James, Boris. *Genèse du Kurdistan: Les Kurdes dans l'Orient mamelouk et mongol (1250–1340)*** (French) — [S] T1
- Éditions de la Sorbonne, **2021**. THE current authority on Kurdish ethnogenesis — argues 12th-c. sources already show the Kurds as a unified ethnic group (not just a label for mountain pastoralists). Directly answers "when did 'Kurd' become a people." Recent, sharp, English-channels-can't-reach (FR).

**[S11] James, Boris. *Saladin and the Kurds: Perception of a Group at the Time of the Crusades*** — [S] T1
- (Fr. orig.; Turkish trans. *Selahaddin ve Kürtler*, 2021). Scholarly companion to [P2] Ibn Shaddad — how Kurdish identity was perceived around Saladin's era. Pairs the primary with the modern interpretive frame.

**[S24] Deniz, Dilşa. *Shâmaran: The Neolithic Eternal Mother, Love and the Kurds*** — [S] T2
- Lexington Books (Rowman & Littlefield), **2020**. Kurdish anthropologist on the Şahmaran serpent-mother myth as a deep Kurdish cultural-identity thread. A recent, angle-rich "wait, what" for the identity act. ⚠️ T2 caveat: the "Neolithic eternal mother" framing leans into contested mother-goddess theory — use for the *living myth / cultural identity* beat, NOT for literal Neolithic-continuity claims.

**[REF1] *Encyclopaedia of Islam* (Brill, 2nd/3rd ed.) — "Kurds," "Kurdistan," "Marwanids," "Shaddadids," "Ayyubids" entries** — [REF] T1
- Minorsky's classic "Kurds" article + successors. Gold-standard reference, dense with primary-source leads and dynastic detail to mine.

**[S4] Özoğlu, Hakan. *Kurdish Notables and the Ottoman State*** — [S] T1
- SUNY Press, 2004. Late-Ottoman Kurdish identity + the autonomy-not-independence preference. Bridges Act 1 → Act 3.

## ACT 2 — The first division (Ottoman–Safavid frontier; Amasya 1555 → Zuhab 1639)
*Job: the Kurds were carved between empires ~280 years before Sykes-Picot. The same mechanism recurs in 1916/1923.*

### CORE
**[S5] Ateş, Sabri. *Ottoman-Iranian Borderlands: Making a Boundary, 1843–1914*** — [S] T1
- Cambridge University Press, 2013. The authoritative account of how the frontier physically split and hardened across Kurdish land.

**[S6] "Treaty of Zohab, 1639: Foundational Myth or Foundational Document?"** — [S] T1
- *Iranian Studies* 52(3–4), 2019. DOI 10.1080/00210862.2019.1653172. The myth-vs-document debate = the **auditor's-edge beat**. ⚠️ Stage C must present both framings; do NOT assert the nationalist myth as fact (Rule 3 watch-item).

### BREADTH / DISCOVERY
**[S13] Allouche, Adel. *The Origins and Development of the Ottoman-Safavid Conflict (1500–1555)*** — [S] T1
- Klaus Schwarz Verlag, 1983. Takes the division back to the **Treaty of Amasya (1555)** — the *first* partition, before Zuhab. Lets us open the division story even earlier.

**[S14] Murphey, Rhoads. *Ottoman Warfare, 1500–1700*** — [S] T1
- UCL Press/Rutgers, 1999. Context for the 1623–39 war that produced Zuhab — why the border landed where it did.

**[P8 ↩] Evliya Çelebi** also load-bears here — his firsthand description of the Kurdish emirates sitting between the two empires is the on-screen primary for "a divided people with real polities."

## ACT 3 — The modern erasure (Sèvres → Lausanne → Mosul oil)
*Job: the promise existed and was deliberately deleted — proven by the treaties' own wording, with oil as the price.*

### CORE
**[P3] Treaty of Sèvres (1920), Articles 62–64** — [P] T1 — public domain (WikiSource / BYU WWI Archive). Art. 64 = the conditional-independence clause.

**[P4] Treaty of Lausanne (1923), Articles 37–45** — [P] T1 — public domain (BYU WWI Archive). The deletion. The damning point: protections cover "non-Moslem minorities" → Kurds, as Muslims, fall outside the category entirely.

**[P5] League of Nations Mosul Commission Report, 1925** (C.400.M.147.1925.VII) — [P] T1 — UN Archives Geneva. The oil quid-pro-quo evidence. `[FLAG: LIBRARY ACQUISITION]` — pursue the actual report; backstopped by [S7]/[P12]/[S17] if needed.

**[S5b] McDowall, David. *A Modern History of the Kurds*** — [S] T1 — I.B. Tauris, 3rd ed. 2004. The authoritative modern narrative spine; cross-checks the treaty texts.

**[S7] Radpey, Loqman. "Kurdistan on the Sèvres Centenary: How a Distinct People Became the World's Largest Stateless Nation"** — [S] T1 — *Nationalities Papers* (Cambridge), **2022**. ✅ IN NOTEBOOK. Near-thesis-shaped peer-reviewed treatment of why the Sèvres clause was weak and how Lausanne reversed it; uses released British + Turkish documents. (Correction: earlier mislabeled as AJIL — it is *Nationalities Papers*.)

### BREADTH / DISCOVERY
**[S25] Tusan, Meline. *The Last Treaty: Lausanne and the End of the First World War in the Middle East*** — [S] T1
- Cambridge University Press, **2023**. Brand-new centennial monograph on Lausanne — the freshest scholarly framing of the settlement that erased the Kurds. Prime Act-3 interpretive spine, post-dates McDowall by ~20 years.

**[S26] *The Lausanne Project: centenary essays*** (eds., Gingko Library, **2023**) — [S] T1
- Co-edited centennial volume reassessing Lausanne 100 years on. Multiple specialist angles competitors won't have touched.
**[REF2] Burdett, Anita L.P. (ed.). *Records of the Kurds: Territory, Revolt and Nationalism, 1831–1979*** — [REF] T1
- Cambridge Archive Editions (multi-volume British Foreign Office documents). **The archival mother lode** — raw FO cables and reports. This is where you find the specific lines (Churchill vs. Cox, the Mosul calculus) no competitor quotes. Single biggest differentiator on the list.

**[P11] *Lausanne Conference on Near Eastern Affairs 1922–1923: Records of Proceedings*** (British Cmd. 1814) — [P] T1
- The verbatim minutes — Lord Curzon and İsmet İnönü *in the room*. Lets us literally quote who deleted the Kurds and what was said. The "inside the room at Lausanne" beat the gap analysis flagged as wholly absent from competitors.

**[P12] Wright, Quincy. "The Mosul Dispute"** — [P] T2→T1 — ✅ IN NOTEBOOK
- *American Journal of International Law*, Vol. 20, No. 3, 1926 (public domain). Contemporary legal analysis of the Mosul settlement as it happened — a primary read on the oil/borders mechanism.

**[S15] Pedersen, Susan. *The Guardians: The League of Nations and the Crisis of Empire*** — [S] T1
- Oxford University Press, 2015. Award-winning on how the mandate/minority machinery actually worked — supplies non-obvious framing for *why* the League pathway was structurally hollow for the Kurds.

**[S16] Olson, Robert. *The Emergence of Kurdish Nationalism and the Sheikh Said Rebellion, 1880–1925*** — [S] T1
- University of Texas Press, 1989. The 1925 revolt and how Turkey's response sealed the post-Lausanne suppression.

**[S17] Sluglett, Peter. *Britain in Iraq, 1914–1932*** — [S] T1
- Columbia UP / I.B. Tauris. Mandate + Mosul oil in granular detail — the British side of the quid-pro-quo, backstops [P5].

**[S18] Mango, Andrew. *Atatürk*** — [S] T1
- John Murray, 1999. The Turkish side — İnönü's hand at Lausanne, why the military victory translated into negotiating leverage.
