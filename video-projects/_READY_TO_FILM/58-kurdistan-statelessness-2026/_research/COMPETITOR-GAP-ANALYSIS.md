## 8. Agent Assessment

### What ALL competitors get wrong or oversimplify

Every competitor treats the Sèvres-to-Lausanne reversal as a single event caused by Atatürk's military victory. This is true but incomplete to the point of being misleading. Britain and France were not forced to drop the Kurds — they chose to. The 1923 Lausanne negotiation was a deliberate trade. Lord Curzon (UK) and the French prioritized:
1. Getting Turkey out of the war-replacement territory disputes
2. Keeping Mosul ambiguous (to be resolved separately — and it was, in 1926, with Kurdish rights as the price)

The military victory narrative lets Britain and France off the hook. The actual mechanism is a political bargain.

No competitor quotes a single line of any treaty. No competitor names Lord Curzon, Ismet Inönü, or the Mosul Commission. This is a depth vacuum that a primary-source channel can walk straight into.

### The #1 question viewers have that nobody answers

"Sèvres promised them a state — what exactly did it say, and who specifically decided to delete it at Lausanne?"

Every viewer of every existing video knows the outcome. None of them know the mechanism. The "who deleted it and why" question is structurally absent from all competitor videos.

### Recommended angle for History vs Hype

**"The clause that erased Kurdistan"** — a treaty-mechanism video that opens on the actual text of Sèvres Article 64 on screen, explains why the promise was conditional from day one, then shows who traded it away at Lausanne and what they got (Mosul oil + Turkish stability). Close with: the minority protection clauses that DID survive Lausanne — for Greeks, Jews, and Armenians — and the fact that Kurds got none of them. That contrast is the most damning evidence that it was a choice, not a casualty.

This framing:
- Shows primary source documents on screen (channel differentiator)
