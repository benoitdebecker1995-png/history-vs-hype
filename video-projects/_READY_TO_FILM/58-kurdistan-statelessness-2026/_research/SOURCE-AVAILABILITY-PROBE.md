## Verdict
Everything sourceable except the Mosul report (archival; backstopped by secondary scholarship).

The identity/deep-origins cluster scores highest on all three historian axes simultaneously:
- **Sources:** all primary + on-screen-able (vs comparative, which rests on secondary syntheses).
- **Surprise:** Saladin-was-a-Kurd + Zuhab-1639-divided-them-280-years-before-Sykes-Picot are genuine "wait, what"s; rebut the comment-mine's loudest reflex ("Kurds never existed").
- **Scope:** chronological arc, single throughline (division by greater powers, recurring 1639 → 1916 → 1923).

## Recommended spine — deep-time identity → division → erasure arc
1. **Who are the Kurds** — Saladin, Sharafnama, medieval dynasties (Marwanids/Shaddadids/Ayyubids) → kills "never existed."
2. **The first division** — Treaty of Zuhab 1639 splits Kurds between Ottomans & Safavids.
3. **The modern erasure** — Sèvres Art. 64 (promise) → Lausanne deletion (Muslim/non-Muslim exclusion = textual proof) → Mosul oil = the price.
4. **Supporting beats** — comparative (why Armenia/Hejaz/Israel/Turkey succeeded) + Mahabad 1946 as "wait, what" interrupts, NOT the spine.

SEO anchoring intact: `kurdistan history` (4 anchor, whole arc), `kurdistan map` (Zuhab + Sèvres maps), `treaty of sèvres` (Act 3 reveal, 4,376/mo indexable).
