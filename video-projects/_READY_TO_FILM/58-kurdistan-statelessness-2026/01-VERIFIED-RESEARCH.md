## ACT 2 — The first "division" (Amasya 1555 · Zuhab 1639) — ⚠️ MYTH-BUST

### ✅ C4 — What Amasya (1555) and Zuhab (1639) actually stipulated — [S-via-P] T1
- **Amasya 1555** — Allouche (src 80e213e3): the Safavids "recognized **Ottoman sovereignty over Arab Iraq and the areas north of Azarbayjan, including Kurdistan**." Ateş (efb3648c): granted "the Ottomans Iraq, most of Kurdistan, and western Armenia, while Iran kept parts of Kurdistan, Azerbaijan, Yerevan, and Nakhchevan." Murphey (fcd350fb): "respective zones of influence" — Iraq Ottoman, Azerbaijan Safavid.
- **Zuhab 1639** — Ateş quoting the Redhouse translation (efb3648c): named forts/landmarks/tribes (Jassan, Badra, Mandali; demolish Zenjir, Kotur, Maku), and "Akhiskha, and Kars, and Van, and Shehr-e Zur, and Baghdad and Bussorah … be not disturbed by the other party."

### ✅ C5 — "1639 divided the Kurds" is a MYTH (auditor's-edge keystone for Act 2) — [S] T1, Ateş
- Ateş (efb3648c): the idea that 1639 fixed the border is "**a myth … powerfully enshrined in the historiographies and nationalisms of the countries sharing this boundary**." The 1639 treaty "**did not lay down a precise line but rather designated entire regions lying between the two states as 'frontier'**"; "there was **no exact line of demarcation**."
- The real border was made **1843–1914**: "the Ottoman–Iranian boundary as we know it today represents the nineteenth-century balance of power between the Ottoman Empire, England, and Russia … rather than … the 1639 Treaty." "the 1639 Treaty proved … **a myth rather than a foundational document**."
- Pre-modern limits were set by Kurdish dynasties themselves: "the boundaries of the **Kurdish Baban dynasty** … and the **Ardalan dynasty** … were effectively the Ottoman and Iranian boundaries in Kurdistan."
- → **The honest Act 2 is the myth-bust, not the myth.** "You'll hear the Kurds were first split in 1639 — 280 years before Sykes-Picot. The document says otherwise: no line, a vague frontier; the division was a 400-year process the great powers finished in 1843–1914." ## ACT 3 — The promise (Sèvres) and the erasure (Lausanne)

### ✅ C6 — Sèvres (1920) explicitly named "Kurdistan" and promised conditional independence — [P] T1
- Treaty text (7200a9c0 / a7887219): heading "**SECTION III. KURDISTAN**." Art. 62 = 6-month local-autonomy scheme for "predominantly Kurdish areas"; Art. 64 = within **one year**, if a majority petition the **League Council** and it "considers that these peoples are **capable of such independence**," Turkey renounces; southern Kurds of "**that part of Kurdistan which has hitherto been included in the Mosul vilayet**" may adhere. → Mosul link present in the promise itself.

### ✅ C7 — The promise was weak/conditional by design — [S] T1, Radpey + McDowall
- McDowall (5ab96a08): "the possibility to form an autonomous region with the right to elect for complete independence one year after … **if the League of Nations were persuaded of their capacity**."
- Radpey (683369c8): application "**contingent upon a 'standard of civilization'** … The Great Powers believed that nations other than whites and Europeans had not reached the capability of self-governing." Excluded ~⅓ of Kurdistan (west of Euphrates → French zone). **Never ratified.**

### ✅ C8 — KEYSTONE: Lausanne (1923) erased the Kurds, and the wording proves it was a choice — [P]+[S] T1
- **Kurds/Kurdistan appear NOWHERE in Lausanne** (treaty text 89088552/7200a9c0; confirmed Radpey + McDowall).
- Minority-protection Articles 37–45 (verbatim, treaty text) protect only "**non-Moslem minorities**."
- Tusan (e2ef8ae7): "the category of '**Moslem and non-Moslem' replaced the Treaty of Sèvres' more diverse characterization**"; for the Turkish delegates "**religion defined national belonging**." → Kurds, as Muslims, categorically outside the protected class.
- McDowall (5ab96a08): Curzon told İnönü "**the Kurds, with their own independent history, customs, manners and character, ought to be an autonomous race**," "**but to no avail. Any provision for … a Kurdish state was abandoned … So also were any safeguards for the Muslim minorities, notably the Kurds, Circassians and Arabs**." İnönü's counter-strategy: claim Kurds and Turks were one people → no separate protection needed.
- → The damning, textually-proven, **deliberate-choice** argument. The Curzon line is the "inside the room" beat even without the Proceedings upload.

### ✅ C9 — The price: Kurdish autonomy abandoned to win Mosul; oil bought Turkey's signature — [P]+[S] T1
- **Explicit trade** — Sluglett (11c2d1ec, p.223): High Commissioner Sir Henry Dobbs to London, late 1923: "I suggest that it might considerably ease the frontier negotiations if we could give preliminary official pledge to Turkey that … we have **abandoned the idea of Kurdish autonomy** included in the Treaty of Sèvres."
- **Oil = the price** — Pedersen (5ed5ec15, p.272): the Iraqi oil concession was the thing Iraq "had been forced—as **the price of British support** for its bid to retain Mosul—to grant." 1926 Treaty of Ankara: Turkey got "**10 per cent of Iraq's oil royalties for 25 years**" to recognize the boundary (Sluglett p.190; Radpey 683369c8, p.1203).
- **The irony** — Wright (e074a6fe, p.453–456): the 1925 League award tied Mosul→Iraq to a 25-year mandate justified as *necessary to protect the Kurds and minorities* — the protection rationale became the vehicle for absorbing them into Arab Iraq.
- ⚠️ **Honesty note (auditor's edge):** sources show NO single "oil-for-Kurds" contract. It's a documented **chain**: Kurdish autonomy dropped to ease the frontier deal → oil royalties to Turkey to win recognition. Present as a chain, not a smoking gun, or a historian catches the overclaim.

## SUPPORTING BEATS — Mahabad + comparative

### ✅ C10 — A Kurdish state DID briefly exist (Mahabad, 1946) — and why it fell — [S]+[P-via-S] T1
- Republic of Mahabad proclaimed **22 Jan 1946**, **Qazi Muhammad** president, lasted **~11 months**; collapsed Dec 1946; Qazi and kin hanged **31 March 1947** (McDowall 5ab96a08, p.218,227; Vali 7ffeace0, p.621). → the documented rebuttal to "the Kurds never had a state."
- **Collapse = Soviet withdrawal + tribal defection.** Vali (p.665–666): the tribal landlords "had already begun **deserting the Republic** … repositioning themselves … in anticipation of … the monarchist order." McDowall (p.227): "The tribes had supported him **only because he held the key** to economic as well as military assistance."
- **Soviet motive = oil leverage**, not Kurdish independence. McDowall (p.?): Azerbaijan & Kurdistan were "**bargaining chips**" for an oil concession. Eagleton (b6ea2e13, p.959): the separatist movements and the oil concession were "**different paths to the same goal**."
- **Internal-coherence gold:** Qazi Muhammad himself named tribal discord the main "**internal danger**" to the republic (Vali p.72). Ties Mahabad straight into the arc's throughline.
- *Minor scholarly disagreement (noted, non-load-bearing):* Eagleton dates KDPI founding Nov 1945; Vali corrects to 15 Aug 1945 via a publicity sheet.

### ✅ C11 — Why others got states and the Kurds didn't (comparative mechanism) — [S] T1
- **(a) No seat at the table.** Poles, Serbs, Czechoslovaks were "officially admitted"; Faisal of the Hijaz was "invited" (Manela 7e8137e4, p.31). The Kurds' Şerif Pasha submitted a "Mémorandum sur les revendications du peuple kurde" but was an *uninvited petitioner* — Manela lists the Kurds among stateless peoples "**simply turned away at the gates** of the peace conference" (p.6,46; Cambridge History 23945b11, p.422,781).
- **(b) No diaspora lobby** in 1919 (the major Kurdish diaspora formed only in the 1980s — Cambridge History); contrast the Zionist/Korean transnational campaigns (Manela).
- **(c) No consistent patron.** The Kurds "lost a powerful potential patron" (Russia) 1917–23; Britain had "little interest in the creation of a Kurdish state" and pushed Mosul into Iraq (Cambridge History p.432,434); contrast Zionists with Balfour + Wilson backing (Manela p.31–32).
- **(d) No unified movement/state nucleus.** "schisms within Kurdish society prevented the emergence of a leadership … akin to the Hashemite emirs in the Hejaz" (Cambridge History p.188). Aver'ianov, 1912: "the Kurds have **neither a clear national self-consciousness** nor a sense of patriotism in the Kurdish-national sense" (Reynolds 2c8a9a6e, p.1471). → directly reinforces Framing B.
- **MacMillan (*Paris 1919*, 68c36cea) — re-query landed:** "**Kurdistan had even less chance than Armenia of finding a protector. The issue had come up only once at the Paris Peace Conference**" (p.?). Lloyd George literally **forgot to list Kurdistan** among Ottoman mandates and "cheerfully admitted that his geography had been faulty." Paris "hummed with schemes … a Kurdistan, an Armenia," petitions "from countries that existed and ones that were just dreams." By March 1921 the Allies "had backed away from the vague promises … they no longer had any interest in an independent Kurdish state."
- **Sharif Pasha dismissed (McDowall 5ab96a08):** the Kurds' Paris advocate had "no constituency in Kurdistan"; a British official: "his qualifications being those of a **Parisian pamphleteer**." → reinforces Framing B (no unified movement behind the petitioner).

### ✅ C12 — The emirates were real polities, not "mountain tribes" (Act 2 on-screen color) — [P] T1
- Evliya Çelebi (0e066e9e), Bitlis under **Abdal Khan**, 1655: a court where "skilled men would come to him from every country," the Khan "a second Jamshid"; a bath so fine **Sultan Murad IV** exclaimed "If only this bath were in my palace!"; a banquet feeding 6,000, the Khan attended by "his **twelve sons and the seventy-seven tribal chieftains who were submissive to him**."
- Sharafnama (4534fea8): the author's own **Rozhiki dynasty had ruled ~450 years from Bitlis**, "intermittently as an independent emirate or as vassal."
- → Shows distinct, sophisticated polities — but *emirates/vassals*, not a unified nation (stays inside Framing B).

### ✅ C13 — Atatürk publicly promised the Kurds autonomy in 1923 — then erased it — [S] T1
- Cambridge History (23945b11, p.163): Mustafa Kemal: "a kind of local autonomy is to be granted. Hence, **provinces inhabited by the Kurds will rule themselves autonomously** … the Grand National Assembly … is composed of the deputies of both Kurds and Turks." The 1924 abolition of the caliphate removed "the only remaining common ground"; Kurdish rebellions ran 1924–1938. → A second broken promise, from the Turkish side, mirroring the Allied one.
