## CANDIDATE 1 — "I wanted one thing, and God wanted another"

**12-word thesis:** *The Prophet ruled for the wife. A verse arrived. The ruling died.*

**Named anchor:** Habiba bint Zayd + her husband Sa'd b. al-Rabi' (Ansari chieftain) + the Prophet himself

**The mechanism (HOW), in one paragraph:**
A man named Sa'd struck his wife Habiba in the face. She and her father went to the Prophet for justice. The Prophet ruled in her favor — Habiba had the right to *qisas*, retaliation. Strike him back. As they walked out of the courthouse to execute the sentence, Q. 4:34 was revealed. The verse reversed the ruling. Men have authority. Men can discipline. The Prophet himself reportedly said: *"I wanted one thing, and God wanted another."* Every Islamic family-law ruling on marital authority — every fatwa permitting wife-discipline — descends from that 30-second walk to the courthouse door.

## CANDIDATE 2 — "One Arabic Letter, Changed by a Scribe"

**12-word thesis:** *They changed one letter. Women lost their right to write for a millennium.*

**Named anchor:** al-Shifā' bint 'Abd Allāh + Caliph Umar + Ibn 'Abd al-Barr (d. 1070) + medieval scribes

**The mechanism (HOW), in one paragraph:**
Caliph Umar appointed a literate woman named al-Shifā' bint 'Abd Allāh to run the Medina market. The Prophet himself encouraged her to teach women to write. The hadith preserving this used the Arabic word *al-kitāba* — "writing." Then medieval scribes changed one letter. *Al-kitāba* became *al-kitāb* — "the Book," meaning the Quran. With a single letter, "the Prophet encouraged her to teach women writing" became "the Prophet encouraged her to teach women the Quran." Then Ibn 'Abd al-Barr (d. 1070) reattributed her market role to her son Sulayman, because compilers couldn't believe a Caliph would entrust a market to a woman. Two textual edits, and a thousand years of female illiteracy gets a religious justification.

**Sources in notebook (all verified):**
- Source 1 (Geissinger or Reda/Amin), p. 11, 21-22, 27 — documents both edits

**Auditor's edge:** This is pure Auditor's Edge — primary-source forensic. Show the Arabic text on screen. Show the letter change. Nobody else does this. Zero competitor coverage.

**Structure mapping:** **A (Document discrepancy)** — channel-native; matches HvH's own Treaty of Tripoli model

**Title direction (declarative, two-sentence, named):**
- *"They changed one Arabic letter. Women lost their literacy for a millennium."*

**Thumbnail direction:** Side-by-side Arabic manuscript scan with the letter change circled in red + overlay **"ONE LETTER"**

## CANDIDATE 3 — "Iran 2024 = Cairo 1438" (586-Year Repeat)

**12-word thesis:** *Iran's 2024 hijab death law isn't new. It's 1438 again.*

**Named anchor:** Sultan Ashraf Barsbay (d. 1438 Cairo) + Ayatollah Khomeini + the Nov 2024 Hijab and Chastity Bill

**The mechanism (HOW), in one paragraph:**
In 1438 CE, plague ravaged Cairo. The Mamluk Sultan Ashraf Barsbay convened jurists to ask what sin caused divine punishment. They blamed *zina* — adultery — and pointed to women's high visibility in the markets. Barsbay decreed all women forbidden to leave the house, on pain of death. The decree is preserved in al-Maqrizi and Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani. In November 2024, Iran passed the Hijab and Chastity Bill: heavy fines, lashing, imprisonment, and the death penalty for repeat offenders. The mechanism is identical — moral disorder narrative + female bodies as scapegoat for institutional failure. 586 years apart. Same procedural move. The hijab death penalty has a precedent — it just isn't the precedent the regime cites.
