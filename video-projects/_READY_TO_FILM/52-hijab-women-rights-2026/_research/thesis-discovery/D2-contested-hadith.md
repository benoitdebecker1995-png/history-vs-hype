## 9 Contested Hadith Surfaced

### 1. Abu Bakra's "Prosperity" Hadith ⭐⭐⭐
- *"Those who entrust their affairs to a woman will never know prosperity"*
- **Narrator:** Abu Bakra
- **Damning context:**
  - "Remembered" 25 years after Prophet's death
  - Recalled precisely when Caliph Ali defeated **'Aisha** at Battle of the Camel
  - Used to justify Abu Bakra's neutrality in civil war
  - Abu Bakra was **flogged for false testimony** (*qadhf*) in famous fornication case under Caliph Umar — Maliki law would disqualify him as reliable source
- **Contested by:** Mernissi; al-Tabari did not consider it legal basis
- Source 7, p. 716-733; Source 9, p. 940-960
- **Used today:** prevent women from leading prayer / state

### 2. "Deficiency" Hadith ⭐⭐⭐
- Women are *"the majority of inhabitants of fire"* / *"deficient in reason and religion"* (*nāqiṣāt ʿaql wa-dīn*)
- **Narrators:** Abu Sa'id al-Khudri or 'Abd Allah b. 'Umar
- **Basis of contestation:**
  - Punishes women for obeying divine law (not praying during menstruation = "deficient in religion")
  - Contradicts Quranic ontological/spiritual equality
  - **Mehdi Mehrizi:** propagated by enemies of **Fatima** (Prophet's daughter) to discount women's testimony in **Fadak oasis inheritance dispute**
- **Contested by:** Bauer (citing Mehrizi + Ayatollah Saanei); Mernissi
- Source 3, p. 233-281; Source 7, p. 769

### 3. "Interruption of Prayer" Hadith ⭐⭐
- Prayer interrupted by passage of *"a dog, an ass, or a woman"*
- **Narrator:** Abu Hurayra
- **Direct refutation by 'Aisha:** *"You compare us now to asses and dogs. In the name of God, I have seen the Prophet saying his prayers while I was there, lying on the bed between him and the qibla."*
- 'Aisha accused Abu Hurayra of being a bad listener
- Source 7, p. 739-741; Source 8, p. 900; Source 9, p. 965-978

### 4. "Bad Luck" Hadith ⭐⭐
- *"Three things bring bad luck: house, woman, and horse"*
- **Narrator:** Abu Hurayra
- **'Aisha's correction:** Abu Hurayra heard only the END of the sentence. The Prophet was actually **refuting a Jewish claim**: *"May Allah refute the Jews; they say three things bring bad luck..."*
- Source 7, p. 753-755; Source 9, p. 974-975

### 5. "Camel and Prostration" Hadith
- Wife must fulfill husband sexually *"even on the back of a camel"*; if prostration to humans allowed, wives would prostrate to husbands
- **Narrators:** various; Zurara in Shi'i sources
- **Theological contradiction:** husband as "demigod" contradicts *tawhid*
- Amina Inloes: inauthentic, reflects **Abbasid-era social ideals**
- **Contested by:** Khaled Abou El Fadl (via Chaudhry); Amina Inloes
- Source 2, p. 72-73; Source 3, p. 358, 371; Source 4, p. 503-510

### 6. "Crooked Rib" Hadith
- Woman created from crooked rib; trying to straighten her breaks her
- **Narrators:** Abu Hurayra / al-Hasan al-Basri
- Categorized as ***isrāʾīliyyāt*** (Judeo-Christian borrowings)
- Quran says both sexes from "a single soul"
- **Contested by:** Riffat Hassan, Ayatollah Tabataba'i, Ayatollah Saanei
- Source 3, p. 285, 322-338; Source 4, p. 518, 592

### 7. 'Aisha's Age Hadith ⚠️
- 'Aisha married at 6, consummated at 9
- **Narrator chains:** Iraqi, often through **Hisham ibn 'Urwa**
- Counter-evidence (her age relative to sister Asma) suggests she was **at least 19**
- Iraqi narrators classified as ***mudallisīn*** (misrepresenters)
- **Contested by:** Yasmin Amin
- Source 4, p. 438, 548, 622-624; Source 8, p. 902
- ⚠️ POLEMIC RISK — handle carefully

### 8. Hadith Against Teaching Women to Write
- *"Do not teach them to read or write... teach them Surat al-Nūr but not Surat Yūsuf"*
- Attributed to 'Aisha (paradoxically)
- Contradicts Prophet's actual encouragement of literacy for women (Hafsa, al-Shifā')
- **Contested by:** Amina Inloes, Aisha Geissinger
- Source 4, p. 509-516; Source 1, p. 27

### 9. Non-Interference in Wife-Beating Hadith
- *"A man should not be asked why he hit his wife"*
- **Narrator:** Al-Ash'ath
- Narrator admits he **forgot the third point** in a list of three — fundamental proof of weakness
- **Contested by:** Ayesha Chaudhry, modern reformists
- Source 2, p. 162-165
