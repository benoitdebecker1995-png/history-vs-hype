### 2. Habiba bint Zayd Case (early Medinan, post-625 CE) — Q. 4:34 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Moment:** Sa'd b. al-Rabi' (Ansari chieftain) struck Habiba bint Zayd, left mark on her face
- **Prophet's first ruling:** granted Habiba **right of retaliation (*qisas*)** — strike her husband back
- **As they walked out to execute the sentence, Q. 4:34 was revealed** reversing the ruling, granting men authority + discipline rights
- **Prophet said: "I wanted one thing, and God wanted another"**
- **Recorded by:** Muqatil ibn Sulayman, al-Tabari (*asbab al-nuzul* literature)
- **Downstream:** cited as legal justification for husband's right to physically discipline wife without state retaliation; "cornerstone" of Muslim family law's permanent marital hierarchy

### 3. Ta'arrud Street Incidents (5 AH) — Q. 33:59 (jilbab)
- **Moment:** Men in Medina harassed women claiming they only targeted slaves
- **Revelation:** free women cover with jilbabs to be "recognized [as free] and not annoyed"
- **Recorded by:** Ibn Sa'd, al-Tabari
- **Downstream:** veil became "license" for harassment of unveiled (slaves); class distinction later universalized
- (Also covered in D5)

### 4. Umm Kulthum bint 'Uqba (6 AH / 628 CE) — Q. 60:10
- **Moment:** Per Treaty of Hudaybiyya, Muhammad must return Meccan refugees. Umm Kulthum fled Mecca alone, brothers came to demand return; she pleaded
- **Revelation:** Q. 60:10 — believing women should not be returned to non-believers
- **Downstream:** rare early example of woman's individual pious agency legally recognized
- COUNTER-EVIDENCE — supports intellectual honesty / what opposing side gets right

### 5. Sultan Ashraf Barsbay's Plague Decree (1438 CE / 841 AH) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **Moment:** Plague devastating Cairo. Mamluk Sultan held assembly of judges and jurists asking what sin caused divine punishment
- **Verdict:** Jurists blamed *zina* (adultery) and women's high visibility in streets and markets
- **Decree:** Sultan **forbade all women from leaving their houses on pain of death**
- **Recorded by:** al-Maqrizi, Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani
- **Downstream:** women as political scapegoats for natural disasters; codified gendered public space; **resisted by women AND merchants** for economic damage
