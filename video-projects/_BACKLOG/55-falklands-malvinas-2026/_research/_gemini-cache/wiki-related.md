## Re-establishment of British rule on the Falklands (1833)

### 1. TIMELINE
1. **1765–1766:** Captain John Byron claims the islands for Britain; Port Egmont settlement established.
2. **1774:** Britain withdraws from Port Egmont for economic reasons but leaves a plaque asserting sovereignty.
3. **1811:** Spain withdraws its garrison from Puerto Soledad.
4. **1820:** David Jewett claims the islands for the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata.
5. **1828–1829:** Luis Vernet establishes a settlement with both Argentine appointment and British sanction.
6. **1831:** USS *Lexington* (Captain Silas Duncan) raids the settlement after Vernet seizes US sealing vessels.
7. **Nov 1832:** Argentine Governor Esteban Mestivier arrives; he is shortly murdered in a mutiny.
8. **Dec 20, 1832:** HMS *Clio* (Captain John James Onslow) arrives at Port Egmont to reassert British rule.
9. **Jan 2–3, 1833:** Onslow arrives at Puerto Luis; the British flag is hoisted, and the Argentine flag is lowered.
10. **Aug 26, 1833:** Antonio Rivero leads a gang in the murder of five senior settlement members, including Matthew Brisbane.
11. **Jan 1834:** Lieutenant Henry Smith is installed as the first British resident.

### 2. KEY FIGURES
1. **John James Onslow:** Captain of HMS *Clio* who executed the reassertion of British sovereignty.
2. **José María Pinedo:** Commander of the Argentine schooner *Sarandí* who surrendered the islands in 1833.
3. **Luis Vernet:** Entrepreneur and "Civil and Military Commandant" whose settlement formed the basis of the dispute.
4. **Matthew Brisbane:** Vernet’s deputy who was murdered during the 1833 uprising.
5. **Silas Duncan:** US Captain of the USS *Lexington* whose raid destabilized the Argentine settlement.
6. **Antonio Rivero:** Gaucho leader of the 1833 murders, later celebrated as a folk hero in Argentina.
7. **Henry Smith:** The first British resident who established formal administrative authority.

### 3. KEY CLAIMS
1. **British Claim:** Based on prior discovery (1592/1594) and formal settlement in 1766. Britain argued sovereignty was never relinquished despite the 1774 withdrawal.
2. **Argentine Claim:** Based on inheritance from Spain (*uti possidetis juris*) and formal acts of possession starting in 1820.
3. **United States Position:** Disputed Vernet’s right to regulate fishing/sealing; viewed his seizures as acts of piracy.

### 4. DEBATES
1. **Expulsion vs. Encouragement:** Argentina claims the population was forcibly expelled in 1833; British sources argue colonists were encouraged to stay under Vernet’s deputy.
2. **Status of Antonio Rivero:** Debated as either a criminal murderer (British view) or a patriotic rebel against British "usurpation" (Argentine view).
3. **Legal Validity of the 1774 Plaque:** Whether a physical marker and formal declaration are sufficient to maintain sovereignty during a period of non-occupation.

### 5. REFERENCES
1. **Samuel Johnson:** *Thoughts on the late Transactions Respecting Falkland's Islands* (1771).
2. **Robert FitzRoy:** *Narrative of the surveying voyages of His Majesty's Ships Adventure and Beagle*.
3. **Charles Darwin:** *The Voyage of the Beagle*.
4. **Julius Goebel:** *The Struggle for the Falkland Islands* (1927).
5. **Mary Cawkell:** *The History of the Falkland Islands*.

### 6. MODERN RELEVANCE
1. **Historical Grievance:** This event serves as the primary historical grievance for Argentina's modern sovereignty claim.
2. **Diplomatic Narrative:** The "usurpation" of 1833 is a central theme in Argentine education and international diplomacy regarding the "Malvinas."

## Falkland Crisis of 1770

### 1. TIMELINE
1. **1764:** French explorer Bougainville establishes Port Louis on East Falkland.
2. **1765:** British Captain John Byron claims the islands and settles Port Egmont.
3. **1767:** France transfers Port Louis to Spain (renamed Puerto Soledad) following Spanish protests.
4. **June 1770:** Spanish Governor Bucareli sends five frigates and 1,400 troops to Port Egmont.
5. **June 10, 1770:** British commander George Farmer capitulates to the Spanish force.
6. **Nov 1770:** News reaches London, sparking a national outcry and naval mobilization.
7. **Dec 1770:** King Louis XV of France refuses to support Spain in a war against Britain.
8. **Jan 22, 1771:** Spain agrees to restore Port Egmont to Britain while reserving its sovereignty claim.
9. **1774:** Britain withdraws from Port Egmont due to the costs of the approaching American Revolution.

### 2. KEY FIGURES
1. **George Farmer:** British commander who surrendered Port Egmont.
2. **Juan Ignacio de Madariaga:** Spanish General who led the expedition to expel the British.
3. **Lord North:** British Prime Minister who negotiated the diplomatic resolution.
4. **Duc de Choiseul:** French minister who pushed for war but was dismissed by Louis XV.
5. **Louis XV:** King of France whose refusal to fight prevented a global conflict.
6. **Francisco de Paula Bucareli:** Governor of Buenos Aires who ordered the attack on Port Egmont.

### 3. KEY CLAIMS
1. **Spain:** Claimed the islands as part of its South American territories based on the Treaty of Tordesillas and prior discovery.
2. **Britain:** Claimed the islands based on discovery and the establishment of a settlement (Port Egmont) before the Spanish arrived at that specific location.

### 4. DEBATES
1. **The "Secret Promise":** A long-standing debate over whether Lord North secretly promised Spain that Britain would abandon the islands shortly after they were restored.
2. **Sovereignty vs. Possession:** The 1771 compromise restored *possession* to Britain but explicitly left the question of *sovereignty* unresolved.

### 5. REFERENCES
1. **Samuel Johnson:** *Thoughts on the late Transactions Respecting Falkland's Islands* (1771).
2. **Julius Goebel:** *The Struggle for the Falkland Islands: A Study in Legal and Diplomatic History* (1927).
3. **Geoffrey Rice:** "British Foreign Policy and the Falkland Islands Crisis of 1770–71" (*International History Review*).

### 6. MODERN RELEVANCE
1. **Sovereignty Umbrellas:** The crisis established the pattern of discussing possession separately from the underlying legal claim.
2. **Geopolitical Flashpoint:** Highlights the islands' role as a long-standing flashpoint in European and Atlantic geopolitics.

## History of the Falkland Islands

### 1. TIMELINE
1. **c. 1275–1420:** Possible visits by Yaghan people based on archaeological findings.
2. **1592:** English explorer John Davis records the first widely accepted sighting.
3. **1690:** John Strong makes the first recorded landing and names the "Falkland Channel."
4. **1764–1766:** Rival French (Port Louis) and British (Port Egmont) settlements established.
5. **1770–1771:** Falkland Crisis between Britain and Spain.
6. **1833:** Britain re-establishes continuous rule.
7. **1841:** Port Stanley is established as the new capital.
8. **1914:** Battle of the Falkland Islands (WWI); British victory over a German squadron.
9. **1982:** Falklands War; Argentina invades on April 2, and Britain recaptures the islands by June 14.
10. **2013:** Sovereignty referendum; 99.8% of islanders vote to remain a British Overseas Territory.

### 2. KEY FIGURES
1. **John Davis:** Credited with the first European sighting in 1592.
2. **Louis Antoine de Bougainville:** Founder of the first colony (French).
3. **Richard Clement Moody:** First British Governor who oversaw the building of Stanley.
4. **Samuel Fisher Lafone:** Merchant whose enterprise led to the formation of the Falkland Islands Company (FIC).
5. **Margaret Thatcher:** British Prime Minister during the 1982 conflict.

### 3. KEY CLAIMS
1. **Pre-Columbian Discovery:** Presence of arrowheads and the *warrah* suggest early human contact.
2. **Discovery Rights:** Disputed between Portugal, Spain, England, and the Netherlands.
3. **Self-Determination:** Modern British claim rests on the right of inhabitants to choose their own government.
4. **Territorial Integrity:** Argentine claim rests on the principle that the islands are a disrupted part of their national territory.

### 4. DEBATES
1. **The Warrah Mystery:** How the extinct Falkland wolf reached the islands.
2. **First Discovery:** Dispute over whether Magellan, Vespucci, or Davis saw the islands first.
3. **Population Status:** Whether inhabitants are a "settled population" with rights to self-determination or an "implanted population."

### 5. REFERENCES
1. **Antonio Pigafetta:** Chronicler of Magellan’s voyage.
2. **Charles Darwin:** *The Voyage of the Beagle* (observations on the warrah and the 1833 settlement).
3. **Lawrence Freedman:** *The Official History of the Falklands Campaign*.
4. **Mary Cawkell:** *The Falkland Story, 1592–1982*.

### 6. MODERN RELEVANCE
1. **Economic Resources:** Significant relevance due to fishing licenses and potential offshore oil and gas.
2. **Strategic Military Presence:** The islands host the Mount Pleasant Complex, a major UK base.
3. **Diplomatic Conflict:** The islands remain a primary focus of the UN Special Committee on Decolonization.
