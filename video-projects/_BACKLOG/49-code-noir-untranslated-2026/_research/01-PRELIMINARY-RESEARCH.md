## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Code Noir is a 60-article royal edict signed by King Louis XIV of France in March 1685. It defined the legal conditions of slavery across the French colonial empire — first in the Antilles, then extended to Louisiana in 1724. It is simultaneously a slave code, a religious ordinance, a property law, and an instrument of racial classification. Historian Tyler Stovall has called it "one of the most extensive official documents on race, slavery, and freedom ever drawn up in Europe." Louis Sala-Molins, the leading academic authority, called it "the most monstrous legal document of modern times."

The standard narrative treats the Code Noir as proof that French slavery was somehow "regulated" and therefore more humane than British slavery. The myth-bust angle is that the "protections" were structurally unenforceable — the same document that required masters to feed slaves also classified those slaves as movable property and authorized the severing of ears for a first runaway offense. The law was not a humanitarian gesture dressed in legal language; it was a property management system that used religious framing to legitimate total human ownership.

The strongest series hook: the Code Noir has never been formally repealed. On May 13, 2025, French Prime Minister François Bayrou — upon being informed of this fact in the National Assembly — admitted he had been unaware of it and committed to presenting a legislative bill to formally abolish it. Slavery ended in 1848. The law that created it is still, technically, on the books.

**Channel angle (consistent with series DNA):** People know slavery was real. Nobody has read the actual law. Reading it clause by clause reveals things no summary can: a law that opens by expelling Jews, classifies humans as furniture, authorizes torture for runaways, and simultaneously mandates Catholic baptism — all in the same document.

## STANDARD NARRATIVE (What competitors will say)

Every other history YouTube channel that covers this topic will deliver some version of the following:

1. Louis XIV issued the Code Noir in 1685 to regulate slavery in the French colonies.
2. It had 60 articles covering everything from religion to punishment.
3. Some articles were surprisingly "protective" — slaves had to be fed, clothed, given Sundays off.
4. But the harsh punishments (Article 38, 44) reveal the law's true nature.
5. It's a historical document showing how France institutionalized slavery.
6. Slavery was eventually abolished in 1848.

**What this misses:** The authorship dispute, the enforcement gap, the connection to the same year's Huguenot persecution, the 1724 Louisiana revision being WORSE, the fact it was never repealed, and — most importantly — the actual reading of the text clause by clause showing the internal contradictions built into the law itself.
