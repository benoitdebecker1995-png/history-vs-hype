## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In December 1971, the Pakistani military surrendered its eastern wing — what became Bangladesh — after a nine-month campaign of mass atrocities against its own Bengali population. President Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto immediately commissioned Chief Justice Hamoodur Rahman to investigate how this happened. The commission spent years gathering evidence, hearing 213 witnesses, and produced a damning report that documented war crimes, named officers, and recommended courts-martial. Then Bhutto buried it. Twelve copies were reportedly made. Eleven were reportedly destroyed. The single surviving copy remained classified for 28 years until Indian media leaked portions in 2000. The supplementary report (1974) — which went further in naming individuals and documenting atrocities — remained partially classified beyond that.

The channel's angle is precise and powerful: this is not an outside accusation. This is Pakistan's own government, its own Chief Justice, investigating its own military — and finding what it found. The report then disappeared. That sequence — investigate, find, suppress — is the story. The modern hook is active: in April 2025, Bangladesh formally demanded a public apology and $4.52 billion in reparations. In 2024, Imran Khan was investigated by Pakistan's FIA for tweeting a link to the report from prison.

The standard narrative (every competitor) focuses on the Bangladesh liberation war as a story of Indian intervention and Bengali nationalism. The underexplored angle is the document itself: what does Pakistan's own investigation actually say, in its own words, and why did the institution that commissioned it immediately suppress it?

## REPORT STRUCTURE (For NotebookLM Navigation)

The Hamoodur Rahman Commission produced two documents:

**1. Initial Report (July 12, 1972)**
- Mandate: Investigate the military surrender, not specifically the atrocities
- Witnesses: 213
- Focus: Military and political failures, chain of command failures
- Fate: Suppressed; 11 copies reportedly destroyed; classified

**2. Supplementary Report (October 23, 1974)**
- Extended mandate: Investigated the conduct of officers, atrocities
- Additional witnesses: 300 POWs + 73 bureaucrats
- Five chapters + annexure
- Volume I: Political background, international relations, military aspects
- Key output: Named 16 senior officers for court-martial; documented rape and killings; found systemic moral and command failures
- Fate: Classified "Top Secret"; leaked December 2000; partially declassified by Musharraf

**Structure of the video should follow the report's own investigation arc:** commission formed → what it found → what it recommended → what happened to the recommendations.
