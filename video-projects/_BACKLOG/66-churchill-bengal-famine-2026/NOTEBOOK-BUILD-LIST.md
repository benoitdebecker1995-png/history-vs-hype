### ⭐ ACQUIRED DURING THE SWEEP

**`_research/library/OGrada-SufficiencyRevisitingBengal-2008-WP.pdf`** — 53 pp., full text, free from
Warwick. This is A5/C8's working-paper version and it is **the single most productive item the sweep
turned up.** It reconstructs the crisis from *The Statesman* and *Amrita Bazar Patrika* day by day,
and it carries the Bengal-ministry material nothing else on the list had. Three things from it that
change the project:

**1. ⚠⚠ It complicates a verdict already filed as settled.** Its opening epigraph:

> *"Mindful of our difficulties about food I told [Fazlul Huq] that he simply must produce more rice
> out of Bengal for Ceylon **even if Bengal itself went short**!"*
> — **Linlithgow to Amery, 26 January 1943**

`01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` carries **R1 as ❌ REFUTED** on the strength of A8 (Bengal a net rice
importer). That aggregate trade fact stands. But this is the **Viceroy instructing Bengal's premier
to export rice while short** — not to England, to Ceylon, and five months before the famine peaked.
The popular claim is wrong about the destination and the mechanism; it is **not simply wrong**.
⚠ **Seen as an epigraph in a working paper, not yet in Mansergh.** Date falls inside **Vol III**
(A2, free) — verify there before it goes anywhere near a script. **Filed as a challenge to R1's
verdict, not an overturning of it.**

**2. Bengal's own food minister ran the "hoarding, not shortage" line first.** The second epigraph:

> *"The doctrine of **sufficiency and sufficiency and sufficiency** must be preached ad nauseam."*
> — **H. S. Suhrawardy, May 1943**

Suhrawardy — Minister of Civil Supplies in Nazimuddin's ministry — promised *"full statistical
details"* proving *"a sufficiency"*; launched a house-to-house food drive on 7 June, vowing to
*"enter every household and look under every taktaposh and drag out the hoards"*; the drive
**unearthed little rice and revealed "an acute shortage"**; the opposition said he had played a
*"colossal hoax"*. By early July he had shifted: *"It appeared to me that insistence on shortages
would only increase panic and stimulate hoarding."* Ó Gráda notes the mantra was shared —
**Amery in London, Linlithgow in Delhi, Suhrawardy in Calcutta.**

**This is the title's promise in three named men, and it predates the War Cabinet's 4 August
diagnosis by three months.** The Cabinet did not invent "hoarding, not deficiency"; it inherited it
from the Indian administration — which is E10 with the actors named.

**3. Two gaps in our own record.**
- **A War Cabinet discussion on 31 July 1943** where Amery argued and lost (Mansergh Vol IV
  pp. 139–141) — **absent from the decision chain in `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md`**, which starts at
  4 August. We hold the volume. Add it.
- **Why the Famine Code was never invoked:** Suhrawardy *"could not apply the Famine Codes because
  the food required to sustain the prescribed rations was lacking."* Non-declaration blocked
  statutory relief from triggering. **This answers A10/R4 without the Famine Manual** — and it is a
  mechanism, which is the channel's lane.
- **⚠ Archival warning, from Ó Gráda's note 35:** Lance Brennan *"suspects the records of
  Suhrawardy's Department of Civil Supplies were destroyed after the famine."* If true, the Bengal
  ministry's own paper trail may not exist — which makes the newspaper record and the Assembly
  proceedings (A12) the only route, and is itself worth saying on screen.
