## TIER 1 — BLOCKS THE SCRIPT

### 1. ⛔ Islam, "The Great Bengal Famine and the Question of FAD Yet Again"
*Modern Asian Studies* **41:2 (March 2007), pp. 421–440** · DOI `10.1017/S0026749X06002435`
Cambridge Core, paywalled. **The single most important missing item in the project.**

**What it blocks:** the hinge verdict. Our conclusion that there was a real food-availability decline
rests on four specialists — and cites **Islam (1978)** as the independent methodological check.
**Islam in 2007, on a fresh look at the same crop statistics, supports Sen's contention that there was
no significant decline.** The named check goes the other way. I have the publisher's abstract verbatim
and nothing more. Until this is read, the hinge verdict stands with a credentialed dissent recorded
against it and the script cannot claim the question is settled.

### 2. ⛔ The Sen ↔ Bowbrick exchange — three pieces, all short
- **Sen, "The causes of famine: a reply,"** *Food Policy* **11:2 (1986), pp. 125–132**
- **Bowbrick, "Rejoinder: an untenable hypothesis on the causes of famine,"** *Food Policy* **12:1 (1987), pp. 5–9**
- **Sen, "Reply: famine and Mr Bowbrick,"** *Food Policy* **12:1 (1987), pp. 10–14**
ScienceDirect/Elsevier, paywalled.

**What it blocks:** we hold Bowbrick's attack and none of Sen's answers. Bowbrick's critique now
carries real weight in our file — it landed three hits on our own claims — and running it one-sided
is the exact failure the video accuses others of.

### 3. ⛔ The published *Food Policy* text of Bowbrick 1986 (11:2, pp. 105–124)
**Our copy carries a 2011 author insertion**, so no Bowbrick quotation can go on screen from it. Given
how much of the current analysis leans on him, this is a live citation block, not a formality.

### 4. ⛔ A citable Ó Gráda
Either ***Eating People Is Wrong, and Other Essays on Famine*** (Princeton UP, 2015) — **ch. 2 begins
p. 38** — or ***Famine: A Short History*** (Princeton UP, 2009).
⚠ **Not borrowable:** `eatingpeopleiswr0000ogra` is `printdisabled`-only. Must be bought.

**What it blocks:** the working paper we hold is stamped **not for citation**. It is the source of
findings **G2** (Suhrawardy and the sufficiency doctrine), **G3** (why the Famine Code was never
invoked), **G5** and **G6**. Those leads stand; their *sourcing* does not.

## TIER 2 — CHANGES WHAT THE VIDEO CAN SHOW

### 5. National Archives (Kew) — the two files Mansergh does not print
- **`MT 59/631`** — "Cross Trade Programme Requirements," **11 August 1943**. Sailing dates and
  allocations: items 3 and 4 of the angle-killing test in one document.
- **`MT 59/657`** — "Import of Food Grains into India," **January 1945**. The delivered-tonnage series
  that settles the competing British "we did ship" tallies.

⚠ **Do not order `CAB 195/1–2` — they are free online.** The 1943 War Cabinet conclusions are already
held via Mansergh Vol. IV.

### 6. Churchill Archives Centre, Cambridge — **`BRGS 2/17`**
Lawrence Burgis's notes of the **4 August 1943** War Cabinet, in which **Churchill reportedly says
shipping 50,000 tons posed no difficulty.** If that is what it says, it is the most damaging single
document in the case, because it is the Prime Minister contradicting the constraint his own Cabinet
minuted that day. **Not at Kew.** Also here: **`CHAR 23/11`**, the October 1943 Wavell directive.

### 7. British Library, India Office Records — **Pinnell Papers, MSS Eur D911**
Specifically **D911/1** ("Causes of the Famine"), **D911/2** (circular to district officers, 25 March
1943), **D911/4** ("Control by Embargo"; "Figures of Despatches of Rice/Paddy ex Calcutta During
1942"), **D911/9** (Government House to Pinnell, 1 Jan 1944, on Governor Herbert's ignored warnings).

**Why it matters:** Brennan published that Bengal's own Department of Civil Supplies and Revenue
Department famine records **"have not been located in the West Bengal Archives."** Every one of his
DCS citations resolves to this collection instead. **Bengal's administrative record survives mainly as
a British official's private file in London** — which is itself a fact the video should state.

### 8. National Archives of India — the **Nanavati Papers**
The Famine Inquiry Commission's evidence was taken *in camera* on the understanding it would be
destroyed. Commissioner **M. B. Nanavati kept a copy.** Both Brennan and Law-Smith build their
accounts of the provincial layer out of it, and Law-Smith says it is on this material that "the
selective use of evidence to protect the Government of India becomes apparent."

### 9. Bengal Legislative Assembly Proceedings, 1943 · the *Calcutta Gazette* · the Bengal **Famine Manual (1941)**
Not located online. The behaviour half of R5 — what Bengal's ministers actually did with powers the
statute genuinely gave them — currently rests on newspaper reconstruction at second hand.

### 10. *The Statesman* (Calcutta) — the **22 August 1943 photographs** and the **16 October 1943 leader**
The act that made the famine visible in Britain, and a candidate on-screen exhibit. Not digitised
anywhere I can reach.

## The honest summary

**Nothing on this list changes the spine of the video.** The War Cabinet minutes, the Shipping
Committee report and its annex, Behrens, the Government of India Act, the Defence of India Rules and
the Famine Inquiry Commission are all held and read, and they carry the argument.

**One item changes what the video may claim: #1, Islam 2007.** Everything else sharpens, corroborates
or provides a citable substitute for something we already have in draft form.

**And two items would change the video's strongest moment: #5 and #6** — the sailing dates, and
Burgis's note of Churchill saying 50,000 tons posed no difficulty.
