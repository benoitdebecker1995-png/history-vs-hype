### 🔴 TIER 1 — THE HINGE. Was there actually a food shortage in Bengal in 1943?

**Why this outranks everything else.** The War Cabinet's central claim (**E2**) was that the shortage
*"was not the result of physical deficiency but of hoarding."* **Amartya Sen's entitlement thesis says
substantially the same thing** — no large decline in food availability; a collapse of purchasing
power. If Sen is right, the Cabinet's **diagnosis was correct** and its failure was the remedy. If the
food-availability-decline school is right — a real crop failure from the October 1942 cyclone and
brown-spot fungus — the Cabinet was **wrong on the facts**, grain shipments *were* the answer, and
Leathers's advice was negligent rather than merely mis-analogised (**E9**).

**These are different videos.** The exhibits, the verdict and the closing all change. Resolve before scripting.

> ⚠ **PARTIALLY ANSWERED from the primary record, NOT resolved — see `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md`
> §F1–F3 and the retraction beneath them.** The Commission's own tables put 1943 supply at **43 weeks**
> of requirement against **39 in 1941**, a worse year with no famine. That is a strong finding.
>
> **But it cannot close the question, because it settles a dispute about the reliability of Bengal's
> supply data by citing that same data.** The FIC was a British-appointed commission reporting on
> British administration; one of its headline figures (the 1.5M death estimate) is already known to be
> superseded; and Afzal Husain's separate minute shows internal disagreement we have not mapped.
>
> **This tier remains a hard gate on scripting.** The scholarship is what tells us whether the
> Commission's numbers survived eighty years of examination — and what the Commission left out.
