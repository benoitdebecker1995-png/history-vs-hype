You are advising a documentary historian who is making a video about the Bengal famine of 1943 and
Winston Churchill's responsibility for it. The video's posture is **referee, not advocate**: state
the strongest version of each side, put the primary evidence on screen, and say where the evidence

I need adversarial verification, not encouragement. Assume I will put your findings on screen in
front of an audience that includes people who will fact-check every sentence in bad faith, from
both directions.

## Part 1 — The question that could kill the framing (answer this FIRST)

**Is "referee" an honest posture on this topic, or is it false balance?**

If the weight of specialist scholarship holds that Churchill and the War Cabinet bear primary
responsibility, and the exculpatory case is largely made by non-specialists or rests on
misrepresentation, then a both-sides framing would be dishonest and I need to know that before I
write anything. Conversely, if the popular prosecution case rests on claims specialists reject,
I need that too.

Tell me plainly which of these is true:
(a) genuine, live disagreement among qualified specialists reading the same archive;
(b) a specialist consensus with a popular contrarian fringe;
(c) a specialist consensus with a popular *prosecution* case that overstates it.

Name who holds which position, their credentials, and whether their work is peer-reviewed or trade.
**Do not soften this to be agreeable. If the honest answer is that one side is simply better
supported, say so and say how much better.**

## Part 2 — Adjudicate five specific claims

These five are the claims actually circulating in the audience. I mined 794 comments across the nine
most-viewed YouTube videos on this topic; the like counts show how widely each is believed. For each:
state what the primary evidence shows, cite it with a locator I can verify (archive reference,
volume and page, document date), and say whether the claim is **supported / partly supported /
unsupported / unresolvable**.

**R1 (6,980 likes — the most-believed claim in the corpus):** *"They forced farmers to produce cash
crops instead of food, and when there was no food they shipped whatever was left to England, as
reserves."*
My working suspicion, which I want tested rather than confirmed: this conflates the real
**Denial Policy** (boat denial and rice denial in coastal Bengal as an anti-invasion measure after
the fall of Burma) with grain export to Britain, and imports a nineteenth-century cash-crop narrative
into 1943. **Was rice or grain exported from India to Britain or Europe during 1942–43?** Give the
actual trade figures with a source. If India was a net importer in those years, say so with numbers.
Separately: what did the Denial Policy actually do, on whose orders, and what did it cost?

**R2 (1,989 likes):** the marginal note attributed to Churchill — *"Why hasn't Gandhi died yet"* —
usually reaching audiences via Shashi Tharoor's Oxford speech.
Trace this to its origin. Who recorded it, in what document, on what date, and where is that document
held? Is it a verbatim quotation, a paraphrase, or a reconstruction? Distinguish it from Churchill's
other recorded remarks about Indians in this period, and give me the ones that ARE securely sourced,
with locators.

**R3 (689 likes):** *"Australia sent tonnes of wheat on ships specifically for the Bengal Famine
which Churchill had diverted."*
Did Australian wheat sail for India? What happened to it? What do the shipping and War Cabinet
records show about relief tonnage requested, allocated, refused, and delivered in 1943? Mukerjee
asserts diversion; her critics dispute it. Give me the documentary basis on both sides.

**R4 (27 likes):** the Indian Famine Code obliged neighbouring provinces to release stockpiles into
Bengal, and they refused.
Which provinces restricted inter-provincial grain movement, under what authority, and when? Was this
lawful under the constitutional arrangements then in force? How much did it matter quantitatively?

**R5 (7 likes — but potentially the biggest reveal):** *"In 1943 Bengal was not ruled by Britain. It
had self-rule. It was governed by Bengali Indians."*
Under the Government of India Act 1935, what powers over food, agriculture and relief sat with the
Bengal provincial ministry versus the Governor versus the Viceroy versus London? Who held the
relevant portfolios in Bengal through 1943, and what did they do? Where were the Governor's reserve
powers used or withheld? **Be precise about the division of authority — this is the claim I am least
confident about and the one most likely to change the video.**

## Part 3 — What am I missing?

The comment corpus is almost entirely moral argument: 5% of comments discuss genocide or intent,
while 1% mention Burma, 1% the 1942 cyclone, and 0.4% the provincial governments. So the audience is
arguing without the facts.

Tell me what the specialist literature considers the decisive causal factors that are **absent from
the popular argument entirely** — and which of them are documented well enough to put on screen.

Also: what is the strongest piece of evidence against Churchill that is NOT in wide circulation? And
the strongest piece of exculpatory evidence that is NOT in wide circulation? I want both.

## Part 4 — Numbers

What is the defensible range for excess deaths, who produced each estimate, by what method, and what
does the disagreement between them turn on? I would rather present an honest range with its method
than a confident figure I cannot defend.

## Rules

- **Cite with locators.** Volume and page, archive reference, document date. "Historians say" is
  useless to me.
- **Never assert that something does not exist** because you did not find it. Say what you searched
  and did not find.
- **Flag your own uncertainty explicitly**, and mark anything you are reconstructing from memory
  rather than from a source you can name.
- **Distinguish** established fact / contested interpretation / genuinely unknowable.
- If a claim is popular but rests on a single contested source, say so plainly.
- Length is not a virtue. I would rather have six well-sourced paragraphs than thirty vague ones.
