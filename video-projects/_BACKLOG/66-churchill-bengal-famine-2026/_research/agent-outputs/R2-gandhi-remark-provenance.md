# R2 — "Why Hasn't Gandhi Died Yet?" — Provenance, Settled

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Project:** #66 Churchill / Bengal Famine
**Verdict: PRIMARY-GROUNDED, and the popular version is WRONG.** The line reaching audiences via
Shashi Tharoor ("he peevishly wrote in the margins of a file, 'Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?'") is a
double distortion of a real, archivally-verified 5 July 1944 exchange. The actual documents say
something narrower, stranger, and — on screen — better.

---

## 0. The three-layer chain, in one table

| Layer | What it actually is | Document | Wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BEDROCK — T1 primary** | Churchill's real telegram to Wavell, 5 July 1944 | Mansergh (ed.), *Transfer of Power 1942–7*, Vol. IV, **Doc. 567**, printed **p.1070**. Archival ref **L/PO/10/25** | *"Surely Mr Gandhi has made a most remarkable recovery as he is already able to take an active part in politics. How does this square with medical reports upon which his release on grounds of ill-health was agreed to by us?"* |
| **T2 — participant's diary, same day** | Wavell's own paraphrase of what the telegram felt like to receive | Wavell, ed. Moon, *The Viceroy's Journal* (OUP, 1973), entry **"July 5"**, printed **p.78** | *"Winston sent me a peevish telegram to ask why Gandhi hadn't died yet! He has never answered my telegram about food."* |
| **Popular circulation (Tharoor, Oxford Union, 28 May 2015, and everything downstream)** | A garbled retelling that treats layer 2 as if it were layer 1's verbatim text, and changes the document type | No document — a speech, unsourced | *"…he peevishly wrote in the margins of a file, 'Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?'"* |

**The popular version is wrong on three independent counts**, not one:
1. **Wrong document type.** Layer 1 is a **telegram** (No. 584, "IMPORTANT… SECRET", sent via the
   India Office). It is not a marginal annotation scrawled on a report.
2. **Wrong words.** Churchill's actual telegram never mentions death, dying, or the famine at all.
   It complains that Gandhi's visible political vigour is inconsistent with the medical case that
   justified releasing him from detention in 1943.
3. **Wrong occasion.** Tharoor's version has "conscience-stricken British officials" writing to
   Churchill about the death toll, and Churchill scrawling the line in reply. No such exchange
   exists in either document. The real telegram is unprompted, about Gandhi's health record, not a
   response to anyone reporting deaths.

**"Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?" is Wavell's own sardonic one-line gloss of Churchill's telegram —
written in his private diary, the same evening he received it — not a quotation of anything
Churchill wrote.** Even as a gloss it overstates the telegram: the real cable never raises death as
a subject.

## 1. The 5 July 1944 entry, read in full, char-exact, with the correct page

### 1a. Edition and pagination — settled

**One edition exists: Penderel Moon (ed.), *Wavell: The Viceroy's Journal* (Oxford University Press,
1973).** No second edition or repagination was found; a contemporary review (Cambridge, *Journal of
Asian Studies*) describes it as "xi, 528 pp., Appendix, Index," consistent with this scan (584 PDF
pages once front matter, unpaginated photo plates and the index are counted). The PDF held at
`library/by-topic/colonialism-slavery/WavellTheViceroysJournal-Moon-1973-OxfordUP.pdf` (an
archive.org scan, `wavellviceroysjoOOOOwave`, 1973 OUP text, digitized 2019) **is that edition.**

**The "p.94 vs p.78" edition trap was never an edition problem — it was two different index entries
under the same "Churchill" heading, one letter apart.** The book's own index, under
**"Churchill, Sir Winston [P.M.]"**, reads (verified off the OCR text layer and cross-checked against
rendered pages):

> *"asks why Gandhi hadn't died, 78"* … *"W. sends long letter to, 94"*

**Page 78 is the "why hasn't Gandhi died" entry.** **Page 94 is a completely different item** — it is
Wavell's own letter *to* Churchill of 24 October 1944, marking his first year as Viceroy (rendered as
`WAVELL-p94-EDITION-TRAP-different-letter-not-gandhi.png` for the record — it opens *"My dear Prime
Minister, I have now completed one year in the high and responsible office…"* and has nothing to do
with Gandhi's health or death). Whoever produced the "p.94" citation in the dossier most likely
mis-copied the index, pulling the wrong line from the same block of "Churchill…Gandhi" subentries.
**Use p.78. Do not use p.94.**

### 1b. The page itself

Page 78 is the **opening page of Chapter 4, "PROPOSAL FOR A POLITICAL MOVE"** — a chapter-title page,
so the number "78" is not printed on the page itself (standard book-printing convention for chapter
openers), but it is unambiguously page 78 by direct count from the numbered pages either side
(p.77, ending "…which seems unlikely." with the June 30 entry — rendered for cross-check; and p.79,
opening "statesman, but he is much too wary…" with the running head "1–17 JULY 1944 79") and by the
book's own index citing 78. Exhibit: `WAVELL-p78-telegram-why-gandhi-hadnt-died.png`.

### 1c. The entry, char-exact off the rendered page image

Four dated entries appear on this page — July 1, July 4, **July 5**, and the start of July 11. The
July 5 entry, in full:

> *"July 5*
> *I sent P.S.V. a note yesterday on preparations for the possibility of the war ending within a*
> *year. I think we must have a plan, political and otherwise, in case there is a sudden collapse of*
> *our enemies. After all, no one in July 1918 believed for a moment that the war would be over in*
> *November. At the moment we seem somewhat stuck in Normandy and I fancy the flying bombs are being*
> *rather more of a menace than we have admitted. However, the Russians seem to be sweeping on fast.*
>
> *Winston sent me a peevish telegram to ask why Gandhi hadn't died yet! He has never answered my*
> *telegram about food."*

**Two sentences, both indirect speech, no quotation marks around anything attributed to Churchill.**
Wavell is summarising the gist of a telegram he received that day — reporting his own reaction to it
("peevish") — in the middle of an entry about the war in Normandy and the flying-bomb campaign. There
is no mention of a "file," a "marginal note," a report, or a document Churchill annotated. The second
sentence is a separate, adjoining grievance — Wavell's own telegram about food imports, which
Churchill (as opposed to Amery, who did reply — see §1e below) had not personally answered.

**What Wavell is describing: the content of a telegram he received, restated in his own words, in his
own private diary, that same evening.** It is testimony about a document, not a transcription of one.

### 1d. What the telegram he's describing actually says — the bedrock

Mansergh's *Transfer of Power*, Vol. IV, reproduces the telegram itself, printed on **p.1070**,
immediately following a private letter from Wavell to Amery (dated 4 July) whose postscript reports
worsening press criticism of the food-import decision, and immediately before Amery's 6 July reply
(Doc. 568). Exhibit: `TOP4-p1070-doc567-churchill-telegram-gandhi-recovery.png`.

> **567**
> *Mr Churchill to Field Marshal Viscount Wavell (via India Office)*
> *Telegram, L/PO/10/25*
> IMPORTANT — 5 July 1944 — SECRET
>
> *"584. Following personal and top secret from Prime Minister. Surely Mr Gandhi has made a most*
> *remarkable recovery as he is already able to take an active part in politics. How does this*
> *square with medical reports upon which his release on grounds of ill-health was agreed to by us?*
> *In one of these¹ we were told that he would not be able to take any part in politics again."*
>
> ¹ *"Presumably No. 495."* [Mansergh's editorial footnote — the earlier medical report is not
> reproduced at this point in the volume and was not chased further for this trace.]

**Same date (5 July 1944), same sender (Churchill), same recipient (Wavell), same subject (Gandhi).**
There is no evidence of a second Churchill-to-Wavell telegram about Gandhi on that date, and the
correspondence Wavell describes ("a peevish telegram... about Gandhi," received that day) matches
this one on every checkable point except tone-in-writing. **This is, to the standard the documentary
record allows, the telegram Wavell is describing.**

**What it is not:** it does not ask why Gandhi hasn't died. It does not mention the famine, food,
or deaths. Its actual subject is a specific, checkable inconsistency — the medical justification
Whitehall had accepted for releasing Gandhi from detention in 1943 (on the grounds he might die in
custody) versus his visible political activity a year later. Read plainly, it is Churchill accusing
the government of India (and, implicitly, Gandhi) of having gamed the medical case for release —
a real, small, characteristically Churchillian point about credibility and being taken for a fool,
not a wish for Gandhi's death.

### 1e. The "telegram about food" in the same diary sentence

Wavell's second sentence — "He has never answered my telegram about food" — refers to his own
telegram of **3 July 1944, No. 1352-S** (Doc. 566, TOP Vol. IV p.1069, `L/E/8/3323: f 31`), warning
Amery that the government's food-import decision had been "too little and too late" and had "a very
bad Press." **Amery answered it** (Doc. 568, 6 July) — but on the Cabinet's behalf, circulating
Wavell's telegram to Ministers rather than Churchill replying personally. That is almost certainly
why Wavell registers it as unanswered by "him" specifically. This is a second, adjacent, genuinely
famine-related grievance in the same diary sentence — but it is Wavell's own telegram going
unanswered, not a Churchill remark, and it should not be merged into the "Gandhi" line on screen.

## 2. Classification — the whole finding

**Wavell's diary entry is his own paraphrase/characterisation of the CONTENT of a real Churchill
telegram — not a verbatim quotation of Churchill, and not a marginal note on any report.**

- It is **not** verbatim Churchill. No quotation marks appear around the "why hasn't Gandhi died"
  phrasing; Wavell writes in indirect speech ("sent me a...telegram to ask why...").
- It is **not** a marginal note, on a famine report or anything else. It is correspondence — a coded
  telegram routed via the India Office, with its own telegram number (584) and file reference.
- It **is** Wavell's own editorialising gloss, written the same day, in a private diary not intended
  for publication at the time (published posthumously, 1973, ed. Moon). That makes it
  **PRIMARY-VIA-TESTIMONY**: a participant's contemporaneous characterisation of a real document he
  had just received, which is a stronger status than hearsay but weaker than a verbatim quotation.
- The telegram it describes **does** survive, verbatim, archivally referenced, and is reproduced in
  full in Mansergh — which is why the overall verdict on R2 is **PRIMARY-GROUNDED, not
  PRIMARY-VIA-TESTIMONY**: bedrock exists, it is showable, and it lets the video correct the record
  rather than merely hedge it.
- Wavell's own gloss is itself an **overstatement** of what the telegram says — a fact worth stating
  on screen, because it shows the distortion did not start with Tharoor. It started, in miniature,
  with Wavell's diary shorthand, and Tharoor's retelling then compounded it by (a) treating the
  shorthand as Churchill's verbatim words and (b) inventing the "marginal note on a file, replying to
  officials who reported deaths" frame that appears in neither document.

**Attribution-mismatch flags, per the house standard:**
- **[FLAG: SOURCE-DRIFT]** — the popular claim names a "marginal note... in a file"; the actual
  document is a telegram. Different act, different kind of document, different degree of
  deliberation (a telegram is composed and sent; a marginal scrawl is private and impulsive).
- **[FLAG: PREDICATE-DRIFT]** — the popular claim's occasion ("conscience-stricken officials" report
  deaths, Churchill responds callously) does not exist in either primary. The real telegram is
  unprompted and about Gandhi's medical/political credibility, not the famine.
- **[FLAG: MIS-CREDIT]** — the words "Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?" are Wavell's, glossing Churchill's
  telegram; they are not composed or written by Churchill in that form anywhere in the record found.

## 5. Verdict and the wording the script can use

**R2 — graded status: CORROBORATED.** (Two independent primaries — Wavell's diary and Mansergh's
reproduction of the telegram itself — converge on the same date, sender, recipient and subject; the
telegram's archival reference is stated in the published volume even though the file itself was not
independently re-pulled from the British Library for this trace.)

**What the script should say, and should not say:**

- ❌ **Do not say** "Churchill wrote in the margin of a famine report, 'Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?'"
  — wrong document, wrong words, wrong occasion. This is the Tharoor-derived version and it does not
  survive contact with either primary source.
- ❌ **Do not present** "Why hasn't Gandhi died yet?" in quotation marks as Churchill's own words on
  screen. It is Wavell's paraphrase.
- ✅ **Do say, and show:** Churchill's actual telegram of 5 July 1944 (§1d) — verbatim, on screen,
  with the archival reference — asking why Gandhi's evident political vigour didn't square with the
  medical case for his release. That is a real, checkable, genuinely unflattering document: Churchill
  picking at Gandhi's credibility in the same week his own Viceroy was pressing him, unsuccessfully,
  about food imports (§1e).
- ✅ **Do say, and can show:** Wavell's own diary reaction that evening — *"a peevish telegram"* — as
  what it is: the Viceroy's private characterisation, not a transcript. This is the honest way to use
  the famous line: as evidence of how Churchill's telegram *landed* on the man managing the famine,
  not as something Churchill wrote.
- 🗣 **A workable formulation:**
  > *"The line you've probably heard is that Churchill scrawled 'Why hasn't Gandhi died yet' in the
  > margin of a famine report. That's not what happened. What happened is stranger. On 5 July 1944,
  > Churchill sent Wavell a real telegram — it survives, it's in the official archive — asking how
  > Gandhi's recovery squared with the medical reports that got him released from British custody a
  > year earlier. Wavell read it, and that same evening wrote in his diary: 'Winston sent me a
  > peevish telegram to ask why Gandhi hadn't died yet.' That's Wavell's own gloss, not Churchill's
  > words. But it tells you how the man running the famine relief effort experienced the Prime
  > Minister that week — while his own telegram about food imports went unanswered."*

**A second, separate finding worth keeping for the "securely sourced" section of the video (§4):**
the 27 July 1943 Cabinet entry (*"He hates India and everything to do with it"*, p.12) is a better
general-purpose exhibit of Churchill's attitude than the Gandhi line, because it is Wavell
characterising Churchill's *manner* (which a diarist is well placed to report) rather than glossing
the *content* of a private communication — and it comes packaged with Amery's genuinely quotable
line about George III, which is a real quotation, correctly attributed to its actual author.

## 7. What was NOT done / honest limits

- **The archival telegram itself (`L/PO/10/25`) was not independently re-pulled from British Library
  India Office Records.** This trace relies on Mansergh's published reproduction, which is standard
  practice for this project (per `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` Source E) and is a peer-vetted scholarly
  edition of the primary — but it is one hop short of the original file if a viewer challenges it.
- **Footnote 1 on Doc. 567 ("Presumably No. 495")** — the earlier medical report Churchill's telegram
  refers to — was not chased. It would sit in TOP Vol. III (the Feb–March 1943 Gandhi fast period)
  and was judged out of scope for settling R2.
- **The "beastly people/beastly religion" line was checked against Wavell only (absent) and not
  traced in Amery's diary**, which this project does not hold. If the video wants that line, it needs
  a separate trace against *The Empire at Bay* (Barnes & Nicholson eds.) — already flagged in
  `NOTEBOOK-BUILD-LIST.md` C11 as "largely redundant," which should be revisited if this specific
  quotation becomes load-bearing.
- **The Butler/Commons material (§3, Dec 1946)** is post-war and outside the famine window; included
  for completeness of "search the whole journal," not recommended for the script.
