## TIER 1 — PRIMARY DOCUMENTS (on-camera material)

### 1. The Karlsruhe verdict — THE spine document
**Bundesgerichtshof, Karlsruhe. Verdict of 19 October 1962. Case 9 StE 4/62.**
Published in the official reports as BGHSt 18, 87.

Language: German. Status: published, citable, findable.

What it establishes: Stashynsky convicted as *Gehilfe* — accomplice, not principal. The court held that the actual murderers were the Soviet state: KGB chairman Aleksandr Shelepin, and above him Khrushchev. Eight years; served four.

**This is the document you hold up.** A West German federal court formally named Moscow as the killer of Stepan Bandera.

⚠️ **Complication you must handle, not hide:** the presiding judge was Heinrich Jagusch — a long-time former NSDAP member — who compared Stashynsky to Eichmann and called him a man acting "automatically under pressure of commands." A Nazi-era judge ruling that a man who follows orders is only an accomplice is not a neutral finding. Address it head-on. It makes you unassailable; ignoring it gets you destroyed in the comments.

### 2. Bandera's Sachsenhausen prisoner card — prisoner no. 72192
Number assigned October 1943. Card records release: **28 September 1944.**

Cited in Rossoliński-Liebe (2014). Provenance to chase: Stiftung Brandenburgische Gedenkstätten / Sachsenhausen Memorial archive.

**Get a scan of this.** It is the single best artifact in the video — a Nazi camp card for the man Moscow calls a Nazi.

### 3. The OUN-B militia instructions, May 1941 — the ethnic cleansing plan
Issued to OUN-B militia units **before Barbarossa.** Key language:

> in a time of chaos and confusion it is permissible to liquidate undesirable Polish, Muscovite, and Jewish activists…

A dedicated "minorities policy" section marks Muscovites, Poles and Jews for "destruction in the course of struggle."

Source: reproduced in Himka's work (English). Original in *ОУН в 1941 році: документи* (Institute of History of Ukraine, NAN). Ukrainian.

**This is the beat-4 killer.** The plan predates the German invasion. Nobody handed it to them.

### 4. Act of Restoration of Ukrainian Statehood — 30 June 1941
Read by Stetsko from the Prosvita balcony, Rynok Square 10, Lviv. Followed by Bandera's own decree appointing Stetsko head of state.

The text pledges to work closely with National-Socialist Greater Germany "under the leadership of its leader Adolf Hitler."

⚠️ **But:** the Germans did *not* approve it. It blindsided them, and they read it as a coup attempt. This is the fact that makes the video honest — Bandera wasn't performing for Berlin, he was trying to force Berlin's hand.

### 5. Einsatzgruppen report, 16 July 1941
Records the OUN-B slogan then circulating: Ukraine without Jews, Poles or Germans; Poles beyond the San; Jews to the gallows. German. In the *Ereignismeldungen UdSSR* series.

### 7. The three conflicting accounts of Zellenbau
- Stetsko (writing 1967): Bandera arrived January 1942
- Bandera, 1950 interview: held by Gestapo in Berlin until 1943
- Bandera, 1956 Munich interrogation: moved winter 1942–43

The man's own story doesn't line up across three tellings. That's a lovely on-camera moment about how myths get built — including by their subjects.

## THE OPPOSITION — read these or get ambushed

You will be accused of parroting Rossoliński-Liebe. Pre-empt it:

- **Marples, Cârstocea, Veselý** all criticise his fascism framing — Cârstocea says his definition of fascism is too wide; Veselý says the book is intentionally hostile.
- **Snyder:** similar violence occurred in regions where the OUN had no presence at all. Organisational blame can't explain everything.
- **Kopstein / Motyl / Kiebuzinski:** stress multiple contributory factors — Soviet occupation trauma, the NKVD prison massacres, pre-existing antisemitism among *both* Poles and Ukrainians, petty criminals rather than ideologues on the ground.
- At the national level, OUN-B's Second Congress (Kraków, April 1941) formally *discouraged* pogroms as a distraction from Moscow. The local instructions cut the other way. Both are true.

## FACTUAL TRAPS — every one of these will be thrown at you

1. **Bandera was not in a camp for the whole war.** Timeline: 5 July 1941 placed under *custodia honesta* in Kraków → Berlin → released 14 July but confined to the city → Spandau prison from 15 Sept 1941 → Sachsenhausen from Jan 1942 → released 28 Sept 1944. **He was at liberty during the Lviv pogrom of 1 July.** Get this sequence exactly right or the whole video falls.

2. **Fridental Castle.** The Germans let the Zellenbau Ukrainians *out* to meet OUN representatives 200 metres from the camp. This single fact demolishes "he was locked up, he couldn't have known."

3. **Both his brothers died at Auschwitz.** Do not skip this. It's the fact that earns you the right to call him a fascist thirty seconds later.

4. **He never repudiated Volhynia after release.** The perpetrators called themselves *banderivtsi*. He kept the name.
