## Strongest evidence

1. **NotebookLM, Avi Shlaim:** a Palestinian national movement emerged during the interwar period and led the 1936–1939 revolt. Source ID `fc8ef116-3baa-4e96-a997-e50703044e0c`.
2. **NotebookLM, Benny Morris:** the national movement was initially elite-led and uneven; many people retained clan, village, regional or wider Arab identities, but the national idea spread during the Mandate. Source ID `1fc5fdc7-bb39-4630-ab55-21d15ed6a278`.
3. **NotebookLM, Kenneth Stein / Rosemary Sayigh:** the Arab Executive emerged from the Muslim-Christian Associations in 1920; the Arab Higher Committee formed in 1936 as the representative leadership of the national movement. Source IDs `e42c39da-376c-4e74-a702-b4f420bc4b84`, `fcda35ac-7392-4756-9f2a-90ffeea4681e`.
4. **Beška and Foster (2021):** their open-access study found more than 170 appearances of *Filastini* in Arabic sources from 1898–1914, including six pieces where writers explicitly asserted their Palestinianness. This directly disproves the absolute claim that Arabic inhabitants never used the term for themselves before 1948.
5. **UN primary material, 1947:** Jamal Husseini appeared as vice-president of the Arab Higher Committee for Palestine and advocated independence for an Arab state in the whole of Palestine. This demonstrates a Palestine-specific political claim before the Mandate ended, even though its proponents also identified as Arabs.

### Source criticism

- The NotebookLM collection contains a genuine historiographical fork about the breadth and maturity of Palestinian Arab identity before 1948. Benny Morris stresses that separate mass identity remained weak and uneven into the 1940s; Morris and Avi Shlaim nevertheless both document an interwar Palestinian Arab national movement. This supports “developing and non-exclusive,” not “invented after 1948.”
- Claims that the KGB manufactured the people or identity rely heavily on Ion Mihai Pacepa, advocacy works, or later repetitions. They can support a claim about Soviet/PLO reframing only with attribution and source caveats; they cannot erase independently documented pre-KGB institutions and self-identification.
