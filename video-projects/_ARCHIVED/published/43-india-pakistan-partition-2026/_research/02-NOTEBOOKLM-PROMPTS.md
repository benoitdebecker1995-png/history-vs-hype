CLAIM 7: The June 3 Plan (also called the Mountbatten Plan) was the specific proposal accepted by Nehru, Jinnah, and the Sikh leader Baldev Singh on June 2, 1947. It proposed partition with a boundary commission.
CLAIM 8: Punjab and Bengal legislative assemblies voted for partition in late June 1947, making the "will to partition" a local democratic reality — not solely a British imposition.

CLAIM 2: This administrative shortcut created bizarre "bridgeheads" — small pockets of Indian territory on the Pakistani side of the river, and Pakistani territory on the Indian side, particularly near the Ferozepore canal headworks.
CLAIM 3: This cartographic anomaly demonstrates that Radcliffe was tracing existing administrative lines on a map in Simla rather than conducting a careful geographic or topographic survey of the actual terrain.
CLAIM 4: The use of district lines rather than the river as a natural boundary created immediate practical problems for military deployment, irrigation management, and civilian administration on both sides.
