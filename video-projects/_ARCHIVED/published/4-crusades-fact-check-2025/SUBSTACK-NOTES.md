## Note 1 (the image)

Nicetas Choniates, a Byzantine senator, told the women in his party to smear mud on their faces so the crusaders wouldn't target them for rape. A Christian senator fleeing Christian soldiers in a Christian city.

## Note 2 (the chaplain)

The crusaders' own chaplain, Fulcher of Chartres, on the massacre at Jerusalem: "Not one of them was allowed to live. They did not spare the women and children." He then described the killers changing into "fresh garments" and walking barefoot to worship. The slaughter was the worship.

## Note 3 (the catch-22)

The Crusades were built on a contradiction: a good knight had to also be a good Christian. At Jerusalem, they resolved it. The massacre was "a just and splendid judgement of God."

## Note 4 (the mechanism)

The Fourth Crusade looted 3.6 million hyperpyra from Constantinople. Civil wars, plague, and Ottoman expansion followed — but the sack broke the treasury. By the 1400s, the entire Byzantine Empire could barely pay 20,000 per year. In 1453, Byzantium mustered 5,000 soldiers against 80,000 Ottomans. The walls held for a millennium. The foundation was gone.

## Note 5 (both sides wrong)

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has a "Deus Vult" tattoo — the crusader battle cry. The Egyptian theorist who inspired al-Qaeda wrote that "the crusading spirit runs in the blood of all westerners." Both sides need the Crusades to be a clash of civilizations. Neither side quotes the sources honestly.

## Note 6 (the Ma'arra entry)

Multiple crusader chroniclers — Fulcher of Chartres, Albert of Aachen, Radulph of Caen — recorded what happened at Ma'arra in 1098. From Fulcher's own recruitment manifesto: "Many of our people, harassed by excessive hunger, cut pieces from the buttocks of the Saracens already dead there, which they cooked and devoured."

## Note 7 (steelman + verdict)

The defenders of the Crusades aren't entirely wrong. The Byzantines lost a catastrophic battle to the Seljuk Turks in 1071. Their emperor really did ask the Pope for military help. But a "defensive" war that destroyed the largest Christian empire on earth and shattered the one frontier holding back the Ottomans? The word stops working.

## Note 8 (closing image)

Fulcher changed into fresh garments after the massacre and walked barefoot to worship. Nicetas smeared mud on his wife's face and carried his infant through the smoke. Two Christians. Two memories of the same war. One wrote a recruitment manifesto. The other wrote an autopsy.
