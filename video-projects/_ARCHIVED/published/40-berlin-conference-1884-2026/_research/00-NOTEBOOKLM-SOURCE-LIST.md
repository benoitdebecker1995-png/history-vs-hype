## PRIMARY SOURCES [P]

### [P1] General Act of the Berlin Conference (1885)
- **Type:** International treaty — primary document
- **Date:** February 26, 1885
- **Repository:** Avalon Project, Yale Law School
- **Access:** https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/beract.asp (free, full text)
- **Also in:** Hertslet, *The Map of Africa by Treaty*, Vol. 2, pp. 468-494
- **Key articles:**
  - Preamble (stated aims: free trade, anti-slavery, "civilization")
  - Article 34 (notification of new territories)
  - Article 35 ("effective occupation" doctrine)
  - Articles on Congo Basin free navigation
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[P1] General-Act-Berlin-Conference-1885.pdf`

## ACADEMIC SOURCES [A] — University Press Publications

### The Conference Itself

#### [A1] Forster, Stig, Wolfgang J. Mommsen, and Ronald Robinson (eds.). *Bismarck, Europe, and Africa: The Berlin Africa Conference 1884-1885 and the Onset of Partition*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press for the German Historical Institute, 1988 (590 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0199205004
- **Access:** Archive.org (free full text); AbeBooks (~$30 used)
- **Why essential:** The ONLY peer-reviewed multi-author scholarly volume dedicated entirely to the Berlin Conference. Brings together German, British, and African historians. Covers Bismarck's motives, each delegation's priorities, economic interests, missionary aspirations. Reviewed in *Journal of African History* (Cambridge).
- **Key chapters:** Conference mechanics, individual delegation strategies, the "effective occupation" debate
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A1] Forster-Mommsen-Robinson-Bismarck-Europe-Africa-OUP-1988.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A6] Minawi, Mostafa. *The Ottoman Scramble for Africa: Empire and Diplomacy in the Sahara and the Hijaz*
- **Publisher:** Stanford University Press, 2016 (240 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0804799270
- **Cost:** ~$30
- **Why important:** Reframes the conference by showing the Ottoman Empire as an active participant (not just Europeans vs. passive Africa). Uses previously untapped Ottoman archival evidence. Reviewed in *African Studies Review* (Cambridge). Gives the "what textbooks don't show" angle.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A6] Minawi-Ottoman-Scramble-Stanford-2016.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

### Legal Framework

#### [A3] Anghie, Antony. *Imperialism, Sovereignty and the Making of International Law*
- **Publisher:** Cambridge University Press, 2005 (Cambridge Studies in International and Comparative Law, No. 37, 356 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0521828925
- **Cost:** ~$40
- **Why essential:** The definitive TWAIL (Third World Approaches to International Law) analysis. Shows "effective occupation" wasn't neutral law — it was designed to legitimize European seizure of non-European territory. Traces the "civilizing mission" embedded in sovereignty. Winner of ESIL Book Prize.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A3] Anghie-Imperialism-Sovereignty-Cambridge-2005.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A17] Craven, Matthew. *The Decolonization of International Law: State Succession and the Law of Treaties*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press, 2007 (Oxford Monographs in International Law)
- **ISBN:** 978-0199577880
- **Cost:** ~$40
- **Why useful:** Explains WHY African states inherited colonial borders at independence instead of redrawing them. The legal mechanism that locked Berlin borders in place.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A17] Craven-Decolonization-Intl-Law-Oxford-2007.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded (Tier 3)

### African Perspectives

- **ISBN:** 978-0801839313
- **Cost:** ~$25

- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A5] Boahen-African-Perspectives-JHU-1987.pdf`

#### [A7] Mamdani, Mahmood. *Neither Settler nor Native: The Making and Unmaking of Permanent Minorities*
- **Publisher:** Harvard University Press, 2020 (416 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0674987326
- **Cost:** ~$20
- **Why essential:** Mamdani (Columbia/Makerere) — leading African political theorist. Argues colonial partition politicized ethnic majorities against manufactured minorities. The "permanent minority" problem IS the mechanism linking Berlin 1884 to civil wars today. Reviewed in *Ethics and International Affairs* (Cambridge).
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A7] Mamdani-Neither-Settler-Native-Harvard-2020.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A14] Mamdani, Mahmood. *Citizen and Subject: Contemporary Africa and the Legacy of Late Colonialism*
- **Publisher:** Princeton University Press, 1996 (2nd ed. 2018, 353 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0691180427
- **Why important:** Winner of 1997 Herskovits Award. Shows how colonial "indirect rule" created citizens (urban) vs. subjects (rural/tribal) — governance template that persisted into independence.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A14] Mamdani-Citizen-Subject-Princeton-2018.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded (Tier 3)

### Ethnic Partition & State Failure

#### [A2] Michalopoulos, Stelios and Elias Papaioannou. "The Long-Run Effects of the Scramble for Africa." *American Economic Review* 106, no. 7 (2016): 1802-1848.
- **Publisher:** American Economic Association
- **Access:** AEA website (free with registration)
- **Key content:**
  - **190 ethnic groups split** (YOUR core statistic — peer-reviewed source)
  - Split groups have significantly higher civil conflict rates
  - Maps showing ethnic territories vs. colonial borders
  - Somali case study included
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A2] Michalopoulos-Papaioannou-AER-2016.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A4] Herbst, Jeffrey. *States and Power in Africa: Comparative Lessons in Authority and Control*
- **Publisher:** Princeton University Press, 2nd ed. 2014 (272 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0691164144
- **Cost:** ~$35
- **Why essential:** Co-winner of 2001 Gregory Luebbert Award (APSA). Argues Berlin's arbitrary borders over low-density populations made it structurally impossible for African states to project authority — producing endemic weakness and conflict. THE foundational political science argument for WHY colonial borders cause state failure.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A4] Herbst-States-Power-Africa-Princeton-2014.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A10] Young, Crawford. *The Postcolonial State in Africa: Fifty Years of Independence, 1960-2010*
- **Publisher:** University of Wisconsin Press, 2012 (438 pp.)
- **ISBN:** 978-0299291440
- **Cost:** ~$30
- **Why important:** Comprehensive comparative analysis of how colonial borders created states without internal legitimacy → ethnic conflict → state failure. Pairs with Michalopoulos (quantitative) by providing institutional mechanism.
- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A10] Young-Postcolonial-State-Wisconsin-2012.pdf`
- **Status:** ☐ Downloaded

#### [A15] Young, Crawford. *The African Colonial State in Comparative Perspective*

- **ISBN:** 978-0300068795

- **NotebookLM filename:** `[A15] Young-African-Colonial-State-Yale-1994.pdf`
