# The Law France Wrote Itself

France didn't need Germany to persecute its own citizens. The archives prove it.

---

In October 1940, the French government stripped tens of thousands of its own citizens of their jobs, their professional standing, and their place in public life, based on their grandparents' race. The law was written in French. It has never been fully translated into English. And the most important thing about it: Germany never asked for it.

I translated the whole thing. Then I found something worse: a draft of the law covered in handwritten notes from the French head of state himself, Philippe Pétain. Those notes had never been translated or walked through in English. They prove he didn't just sign this law. He personally made it harsher.

**Why it matters:** The myth that Germany imposed anti-Jewish legislation on Vichy France was the official French position for fifty years. The primary source, the actual text of the law, remained inaccessible to English readers. That inaccessibility let the myth survive.

---

## The Lie That Lasted Fifty Years

After the war, Vichy's own Justice Minister, Joseph Barthélemy, said this:

> "This legislation is not a French initiative. It is entirely of German origin."

The archives prove that was false.

I've spent a lot of time on this channel looking at how governments use documents to justify what they've already decided to do. My video on the Berlin Conference covers a room full of European diplomats drawing lines across a continent they'd never visited. My video on Belize traces a border dispute to a treaty one side may not have even signed. In both cases, the document did the work. It made the decision look orderly, legal, inevitable.

The Statut des Juifs works the same way, only in reverse. The document was hidden. And for decades, the absence of the document in English helped sustain a comfortable story: that France was just following orders.

Three pieces of evidence destroy that story.

First, the armistice France signed with Germany in June 1940 said nothing about Jews. Historians Michael Marrus and Robert Paxton went through both the German and French archives. Their conclusion:

> "Close study of German and French archives reveals no German demand in 1940 that Vichy adopt an anti-Jewish policy."
> — Marrus & Paxton, *Vichy France and the Jews*, p. 3

Second, France was already passing anti-Jewish measures before Germany issued anything. In July 1940, they restricted public service jobs to people born of French fathers. In August, they restricted the medical profession. In late August, they repealed the Marchandeau Law, the law that had made antisemitic attacks in the press illegal. All of this before a single German ordinance on Jews.

Third, when Germany finally did act on September 27th, 1940, what they issued was limited: a census, property registration, only in the occupied zone, using a religious definition of who was Jewish. Six days later, France responded with something far broader. A racial definition. A full professional purge. Applied across the entire French empire.

France didn't follow Germany. France went further.

---

## Race, Not Religion

The law's first move is to define its target. The word it uses is "race." Not religion. *Race juive*, the Jewish race.

> "Article 1 — For the application of the present law, any person descended from three grandparents of the Jewish race, or from two grandparents of the same race if their spouse is themselves Jewish, is regarded as Jewish."

Three Jewish grandparents made you Jewish under the law. It didn't matter if you were baptised Catholic. It didn't matter if you'd never been inside a synagogue. Two Jewish grandparents? You could escape the definition, unless your spouse was Jewish. Then you were fully Jewish under the law.

I knew the Vichy laws were bad. I knew the general outlines. But reading the actual text, clause by clause, and seeing that the law never defines what makes a grandparent Jewish gave me a specific kind of discomfort. The definition is circular on purpose. A vague definition gave authorities maximum discretion to apply it however they wanted.

Marrus and Paxton put it plainly:

> "Vichy's Statut des juifs went further than the German ordinance. Whereas the German ordinance spoke only of religion, the Vichy law spoke openly of race."
> — Marrus & Paxton, *Vichy France and the Jews*, p. 8

This was the first time the word "race" appeared in French law. It broke a 150-year republican tradition of legal blindness to religion and ethnicity. And by April 1941, in a reversal of influence, the Germans actually adopted Vichy's broader definition.

The student became the model.

## The Purge List

The racial definition was the foundation. Article 2 was the machine built on top of it.

Head of State. Every court, from the supreme court down to the local magistrate. Every elected assembly. All prefects. All police. All diplomats. All colonial governors. All military officers. All teachers. Any director of a company receiving public money.

That's not a ban on a few senior positions. That's a systematic removal from every institution that exercises authority, shapes culture, or controls information. By 1942, roughly 3,400 public servants had been dismissed in mainland France. Another 2,500 in Algeria.

On top of that, Article 5 bans Jews, "without condition or reservation," from directing, managing, or editing any newspaper, magazine, or periodical. Then cinema: directors, producers, screenwriters. Then radio. No war-hero exceptions. No exemptions at all. The only thing Jews could still publish was scientific journals.

That one exception tells you everything about the law's purpose. A Jewish scientist writing about chemistry isn't dangerous. A Jewish journalist shaping what French people think is. This was about controlling who tells France its stories.

The purge was domestic. But the law didn't stop at France's borders.

---

## Twenty-Two People in Guadeloupe

Article 9 is one sentence:

> "Article 9 — The present law is applicable to Algeria, to the colonies, protectorate countries, and mandated territories."

One sentence, and the entire law extended across the French empire.

In Morocco, 32 of 37 Jewish lawyers were removed from the bar. Four days after the statute, a separate law stripped over a hundred thousand Algerian Jews of the citizenship they'd held for seventy years. French administrators applied the law everywhere, including territories thousands of miles from the nearest German soldier. In French Indochina, they counted 140 Jews. French West Africa, 110. Martinique, 36. Guadeloupe, 22. Madagascar, 26.

There was no military requirement to register 22 people in Guadeloupe. There were no German troops in Martinique. Marrus and Paxton call this the "definitive proof" that Vichy's antisemitism was indigenous. No German pressure. No German troops. Just French administrators, French law, French zeal.

We have to keep in mind that the scholarly consensus is clear on one point, even while it's debated on others: Germany's occupation was the necessary catalyst. Historian Julian Jackson is careful to note the legislation "could not have occurred" without the 1940 defeat. But the initiative, the drafting, the racial language, the colonial application, all of that was French.

The law was already the most comprehensive anti-Jewish legislation in Western Europe. And then someone made it worse.

## The Draft That Was Hidden for Seventy Years

In 2010, seventy years to the day after the statute was signed, a lawyer named Serge Klarsfeld held a press conference at the Mémorial de la Shoah in Paris. He had a typed draft of the statute, stamped "DOCUMENT CONFIDENTIEL," covered in handwritten annotations. Handwriting experts confirmed they were Philippe Pétain's.

I have this document. I translated his notes.

The typed draft listed specific educational administrators: rectors, inspectors, school directors. Pétain wrote in the margin: *"tout personnel enseignant."* All teaching personnel. Not just the people who run the schools. Every teacher at every level.

The draft banned Jews from specific named institutions. Pétain added: *"toutes assemblées issues de l'élection."* All assemblies arising from election. Every municipal council, every departmental council, every elected body in France and its empire.

The draft banned Jews from the higher courts. Pétain extended it to the *"justices de paix,"* the village magistrate courts. The lowest level of French justice.

And then there's the change that matters most.

The typed draft, already harsh, had a protection clause. It read: *"être descendant de Juifs nés français ou naturalisés avant l'année 1860."* To be a descendant of Jews born French or naturalized before the year 1860. That's an eighty-year grandfather clause. If your family had been French since before Napoleon III, you were protected. These were France's most established, most assimilated Jewish families. Families who'd been French for generations.

Pétain drew a line through the whole sentence.

One pen stroke. Eighty years of citizenship, gone. It didn't matter if your great-grandfather fought for France. If you fit the racial definition, you were out.

## France, Not Vichy

After the war, Charles de Gaulle declared all of Vichy's laws null and void. His position: Vichy was never the real France. The Republic had just been interrupted. It went to sleep in 1940, woke up in 1944. If Vichy wasn't the real France, then France bore no responsibility.

That was the official position for fifty years.

It held until 1995. Jacques Chirac, standing at the site where French police had arrested over thirteen thousand Jews in 1942, said what no French president had said before:

> "France, the home of the Enlightenment and of the rights of man, delivered those she should have protected to their executioners."
> — Jacques Chirac, Vel d'Hiv commemoration, July 16, 1995 (via Jackson, *France on Trial*, p. 341)

Not Vichy. France.

I want to be fair about one thing. Seventy-five percent of Jews living in France survived the war. That's a higher survival rate than almost any other occupied country (in the Netherlands, 73% of Jews were killed). But that survival wasn't because of this law. It was because of ordinary French people who chose to protect their neighbours despite what their government was doing.

The law we just read didn't protect anyone. The law created the categories, built the lists, and gave the bureaucracy its instructions. The protection came from people who ignored it.
