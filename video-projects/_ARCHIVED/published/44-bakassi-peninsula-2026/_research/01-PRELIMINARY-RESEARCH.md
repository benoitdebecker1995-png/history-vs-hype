## THE CORE STORY

### What Happened

In 1884, the Kings and Chiefs of Old Calabar signed a "Treaty of Protection" with Britain — handing London a stewardship role over their territory, including what is today the Bakassi Peninsula. Twenty-nine years later, in 1913, Britain signed the Anglo-German Agreement, which transferred Bakassi to German Kamerun via Articles XVIII-XX. The Old Calabar chiefs were not consulted. The peninsula changed hands without the indigenous rulers knowing a treaty had been signed over their land.

After World War I, German Kamerun became a British mandate, then a trust territory. In 1961, the Southern Cameroons voted by UN plebiscite to join French-speaking Cameroon rather than Nigeria. Bakassi went with it — and Nigeria didn't formally contest this until the 1990s.

In 2002, the International Court of Justice ruled 13-3 that Bakassi belonged to Cameroon, citing the 1913 Anglo-German Agreement as legally binding. In 2008, Nigeria completed the handover, ceding 665 km² containing between 150,000 and 300,000 people. The Greentree Agreement (2006) was supposed to protect Nigerian nationals who remained — but as of 2019, its provisions were still largely unimplemented. By 2021, a new Biafran separatist insurgency (the Black Marines, armed wing of the Biafra Nations League) had launched attacks against Cameroonian forces inside Bakassi.

### The Myth vs. Reality Angle

**Standard Nigerian narrative:** "Obasanjo betrayed us. Nigeria was robbed of its territory by an ICJ biased toward colonial-era maps. The 1884 Treaty of Protection gave Old Calabar sovereignty, which passed to Nigeria at independence."

**What the documents actually show:** The 1884 Treaty did NOT transfer sovereignty from Old Calabar chiefs to Britain — the ICJ majority agreed with Nigeria on this point. But Britain signed the 1913 treaty anyway. The real question is: did Britain have the legal authority to transfer what it did not formally own? The ICJ said yes — Britain exercised de facto control sufficient to conclude a valid treaty. Three dissenting judges, including Judge Koroma, said no.

**The deeper layer competitors miss:** Nigeria itself implicitly accepted Cameroon's claim for decades. In 1961-1962, Nigerian leaders raised no objection when Southern Cameroons joined Cameroon, taking Bakassi with it. In 1975, Nigerian head of state Yakubu Gowon signed the Maroua Declaration, which set a maritime boundary consistent with Cameroon's claim. His successor Murtala Mohammed repudiated it — but the ICJ said Gowon's signature was legally binding. Nigeria was not robbed of something it was actively asserting. It lost something it had tacitly conceded.

## STANDARD NARRATIVE (What competitors will say)

This is the version every other YouTuber will tell. The script must go beyond it.

1. Bakassi was an oil-rich peninsula that Nigeria lost to Cameroon in a 2002 ICJ ruling.
2. The ruling was based on colonial-era British treaties that Nigerians say were illegitimate.
3. Nigeria's president Obasanjo accepted the ruling, sparking domestic outrage and accusations of betrayal.
4. 150,000-300,000 Nigerians were displaced.
5. Bakassi is now Cameroonian territory, though insurgents continue to fight.

**What this misses:** The standard narrative treats this as a clean "colonialism screwed Nigeria" story. It does not explain that: (a) Nigeria silently accepted Cameroonian jurisdiction for 30+ years after independence; (b) a Nigerian head of state explicitly signed the maritime boundary in 1975; (c) even the ICJ partially agreed with Nigeria's reading of the 1884 treaty but upheld the 1913 agreement anyway; (d) the oil claim is contested — no commercially viable deposits have been confirmed on the peninsula itself.

## RAW NOTES: ARTICLE XVIII-XX TEXT (Anglo-German Agreement 1913)

For on-screen display — these are the exact treaty provisions that decided Bakassi:

**Article XVIII:** "Thence it follows the thalweg of the Akpakorum (Akwayafe) River, dividing the Mangrove Islands near Ikang in the way shown on the aforesaid map T.S.C.S. 2240, sheet 2. It then follows the thalweg of the Akwayafe as far as a straight line joining Bakasi Point and King Point."

**Article XX:** "Should the lower course of the Akwayafe so change its mouth as to transfer it to the Rio del Rey, it is agreed that the area now known as the Bakasi Peninsula shall still remain German territory."

These provisions were the primary legal basis for the 2002 ICJ ruling. Verify exact text against the primary source before using in script.
