### §II CONCEDE-BEAT / Steelman (1:00 – 1:45)

**Highest-impact port in the script.** The 1,000 vs 80,000 multiplier is the most quantitative beat, currently delivered as a bullet list with two citation tags. Fig Tree's anatomy-cast / cranial-capacity comparison move applies directly — spatial pointer on a numeric comparison graphic.

**Proposed:**
```
[STAY ON TALKING HEAD]

People like Thomas Sowell are right: slavery existed in Africa before
Europeans arrived. It existed globally — from the Trans-Saharan routes
to the Mediterranean.

But there's a massive difference between a regional practice and an

[CUT TO: comparison graphic — Trans-Saharan ~1,000/year (LEFT) vs Atlantic peak ~80,000/year (RIGHT), with source labels]

[POINTER — Trans-Saharan figure, LEFT]

[POINTER — Atlantic figure, RIGHT]

[BACK TO TALKING HEAD]

It didn't just scale the numbers. It fundamentally changed the kind
```

### §III THE TURN / Afonso letter (1:45 – 2:30)

**Port — stay on face for both Afonso quotes.** Both quotes are emotional/performable (an African king's protest, an explicit demand). Fig Tree's Antigone-performance pattern: stay on talking head for verbatim historical-figure speech; citation as text overlay; B-roll AFTER the quote, for analysis.

**Proposed edits:**
```
[STAY ON TALKING HEAD — citation overlay only on both quotes below]

[SOURCE OVERLAY: Howard French, Born in Blackness, p. 296]

Here are Afonso's exact words to his European counterpart:

> "The said merchants take our countrymen the sons of our land and
> the sons of our noblemen every day... our land is being all but
> depopulated."

He didn't stop there. He issued an explicit demand to shut the system
down:

> "Our will is that in this Kingdom there be no slave trade nor
> [any] outlet for it."

[NOW CUT TO LETTER B-ROLL for the rhetorical question that follows]

If the Atlantic system was just Europeans tapping into a pre-existing
African market, why is a reigning African monarch on the record
demanding a cessation of the trade while it was happening?
```

Net delta: +0. Just visual blocking. Performance > display for verbatim quotes.

### §V SECOND HOOK / João III gaslighting (4:00 – 5:00)

**Single port — spatial pointer on the confession-inside-the-denial.**

The João III gaslighting quote contains the indictment in one clause: *"as a result of the suffering that the Portuguese cause you."* He admits the Portuguese are causing suffering in the same sentence he denies the depopulation. Fig Tree's spatial-pointer move makes this audible.

**Proposed:**
```
[STAY ON TALKING HEAD for the question]

So how did Europe respond when an African monarch begged them to stop
the kidnappings?

In 1529, the King of Portugal, João III, replied to Afonso. He told
the King of Kongo that the depopulation he was protesting wasn't
actually happening.

[CUT TO: João III's letter, Newitt Doc 36 — full text]

[POINTER — highlight operative clause inside the longer passage]

Read it. The denial contains the confession.

> "You say in your letters that you do not want there to be any
> slave trade in your kingdom because it is depopulating your land.
> I can believe that you only say this as a result of the
> *suffering that the Portuguese cause you*, because I am told of
> the great size of Kongo and how it is so populated that it
> appears that not a single slave has left it."

[POINTER — re-highlight "suffering that the Portuguese cause you"]

He admits the suffering inside the same sentence that denies the
demographic effect. The buyer concedes the crime even as he denies
the cost.
```

Net delta: +25 words ("Read it. The denial contains the confession" + "He admits the suffering inside the same sentence that denies the demographic effect. The buyer concedes the crime even as he denies the cost."). This addition is the highest-leverage edit in §V because it converts a long quote into a pointed indictment.

### §VII EVIDENCE 3 / Romanus Pontifex (7:00 – 9:00)

**THE Fig Tree moment of the script.** Latin/English side-by-side is the Rosetta Stone pattern applied verbatim — Fig Tree's exact technique. Plus the bull's grant of five permissions ("invade, search out, capture, vanquish and subdue") is a clause-list ripe for §I-style MAL 40 treatment.

**Proposed:**
```
[CUT TO: Split screen — Latin Davenport p. 16 (LEFT) // English translation p. 23 (RIGHT)]

On the left — the original Latin, 1455.
On the right — the English translation.

[POINTER — highlight Latin operative phrase]

> "...illorumque personas in perpetuam servitutem redigendi..."

[POINTER — highlight matching English]

> "...and to reduce their persons to perpetual slavery..."

[CUT TO: Full English text of the bull's grant, Davenport p. 23]

The bull grants Portugal the right to do five things.

[POINTER 1 — "invade, search out, capture"]
To invade. To search out. To capture.

[POINTER 2 — "vanquish, and subdue"]
To vanquish and subdue all Saracens and pagans whatsoever.

[POINTER 3 — "and to reduce their persons to perpetual slavery"]
And to reduce their persons to perpetual slavery.

[BACK TO TALKING HEAD]

This was 1455. The massive trans-Atlantic crossing hadn't even started
yet. The legal architecture for permanent, perpetual slavery was drawn
up before the commerce existed.

You don't write a legal framework to reduce persons to perpetual
slavery if you're just passively buying people from a market. You
write it when you are building an empire.
```

Net delta: +20 words. This is the smoking-gun moment of the entire script. Spatial-pointer treatment turns it from a citation into an indictment.
