### Contextual support (1441-1448 raid pattern)

The 1444 raid is not isolated. Zurara documents multiple direct-capture episodes:

## BEAT 5 — 1518 Gorrevod Asiento ✅ VERIFIED 2026-05-25

**Refutes implicit claim:** That the Atlantic slave system was a loose decentralized market, not a state-licensed industry.

**Source:** Royal License (asiento) issued by King Charles I of Spain (Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor) to Lorenzo de Gorrevod, August 1518. Reproduced in Elizabeth Donnan, *Documents Illustrative of the History of the Slave Trade to America*, Vol. I (Carnegie Institution, 1930), pp. 41-42, Document 2.

**Authority:** A royal contract — the Spanish crown licensing a private operator to transport 4,000 Africans directly to the Indies. Operationalizes the papal license (Romanus Pontifex, 1455) as a commercial state contract 63 years later. **The bull authorized perpetual slavery; the asiento contracted for it by the thousand.**

**Decision rationale (B.1, user approved 2026-05-25):** This promotes asiento to 5th document, breaking the original "four documents" brand promise in the Hook. Decision was accepted because: (a) the asiento operationalizes the 1455 papal license in a single clean narrative step (bull → contract), (b) structurally it closes the loop from Beat 4 (license) to Beat 5 (commercial operationalization), and (c) the closing verdict "He chartered an industry" lands harder with the contract receipt as the final exhibit. The hook and closing were updated to "five documents" framing in v3.

**Translation cited on screen:** Elizabeth Donnan, *Documents Illustrative of the History of the Slave Trade to America*, Vol. I (Carnegie Institution, 1930), pp. 41-42.

### Verbatim quote (✅ VERIFIED 2026-05-25)

> "Know ye that I have given permission... to Lorenzo de Gorrevod... whereby he... may proceed to take to the Indies, the islands and the mainland of the ocean sea... four thousand negro slaves both male and female, provided they be Christians... without coming to Seville, as many as possible being taken from Cape Verde."

**Location:** Donnan, *Documents Illustrative*, Vol. I (Carnegie Institution, 1930), pp. 41-42.
**Validated for:** verbatim text + volume + page numbers + document number (Doc 2)

**Validation date:** 2026-05-25

**Filming gate:** ✅ CLEARED. Verbatim + page + volume + document confirmed via Saltwater Slavery notebook round-trip 2026-05-25.

### Why the asiento matters

1. **Four thousand people in a single contract.** Not a diffuse market — a numbered license.
2. **Cape Verde sourcing.** By 1518, Cape Verde was a Portuguese entrepôt feeding African captives into the Atlantic system — the same Atlantic-island infrastructure the Bridge beat established.
3. **"Provided they be Christians."** The Christianization requirement meant Africans were forcibly baptized before transport — the ecclesiastical veneer on the commercial extraction.
4. **Sixty-three years after Romanus Pontifex.** The bull (1455) → the contract (1518) arc spans one human lifetime. The legal license becomes a commercial instrument within living memory of the pope who issued it.

**Validated for:** verbatim text (Smallwood's reproduction of van Dantzig/Jones translation) + edition+page (both Smallwood p. 30 and van Dantzig/Jones p. 176)

### CRITICAL FRAMING CORRECTION (2026-05-13)

De Marees is describing the **Gold Coast specifically**, NOT Africa as a whole. He explicitly distinguishes the Gold Coast from Angola/Kongo, where the supply chain *did* produce captives at scale. The argument is regional, not continental.

**Use this framing on screen:** "Even where indigenous African slavery existed at scale, on the Gold Coast it couldn't produce enough captives to fill European ships. Where Europeans found supply (Angola/Kongo), they found it inside a system that had already been transformed by Atlantic demand — which is exactly the system the King of Kongo was protesting." This routes Beat 3 back into Beat 2.

### Mechanism support — Manning's two-tiered price structure (**NEWLY DEPLOYED in v3 script, 2026-05-25**)

> "The process of enslavement resulted in a two-tiered price structure, distinguishing the very low price of a newly taken captive (a price based not on the value of the person but on the cost of capturing him or her), and the higher price of a slave ready to be put to work."

**Location:** Patrick Manning, *Slavery and African Life*, Cambridge UP, 1990, ch. 4.
**Validated against:** Already in Saltwater Slavery notebook as scholarly source. Citation tag used in v3 EVIDENCE SECTION 2 (De Marees beat) — not on-screen exhibit per Auditor's Edge rule.
**Validation date:** 2026-05-25 (newly deployed in v3; previously only in scholarly sources table)

**Implication for script:** Most people sold to Europeans were *not* pre-existing domestic slaves. They were *newly captured* people, priced based on the cost of their capture. European demand created the incentive to capture. The "they were already slaves" framing is wrong both empirically (newly captured, not pre-existing) and structurally (the supply was demand-driven).

### Mechanism support — Lovejoy's transformation thesis

> "The pull of the market had the effect of pushing indigenous forms of slavery further away from a social framework... to a system in which slaves played an increasingly important role in the economy."

**Location:** Paul E. Lovejoy, *Transformations in Slavery: A History of Slavery in Africa*, 3rd ed., Cambridge UP, 2012, Introduction.

Lovejoy's argument: European contact caused a "radical break" — slavery in Africa was a marginal social institution before 1450; the Atlantic trade transformed it into a "mode of production" where slavery became central to the economic system. **The kind of slavery the talking point invokes (mass commodity slavery) was created by the Atlantic trade in most regions, not pre-existing it.**

### Mechanism support — the "right not to be sold" linguistic evidence

Many African languages distinguished between people who could and couldn't be sold:
- Wolof: *jaam sayor* (saleable captive) vs. *jaam juddu* (born into the household, legally not saleable)
- Bamana (Segu): *jonfin* (war captive, saleable) vs. *jonba* (non-saleable)

European demand pressured African rulers to *"progressively and opportunistically seek ways around existing strictures on the enslavement of one's own people."*

**Location:** David Brion Davis, *Inhuman Bondage*, Oxford UP, 2006, p. 221 (citing Klein on Wolof terminology).

## BEAT 4 — *Romanus Pontifex*, 8 January 1455 ✅

**Refutes Asmongold's implicit claim:** That the Atlantic system emerged organically from African supply rather than being engineered top-down by European political authority.

**Source:** Bull *Romanus Pontifex*, issued by Pope Nicholas V on 8 January 1455. Extends and confirms the earlier bull *Dum Diversas* (18 June 1452).

**Authority:** The Pope — the highest moral and legal authority in 15th-century Europe. ### Verbatim Latin (✅ VERIFIED)

### Critical context (all ✅ verified via Davenport editorial apparatus)

1. **Issued at King Alfonso V of Portugal's request.** The *motu proprio* clause ("of his own accord") is a tax-exemption boilerplate, not a statement that the Pope acted unilaterally. Davenport's editorial commentary: bulls of this type were "almost always the result of a formal petition (*supplik*) from the recipient."

2. **Confirms and extends *Dum Diversas* (18 June 1452).** *Dum Diversas* granted Alfonso general powers to "search out and conquer all pagans, enslave them and appropriate their lands and goods." *Romanus Pontifex* applies those powers specifically to Africa and grants exclusive rights south of Cape Bojador.

3. **Grants Portugal an exclusive trading and territorial monopoly** "from Capes Bojador and Não through all Guinea, and beyond toward that southern shore." Any other Catholic power trading there faces excommunication.

4. **Geographic scope extends "all the way to the Indians who are said to worship the name of Christ"** — i.e., from West Africa to a fabled Christian kingdom in Asia (the Prester John myth). The bull is the legal architecture for the entire Portuguese maritime empire.

### Resolution 2026-05-17 — "perpetual" vs "heritable" (per `03-FACT-CHECK-VERIFICATION.md` ISSUE 4)

Romanus Pontifex 1455 explicitly says *"perpetuam servitutem"* — perpetual servitude (open-ended, without temporal limit). The text does **NOT** explicitly grant heritable status. Heritability of slave status evolved as a separate legal practice — Portuguese colonial law in subsequent decades, English colonial law via *partus sequitur ventrem* (1662). 16th-century chattel slavery WAS heritable in practice, but the bull authorizes perpetuity, not heritability.

**Script v2.3 uses "open-ended and permanent"** — drops "heritable" to avoid attributing later legal evolution to the original 1455 text. The argument doesn't need it; perpetuity carries the load.
