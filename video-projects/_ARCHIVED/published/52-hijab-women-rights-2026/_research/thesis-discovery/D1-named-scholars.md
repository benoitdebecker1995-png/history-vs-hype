## 10 Distinct Named-Scholar Interventions Surfaced

### 1. al-Tha'labi (d. 1035) — extends Q. 33:33
- Verse originally addresses Prophet's wives only ("You are not like any other women")
- Extended to **all believing women**
- Mechanism: **extension** of specific to universal
- Source 2, p. 45; Source 8, p. 54
- Contested: text explicitly limits scope, but no named scholar opposes here

### 2. Ibn al-Jawzi (d. 1200) — overrides Q. 24:60 ⭐
- Verse grants exception for older post-menopausal women to remove outer garments
- Override: *"young or old, a (free) woman's hair must always be covered"*
- Mechanism: **negating a scriptural exception**
- Source 4, p. 163
- **Contested by al-Mawardi (d. 1058)** who cited broader interpretations

### 3. al-Biqa'i (d. 1480) — Q. 33:33
- Domestic instruction to Prophet's wives
- Extended to total gendered division of public/private space
- Mechanism: **analogical reasoning** — contrasted with Q. 9:41 ("Go forth, lightly and heavily armed" to men)
- Source 2, p. 45

### 4. al-Tabari (d. 923) — Q. 33:59 (jilbab verse)
- Verse commands "draw their cloaks close" for recognition
- Specified to mean **"veiled their faces and allowed only one eye to be visible"**
- Mechanism: **specific specification**
- Source 7, p. 186

### 5. al-Tabari (d. 923) — Q. 4:34 ("shun them in the beds") ⭐⭐
- Common reading: sexual avoidance
- Reinterpreted via root *hijār* (camel-tying rope) to mean **"tying them to their beds"** / **"securing them with shackles"**
- Mechanism: **linguistic narrowing**
- Source 3, p. 1, 319, 502
- **Contested by al-Tusi (d. 1066)** as "deviant interpretation"; **Ibn al-'Arabi (d. 1148)** "amazed by the boldness of such a mistake"

### 6. Ibn Kathir (d. 1371) — Q. 2:228 ("degree" over women)
- Original: legal context of divorce/maintenance
- Extended to **"in this world and the next"** — cosmic male superiority
- Mechanism: **universalization**
- Source 3, p. 203
- **Contested by al-Tabari (d. 923)** who read "degree" narrowly as moral responsibility

### 7. al-Mawardi (d. 1058) — Q. 24:60
- Narrowed the post-menopausal exception, allowed outer cloak removal but kept headscarf
- Mechanism: **narrowing**
- Source 4, p. 162

### 8. al-Zamakhshari (d. 1144) — Q. 4:34 (qawwamun)
- Extended male authority by listing women's "skill deficits": **"writing" and "archery"**
- Mechanism: **extension via extra-Quranic social justifications**
- Source 3, p. 186
- **Contested by Ibn al-Jawzi (d. 1200)** citing **al-Kalbi (d. 763)** — only applies to men with actual qualities

### 9. al-Baydawi (d. 1286) — Q. 4:34
- Asserted men's **"perfection of intelligence"** (*kamāl al-ʿaql*) to justify reclusion
- Mechanism: **ontological claim of mental superiority**
- Source 4, p. 112
- Contested himself by reading *qānitāt* (obedient) as obedient to God alone, not husbands

### 10. Ibn Sa'd (d. 845) — Q. 33:33 ⭐⭐⭐
- Portrayed Prophet's wives (especially Aisha) as having **deep regret** for public roles
- Mechanism: **biographical extension** — using exemplary figures to universalize command
- Source 4, p. 184-185, 201
- **Contested by Ibn 'Abd al-Barr (d. 1070)** — focused on Aisha's extensive public role as religious/political advisor with no domestic regret
- **THIS IS THE ERASURE MECHANISM**
