## Claim 3: France 2010 ban

**Script claim verbatim:** In 2010, the French parliament banned the full-face veil in any public space, with fines for the woman wearing it.
**Status:** VERIFIED
**Findings:**
- **Loi number:** Loi n° 2010-1192.
- **Exact date the law was passed and entered into force:** The law was promulgated on October 11, 2010, and entered into force on April 11, 2011.
- **Exact fines:**
    - For the wearer: A fine of up to €150. A citizenship course may also be imposed.
    - For someone forcing a woman to wear it: One year of imprisonment and a €30,000 fine. These penalties are doubled (two years and €60,000) if the victim is a minor.
- **Does it ban the full-face veil specifically, or all face coverings?:** It bans "the concealment of the face" in public spaces, making it applicable to all face coverings, not exclusively the full-face veil, but it is commonly referred to as the "full-face veil ban."
- **Provide primary-source citation:** Legifrance, justiceinitiative.org, gip-recherche-justice.fr, Amnesty International.

**Recommended script change:** To be ruthlessly precise, consider clarifying that the ban applies to "all face coverings" rather than exclusively "the full-face veil," even though the latter is the common understanding. A possible rephrase: "In 2010, the French parliament banned all face coverings in public spaces, with specific fines for the wearer and more severe penalties for those coercing others."
