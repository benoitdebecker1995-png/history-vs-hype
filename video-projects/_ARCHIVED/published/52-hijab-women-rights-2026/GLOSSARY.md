## Veil / Hijab terminology

**Hijab (Quranic)**:
A physical curtain or spatial barrier. In the Quran, the word *hijab* refers to the screen drawn between the Prophet's household and male visitors (Q. 33:53). It is never addressed to women as a dress requirement.
_Avoid_: Using "hijab" interchangeably with "headscarf" in research or scripts without flagging the semantic drift.

**Khimar**:
A pre-Islamic head wrap worn by men and women for protection. The Quranic innovation (Q. 24:31) was to instruct women to draw their khimars over their chests — not to introduce a new garment.
_Avoid_: Treating khimar as a new Islamic invention.

**Jilbab**:
An outer cloak. Q. 33:59 instructs free Muslim women to wear it so they are "known and not abused" — explicitly a legal identification marker, not a spiritual modesty requirement.
_Avoid_: Conflating jilbab with modern abaya or niqab.

**Modern headscarf**:
The pinned, form-fitting style common today. A 1970s development, emerging as a political identity marker in Egypt and Turkey during the Islamist revival. Not a continuation of classical Islamic dress.
_Avoid_: Treating the modern headscarf as the historical default.

## Mechanism terms

**Class marker**:
The pre-Islamic function of the veil (c. 1300 BCE onward). Assyrian law mandated veiling for noble/free women and explicitly forbade it for enslaved women and sex workers. The veil signalled legal status and class privilege — not modesty.
_Avoid_: "Modesty garment," "religious symbol" when describing the pre-Islamic function.

**Legal extension**:
The jurisprudential move by named male scholars (8th–11th c. CE) that converted rules explicitly addressed to the Prophet's wives into universal obligations for all Muslim women. The three moves: (1) al-Tha'labi and al-Biqa'i apply Q. 33:33 ("you are not like other women") to all believers; (2) Ibn al-Jawzi overrides Q. 24:60's explicit exception for older women; (3) the slave/free distinction of Q. 33:59 is dropped, making the obligation class-blind and universal.
_Avoid_: "Interpretation," "scholarly consensus" — these terms obscure the specific procedural nature of the moves.

**Universal obligation**:
The result of legal extension: hijab as *fard* (religiously mandatory) for all Muslim women regardless of status. This is the endpoint of the mechanism — the thing the video explains how we got to. The Quran never states it.
_Avoid_: Treating universal obligation as the starting point or as Quranic in origin.

**Named scholars**:
The specific historical actors who performed the legal extension. Canonical names for this video: al-Tha'labi, al-Biqa'i, Ibn al-Jawzi. Named because the mechanism requires individual agency — it was not anonymous consensus.
_Avoid_: "Islamic scholars," "medieval exegetes" (too vague — loses the auditor's edge).

**Five-civilization pattern**:
The hook frame for video #52. Across Assyrian (c. 1200 BCE), Athenian (5th-4th c. BCE), Jewish/Talmudic (3rd-6th c. CE), Byzantine (5th-6th c. CE), and Sasanian Persian (224-640 CE) law — the rule was identical: free women must wear the veil; enslaved women are forbidden. Islam entered a world where this system was already universal. The pattern, not a single case, is the opening argument.
_Avoid_: "The oldest depiction of the veil" — earlier artistic representations exist. Say "the oldest law governing who could wear the veil" when citing Assyria specifically.
(Note: as a reusable hook-frame *template*, this pattern is also referenced channel-wide.)

**Single mechanism**:
The structural constraint for this video: one jurisprudential conversion move (class marker → universal obligation), traced from origin to codification. Not a panoramic sweep.
_Avoid_: "Full history of the veil," "comprehensive overview."

## Relationships (#52-specific)

- A **class marker** was the pre-Islamic function of the **jilbab** and veil
- **Legal extension** converted the **class marker** function into a **universal obligation**
- **Named scholars** performed the **legal extension** through three specific procedural moves
- **Hijab (Quranic)** ≠ **modern headscarf** — the word's referent drifted across 14 centuries
- **Khimar** and **jilbab** are the actual Quranic garment terms; **hijab** is not

## Flagged ambiguities (#52-specific)

- "Veil" is used colloquially to mean everything from a face covering to a headscarf to a room divider. In scripts: define the specific garment or function on first use. Never use "veil" as a catch-all without a qualifier.
- "Hijab" in modern Arabic = headscarf; in classical Arabic/Quran = curtain/barrier. Scripts must flag this on first use — it is the central linguistic turn of the video.
- "Modesty" is disputed: the Quran links garments to legal status and safety (Q. 33:59), not spiritual modesty. Avoid "modesty" as the primary frame unless quoting a source that uses it.
