## THE BATTLE NARRATIVE — FIVE KEY MOMENTS COMPARED

### Claim: First days of fighting — all three describe Persian slaughter, but details differ
**Status:** ✅ VERIFIED
**Sources:**
1. Herodotus (Tier 1) — 7.211, Landmark ed. p. 558: "The Lacedaemonians fought remarkably well... they would turn their backs and feign flight all together... then suddenly turn around to face them, at which point they would slay countless numbers of them."
2. Diodorus (Tier 1) — 11.7.2, p. 614: "the men stood shoulder to shoulder... the Greeks were superior in valour and in the great size of their shields"
3. Ctesias (Tier 1) — Fragment 27, p. 183: "the great Persian horde was cut down, while only two or three Spartans were killed. Then Xerxes ordered them to attack with 20,000 men: and these were defeated, too. Then they were flogged to fire them up for war"
**Contradiction:** Herodotus emphasizes tactical sophistication (feigned retreat). Diodorus describes a static phalanx. Ctesias claims absurd casualty ratios and introduces the "flogging" trope.
**Script-Ready:** YES

### Claim: The betrayal — three different versions of who revealed the mountain path
**Status:** ✅ VERIFIED
**Sources:**
1. Herodotus (Tier 1) — 7.213, Landmark ed. p. 560: "Ephialtes of Malis, son of Eurydemos, came to speak with him, expecting to win some great reward for telling the King of the path that led through the mountain to Thermopylae."
2. Diodorus (Tier 1) — 11.8.4, p. 617: "there came to the king a certain Trachinian, a native of the region, who was familiar with the mountainous area"
3. Ctesias (Tier 1) — Fragment 27: **Ephialtes does not appear by name.** Ctesias says Xerxes sent 40,000 men over the path, guided by "the two leading men of Trachis" (COMPETITOR-GAP-THERMOPYLAE.md, line 102, sourced from Llewellyn-Jones & Robson). Llewellyn-Jones & Robson p. 28: Ctesias' account "caused the complete omission of the traitor Ephialtes" — but the flanking itself IS present, just with different guides.
**Contradiction:** Herodotus names Ephialtes specifically. Diodorus says "a Trachinian." Ctesias has "two leading men of Trachis" — anonymous, plural, and possibly willing guides rather than a traitor. Three different versions of the same event.
**Script-Ready:** YES — KEY CONTRADICTION

### Claim: The final stand — Herodotus describes a daytime hillock defense; Diodorus describes a suicidal night raid
**Status:** ✅ VERIFIED
**Sources:**
1. Herodotus (Tier 1) — 7.225, Landmark ed. p. 570: "On this spot they tried to defend themselves with their daggers if they still had them, or if not, with their hands and their teeth. The barbarians pelted them with missiles, some running up to face the Hellenes directly and demolishing the defensive wall, and others coming to surround them on all sides."
2. Diodorus (Tier 1) — 11.10.1-3, p. 620: "the Greeks broke into the pavilion and slew almost to a man all whom they caught there. So long as it was night they wandered throughout the entire camp seeking Xerxes."
3. Ctesias (Tier 1) — Fragment 27: **Does not describe a final stand at all.** Summary ends before the last engagement.
**Contradiction:** These are fundamentally different accounts of the same event. Herodotus: desperate daytime defense. Diodorus: bold night assassination attempt. Ctesias: no detail.
**Modern assessment:** Matthews p. 234: the night raid is "almost certainly fictional" — likely inherited from Ephorus's rhetorical tradition.
**Script-Ready:** YES — KEY CONTRADICTION

**Status:** ✅ VERIFIED
