## Description

The Piri Reis map doesn't hide a lost civilization — it lists its own sources.

We translate the Ottoman Turkish that Piri Reis wrote on the map itself, including inscription no. 6: the paragraph where he names roughly twenty source maps he worked from — one of them a lost chart drawn by Christopher Columbus.

Since Charles Hapgood's 1966 book, and again through Graham Hancock on Joe Rogan, the Piri Reis map has been sold as proof of an advanced lost civilization that mapped an ice-free Antarctica. But the map answers the charge in its own captions. The "Antarctica" everyone points to is the coast of Brazil, drawn from the toponyms Piri Reis copied, joined to the speculative *terra australis* that every 16th-century mapmaker added. The Caribbean island isn't Bimini — it's Cuba, exactly where Columbus thought he was. And the reason none of this is famous is the quiet part: nobody read the Ottoman Turkish. The map wasn't hidden. It was just left untranslated.

This is the primary-source version — the inscriptions on screen, the scholars who actually read the script, and what the Piri Reis map really says when you let it speak.
