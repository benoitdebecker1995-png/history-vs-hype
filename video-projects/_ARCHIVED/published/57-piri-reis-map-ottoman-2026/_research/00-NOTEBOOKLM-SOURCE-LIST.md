## PRIORITY 1 — Must Have Before Scripting

### [A1] McIntosh, Gregory C. *The Piri Reis Map of 1513*
- **Publisher:** University of Georgia Press, 2000
- **ISBN:** 978-0820321486
- **Why essential:** THE authoritative academic analysis. Full translation of Ottoman text on map. Definitive debunking of Hapgood/Antarctica claim. Chapter-by-chapter source analysis.
- **STATUS: FREE** — Internet Archive direct PDF download (11.7MB): https://archive.org/details/gregory-c.-mc-intosh-the-piri-reis-map-of-1513
- **Upload as:** `[A1] McIntosh-Piri-Reis-Map-2000.pdf`

### [A2] Soucek, Svat. *Piri Reis and Turkish Mapmaking After Columbus: The Khalili Portolan Atlas*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press (for Nour Foundation / Khalili Collections), 1996
- **Why essential:** Ottoman cartographic tradition context; Piri Reis biography; methodology analysis. Written by a Turkish language scholar.
- **Purchase:** ~$50-80 used; check university library
- **Upload as:** `[A2] Soucek-Piri-Reis-Turkish-Mapmaking-1996.pdf`

### [P1] Piri Reis. *Kitab-ı Bahriye* (Book of Navigation), 1521
- **Publisher:** Historical Research Foundation Istanbul Research Centre, 1988 facsimile edition (with English translation)
- **Why essential:** Piri Reis's own explanation of his methods and sources — primary source for the video's core argument
- **Access:** Major research libraries; Turkish National Library digitized version
- **Upload as:** `[P1] Piri-Reis-Kitab-Bahriye-1521-translation.pdf`

## PRIORITY 2 — High Value for Key Claims

### [A3] Kahle, Paul. "A Lost Map of Columbus"
- **Publisher:** Geographical Review, Vol. 23, No. 4, 1933, pp. 621-638
- **Why essential:** First scholarly identification of Columbus's map as Piri Reis source. Foundational article.
- **STATUS: JSTOR PAYWALLED** — https://www.jstor.org/stable/209478. Article is from 1933 (public domain) but JSTOR blocks direct access. Options: (1) JSTOR free account allows 100 articles/month, (2) request via ResearchGate, (3) HathiTrust Vol.23 1933 catalog record exists

## NOTE ON RECENCY

**Why the list skews 1990s-2000s:** McIntosh (2000) and Soucek (1996) remain the authoritative academic treatments of the Ottoman annotations specifically. No monograph has superseded them on the question we care about (what the text says + Columbus identification). More recent scholarship has focused on cartographic tradition and iconographic context rather than re-translating the Ottoman text. This is not a gap in the source list — it reflects where the field actually is.
