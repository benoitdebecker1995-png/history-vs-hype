## THE CORE STORY

In 1494, Spain and Portugal signed a treaty splitting the entire non-Christian world between them. A line drawn 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands gave everything west to Spain, everything east to Portugal. No one else was consulted — not other European powers, and certainly not the billions of people living in the lands being divided.

That line is still visible today. It's why Brazil speaks Portuguese and every other South American country speaks Spanish. It shaped modern borders, modern inequality, and modern indigenous rights law. In 2023, Pope Francis formally repudiated the Doctrine of Discovery that justified it — 529 years later.

## KEY CLAIMS TO VERIFY

### 1. The Treaty Itself

- **Demarcation line:** 370 leagues west of Cape Verde Islands (pole to pole)
  - ✅ VERIFIED — Treaty text p.895: "three hundred and seventy leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands"
  - **CORRECTED LONGITUDE:** Consensus estimate is **48°42' W (48.7°)**, NOT 46°37' as previously stated
  - Laudares paper uses 48.7° as "consensus estimate" (p.14); alternative: Oviedo calculated 45°17' W
  - HEDGE: "While modern scholars generally place the line at 48.7° West, historical estimates range from 45° to 49°"

- **Key provision:** "This boundary or line shall be drawn straight... at a distance of three hundred and seventy leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands"
  - SOURCE: Treaty text (Avalon Project), p.895
  - STATUS: ✅ VERIFIED

- **Survey clause:** Joint voyage of pilots and "astrologers" mandated within 10 months
  - **NEVER HAPPENED** — "there was no means of determining precisely where the line ran; nor was it even clear from which of the various Cape Verde Islands the 370 leagues was supposed to be measured" (Disney p.291)
  - STATUS: ✅ VERIFIED (PARTIALLY TRUE — mandated but never executed; no definitive evidence of whether the maritime expedition was physically attempted)

- **Critical measurement failures:**
  - Did NOT specify which Cape Verde island to measure from (changed border by dozens of miles) — Benton p.76
  - Did NOT specify length of the league — Disney p.291
  - No technology existed to measure longitude accurately in 1494
  - STATUS: ✅ VERIFIED

## MECHANISM DETAILS (verified via NotebookLM — 2026-03-06)

### Mechanism 1: The Dual Monopoly

**How it worked:** *Romanus Pontifex* (1455) established *mare clausum* — the Atlantic beyond Cape Bojador was an exclusive Portuguese navigation zone. Tordesillas (1494) added Brazil. Portugal controlled BOTH ends of the slave trade pipeline.

**The Asiento System:** Spain lacked direct access to Africa under Tordesillas. The *asiento* was a monopoly contract: contractor (usually Portuguese) paid the Spanish Crown a lump sum for the right to supply a specific number of slaves (e.g., 4,250/year) and recouped costs by selling licenses to individual traders. Portugal traded African labor for Spanish American silver.

**Key Feitorias (Trading Posts):**
- **Arguim (Mauritania):** First fortified feitoria (1449) — cloth and brass for gold and slaves
- **Sao Jorge da Mina (Gold Coast):** Premier 15th-century stronghold
- **Whydah (Ajuda):** Critical later node (1721) — "third-grade" Bahian tobacco traded specifically for slaves

**On-screen quote:** "*Romanus Pontifex* gave Portugal a legal justification for applying... the principle of *mare clausum*" — Disney
**Sources:** Disney pp.27, 30, 35, 41-42, 94-97; Laudares pp.763, 774, 786; Boxer pp.372, 665

### Mechanism 2: The Donatario Franchise Model

**Successes:** Pernambuco (Duarte Coelho) and Sao Vicente (Martim Afonso de Sousa) — secured Amerindian allies + introduced sugar as high-value export

**Failures:** Most donatarios had no Brazilian experience; four northernmost captaincies never settled; others collapsed due to "wars with native peoples" and internal squabbles

**Centralization (1549):** Failure + French interlopers forced Crown to appoint **Tome de Sousa** as Governor-General. Founded Salvador (1549) as royal capital.

### Mechanism 3: The Bandeirante Breach

**What drove them:** Slave labor + mineral wealth. Bandeirantes from Sao Paulo pushed deep into interior.
