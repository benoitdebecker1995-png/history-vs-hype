### 5. 1859 Treaty — Land Only

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Status | ✅ VERIFIED |
| Finding | Treaty defines ONLY land/river boundaries. No maritime boundary. No mention of Sapodilla Cayes or offshore islands. |
| "Islands" in treaty | Article 6 refers to river islands within the Sarstoon boundary — NOT offshore cays |
| Quote | During 1859 negotiations, it was deemed "unnecessary to describe, in a treaty with Guatemala, the sea-frontier" |

### 8. UNCLOS Article 121 — Islands vs. Rocks

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Island (Art. 121 para. 1) | "Naturally formed area of land, surrounded by water, above water at high tide" → generates full EEZ (200nm) |
| Rock (Art. 121 para. 3) | "Rocks which cannot sustain human habitation or economic life" → territorial sea only (12nm) |
| Economic difference | Rock: ~450 sq nautical miles. Island: up to 125,000 sq nautical miles. |
| Sapodilla status | Threshold case. Largest ~300m across. Belize argues: permanent structures (Hunting Caye base) + historical residency (Cabral family, Lime Caye) = not "rocks" |
| Precedent | Nicaragua v. Colombia (2012): QS 32 = rock, no EEZ. South China Sea Arbitration (2016): Spratly features all classified as rocks. |

## CAUSAL CHAIN (VERIFIED)

1. Spain claims Gulf of Honduras but never administers Sapodilla Cayes
2. Britain gradually expands south: 1786 Sibun → 1798 Battle → 1825 Sarstoon → 1859 Treaty formalizes
3. 1859 Treaty draws LAND boundary only — says nothing about offshore islands
4. Britain administers Cayes for 200 years — magistrates, lighthouses, military, licenses
5. 1913: Honduras acknowledges British sovereignty ("Islands belonging to the Colony")
6. September 21, 1981: Belize independent — challenging a small new country ≠ challenging the British Empire
7. December 10, 1982: UNCLOS opens for signature — whoever owns islands controls EEZ
8. January 20, 1982: Honduras Constitution Article 10 claims "Cayos Zapotillos" for the first time
9. Two-way → three-way dispute. Not because of history — because of timing and economics.
10. 2022: Belize takes Honduras to ICJ (Case 185)
11. 2023: Guatemala intervenes — these islands are part of their broader claim against Belize (Case 177)
12. March 19, 2026: ICJ unanimously permits Guatemala's intervention

## ARTICLE NUMBER CORRECTION

The script skeleton references "Article 11" for the constitutional claim. **The correct article is Article 10** (lists territory including "Cayos Zapotillos"). Article 11 defines maritime zones (12/24/200nm). Both articles matter but the territorial claim is in 10.
