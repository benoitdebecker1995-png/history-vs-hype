## Description

```
Honduras spent 160 years calling these islands British territory. In 1913, its own foreign minister asked Britain to send a warship to police them. Then in 1982, Honduras wrote them into its constitution as national territory. Now three countries are fighting over them at the International Court of Justice.

This video traces the Sapodilla Cayes dispute from Spanish colonial claims through the 1786 Convention of London, the 1859 Wyke-Aycinena Treaty, the 1913 Honduran recognition letter, Honduras's 1982 constitutional claim, and the ICJ's unprecedented decision to let Guatemala intervene as a non-party — only the fourth time in eighty years the court has done this.

The same judges will rule on whether Guatemala owns half of Belize in 2027. The legal reasoning they develop here carries straight into that case.
```
