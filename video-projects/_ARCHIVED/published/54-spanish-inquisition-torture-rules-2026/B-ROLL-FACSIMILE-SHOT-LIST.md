## Production Notes for Nanobana

1. **Aesthetic consistency** — all primary-document shots should look like reproductions of pages from a 1630 Spanish ecclesiastical imprint. Aged parchment, blackletter or early Roman type, period-accurate ink darkness.

2. **Folio numbering** — small "f. 6v" / "f. 33r" / "f. 29r" labels in the corner of each facsimile (the way an archive citation would render it) reinforce the Auditor's Edge.

3. **Highlight technique** — use modern yellow/red transparent overlays for the per-clause highlights so the contrast against period parchment is high. Don't try to fake archival marginalia.

4. **Translation overlay placement** — always BELOW the Spanish original, in clean modern sans-serif, so viewer reads original first / translation second. This is a teaching choice — the audience is being shown the document, then shown what it says.

5. **Frame timing** — give each facsimile minimum 2.5 seconds on screen for the viewer to read the Spanish + register the highlight + read the English overlay. Holding longer is fine; cutting shorter destroys the Auditor's Edge.

6. **Citation tags** — every shot needs a small citation tag in the lower-right (e.g., "Argüello 1630, f. 6v" or "Hassner, *Anatomy of Torture*, p. 25"). This is non-negotiable per channel rule.

**Auditor's-Edge note:** This is **NOT** a primary-text rule from the Compilación. The 1484 / 1561 Instructions don't contain an explicit "silence = innocence" article. The rule is the *standard legal interpretation of outcome*, documented by Hassner and Kamen as the recurring inquisitorial practice. So the on-screen treatment must be a scholar-quote card, not a faked primary-document facsimile.

**Why the script said "Under Inquisition law" and NOT "Under §XV":** Per post-mortem, this was a deliberate live edit during recording. "Under §XV" would have been over-attribution because §XV doesn't say it. "Under Inquisition law" is correct — silence-as-acquittal is documented Inquisition practice, just not codified in §XV.

**Primary scholar quotes (notebook-verified):**

| Source | Page | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| Hassner, *Anatomy of Torture* | **p. 145** | *"[The Inquisition] was skeptical of any and all claims made under torture, except silence, which it tended to interpret as a divinely inspired proof of innocence."* |

| Kamen, *The Spanish Inquisition: A Historical Revision* | **p. 242** | *"The Inquisition viewed the ability to withstand torture without declaration of guilt as strong proof of innocence. Not once did the court punish with death those who withstood torture in silence."* |
