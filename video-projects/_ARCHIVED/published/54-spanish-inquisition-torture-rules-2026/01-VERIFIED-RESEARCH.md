| # | Claim | Status | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2.5 | **8-liter water cap** — ❌ REFUTED | ❌ | No primary source. Amount was inquisitor's discretion. Díaz de Cáceres (1601) received 7 jars (~14L). Kamen: "severity varied with number of jars" (p. 240). DO NOT USE. |
| 2.6 | Torture required prior three formal warnings (*moniciones*) on separate days urging the accused to "tell the truth" | ✅ | Hassner; Kamen p. 235; Homza p. 225 (1561 Instructions) |
| 2.7 | A confession under torture was legally INVALID unless voluntarily ratified the next day outside the torture chamber | ✅ | Kamen p. 239; Rawlings; Peters p. 93; Hassner p. 27 |

| 2.11 | Only capital heresy cases qualified for torture; minor offenses (bulk of cases) did not | ✅ | Kamen p. 239; Hassner; Rawlings |
| 2.12 | Torture was "exploratory" in early period (1484-1500), shifted to "corroborative" (end-of-trial confirmation only) by mid-16th century — corroborative torture was more regulated and more reliable | ✅ | Hassner ch. 3-4 (central argument of *Anatomy of Torture*) |

**Article XV verbatim (Homza pp. 67-68):**
> "Next, if the crime appears half proven, the inquisitors, in consultation with the ordinario, shall consider putting the accused to the question of torture. If the accused confesses the crime under torture, and afterward ratifies or confirms his confession on the next or third day, he shall be punished as convicted. If, after torture, he revokes the confession and retracts it (and if the crime is still not completely proven), the inquisitors must order him to publicly abjure the error of which he is defamed and suspected, on account of infamy and the presumption that results against him. The inquisitors shall give him some arbitrary penance and treat him mildly."

**On being present at torture (Homza p. 74):**
> "Moreover, they decided that when torture is given, the inquisitors and ordinario must be present, or [at least] one of them… They must observe that the attending person is a wise, faithful man, of good reputation and conscience."
