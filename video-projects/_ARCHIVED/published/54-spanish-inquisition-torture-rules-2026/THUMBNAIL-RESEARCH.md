**Three patterns are universal across the 11-thumbnail comparable set:**

1. **100% feature a recognizable inquisitor figure.** Every single one — animated character, silhouette, period painting, manuscript figure, talking-head academic, costumed actor, or cartoon villain. ZERO outliers led with a document/text scan as the primary visual. The closest doc-led one (BBC Timewatch from 1994) is the ONE no-figure thumbnail and it earned its views from age + BBC brand, not packaging.
2. **Overlay is 2-3 words OR full title repetition** when text exists. None use a single bare word (no "LOOPHOLE" alone). Phrases that work: "DEBUNKING THE MYTHS", "SPAIN'S BLACK LEGEND", "TORTURE vs MAGIC", "TACTICS OF TORQUEMADA". The thesis WORD becomes a thesis PHRASE.

**The closest brand-fit comparable is Gresham College (176K).** Academic talking head + magenta background + "SPAIN'S BLACK LEGEND" 3-word thesis overlay. This is the most directly transferable template for HvH — same content discipline, same audience expectation, hits 176K on the topic.

**Common failure mode:** dossier-style document collage with **3+ competing focal points** and **multiple text chunks**. The PolyMatter dossier playbook says this format works — but PolyMatter's actual outliers use ONE concept + ONE short overlay phrase. Our Format C thumbnails over-stuff the frame and lose mobile legibility.

**Implication:** the next Format C thumbnail must be **simpler** than what we've been shipping, not "another well-researched dossier."

## 2. The leverage we have here that we didn't have on Vichy/Tripoli/Bermeja

**Spanish Inquisition is a cultural touchstone.** Monty Python sketch, common-knowledge punchline, recognized-by-everyone topic. Vichy/Tripoli/Bermeja were obscure — viewers had no anchor.

This unlocks playbook options that don't work on obscure topics:

- **Shaun TITLE REPETITION** — works only when the topic IS the click. Spanish Inquisition qualifies. Vichy didn't.
- **Knowing Better COMPRESSION on charged single word** — works because viewers recognize the topic and the word triggers their existing schema (see "Neoslavery" 7.7x, "Indian Removal" 3.2x).
- **Lindybeige MECHANISM REFRAME (twist/reveal)** — works because the audience has a preconception (cartoon brutality) that the overlay can subvert in 3 words.

The competitor data confirms this. Same topic, three thumbnails, three outcomes:
- **History Matters (1.4M)** — cartoon Spain map + 2 cartoon inquisitors + giant 5-line text "HOW BAD WAS THE SPANISH INQUISITION?". Question framing on a touchstone topic.
- **Julius Caesar (153K)** — staged torture wheel + costumed inquisitor + torch. NO TEXT. Pure visceral pull.
- **BBC Timewatch (231K, 2012)** — open book + altar + crucifix. No text. Document aesthetic. Old upload, visually murky.

Read carefully: the 1.4M winner is the **simplest** composition and uses **the most generic question**. The visual sells it (recognizable inquisitor figures + map silhouette). Our channel's 0/3 Format C record is partly an over-design problem.

## 4. Niche-wide floors (assume these; they don't differentiate)

From the 8-channel outlier corpus (n=30 outliers, n=57 losers):

- Text overlay 90-96% in BOTH outliers and losers — **floor, not predictor**. Don't score "has text overlay" as a positive. Score what the overlay DOES.
- 6/30 outliers (20%) have no text or sub-7-char overlays — text is not mandatory if the visual is iconic.
- Faces in aggregate slightly hurt (-5pp), but the signal is channel-anchored — for myth-busting on a recognizable topic (Knowing Better pattern), face HELPS (+67pp at Knowing Better).
- Maps don't differentiate in aggregate (+2pp). Drop the reflexive map.
- Arrows correlate with losers (-6pp). Drop the arrows our Vichy and Bermeja thumbnails used.
- Mobile legibility is a hard floor; the Vichy thumbnail has 4 text zones — fails.

## 5. Title flexibility note

Current locked title: *"The Spanish Inquisition Regulated Its Torture Methods. The Loophole Was in the Same Paragraph."*

The mechanism word "**LOOPHOLE**" is the click magnet — it's the twist that contradicts the cartoon-brutality myth. Whatever the final title, **the thumbnail should preserve "LOOPHOLE" or "THE RULES" as the central conceptual anchor**, because that's the differentiator vs the existing competitor field (which all frame the topic as "how bad was it" / torture imagery).

If we shift the title, the thumbnail needs to shift too. Three viable title-direction families and their thumbnail implications:

| Title family | Example | Thumbnail anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Loophole reveal | "The Inquisition Wrote Rules Against Torture. Same Page Had the Loophole." | Document with one underlined phrase + "LOOPHOLE" overlay |
| Authority subversion | "Torquemada Banned Excessive Torture. Then He Defined Excessive." | Torquemada portrait + "DEFINED 'EXCESSIVE'" overlay |
| Rules-existed reframe | "The Spanish Inquisition Had a Torture Manual. Most People Never Read It." | Manual page + "THE MANUAL" overlay |

## 6. Three thumbnail directions worth prototyping (REVISED v2 after comparable audit)

**The comparable audit changes the ranking.** Direction A is reinforced (matches Gresham 176K + Pax Tube 497K + Knowing Better playbook all converging). Direction B drops sharply — zero outliers in the 11-thumbnail comparable set used a document scan as primary visual. Direction C splits into two sub-variants.

### Direction A — Inquisitor face + 2-3 word thesis phrase (HIGHEST CONFIDENCE — multi-source convergence)

- **Visual:** Inquisitor figure as primary anchor. Three viable variants:
  - **A1 — period portrait** (Torquemada engraving, dark dramatic crop) — most "intellectual competence" signal, closest to Knowing Better/Gresham
  - **A2 — silhouette / hooded figure** (period art, dramatic backlight) — Forgotten/Into-the-Shadows aesthetic, lowers recognition burden
  - **A3 — talking head HvH (you)** in costume / dramatic frame on inquisitor-themed B-roll background — closest to Gresham, plays the "academic referee" brand directly
- **Overlay:** 2-3 word THESIS PHRASE, not single word. Candidates:
  - `THE LOOPHOLE` (preserves the title hook word in phrase form)
  - `HIS OWN LOOPHOLE` (3 words, ownership tension)
  - `THE RULES` (purest reframe — myth-busting at the genre level)
  - `WROTE THE RULES` (3 words, action verb)
  - Avoid single-word `LOOPHOLE` — comparable corpus shows zero single-word outliers; phrase reads better at thumbnail size
- **Why:** Three independent signals converge:
  1. Knowing Better playbook (channel-level): face + COMPRESSION = 100% of outliers
  2. Comparable audit (topic-level): 100% of comparable outliers feature an inquisitor figure
  3. Channel evidence (own-channel): JD Vance face+COMPRESSION = highest CTR at 9.46%
- **Risk:** Torquemada portrait less recognizable internationally than Vance face. Mitigated by silhouette/hooded variant (A2) or HvH-on-camera (A3) which work without name-recognition.
- **Variant ranking:** A3 > A1 > A2 — A3 has the strongest direct comparable (Gresham 176K is structurally identical: academic head + magenta bg + 3-word thesis). A1 is second because period portrait carries authority. A2 is the safest fallback.

### Direction B — Manuscript ART (not document scan) + thesis phrase (REVISED — the Esoterica template)

**Important revision:** The original Direction B (page of *Compilación* Folio 6v) is NOT supported by the comparable audit. Zero outliers used a scan of legal text as primary visual. The closest doc-aesthetic outlier (Esoterica 223K) used a medieval ILLUMINATED MANUSCRIPT — a piece of period ART showing people being tortured, not a page of rules.

- **Visual:** Period illuminated manuscript / engraving showing a SCENE (tribunal, interrogation, judgment) — humans visible, action visible. NOT a page of regulations text.
- **Overlay:** 2-3 word reframe — `TORTURE vs RULES` or `HIS OWN RULES` or `THE EXCEPTION` — bold contrasting color (Esoterica uses red top + white center; Gresham uses white on magenta).
- **Why this works when document-scan doesn't:** The eye reads humans-in-scene as story; it reads parchment text as homework. Esoterica's 223K is THE Format C analog and they understood this. Our Vichy/Bermeja/Tripoli thumbnails all violated this — they led with text-bearing parchment, not human-bearing scene art.
- **Risk:** Finding the right manuscript image. The Compilación itself is text-only; we'd need a separate piece of period art (e.g., illustration from Limborch's *Historia Inquisitionis* 1692, or a 16th-century Spanish auto-da-fé painting like Berruguete). Low risk to source — Wikimedia has several public-domain options.
- **Differentiator:** None of the 3 same-topic competitors use the rules-vs-application contradiction visually. Esoterica did "torture vs magic" — we'd do "torture vs rules" — same operation, fresh angle.

### Direction C — Visceral scene (REVISED — split into C1 staged vs C2 caricature)

The comparable corpus shows two viable visceral routes that both work, plus one route that doesn't:

- **C1 — staged real-action torture scene** (Julius Caesar 153K, Banijay 10K). Costumed actors, period-accurate setting, real lighting. **Not viable for HvH** — requires production budget and casting we don't have. Skip.
- **C2 — caricature/cartoon villain** (Infographics 2.57M, History Matters 1.43M). Stylized exaggerated character art. **Not viable for HvH** — wrong brand register, would corrode the "evidence-based referee" trust signal that drives our subscriber-anchored traffic. Skip.
- **C3 — period painting/engraving of an actual auto-da-fé or torture scene** (Pedro Berruguete 1495, Goya 1816). **Viable.** This is the channel-appropriate version of visceral imagery — real historical art, real moral weight, no cosplay or cartoon. Pair with same 2-3 word overlay as Direction A.

C3 is the actually-shippable Direction C. The other two are non-starters for our brand.

## 7. Anti-patterns — do NOT do these

Direct lessons from the 0/3 Format C record:
- **No multi-zone document collages.** One document, one focal point. Vichy had Pétain + 2 arrows + dense law text — 4 zones competing.
- **No arrows pointing at things.** Arrows correlate with losers in the outlier data and made our thumbnails look like infographics, not magazine covers.
- **No two-paragraph text on the thumbnail.** Tripoli's English card had 5 lines of small print — invisible at mobile.
- **No verdict overlays.** Per memory `feedback-thumbnail-process.md`: titles can declare; overlays should NOT spoil the verdict. "WAS HUMANE" / "WAS BRUTAL" is forbidden. "LOOPHOLE" is fine — it's a noun anchor, not a verdict.
- **No reflexive Spain map.** Not a territorial video. The map is what History Matters does because their brand IS country shapes; we're not them.
- **No AI-generated faces or scenes.** Real historical material only — the channel's brand depends on it.
