## MYTH vs. REALITY — Executive Summary

**The myth:** "In 1626, the Dutch bought Manhattan from the Indians for $24 in beads and trinkets."

**Every element is wrong:**
- "$24" — invented by E.B. O'Callaghan in 1844, arbitrary conversion of 60 guilders
- "beads and trinkets" — invented by painter William Ranney in 1853; no source mentions beads
- "bought" — Lenape understood this as a shared-use agreement, not a permanent sale
- "from the Indians" — identity of sellers is entirely unknown; "Canarsee" claim is a 1959 fabrication
- "Manhattan" — no deed survives; we have one sentence in a cargo manifest letter

## SECTION 1: THE $24 FIGURE

### ✅ The $24 was invented by E.B. O'Callaghan in January 1844
- Published in the Albany journal *Northern Light*
- Exact quote: "the purchase of the Island of Manhattans, two hundred and seventeen years ago, by the Dutch, from the Indians, for the sum of sixty guilders, or twenty-four dollars!"
- Conversion methodology not documented — described as arbitrary
- **Source:** McErleane, Stephen. "Imagination Aided by the Painter's Brush." Gotham Center, 2020.

### ✅ The $24 figure has "no relationship to buying power two hundred years earlier"
- "We can dismiss the twenty-four-dollar figure because it dates to the mid-nineteenth century"
- **Source:** Shorto, Russell. *The Island at the Center of the World.* Doubleday, 2004.

### ✅ Modern equivalent is approximately $1,000
- Howe equates 60 guilders to roughly $1,000 today
- A WIC soldier earned ~100 guilders/year — Manhattan cost more than half a year's salary
- In 1638, 100 acres of Long Island sold Dutch-to-Dutch for 52 guilders
- "in its wilderness state New World land was dirt cheap" — Shorto
- **Source:** Howe, Richard. "Notes on the Manhattan Purchase." Gotham Center, 2012. | Shorto (2004)

### ✅ John Romeyn Brodhead's actual role: legitimizer, not inventor
- Brodhead (1853) used the purchase to argue Dutch took Manhattan by "justice and right"
- Compared it favorably to William Penn's treaty at Shackamaxon
- "one of the most interesting [events] in our colonial annals"
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

### ✅ The story spread rapidly after 1844
- "usually as brief mentions in newspapers and periodicals with headings like 'Cheap!'"
- Cemented by 1909 Hudson-Fulton Celebration: 1M+ spectators, paper-mâché float
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

## SECTION 2: THE BEADS MYTH

### ✅ The Schagen letter mentions NO beads or trinkets
- Says only: "They have purchased the Island Manhattes from the Indians for the value of 60 guilders"
- The letter is a brief notification to the States General, surrounded by a cargo list of pelts and timber
- **Source:** Schagen letter (Nov 5, 1626) — Yale Avalon Project; NYSL translation; New Netherland Institute

### ✅ William Ranney's 1853 painting introduced the beads myth
- "contains the first inclusion in an image or text of the myth's infamous beads"
- Painting debuted at the National Academy of Art, NYC, 1853
- Deliberately copied Benjamin West's 1771-72 *Penn's Treaty with the Indians*
- Invented: beads, the tree (co-opted Penn's elm), the kneeling figure
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

### ✅ Martha Lamb was the first historian to put beads in text (1875)
- "offered beads, buttons, and other trinkets"
- Lifted directly from Ranney's painting
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

### ⚠️ NUANCE: Beads WERE legitimate trade goods in other Dutch-Lenape transactions
- 1638 Long Island deed lists "some knives, beads and awls" alongside kettles and axes
- Beads were not "trinkets" — they were valued commodities
- **Source:** Land Papers (Gehring trans.), GG 27

## SECTION 3: LENAPE LAND CONCEPTS

### ✅ The Lenape held land under "usufruct" — communal shared-use rights
- "Sachems and councils charged with administering family lands used a custom called usufruct—the lawful right to something so long as it is being utilized"
- "Usufruct did not mean that Indians thought land was free, like air and water, open to anyone wishing to share it"
- **Source:** Grumet, Robert S. *First Manhattans.* University of Oklahoma Press, 2011. p. 61.

### ✅ The Dutch held land under "fee simple" — absolute private property
- "European law... defined all land under Crown or corporate control as private property and regarded land sales as final"
- **Source:** Grumet, *First Manhattans.* p. 62.

### ✅ The Lenape understood the transaction as a shared-use agreement
- "Indians of the Northeast saw a real estate deal as a combination of a rental agreement and a treaty or alliance between two groups"
- "Indians probably regarded these deals as temporary arrangements that could be renewed or cancelled by either party at any time"
- **Source:** Shorto (2004) | Grumet, *First Manhattans.* p. 29.

### ✅ "It is virtually certain that the Native Americans did not understand the Dutch and English land purchases the same way these Europeans did"
- **Source:** Howe, "Notes on the Manhattan Purchase." Gotham Center, 2012.

### ✅ Lenape society was matrilineal — clan mothers chose sachems
- Male sachems could NOT sell land unilaterally
- "No land can be sold without all the Indians round being made acquainted with the matter" — Nutimus, Lenape sachem, 1737
- "Squaw sachems" like Mamanuchqua actively signed treaties and sold land
- **Source:** Soderlund, *Lenape Country.* UPenn Press, 2015. | O'Connell, *Manifest Deception.* Rutgers, 2024.

### ✅ LENAPE QUOTES ON LAND (for script — direct quotes)

**Hitakonanu'laxk (Treebeard), modern Lenape elder:**
> "Land to us was like the air, sunlight, and water, something that was necessary for our survival, for our very lives. Our thought was that we were giving the whites some land to live on for a while. We saw it as sharing with them and did not consider giving them use of the land permanently. We certainly had no intention of excluding ourselves from using it."
— Source: O'Connell, *Manifest Deception.* Rutgers, 2024. p. 12.

**Nutimus, Lenape sachem (1737):**
> "No land can be sold without all the Indians round being made acquainted with the matter... the chief always – with the Leave of the others – undertook to sell and when he had agreed he called together the head of the families who had any right in the land sold and divided among the goods he got for the land."
— Source: O'Connell, *Manifest Deception.* Rutgers, 2024.

**Sehoppy, Lenape sachem (1717):**
> "if [the land] be taken from us where must the Indians go... he had lived amongst the English ever since they came into the country and... they had lived lovingly and like brothers together and that a little land would serve the Indians and that there was enough in the country for both."
— Source: Soderlund, *Lenape Country.* UPenn Press, 2015.

**Teedyuscung, Lenape sachem (1756):**
> "I have sold great tracts of land at unreasonably low rates, to the English, far below what any person would allow to be their value... the proprietor has purchased the lands so cheap, yet he sells them again too dear to the poor people, that they do not use the Indians well and think they owe us nothing."
— Source: Grumet, *First Manhattans.* pp. 69-70.

## SECTION 4: WHO SOLD MANHATTAN?

### ✅ The identity of the sellers is entirely unknown
- "The identity of the Indians who sold the island is not known" — Grumet, *First Manhattans.* p. 26
- "We do not know the identity of the Indians present, so we can't say that it was purchased from the 'wrong' Indians" — McErleane (2020)
- **Source:** Grumet (2011); McErleane (2020)

### ✅ The "Canarsee sold Manhattan" claim is a 1959 fabrication
- "This farcical take likely originated in a 1959 article by Nathaniel Benchley"
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

### ✅ The "Manhattans" were a distinct community living on the island
- De Laet (1624): "on the east side, upon the main land, dwell the Manatthans, a bad race of Indians"
- De Rasiere (1628): "the old Manhatesen are about 200 to 300 strong, women and men, under different chiefs, whom they call Sackimas"
- Van der Donck (1655): Listed Manhattan among four Indian languages spoken in New Netherland
- **Source:** Grumet, *First Manhattans.* pp. 10-11, 16.

### ✅ The Canarsee were based in Brooklyn, NOT Manhattan
- "claimed the chief part of the lands in what is now called Kings county, Jamaica, and some part of Newtown"
- **Source:** O'Callaghan, *History of New Netherland.* 1846. p. 47.

### ✅ The Wappinger were north of Manhattan, NOT on it
- Territory: East side of Hudson, from southern Dutchess County into Westchester
- **Source:** Van der Donck, *A Description of New Netherland.* (Gehring trans.) p. 151.

### ✅ No subsequent Dutch payments for Manhattan specifically
- Staten Island was sold multiple times (1630, 1657, 1670) — but Manhattan was not
- English record (1670) confirmed island was "bought & paid for 44 yeares agoe"
- **Source:** McErleane (2020)

## SECTION 5: THE MISSING DEED

### ✅ No deed for Manhattan has ever been found
- "Few documents—and, significantly, no deed or bill of sale—chronicle the purchase of Manhattan"
- **Source:** Grumet, *First Manhattans.* p. 26.

### ✅ The Schagen letter is not the ONLY reference — but it's the only contemporary one
- De Laet (1633): "our people have bought from [the Manatthans] the island separated from the rest of the land by the Hellgate"
- Stuyvesant wrote about it c. 1649
- English record (1670): "bought & paid for 44 yeares agoe"
- **Source:** Grumet (2011); McErleane (2020); Howe (2012)

### ✅ The Dutch archives were destroyed in 1821
- "the government of the Netherlands... sold for scrap paper what remained of the archives of the Dutch East and West India Companies prior to 1700"
- "eighty thousand pounds of records—had vanished"
- Brodhead discovered this loss in 1839 with "surprise, mortification, and regret"
- **Source:** Shorto, *The Island at the Center of the World.* (2004)

### ✅ The Schagen letter: physical description
- Single page, handwritten in "crabbed, loopy script of seventeenth-century Dutch"
- Contains: greeting, one paragraph reporting the purchase, cargo list, sign-off
- Bears oval stamp "'S RYKS ARCHIEF" (added later by archives)
- Held at Nationaal Archief, The Hague
- NYSL has a facsimile printed on 300-year-old paper, so authentic it bears a stamp certifying it's NOT the original
- **Source:** Jacobs (2009); Shorto (2004); NYSL facsimile page

## SECTION 6: THE STATEN ISLAND DEED (COMPARISON DOCUMENT)

### ✅ The surviving Staten Island deed is dated August 10, 1630 — NOT 1626
- ~~Previously claimed as 1626 in preliminary research~~
- **Source:** Land Papers (Gehring trans.), GG 6; Howe (2012)

### ✅ The 1630 deed does NOT itemize goods
- Says only "a certain lot of merchandise"
- **Source:** Land Papers, GG 6

### ✅ Later deeds DO itemize specific trade goods (NOT beads)
- 1649 Westchester deed: "6 fathoms of duffels, 6 fathoms of sewant, 6 kettles, 6 axes, 6 adzes, 10 knives, 10 awls, 10 corals, 10 bells, 1 gun, 2 staves of lead, 2 lbs. of powder, 2 cloth coats"
- **Source:** Land Papers (Gehring trans.), GG 222

### ✅ Eight Indigenous signers on the Staten Island deed
- Named: Krahorat, Tamekap, Tetemakwemama, Wieromies, Siearewach, Sackwewew, Wissipoock, Saheinsios
- Described as "inhabitants, owners and inheritors of the island called by us Staten-Island"
- **Source:** Land Papers, GG 6

## SECTION 7: KIEFT'S WAR (1640-1645) — CONTEXT, NOT PROOF

### ⚠️ CORRECTED: Kieft's War was NOT about contesting the 1626 purchase
- ~~Previously framed as "Wappinger rejected the purchase"~~
- Actual causes: Dutch maize levy, stolen hog dispute, Kieft's unprovoked massacre of Indian refugees at Pavonia and Corlaers Hook
- **Source:** Grumet, *First Manhattans.* pp. 33-36.

### ✅ Useful for: showing Dutch-Lenape relations deteriorated into extreme violence
- Dutch troops "broke into Indian houses and storage pits, burned the Indians' fields, tortured a sachem's son, and killed several townspeople"
- Kieft ordered massacre of refugees who had fled TO the Dutch for protection
- Lower River Indians formed at least 4 large-scale coalitions to fight the Dutch
- **Source:** Grumet, *First Manhattans.* pp. 34-36.

## SECTION 8: WHY THE MYTH PERSISTS — DIAMOND CHAIN

### ✅ Level 1 (Event): Ambiguous, undocumented transaction
- No deed, one-sentence reference, no specifics about participants or goods
- Created a "blank canvas" for myth construction

### ✅ Level 2 (Institution): 19th-century deliberate construction
- O'Callaghan (1844): Invented $24
- NY Historical Society (1844): Bloodgood argued "honest business deal" founding
- Brodhead (1853): Weaponized to rival Penn's Treaty
- Ranney (1853): Invented beads imagery, copied West's Penn painting
- Martha Lamb (1875): First beads in text
- 1909 Hudson-Fulton Celebration: Mass culture cementation

### ✅ Level 3 (Structure): Why it works
- **Power asymmetry:** Lenape accepted "negatively reciprocal" exchanges — "lacked sufficient power to completely refuse colonial demands" — Grumet p. 70
- **Cognitive biases:** $24 anchoring; "noble savage" stereotype — "guileless innocents" — Grumet p. 66
- **Legal persistence:** "The Manhattan purchase was the primal Manhattan real estate transaction: from it is descended every other Manhattan property conveyance, on down to the present day" — Howe
- **Imperialism through identity:** "an imperialism of a more sophisticated sort than brute conquest or simple deceit" — Howe

## SECTION 9: STEELMAN — WHAT THE OTHER SIDE GETS RIGHT

### ✅ Indigenous agency — the Lenape were NOT naive
- "the Indians were far from guileless dupes, and in the short term, which was what mattered at the time, they got considerably more out of a simple land transaction than the amount of the purchase indicates" — Shorto, *Island at the Center of the World* (Van Slichtenhorst 1648 passage; NLM-verified 2026-04-17)
- Lenape adapted to European deed system quickly — "within a few years such transactions were taking place among the Native Americans themselves" — Howe
- Sachems used deeds strategically to extract military protection promises

### ✅ Dutch good faith (relative to other colonizers)
- WIC 1625 instructions explicitly ordered negotiation: "induce them to give up ownership and possession to us, without however forcing them thereto in the least"
- Dutch "followed a much more formal protocol" than the English — O'Connell
- Lenape regarded the formal nature of Dutch compacts "highly" — O'Connell

### ✅ 60 guilders was not absurdly low by 1626 standards
- More than half a WIC soldier's annual salary
- Comparable to Dutch-to-Dutch land prices of the era
- "in its wilderness state New World land was dirt cheap" — Shorto

### ✅ Legal foundation still valid
- English upheld Dutch titles after 1664 seizure
- Unbroken chain of conveyances traceable to present day
- Transaction brought Manhattan into modern contract-based legitimacy

- "The 'doctrine of discovery' is not part of the teaching of the Catholic Church"
- Repudiated papal bulls Dum Diversas (1452), Romanus Pontifex (1455), Inter Caetera (1493)

## SECTION 12: PHYSICAL DOCUMENTS (ON-SCREEN VISUALS)

### Schagen Letter (1626)
- Single handwritten page, 17th-century Dutch script
- One sentence about the purchase amid a cargo manifest (7,246 beaver skins)
- Oval "'S RYKS ARCHIEF" stamp (added later)
- Held: Nationaal Archief, The Hague
- Facsimile at NYSL printed on 300-year-old paper

### Ranney Painting (1853)
- Chest overflowing with beads, cloth, trinkets (ALL INVENTED)
- Tree = stolen from West's Penn painting; kneeling figure = copied
- "Discovered" at Rutgers (1923) after hanging unnoticed "for many years"
- Currently in private collection
- First image of the event; model for ALL subsequent depictions

### Staten Island Deed (1630)
- Dutch, recorded by provincial secretary
- 8 Indigenous signers named
- Standard deed format: time, place, parties, payments, boundaries
- Signed "with signatures or marks"
