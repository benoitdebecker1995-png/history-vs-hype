## Prompt 1: The Five Myth Layers — Claim Verification

```
I need to verify the following claims about the 1626 Manhattan "purchase" using the sources you have access to:

CLAIM 1: The "$24" figure was NOT recorded by anyone present at the transaction. It was created by historian John Romeyn Brodhead in 1844 by converting 60 guilders to US dollars.

CLAIM 2: The Schagen letter (November 5, 1626) mentions NO beads or trinkets. It says only "for the value of 60 guilders." The "beads" image was created by 19th-century painters, particularly William Ranney (1855).

CLAIM 3: The Lenape people had a usufruct-based concept of land — the right to USE land, not to permanently own and transfer it. The transaction was understood differently by both parties.

CLAIM 4: The group involved in the transaction was the Canarsee, who were primarily based in Brooklyn/Long Island — NOT the Wappinger, who held territorial claims to Manhattan itself.

CLAIM 5: No deed for the Manhattan transaction has ever been found. The Schagen letter — a one-paragraph note in a cargo manifest — is the ONLY primary source.

CLAIM 6: The Staten Island deed (August 10, 1626, same colony, same Director-General Minuit) lists specific trade goods including muskets, kettles, axes, and gunpowder — NOT beads or trinkets.

CLAIM 7: Kieft's War (1640-1645) — the Wappinger attacked Dutch New Netherland — proves the 1626 transaction was NOT accepted by the people who actually claimed Manhattan.

For each claim, provide:
1. VERDICT: VERIFIED / INACCURATE / PARTIALLY TRUE / NO EVIDENCE FOUND
2. EXACT QUOTE from source with page number
3. NUANCE: What context or complexity does the source add?
4. COUNTER-EVIDENCE: What do other sources say that contradicts or complicates this?

Use [1], [2] citation markers in your response and include a SOURCES section at the end formatted as:
1. Author Last, First. "Full Title." Publisher, Year. p. XX. [Translator: Name, if translated work]
```
