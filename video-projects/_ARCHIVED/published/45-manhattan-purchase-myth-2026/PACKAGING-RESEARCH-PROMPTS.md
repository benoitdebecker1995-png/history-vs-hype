## GEMINI DEEP RESEARCH PROMPT

Paste into Gemini with Deep Research enabled. This generates a competitor landscape and packaging strategy report.

```
I'm a small YouTube history channel (515 subscribers, 219K+ views, 47 videos). I'm making a video about the Manhattan Purchase myth — debunking the "$24 in beads" story. I need a packaging analysis. Here is my context:

MY VIDEO ANGLE: "The Dutch vs The Lenape. Manhattan Was Never $24." Every element of the famous story was invented centuries later. The $24 figure was invented by a journalist named O'Callaghan in 1844. The beads were invented by a painter named Ranney in 1853. The Canarsee trickster story came from a 1959 magazine article. The only document from 1626 is one sentence in a cargo manifest. The video reads primary sources on camera and traces how a one-sentence trade record became America's founding real estate myth.

MY CURRENT TITLE OPTIONS:
A: "The Dutch vs The Lenape. Manhattan Was Never $24." (scored 90/100)
B: "The $24 Manhattan Myth Was Invented Two Centuries Later" (scored 85/100)
C: "A Journalist Invented the $24 Manhattan Myth" (scored 80/100)

MY THUMBNAIL CONCEPTS:
A: Schagen letter (aged parchment) with strikethrough over "$24"
B: Split — Ranney's 1853 painting / Schagen letter, text "INVENTED"
C: Split frame — Dutch trading scene / modern Lenape, text "$24" with red slash

RESEARCH TASKS:

1. COMPETITOR SCAN: Find every YouTube video about:
   - Manhattan purchase $24
   - Manhattan bought for beads
   - Peter Minuit Manhattan
   - Dutch buy Manhattan
   - Lenape Manhattan history
   - Manhattan founding myth
   For each video found, record: title, channel, view count, upload date, video length, like count if visible. Note the dominant framing (simple retelling? myth-busting? Native perspective? real estate angle?).

2. TITLE ANALYSIS: Which of my three title options is strongest based on:
   - Keyword placement (what terms have the most search volume?)
   - Emotional vs analytical framing
   - Competitor differentiation (does anyone else use this structure?)
   - What SPECIFIC title changes would you recommend?

3. THUMBNAIL ANALYSIS: For every competitor video found, describe their thumbnail (colors, text, imagery). What's the dominant visual pattern? Which of my three concepts would stand out most?

4. AUDIENCE ANALYSIS: Who searches for Manhattan purchase content? American history students? New York locals? Indigenous history enthusiasts? General myth-busting audience? What does the audience composition suggest about packaging?

5. DEMAND SIGNALS: Are there Reddit threads, forum posts, Quora questions, or social media discussions about "Manhattan for $24" or "Manhattan beads" that indicate unmet demand? What specific questions do people ask? Is the NY400 anniversary (2026) generating fresh search interest?

6. GAP ANALYSIS: What angle does NO existing video cover? What question does nobody answer? Specifically: does any video trace each myth element to its exact origin (O'Callaghan 1844, Ranney 1853, Lamb 1875, Benchley 1959)?

IMPORTANT — What I need from you vs what I'll ignore:
- I NEED: Raw data (view counts, upload dates, titles, thumbnail descriptions, search volumes)
- I NEED: Observations about patterns you actually see in the data
- I WILL IGNORE: Made-up metrics (don't invent "Creator Trust scores" or "estimated CTR")
- I WILL IGNORE: Generic strategy advice ("optimize for SEO" — I know)
- I WILL IGNORE: Historical claims (you're not a historian, don't fact-check my angle)
- DO NOT fabricate view counts or subscriber numbers — if you can't find them, say so

Format your response as:
1. Competitor inventory (table: title, channel, views, date, length, angle)
2. Title recommendation (with specific reasoning tied to data)
3. Thumbnail recommendation (with specific reasoning tied to competitor patterns)
4. Audience profile
5. Demand signals
6. Gap analysis
```
