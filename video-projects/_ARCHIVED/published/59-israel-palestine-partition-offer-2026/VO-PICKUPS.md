## Pickup 3 — the Beersheba population  (cut ~01:03:18–01:03:30) — **RECORD (small fix)**

**On tape:** *"…Britain's own 1946 census counted about a hundred and three thousand Bedouin Arabs to a thousand Jews."*
**Problem (raw-read of A/AC.14/32 Appendix 3, "Note on the Bedouin Population of Palestine, presented by the UK representative," p.65):** the **103,820** is **Sub-Committee 2's** (Arab-case body's) Beersheba-*area* Arab tally (para 65, p.41), not a census count. Britain's OWN direct nomad enumerations: 1922 = 103,000 (all Palestine); 1931 = 67,000 (≈84,000 corrected); **May 1946 (Beersheba area only) = 92,000** (direct count, deemed most accurate). So "Britain's 1946 census counted ~103,000" fuses the wrong body + wrong number — the 1946 Beersheba census was **92,000**; the 103,820 is the sub-committee's higher figure (coincidentally the 1922 all-Palestine figure).

**RECORD (recommended — use Britain's own direct count):**
> "In the Beersheba district, Britain's own May 1946 census of the Bedouin counted more than ninety thousand — against about a thousand Jews."
- Card: **"Note on the Bedouin Population of Palestine, presented by the UK representative — A/AC.14/32, Appendix 3, p.65"** (1946 Beersheba count = 92,000).

**Alternative (keep the 103,820 card, attribute correctly):**
> "The UN's own committee put the Beersheba area at over a hundred thousand Arabs — almost all Bedouin — to about a thousand Jews."
- Card: **"UN Ad Hoc Committee — Report of Sub-Committee 2, A/AC.14/32, p.41 ¶65"** — NOT "1946 census."

Substance is solid either way: Jews were **under 1%** of the Beersheba area.
