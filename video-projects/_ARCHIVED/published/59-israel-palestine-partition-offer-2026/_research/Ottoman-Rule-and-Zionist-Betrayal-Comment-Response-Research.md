## Scope check against the video

The video explicitly defines its anchor as Resolution 181 and its throughline as the document's allocation, legal status, and failed implementation. The script begins its narrative in 1947 with a short thirty-year British-Mandate setup. It is not presented as a complete history of Palestine or Zionism.

The Ottoman period is nevertheless relevant background. Palestine was governed by the Ottoman Empire from 1516/17 until British occupation in 1917/18. Ottoman law and institutions did not simply disappear under the Mandate: land law, civil law, land registration, and communal personal-status courts continued in important part, although British administrators translated, altered, and reinterpreted them. That legal inheritance helps explain later arguments about title, sovereignty, and communal status. Its omission narrows the account, but does not by itself invalidate findings drawn directly from the text and implementation record of Resolution 181.

## Steelman and concessions

The strongest version of the comment is that beginning in 1917 can make British rule look like the natural starting point, when it was actually a wartime rupture in a territory with four centuries of Ottoman government and an existing legal order. It is also true that Zionist organizations and individuals sometimes circumvented Ottoman restrictions and that Jabotinsky's British military project and NILI's espionage were acts against the Ottoman war effort.

The overreach is treating “the Zionists” as one actor and turning that conduct into a single sufficient cause of Resolution 181. Official WZO neutrality, Yishuv opposition to NILI, Ottoman repression, British imperial strategy, and the thirty-year Mandate-to-UN sequence all prevent that formulation from carrying the historical weight assigned to it.
