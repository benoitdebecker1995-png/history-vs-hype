## THE PRIMARY (most-primary reached)
- **Document:** Memorandum by the Acting Secretary of State (Robert A. Lovett) to President Truman — reporting Ambassador O'Neal's telegram of a **Nov 30, 1947** conversation with Philippine President Manuel Roxas.
- **Date:** Washington, **December 10, 1947** · classification **top secret**.
- **Citation:** decimal file **501.BB Palestine/12–1047** = *FRUS 1947, The Near East and Africa, Vol. V, Document 910*, pp. 1305–1307.
- **Verbatim (char-exact off the FRUS official transcription):**
  - Memo body: *"As you will recall, the Philippine Delegate to the United Nations, Ambassador Romulo, in an address before the General Assembly of the United Nations on November 26, 1947 indicated that the Philippines would vote against Palestine partition. When the vote was taken, however, on November 29, the Philippines voted in favor of partition."*
  - Roxas's stated reason (memo body): *"…President Roxas stated that he had instructed the Philippine Delegation to change its position and vote in favor of partition because of his fear, based on a report from Ambassador Elizalde and a telegram from some ten United States Senators, that a vote against partition would have an adverse effect on United States-Philippine relations."*
  - Annex (O'Neal's telegram, verbatim): *"President Roxas stated that he was greatly disturbed over the position in which he had been placed with regard to the Palestine question. He had received a report from Ambassador Elizalde that a 'United States representative' had intimated that failure to support the United States position on the Palestine question might have an adverse effect upon Philippine-American relations. President Roxas had also received a 'high-pressure telegram' signed by some ten United States Senators."*
  - The State Department's official denial (annex): *"this Government favored the proposed partition of Palestine but had not sought to bring pressure in the matter on any Government and does not regard failure by any Government to support the plan as an indication of unfriendliness to the United States."*
  - Editorial footnote 4: *"Department of State files indicate additional charges by Arab sources that the United States exerted pressure on various countries to support partition."*
- **Showable file:** https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1947v05/d910 (clean official transcription — screenshot-ready; no PDF render needed). Supporting "before" visual optional: A/PV.128 (Nov 29 plenary roll-call, notebook `018feaa7`) shows the Philippines voting yes.

## ATTRIBUTION MISMATCHES
- **MIS-CREDIT / SOURCE-DRIFT (Cohen's telegram vs the primary):** Cohen's on-screen verbatim — the Murphy & Frankfurter *"isolate millions…"* telegram — is **secondary-only (Urofsky/Donovan, fn97)** and **does NOT appear in the FRUS primary.** The primary documents a *different* set of pressures: (a) a "high-pressure telegram" from **ten US Senators**, and (b) a report (via Ambassador Elizalde) that an unidentified *"United States representative"* intimated a no-vote could hurt Philippine-American relations. Do not put the Justices' "isolate millions" quote on screen as if it were documentary — it is a historians' account.
- **PREDICATE-DRIFT ("US officials"):** the claim's phrase "US officials" is loose. The primary attributes the pressure to **ten US Senators** (legislative — technically "officials," but not the executive) plus an anonymous "representative"; meanwhile the **executive branch (State Dept) explicitly DENIED** bringing pressure. Naming "ten US Senators" is both more accurate and more striking, and avoids implying a coordinated executive campaign the same document denies.
- **NAME ERROR (do not propagate):** Cohen writes **"Philippine President Carlos Rojas."** The president was **Manuel Roxas** (FRUS spells it correctly, "Roxas"); "Carlos" appears to bleed in from delegate **Carlos P. Romulo**. Do NOT copy "Rojas" or "Carlos Rojas" onto any card.
- **Vote-switch fact itself: MATCHES.** "Switched its UN vote" is char-exact primary: Nov 26 Romulo indicated "against"; Nov 29 voted "in favor"; Roxas "instructed the Philippine Delegation to change its position."
