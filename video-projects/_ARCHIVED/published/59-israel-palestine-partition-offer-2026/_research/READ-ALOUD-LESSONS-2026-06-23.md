## A. HOW HE WANTS THINGS STRUCTURED & EXPLAINED (the big ones)

1. **Lead the cold open on the audience's EXISTING OPINION, not the obscure document.** Nobody knows "Resolution 181"; everyone has a take on the *conflict* (occupation, self-determination, "they rejected peace," dispossession). Door = the takes people already hold; the document = the *reveal underneath* them. (Still seat the SEO keyword — "UN Resolution 181" — as the first content noun.)
2. **SHOW why something matters; never assert it.** "It's the document under every argument" was rejected until it *earned* the claim (181 = the first time the outside world voted a verdict on Palestine → that's why every modern take traces back to it). If you find yourself asserting significance, go to the notebook and find the actual mechanism.
3. **Don't include a link that isn't real.** "Apartheid" was cut from the open because the notebook confirmed it does NOT trace to 181 (181 *required* equal rights; apartheid is a post-1967 charge). Verify every claimed connection; drop unearned ones.
4. **Consensus-first, THEN the fork — with each side's reasoning.** On any contested fact: state what BOTH sides agree on (the uncontested floor) FIRST, then lay out the disputed part, attributing each view AND explaining *how* each reached it. Never state a contested claim as flat fact. (The Bedouin demographics rebuild: floor = "even by the UN's own count, ~45% Arab"; fork = "do nomads count as residents?" with UNSCOP's reasoning vs the Arab side's reasoning.)
5. **Never rest a damning number on a contested judgment call.** Fall back to the bulletproof floor ("even by their own count…"), then add the contested push as clearly-attributed.
6. **Every beat needs a transition IN and OUT.** His #1 standing complaint. "Feels random, no flow in or out" = the failure. A beat can't open cold on a new subject — connect it to the previous beat's subject, and tee the next. (The demographics para failed this; fixed by bridging land→people.)
7. **Set up every referent before you use it.** "As the delegate put it on the floor" failed — "what floor? never set up." Don't use a place/term/noun the viewer hasn't been given. (Fixed: "When the plan came up for debate at the UN…")
8. **Name primary-source speakers.** "One of the Arab delegates" → "Syria's delegate, Fares al-Khoury." Gives the quote a face and authority.
9. **Steelman each side fairly — concede their strongest point.** "The Jews said yes — even though they'd wanted all of Palestine" (concedes the compromise was real). Mirror it: the Arab "no" framed as *defending their country*, never "they wanted war." Air the opponent's best crop against your own thesis (Karsh's "80% empty desert" gets stated straight before it's answered).
10. **Verify mechanisms against the notebook BEFORE writing/rewriting a substantive beat** — don't render legal/economic/causal mechanisms from memory. Borrow the scholars' OWN plain phrasings (anti-cringe). Raw-grep comparative/scholar-on-scholar quotes (NLM invents them).

## B. PHRASING / VOICE (anti-prose — he cuts writerly flourish HARD)

11. **No prosey constructions.** Killed this session: "came inside the borders," "doing a lot of work in that sentence" (kept only as deliberate idiom), "theft, with a UN stamp on it," the "— a homecoming / — theft" appositive tags, "the scales of statehood," "it comes out reversed." If it sounds written, not spoken, cut it.
12. **"break" is NOT his word** for a claim failing → use "fall apart," "doesn't hold up," "fails on."
13. **"doesn't hold" → "doesn't really hold up"** (he adds the colloquial "really…up").
14. **"slogan" is NOT his word.** "usually" → he prefers **"like so often."**
15. **Kill repetition ruthlessly.** He flags EVERY repeat (split/two, "these pages," "two readings," "world," "start," "side"). Same word or idea twice = cut one, unless it's a deliberate apples-to-apples parallel ("55% of the land / 7% of the land" — that one he KEPT, because the repeated unit IS the point).
16. **No staccato / period-stabs.** "The Jews said yes. The Arabs said no." → comma-flow. (Clean short *complete* declaratives for genuinely distinct steps are still OK.)
17. **Shorter, always.** He repeatedly asks to cut. Tighten paragraphs; drop elaboration; a clarity fix is a rewrite at constant-or-shorter length, never an expansion.
18. **His neutrality line, in his words:** "This isn't me taking sides. It's checking each claim against the document itself — and what historians across the board actually agree on. And like so often, the truth ends up more complicated than either side makes it sound." (NOT "I'm not arguing for Israel or Palestine"; NOT "the truth is in the grey middle" = false-balance — it's "more complicated than the simple versions," because each clean claim is wrong in a *specific* way.)
19. **Plain numbers, no forced comparisons.** Don't barrage with number pairs; state the floor figure, then "tips to a majority" rather than a second pair.
