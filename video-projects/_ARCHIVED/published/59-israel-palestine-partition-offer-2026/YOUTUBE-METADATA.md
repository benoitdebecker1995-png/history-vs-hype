## Description

```
By November 1947 the UN had voted to split British Palestine into a Jewish state and an Arab one — but the plan it passed, Resolution 181, only *recommended* a border. It was never binding, and no one ever enforced it.

This is a document-by-document look at the 1947 partition plan: what UN Resolution 181 actually gave each side, whether the UN even had the legal power to impose it, and why the real border was drawn by the 1948 war — not the vote.

Both the Israeli and the Palestinian version of 1947 fall apart once you read the plan itself. The map handed the Jewish state about 55% of the land when Jews were roughly a third of the population — and the Arab state it drew never came into being at all. But "imposed by the UN" doesn't survive the text either: the General Assembly can only recommend, the UN's own lawyers called Resolution 181 a document of "no obligatory character whatsoever," and when the vote passed, not a single power was willing to send an army to enforce it. What both sides argue about was an offer that was never actually enforceable.
```
