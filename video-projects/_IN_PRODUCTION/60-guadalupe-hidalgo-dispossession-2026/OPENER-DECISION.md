> "When the United States took half of Mexico in 1848, it made a promise — in writing, in the treaty itself: the people already living there could keep their land. That's the part that should make this an honorable story. It's also the part that turned out to be a lie. The land wasn't taken by an army. It was taken by a court — slowly, legally, and with a straight face — over the next fifty years."

## Candidate B — The Scene (Barros, 1894)
> "In 1894, an eighty-year-old man named Mariano Barros stood in a courtroom in Santa Fe and explained — in Spanish, through an interpreter, to five judges from places like Iowa and Tennessee — that his village had shared the same mountains for a hundred years. Three years later, the Supreme Court ruled those mountains had never belonged to his village at all. They belonged to the government. And, the Court said, they always had."

This is the story of how a court took it back without firing a shot."
