### ACT 1 — 1903: the treaty no Panamanian signed

**C1.** Bunau-Varilla was a **French citizen** who signed the 1903 treaty as Panama's "Envoy Extraordinary and Minister Plenipotentiary." ✅ [P `2950fbd0` (signature block); S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber] · T1

**C2.** He held a **massive financial stake** in the bankrupt New Panama Canal Company, which stood to collect **$40 million** from the US if the Panama route was chosen; LaFeber: he "wanted to pocket the $40 million for his New Panama Canal Company as quickly as possible." ✅ [S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber] · T1

**C3.** Bunau-Varilla **signed before the Panamanian delegation arrived.** LaFeber: signed at **6:40 p.m., Nov 18, 1903** in Hay's home; Amador and Boyd "arrived in Washington three hours later"; Bunau-Varilla met them at Union Station, "Amador nearly fainted on the train platform." Jorden: "signed … just two hours before a delegation of leading Panamanians arrived." ✅ [S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber; S `7c0cb033`/`cb030630` Jorden] · T1
> ⚠ Minor source discrepancy on margin (LaFeber "three hours later" vs Jorden "two hours before"). Both confirm: signed before the delegation could negotiate. Use LaFeber's precise version on screen.

**C4. (GEM — the differentiator)** Bunau-Varilla **personally made the deal worse than Hay-Herran:** "widened the zone area from ten kilometers (or six miles) to ten miles, and lengthened Hay's 99-year lease to 'perpetuity.'" ✅ [S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber] · T1

**C5.** **Verbatim, 1903 treaty:**
- Art. II — "The Republic of Panama grants to the United States **in perpetuity** the use, occupation and control of a zone of land … of the width of ten miles…"
- Art. III — "all the rights, power and authority … which the United States would possess and exercise **if it were the sovereign** of the territory … to the entire exclusion of the exercise by the Republic of Panama of any such sovereign rights…"
✅ [P `2950fbd0` Avalon; corroborated S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber pp. 226–227] · T1

**C6.** Financial terms (Art. XIV): **$10,000,000** in gold on ratification + **$250,000** annual "beginning nine years after." ✅ [P `2950fbd0`; S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber] · T1

**C7.** Sequence: Colombian Senate **unanimously rejected** Hay-Herran (Aug 1903); Panama declared independence **Nov 3, 1903**; US gunboat *Nashville* at Colón Nov 3, "within ten days" nine US warships present; treaty signed **Nov 18, 1903.** ✅ [S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber; S `7c0cb033` Jorden] · T1

**C8.** LaFeber: "Panama, however, did accept under duress." A 1970s Panamanian documentary was titled **"The Treaty that No Panamanian Signed."** ✅ [S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber] · T1 — *callback to the comment-mine top correction.*

### ACT 2/3 — 1977–78: the treaties and the clause

**C9.** Two treaties signed **Sept 7, 1977.** The **Panama Canal Treaty** expired with the handover (Dec 31, 1999); the **Neutrality Treaty is permanent** ("notwithstanding the termination of any other treaties"). ✅ [P `7a62045b`/`8830526a` Art. IV; S `d40e254a` senate.gov] · T1

**C10. DeConcini condition — VERBATIM (Condition (b)(1) of the US resolution of ratification):**
> "Notwithstanding the provisions of Article V or any other provision of the Treaty, if the Canal is closed, or its operations are interfered with, the United States of America and the Republic of Panama shall each independently have the right to take such steps as each deems necessary, in accordance with its constitutional processes, **including the use of military force in the Republic of Panama**, to reopen the Canal or restore the operations of the Canal, as the case may be."
✅ [P `8830526a` UN TS; P `1eeeb2fe` Cong. Record; S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber appendix; S `7c0cb033` Jorden p. 539 (omits "Republic of")] · T1

**C11. Votes:** DeConcini condition adopted **75–23 on March 15, 1978**; Neutrality Treaty ratified **68–32 on March 16, 1978** (one above the two-thirds threshold). ✅ [S `58fe0d95` Maier; S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber; P `8830526a`] · T1

**C12. Leadership Amendment — VERBATIM** (incorporated into Art. IV via the Oct 1977 Statement of Understanding):
> "This does not mean, nor shall it be interpreted as, a right of intervention of the United States in the internal affairs of Panama. Any United States action will be directed at insuring that the Canal will remain open, secure, and accessible, and it shall **never be directed against the territorial integrity or political independence of Panama**."
✅ [P `8830526a` UN TS; P `1eeeb2fe` Cong. Record; S `8aa3caf3` LaFeber; S `7c0cb033` Jorden p. 481] · T1

**C13. US Reservation (a)(1) — non-intervention (VERBATIM):**
> "Pursuant to its adherence to the principle of nonintervention, any action taken by the United States of America … shall be only for the purpose of assuring that the Canal shall remain open, neutral, secure, and accessible … and shall not have as its purpose or be interpreted as a right of intervention in the internal affairs of the Republic of Panama or interference with its political independence or sovereign integrity."
✅ [P `3230f066` Panama Canal Treaty resolution] · T1

**C14. Panama's counter-reservation — VERBATIM** (Understandings and Declarations upon ratification, UN TS Vol. 1161 pp. 201–202; Jorden p. 621):
> "…the actions which either Party may take … including measures to reopen the Canal or to restore its normal operation … will be effected in a **manner consistent with the principles of mutual respect and co-operation** … The Republic of Panama … will **reject, in unity and with decisiveness and firmness, any attempt by any country to intervene in its internal or external affairs**."
✅ [P `8830526a` UN TS; S `7c0cb033`/`cb030630` Jorden p. 621] · T1
> Jorden's gloss (p. 621): Panama's "mutual respect and cooperation" understanding "did not rule out unilateral action, which the United States had insisted upon … It did suggest such action would stretch the rules of the game and should be considered only *in extremis*." — keep this nuance (anti-yes-man: the counter-reservation did not flatly nullify DeConcini).

### THE CENTRAL LEGAL QUESTION (debunk core — honesty-guarded)

**C15. DeConcini's OWN stated intent (the strongest pro-broad-right evidence):** On the floor he said the words "establish the American right … to take military action" and "make it clear that the United States can take military action **on Panamanian soil without the consent of the Panamanian Government**." He framed it as covering internal Panamanian threats (strikes, sick-outs, upheaval). ✅ [S `7c0cb033`/`cb030630` Jorden] · T1

**C16. The contradicting weight (Maier, the scholarly verdict):**
- The DeConcini reservations "taken together, **give and take away with the same hand**." [S `58fe0d95`/`d9a0b636` Maier p. 223]
- The Leadership Amendment (against "territorial integrity or political independence") "clearly incorporates by reference … article 2, paragraph 4, of the United Nations Charter," which can make the territory "absolutely inviolate"; under that reading it "explicitly denied the right of the United States to enter Panamanian territory to defend the Canal" (limiting US action to the high seas). [Maier p. 220]
- The amendment is **treaty text**; the DeConcini language is "**solely a unilateral reservation**" — so the amendment carries greater legal weight. [Maier p. 223]
- **Conclusion (on screen):** *"In conclusion, there is no right for the United States to enter Panama to protect the Canal under the existing Treaty provisions, amendments and reservations. There also is no such right in customary international law."* [Maier p. 223]
✅ T1

**C17. (auditor's edge) DeConcini himself cosponsored the Leadership Amendment** "that said in effect that the DeConcini approach was not the policy of the United States." ✅ [S `7c0cb033` Jorden] · T1 — *the author of the clause voted to fence in his own clause.*

### THE ROOT — the fiction started in 1846, not 1903
**C35.** **1846 Bidlack-Mallarino Treaty:** in exchange for transit rights, the US "**guaranteed both the rights of transit across the Isthmus AND Colombian control over the area.**" Polk's sell to the Senate: if the US didn't act, Britain would — a warning "as catalytic as an anti-Soviet appeal would be a century later." ✅ LaFeber · T1
**C36. (THE INVERSION — thesis spine)** In 1903 TR used that same 1846 treaty as his legal pretext — "a justification that **had no legal or historical basis**." The treaty "**justified United States intervention in order to preserve Colombia's sovereignty. TR intervened, however, to destroy that sovereignty.**" He leaned on lawyer Oscar Straus's "**covenant running with the land**" sophistry; the State Dept's own top lawyer **John Bassett Moore "exploded Straus's sophistry.**" ✅ LaFeber · T1
**C37. (GEMS — pre-1903 interventions)** US landed troops in Panama "**at least four times (some historians say six)**" 1846–1903. The **1856 "Watermelon War"** — first landing under the 1846 treaty — was set off when a US traveler **refused to pay for a slice of watermelon**, sparking a riot and "the first of many anti-Yankee riots." 1885: Cleveland landed 400 men + a **"moving fortress"** (flatcars with a Gatling gun, cannon, howitzer behind boiler-iron). ✅ LaFeber · T1
**C38.** **Clayton-Bulwer Treaty (1850):** US + Britain would co-build any canal, neither to "fortify or control it exclusively." US escaped it via **Hay-Pauncefote (1901)** — "directly abrogated the 1850 pact," sole US right to fortify — while Britain was tied down by the Boer War. ✅ LaFeber · T1

### THE RECLAMATION MECHANISM — how Panama actually won (HOW>WHY)
**C39. Omar Torrijos** — military ruler 1968 (coup)–1981; Jorden: "tough, shrewd, egotistical, ill-educated, charismatic, incredibly patient, yet often volatile." Strategy shift: from "enumerating grievances" / begging → **internationalizing** the dispute. ✅ Jorden · T1
**C40. **C41.** **1974 Tack-Kissinger Statement of Principles** (Feb 7 1974): the US conceded the new treaty would **abrogate 1903 and KILL "in perpetuity,"** set a fixed end date, and return the Zone to Panamanian sovereignty. The legal foundation for 1977. ✅ NSArchive `dd8ab3d7`, LaFeber, Jorden · T1
