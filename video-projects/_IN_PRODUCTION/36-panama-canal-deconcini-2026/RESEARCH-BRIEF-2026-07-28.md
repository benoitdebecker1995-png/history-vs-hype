## 1. The video, in one paragraph

A viral claim says the United States kept a right to take the Panama Canal back. It's the single
most-endorsed comment in a 1,711-comment corpus — **8,500 likes, unsourced, and wrong in its details.**
The video answers it by going to the documents: the 1903 treaty **no Panamanian signed**, the seventy-five
years of a foreign strip cutting a country in half, and the 1978 clause people are half-remembering. The
answer is not a slogan. **The documents grant force for one purpose only — reopening the canal — and
expressly deny it is a right of intervention. But they leave American defence responsibility intact, and
in 1989 Washington invaded on a different argument entirely.** That's a mechanism, not a verdict, and it's
what the channel is for.

**Working title (LEAD):** *America Took the Panama Canal With a Treaty. Panama Took It Back.*

## 3. The spine, and what stands behind each beat

**1903 — the manufacture.** A Frenchman signed away a country's canal two hours before its own delegation
reached Washington. The treaty grants the US rights *"as if it were the sovereign"* — a formula that exists
precisely because it wasn't. Hay invented *"titular sovereignty"* for Panama the next year; Taft privately
called it a *"barren scepter."*
→ *LaFeber, Jorden, and the 1903 treaty text.*

**1903–1964 — the colony lived.** A ten-mile strip splitting the country. Gold Roll and Silver Roll — Jim
Crow exported. Zonians who never learned Spanish and were proud of it. Then Martyrs' Day, a torn flag, and
28 dead.
→ *And the OAS's own investigation found it was "not communist prompted."*

**1973 — Panama internationalises it.** Panama held the rotating presidency of the UN Security Council and
used it to seat the Council in its own legislature, **with Torrijos personally in the chair.** From there:
*"In what modern legal dictionary is there set forth the concept of perpetuity as the basis for
negotiation?"* and *"colonialism is the prison cell of the free man."*
→ **S/PV.1695.** Two years later Kissinger told Ford privately that holding on *"looks like pure
colonialism."* **Public accusation, private admission, same noun.**

**Why Washington moved.** Not embarrassment — arithmetic. A canal is fifty miles of locks run by
Panamanians, and the only real defence against sabotage is their consent. The Joint Chiefs conceded it was
*"nearly impossible to protect against sabotage."* Carter's own diary, April 1978: the White House was
**planning for massive violence** if the treaties failed.
→ *And the counter-case is on the record too: the Pentagon was split, and four former Chiefs of Naval
Operations argued the other way. We say so.*

**1978 — the clause.** DeConcini forces in *"including the use of military force in the Republic of
Panama."* Torrijos threatens to reject the treaties. Senators Byrd, Church and Sarbanes type the antidote
into their own reservation.
→ **All four texts verified character-exact against the deposited treaty.**

**The payoff — and this is the part that's new.** Three actors, three sides, one finding:
- **Ford**, in a signed decision memorandum: get Panama's agreement to keep the talks quiet **so the canal
  doesn't enter the 1976 US election.**
- **Panama's chief negotiator**, to his own National Assembly, three weeks before signing: they don't need
  this pact to intervene — *"They need it to present it to their Congress."*
- **Carter**, privately on the day the clause passed: *"one sentence in the Resolution of Ratification"* —
  *"it doesn't change the meaning of the treaties."*

**And the fork is documented on both sides:** DeConcini told the Senate his clause created a right to act
on Panamanian soil without Panama's consent. Escobar Bethancourt told Panama's Assembly the treaty granted
no right of intervention at all. **Two governments told their own legislatures opposite things about the
same text.** Both were selling ratification.

**The close.** Carter, in his diary, 27 August 1977 — eleven days before he signed — having spent the
afternoon reading McCullough: *"It's obvious that we cheated the Panamanians out of their canal. As a
matter of fact, no Panamanian ever saw the treaty at all before it was signed."*
→ **The sitting President wrote the video's own title claim, privately, in 1977.**

## 4. Why this could be the breakout — the honest case

The channel's problem is **not** that videos convert badly. It's that most are never shown to anyone.
**63.5% of all browse impressions in channel history went to a single video. Forty-one of fifty-six were
never meaningfully served.** Serve size is set by the size of the topic pool, not by how good the packaging
is. Great packaging on a small pool stays small.

So the question isn't "is this good." It's "**is there a pool, and is there a reason the algorithm hands
this to strangers.**"

**Four things say yes:**

**a) The pool is large and it is being served the wrong question.** Top Panama videos: 12.1M, 2.8M, 2.2M,
1.7M. Across the top twelve, the saturated words are *built* (×4) and *engineering* (×3). **Controversy
frame: 0 of 12. Evidence promise: 0 of 12.** The shelf sells how it was dug. Nobody answers who owns it and
by what document.

**b) There is a named audience, and it reads English.** This was the open question — Belize is
English-speaking, Panama is not. **23 self-identified Panamanian comments in the corpus; exactly one
Spanish-language comment in 1,711 lines.** They're watching English videos and saying the same thing
Belizeans said: surprised anyone covers them, and explicit that the coverage is incomplete.

**c) The audience stated the video's own title as its request.** The two highest-liked Panamanian comments,
on two different channels, ~2,800 likes combined, ask the same thing: **how the Panamanians got the canal
back.** The current title promises exactly that. The old one promised a grievance.

**d) A stranger can parse it with zero prior knowledge.** That is the only structural property the
channel's one breakout had that nothing else has had. *"America took it with a treaty. Panama took it
back."* needs no setup. Neither does a country with a red strip cut through it.

**And the new argument, from today:** this video can now **adjudicate a live argument using the primary
record from all three sides** — the American government's, the Panamanian government's, and the
opposition's. The channel's subscriber trigger is proving you understand systems rather than narratives.
**Nothing demonstrates that like showing the same clause described three different ways by the three people
who wrote, signed and sold it.**

## 5. What could kill it — stated plainly

**The thumbnail. This is the binding constraint and nothing else is close.** One video above 4% CTR in
twelve this year; no image above 7% since November. Research does not fix this. If the image converts at
3%, everything in this brief is wasted.

**There is no distribution wave to ride.** Nothing published on this topic since April has cleared 1,700
views. Cold start. That makes it a cleaner experiment and a worse lottery ticket — both are true.

**None of this buys a serve.** Four predictors were tested and all four failed. Escalation is not
predictable from any data we hold. **What this brief describes is every controllable, maximised. The serve
is the experiment.**

**Pre-registered, so we can't move the goalposts afterwards:** confirm at **≥9,000** first-28-day
impressions; kill below **4,500**. CTR confirm at **≥4%**. And the falsifiable one — **if it breaks out but
the geography is generic-US rather than Panama and the US-Panamanian diaspora, our explanation is wrong**
and something else drove the Guatemala breakout.
