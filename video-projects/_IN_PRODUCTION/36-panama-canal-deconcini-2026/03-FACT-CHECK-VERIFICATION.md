## Topic mastery — the honest nuances (so the read-aloud holds)

The story is **one move repeated, then reversed**: the US takes Panama's sovereignty while producing a document that says it isn't — and Panama reclaims it the same way, on paper. The five things easy to get *subtly* wrong, and how v2 handles them:

1. **Bunau-Varilla was Panama's *legal* envoy.** He wasn't an impostor — the junta empowered him (he won a "mini-power struggle"; held "credentials as their official agent," LaFeber/Jorden). What's outrageous is *legitimate-but-rigged*: a Frenchman with a $40M stake, signing before the actual Panamanians arrived. The debunk-proof framing is "no Panamanian *signed* it" (true) — **not** "he had no authority" (false). v1 got this wrong; v2 fixed.
2. **The 1903 revolution wasn't purely a US invention.** Panamanians had revolted 40+ times; they genuinely wanted out of Colombia. US muscle was *decisive*, not *sole cause*. v2 keeps both halves (anti-oversimplification rule 7).
3. **The debunk is scoped to the popular myth, not the narrow mechanism.** The false belief = "the US secretly kept the right to *take the canal back / invade* whenever it wants." That is false to the text. DeConcini's *actual* narrow purpose (act to *reopen* a closed canal) is real — v2 says so ("one line let America act to keep the canal open; the next made sure that could never become a right to invade").
4. **Maier's verdict is the dominant reading, not unanimous law.** v2 attributes it to him by name and shows DeConcini's contrary stated intent first. Jorden's nuance (counter-reservation "did not rule out unilateral action… only *in extremis*") means we don't claim the question never had two sides — we claim the *text* (amendment, signed by both) outweighs a *unilateral* note. "Settled" softened to "on the documents themselves, the answer is no."
5. **Window:** 1989 (Just Cause), the 2026 court ruling, and Trump's full China claim are **deliberately out of frame** — referenced only as the modern *question*. Don't let them creep into the read.
