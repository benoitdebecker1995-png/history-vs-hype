## Controlling idea (one sentence)
**They took Panama on paper. Panama took it back on paper.** The same instrument — a document, a legal fiction — that dispossessed a country became the tool it used to reclaim itself. (McKee Value+Cause: *sovereignty is restored when a people master the legal fictions used to dispossess them.*)

## Structural decision: "the same trick, twice" — NOT a chronicle
Two loops run in parallel so the click-promise never waits:
- **Debunk loop** ("can the US take it back?") — opened at 0:30, teased through the body, resolved at ~8:00.
- **History/emotional spine** (taken on paper → bone in the throat → reclaimed on paper → Ancon Hill) — the Center of Good is **Panamanian sovereignty**; that's the protagonist the old draft lacked.

Validated rhythm: inciting incident/turn at **~20%** (McKee <25%); the 1964 crack at **50%** (Yorke midpoint / point-of-no-return); acts alternate charge (death → UN win → the clause's threat) so the two climaxes don't halve each other.

## Debunk mechanics (Debunking Handbook)
- **Truth sandwich:** fact → explicit warning → myth stated **once** → alternative model that fills the gap (people are half-remembering DeConcini's words without the amendment beside them).
- **Don't headline the myth.** Three clean points, not twelve (overkill backfire).
- **Worldview framing** for the Trump-leaning viewer: lead on a shared value ("read the document, don't trust pundits"), not identity attack. **Graphics > narration** for the two-clause comparison (the densest, highest-strain moment — Kahneman).
