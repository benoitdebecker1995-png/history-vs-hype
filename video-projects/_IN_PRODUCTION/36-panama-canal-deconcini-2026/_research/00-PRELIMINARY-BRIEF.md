## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This is a document-forensics video about two treaties and one sentence. In 1903 a French national, Philippe Bunau-Varilla, signed away Panama's canal sovereignty "as if it were the sovereign" and "in perpetuity" — no Panamanian signed it. In 1978 the US Senate bolted the DeConcini Reservation onto the Neutrality Treaty, reserving a US right to use military force inside Panama to keep the canal open. The standard narrative ends at "Carter gave it back in 1999." The channel's edge is the legal mechanism nobody reads: whether DeConcini still binds, whether Torrijos's word-for-word counter-reservation nullified it, and how that 1978 clause shadowed the 1989 invasion and Trump's 2025 "take it back" rhetoric. The 2026 hook is now harder than the old file assumed: Panama's Supreme Court voided the CK Hutchison ports concession as unconstitutional (Jan 30, 2026), a $2B ICC arbitration is live, and the ports went to Maersk/MSC under an interim decree — a real, document-driven sovereignty story, not just Trump quotes.
