## The core story (as currently understood)

1. ❓ The Treaty of Tripoli was signed at Tripoli on **November 4, 1796** (by Joel Barlow), ratified by the **US Senate unanimously on June 7, 1797**, and signed by **President John Adams on June 10, 1797**.
2. ❓ The English-language version — the **Barlow translation** — contains **Article 11**, which states (substantially): *"As the Government of the United States of America is not, in any sense, founded on the Christian Religion..."*
3. ❓ The Senate's ratification vote was **unanimous** — one of only a handful of unanimous treaty ratifications in the Early Republic.
4. ❓ In **1931**, State Department historian **Hunter Miller** examined the Arabic original and discovered that **Article 11, as it appears in the Barlow translation, does not exist in the Arabic.** In place of what should be Article 11, the Arabic text contains **a letter from the Dey of Algiers to the Pasha of Tripoli** — unrelated content.
5. ❓ Miller's quote (from his 1931 notes): *"Most extraordinary (and wholly unexplained) is the fact that Article 11 of the Barlow translation... does not exist at all. There is no Article 11."*
6. ❓ The treaty was **superseded** by the 1805 Treaty of Tripoli after the First Barbary War; the 1805 treaty did **not** contain an equivalent Article 11.

**Publisher citation for Miller's analysis (verified via Avalon Project):**
> Hunter Miller (ed.), *Treaties and Other International Acts of the United States of America. Volume 2, Documents 1-40: 1776-1818.* Washington: Government Printing Office, 1931.

The Avalon Project (Yale Law School) hosts the Miller notes: `avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/bar1796t.asp` and related pages.

## What makes this a Correction-format video (not a debate video)

The internet argument is "was America founded Christian?" That's a trap — it has no clean empirical answer because it depends on what "founded" means.

**This video doesn't answer that question.** This video answers a narrower one: *What does the 1797 treaty, as the Senate ratified it, actually say, and what is the Arabic manuscript's relationship to that English text?*

Two forensic facts:
- The Senate unanimously ratified an English document that explicitly disavows a Christian-nation framing of the government.
- The Arabic original does not contain a translation of that article. Miller (1931) says so in a government publication.

**The "correction" is against both sides of the modern argument:**
- Against "America was founded Christian" → here is the 1797 ratified text.
- Against "the treaty proves secular founding" → the Arabic doesn't back that specific article; Miller flagged it in 1931.

That double-correction is the differentiator. It's why this is a history video, not a politics video.

## Open questions for NLM Phase 2

1. What is the exact, full, verifiable text of Article 11 as the Senate ratified it on June 7, 1797? (Need the Senate Journal record, not just the Barlow translation.)
2. Who translated the English version — Joel Barlow alone, or was there a State Department revision before Senate transmission?
3. Was the unanimity of the Senate ratification actually unanimous, or unanimously-by-those-present? (This distinction matters.)
4. Did any contemporary commentary (1797-1800) specifically remark on Article 11 as controversial? (If not, that's the story: it was *uncontroversial* in 1797.)
5. What specifically is in the Arabic manuscript where Article 11 would be? Miller calls it a letter from the Dey of Algiers — can we get an English translation of that letter, and date it?
6. Did Hunter Miller propose any theory for the Arabic discrepancy? (He says "wholly unexplained" — but did later scholars propose explanations?)
7. What happened to Article 11 in the 1805 superseding treaty? Why was it dropped?

These become the NotebookLM prompts in `02-NOTEBOOKLM-PROMPTS.md`.
