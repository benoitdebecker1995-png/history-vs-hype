### Beat 5 — Forensic interpretation + Cobbett + Dwight (2:30 – 4:00, ~90s) ⬅️ EXPANDED

**Function:** Forensic interpretation. Add the contemporary reactions (Cobbett + Dwight). Resist political over-interpretation. Now the modern-argument acknowledgment lands — AFTER the audience has seen the contradiction.

**Three clean points:**

1. **What the Senate ratified is the English text.** Article 11 was the article the United States agreed to. The Arabic is a separate document; in 1797 international law the language of the ratifying state binds the ratifying state. The Christian-nation language is real, ratified, and carries the legal force of treaty.

2. **The contemporary reaction was almost silent — but not entirely.**
   - **William Cobbett**, *Porcupine Gazette*, June 23, 1797: "...it certainly looks a little like trampling upon the cross."
   - **Timothy Dwight, president of Yale**, Barlow's former mentor — removed Barlow's portrait from Yale and replaced his Psalm translations. (F13 — Haselby p. 109.)
   - That's the entirety of preserved contemporary objection. No Senate debate. Adams silent. Jefferson silent. The 1797 reaction was *near silence.*

3. **The Arabic discrepancy is real and unexplained.** Miller called it "wholly unexplained" in 1931. No academic since has resolved it. It is one of those small documentary mysteries historians acknowledge and shrug at.

**Pattern interrupt:** Joel Barlow portrait → goes dark/empty (Dwight removing it). 3 seconds.

**Modern-argument acknowledgment (one sentence only, here, AFTER the forensics):**
"In our century, this passage has become central to an argument about whether the United States was founded as a Christian nation. That is not the argument this video is making."

**Stage 1 — Miller's mystery:**
- Restate: the Arabic doesn't contain Article 11. We don't know why. Miller didn't know. No one since has known.

**Stage 2 — Crane's mystery:**

- Closing voice line: "Both sides of the modern fight read themselves into a 1797 document that, in 1797, almost nobody argued about. The fight is real. It just isn't from 1797."
