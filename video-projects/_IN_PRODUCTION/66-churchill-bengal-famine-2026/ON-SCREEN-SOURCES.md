## EXHIBIT 1 ✅ — "There is no evidence… physical scarcity"

**File:** `FIC-p32-denial-caused-hoarding.png` (rendered 200 dpi, fully legible)
**Caption on screen:** *Famine Inquiry Commission, Report on Bengal (Government of India, 1945),
Part I, p. 26*
**Highlight this line:**

> *"There is no evidence to show that the purchases led anywhere to physical scarcity. But, on the
> other hand… they increased local nervousness and probably encouraged cultivators to hold on to
> their grain as an insurance against invasion and isolation."*

**What it proves:** rice denial did not create the shortage — it created the **hoarding**. Mechanism,
not malice. This is the finding none of the nine competing videos uses.

## EXHIBIT 2 ✅ — The boat table: 66,563

**File:** `FIC-p32-denial-caused-hoarding.png` (same page, lower half)
**Caption:** *Famine Inquiry Commission, Report on Bengal (1945), Part I, p. 26 — "effect of the boat
denial policy at the end of November 1942"*
**Push in on:** the figure **66,563**, then **3,373 destroyed**.
**Read alongside (same page, do not omit):** *"Those deprived of their boats were granted
compensation"* and the Commission's own hedge that *"it appears unlikely that the area under
cultivation was reduced."*

**What it proves:** the scale of the transport seizure, in the Commission's own numbers — and the
mitigations, in the same breath. The strongest single showable exhibit currently held.

## EXHIBIT 3 ✅ — Bihar seals its border, 1 July 1942

**File:** `FIC-p30-provincial-export-bans.png`
**Caption:** *Famine Inquiry Commission, Report on Bengal (1945), Part I, p. 24*
**Highlight:**

> *"The Bihar Government therefore decided to prohibit the exports of rice to any market outside the
> province by rail, road or river, except under permit. This prohibition took effect from the 1st
> July 1942."*

**What it proves:** the title's promise, in a document. **An Indian provincial government sealed its
border against Bengal**, dated, in the official record. Orissa follows in the same month.

## EXHIBIT 4 ✅ — Bengal bans its own exports

**File:** `FIC-p35-bengal-export-ban-july.png`
**Caption:** *Famine Inquiry Commission, Report on Bengal (1945), Part I, p. 29*
**Highlight:** *"On the 16th July the Government prohibited all exports of rice and paddy from the
province except under permit."* (Earlier: 1 June 1942, exports outside the province prohibited
except under permit.)

**What it proves:** kills R1 on screen. Bengal was not shipping its rice away — it had **banned**
exports by mid-1942.

## EXHIBIT 5 ✅ — 58,000 of 600,000

**File:** `BEHRENS-p421-shipping-58000.png` (and `-p422-` for the continuation)
**Caption:** *C. B. A. Behrens,* Merchant Shipping and the Demands of War *(HMSO, History of the
Second World War, UK Civil Series), **p. 352, Ch. XVI "The Shipping Shortage"***
**Highlight:**

> *"In the event, India received 58,000 tons in the spring of 1943."*

**Then push out to the same page for the defence** — *"The demands when they came were often out of
all reason"* — and the calculation that 600,000 tons in four months needed ~250,000 gross tons of
shipping that did not exist in the Indian Ocean trades.

**What it proves:** the indictment and the mitigation, in Britain's own official history, on one
page. This is the referee's document and the spine of the video.

## EXHIBIT 6 ✅ — "perhaps one and a half million"

**File:** `FIC-p89-mortality-and-profiteering.png`
**Caption:** *Famine Inquiry Commission, Report on Bengal (1945), Part I, p. 83*
**Highlight:** *"unprecedented profiteering and the enrichment of those on the right side of the
fence; on the other, the rise of prices to fantastic heights and the death of perhaps one and a half
million people."*

⚠ **Frame carefully on screen:** this is the **1945 official estimate**, the floor of the modern
defensible range (1.5–3M). Do not present it as the answer. Caption must date it.

## ⭐ EXHIBIT 8 ✅ — THE WAR CABINET MINUTE, 4 AUGUST 1943 (the centrepiece)

**Files:** `TOP4-p155-warcabinet-4aug1943-A.png` → `-D.png` (four pages, 210 dpi, clean printed type)
**Caption on screen:** *War Cabinet, W.M. (43) 111th Conclusions, Minute 1 — 10 Downing Street,
4 August 1943, 6 p.m. In* Mansergh (ed.), The Transfer of Power 1942–7, *Vol. IV, pp. 155–158 (HMSO,
1973)*

**Shot 1 — establish the room.** Page 155: *"Those present at this meeting held at 10 Downing Street,
S.W.1, on 4 August 1943 at 6 p.m. were: **Mr Churchill (in the Chair)**, Mr Attlee, Mr Anthony Eden…
Also present during discussion of item 1… **Mr Amery… Lord Leathers**…"*

**Shot 2 — the ask.** *"a request had been received from the Government of India for **500,000 tons of
wheat**"* — and on p.156, *"It was true that the 500,000 tons asked for was **only 1% of India's total
production**."*

**Shot 3 — THE LINE.** Page 156, push in:
> *"**In the view of the War Cabinet, the shortage of grain in India was not the result of physical
> deficiency but of hoarding, due to inflationary conditions.**"*

**Shot 4 — the remedy, and the provinces.** Same page, (a): *"to induce hoarders to release grain. It
was most important to reverse the tendency which existed in one or two provinces for those in
authority to encourage peasants to hold on to their grain."*

**Shot 5 — why the grain didn't sail.** Same page, (c): *"**THE MINISTER OF WAR TRANSPORT** reminded
the War Cabinet that in January 1941 [1943] a demand had been made for an additional 600,000 tons of
wheat, but in the result **it had not been necessary to ship anything approaching this quantity. He
expected that much the same would happen on this occasion.**"*
⚠ **Note the editorial correction in square brackets** — the minute reads "1941", Mansergh corrects to
"1943". Show the bracket; do not silently amend it.

**Shot 6 — Amery objects and loses.** Page 157: the token *"not more than 50,000 tons… **not be
earmarked for India but should be ordered to Colombo**"*, Amery asking the Cabinet *"not to take a firm
decision to limit the help"*, and the resolution that *"**no further action should be decided upon at
the present time**."*

**What it proves:** the central charge, at the level of a documented decision — who was in the room,
what they were told, what they concluded, who dissented, and what they authorised. This is the
strongest exhibit in the project and the reason the video exists.

**⭐ Provenance note worth saying on screen:** `W.M. (43)` is the War Cabinet's own minute series —
these are the Cabinet papers, published by HMSO. The viewer is not being asked to trust a historian's
summary.

## Provenance discipline

1. **Every on-screen document gets its locator in vision** — title, year, page. No exceptions.
2. **Show the page, then the line.** Provenance first, claim second.
3. **Where a page contains both sides, show both.** Exhibits 2 and 5 each contain their own
   counter-evidence; cutting that out would make this an advocacy video with better footnotes.
4. **Nothing from the GPT dossier goes on screen unverified.** Two of its citations checked so far:
   Behrens held exactly; the "188,000 tons" attribution to this report did not (0 hits in 113 pages).
