### BEAT 1 — COLD OPEN  *(v1 — PENDING CREATOR READ-ALOUD LOCK)*

[ON SCREEN 0:00: slow push-in on the 1947 UN partition map. Chyron: "UN Plan of Partition — 29 November 1947."]

This is the map at the center of almost every argument about Israel and Palestine. The UN drew it in 1947 — a plan to divide the land into two states, one Jewish, one Arab.

You've heard what it supposedly settled: that the Arabs were handed a fair deal, said no, and chose war instead. It's probably the most repeated line in the whole conflict.

[VISUAL: anonymized comment-cards, usernames blurred — both poles, see `TWO-EXTREMES-EVIDENCE.md`.]

That "deal" was a single document — the 1947 partition plan. People cite it constantly. Almost nobody has actually read it.

So I did — the plan itself, the committee that drew the borders, and the record of the night they voted.

And some of it falls apart when you read it. The map gave the Jewish state more than half the land, at a point when Jews were about a third of the population and owned around seven percent of it — and that figure comes from Benny Morris, one of Israel's own historians, not a Palestinian one. Five days before the vote, the Arab states tried to put one question to the World Court: whether the UN even had the authority to split a country against the will of most of the people living in it. It lost by a single vote.

This isn't an attack on either side. It's one document, and what it actually says when you sit down and read it — because the two things people actually say — that the Arabs were offered a state and turned it down, or that the partition was colonial theft forced on people who never agreed to it — neither one quite survives reading the document.

— **BODY = THE LEDGER** (three independent things the story gets wrong) —

**3 — ONE: IT WASN'T A FAIR SPLIT** (1:45–3:15) — *fairness axis.* ~55% of the land to ~a third of the people who owned ~7% — **C19** (Morris, Israel's own side, defuses "partisan"). The proposed "Jewish state" was ~45% Arab — **C1**; count the Bedouin the way the UN's own Sub-Committee 2 did and it had an Arab *majority* — **C2** *(LABEL as a contested interpretive move — auditor's edge)*. Concrete-first: map + numbers on screen. Modern pivot.

**4 — TWO: IT WASN'T A WORKABLE STATE** (3:15–4:45) — *viability axis.* The map didn't draw two countries; it drew six pieces meeting at two crossing points — **C4**. It chained them in an economic union (shared currency, customs, joint board) — **C5**. The UN's own committee said the Arab state couldn't survive without it and called economic unity "indispensable" — **C6**. The "state" on offer was an abstraction.

**5 — THREE: NOBODY COULD MAKE IT HAPPEN** (4:45–6:30) — *enforcement axis = THE SPINE.* The plan gave the UN no army — **C7**; enforcement was handed to a Security Council that never acted — **C20**; Britain refused to hand over piecemeal — **C8** *(FORK 3, one line: exhaustion vs deliberate tilt)*; the UN's own Palestine Commission put its own expected collapse in writing — **C9**. Entity disambiguation: **GA recommends, SC enforces — neither did.** Throughline lands.

**6 — FOUR: IT ALMOST NEVER GOT THE CHANCE** (6:30–7:45) — *legality near-miss = THE GEM.* The resolution called itself a recommendation, not law — **C11**. The Arab states moved to ask the World Court whether the UN even had the right to do this — and lost **by a single vote** — **C12** (five days before the partition vote). The legality everyone assumes was never settled.

**7 — THE NIGHT OF THE VOTE** (7:45–9:00) — *what was actually said.* 33–13–10 — **C15**. On the floor the rejection wasn't "no to peace" — it was "illegal, imposed without consent" — **C16**; and Lebanon tabled a federal counter-offer that was procedurally sidelined — **C17**. **SHOWCASE FORK** (the "Claims on Trial" beat): rejection *justified-vs-intransigent* — Morris ("a flat no," maximalism) vs Khalidi/Said (refusal to ratify their own erasure). Both agree the facts, differ on the verdict.

**8 — THE VERDICT** (9:00–10:00) — *split / mirror test* — **C22**. "Fair deal rejected" breaks on **fair** (Beats 3–4). "Colonial theft imposed" breaks on **imposed** — nothing was imposed: no army, Britain walked, the SC declined, the UN abandoned it in four months. The line on the ground was drawn by the **1948 war**, not the map. Both claims share one false premise: that "the offer" was ever a real, enforceable thing. It only ever existed **on paper**. Callback close.
