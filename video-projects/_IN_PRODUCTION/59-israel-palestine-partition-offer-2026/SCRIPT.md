[ON SCREEN 0:00: slow push-in on the 1947 UN partition map. Chyron: "UN Plan of Partition — 29 November 1947."]

This is the 1947 partition plan — the map at the heart of almost every argument about Israel and Palestine. A plan that, on paper, would split the land into two states: one Jewish, one Arab.

You've heard what it supposedly settled: that the Arabs were handed a fair deal, said no, and chose war instead. It's probably the most repeated line in the whole conflict.

[VISUAL: anonymized comment-cards, usernames blurred — both poles, see `TWO-EXTREMES-EVIDENCE.md`.]

That "deal" was a single document — the 1947 partition plan. People cite it constantly. Almost nobody has actually read it.

So I did — the plan itself, the committee that drew the borders, and the record of the night they voted.

And start with the simplest question: was any of this even legal? Five days before the vote, the Arab states tried to put exactly that to the World Court — did the UN even have the authority to split a country against the will of most of the people living there? They lost by a single vote, twenty-one to twenty. Five days later the map passed anyway, and whether it was ever legal was never answered.

This isn't me arguing for Israel or for Palestine — it's about one document, and what's actually written in it. You've already heard the first claim — the Arabs were offered a fair deal and said no. The opposite claim is just as common: that partition was colonial theft, forced on people who never agreed to it. They can't both be true — and they're both arguing about the same document. So let's actually read it, and put each claim to the test. Neither one survives.
