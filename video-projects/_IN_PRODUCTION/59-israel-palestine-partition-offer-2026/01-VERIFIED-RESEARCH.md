### A. WHAT THE MAP ACTUALLY ALLOCATED (demographics inside the zones)

**C1 — UNSCOP's own figures: the proposed Jewish state was barely majority-Jewish.** Inside the proposed Jewish state, UNSCOP counted **498,000 Jews vs 407,000 Arabs & others** (total ~905,000). The proposed Arab state: **725,000 Arabs vs 10,000 Jews** (total ~735,000). [P] T1 — A/364 figures as reproduced + audited in Sub-Cttee 2 report, NLM `2ece8af6`. ✅
- *On-screen value:* even on the partition's OWN demographic basis, the "Jewish state" was ~45% Arab from day one.

**C2 — The Bedouin reclassification flips the Jewish state to an Arab MAJORITY (the mechanism beat).** UNSCOP excluded ~90,000 Bedouin "on the assumption that the Bedouins were nomads and should not be counted among the settled population." Sub-Cttee 2, citing the UK's May 1946 Beersheba census, revised the figure to **127,000 Bedouin, ~105,000 of them inside the proposed Jewish state**, and recomputed the Jewish state as **499,020 Jews vs 509,780 Arabs** — i.e. *"at the outset, the Arabs will have a majority in the proposed Jewish State."* [P] T1, NLM `2ece8af6`. ✅
- Verbatim (⚠️ OCR-reconstructed, re-verify against clean A/AC.14/32 before on-screen): *"The above statistics of population and of land ownership prove conclusively that the Arabs constitute a majority of the population of the proposed Jewish State, and own the bulk of the land."*
- *Auditor's edge:* this is a contested **interpretive** move (are nomadic Bedouin "residents" of a fixed state?), not a neutral fact — label on screen as Sub-Cttee 2's correction, not "the truth."

**C3 — The Jewish state got the economically developed land.** Sub-Cttee 2: the Jewish state was *"allotted the best parts of the country,"* *"the more economically developed part… embracing practically the whole of the citrus producing area"* (citrus ≈ the main export crop), and *"the best agricultural lands,"* leaving the Arab state *"certain mountainous regions, largely uncultivable."* [P] T1, NLM `2ece8af6`. ✅ ⚠️ OCR — re-verify verbatim.
- **[FLAG — RESOLVED, see C19]:** the ~55% territorial figure isn't stated as a percentage in Res 181 itself (boundaries defined village-by-village), but is confirmed by the academics, incl. Morris. → C19.

**C19 — ⭐ THE KILLER NUMBER, confirmed by the MAINSTREAM Israeli historian: ~55% of the land to a third of the population owning ~7% of it.** Across the scholarly spectrum:
- **Morris** (mainstream, T1, NLM `234bb4fe`): *"37 percent of the population had been given 55 percent of the land (of which they owned only 7 percent)."* Jews ≈ 630,000 of ~1.93M in late 1947.
- **Khalidi** (T1, NLM `4d114b2e`): *"as late as 1948, Jewish-owned land in Palestine amounted to only about 7 percent of the country's total land area"*; Arabs ≈ 1.4M of 2M (a **65% majority**) and owned *"nearly 90 percent of… privately owned land."*
- **Said** (T1, NLM `d8189d95`): at statehood Israel *"legally owned a little more than 6 percent of the land of Palestine."*
- *Why it matters: because the disproportion is conceded by Morris — not just the Palestinian-narrative scholars — it survives the "that's just one side's talking point" objection. This is the strongest single on-screen fact for the "imposed" pole, and it does NOT depend on the contested Bedouin reclassification (C2).*

### B. WAS EITHER STATE VIABLE? (geometry + economic union)

**C4 — Neither state was contiguous: 3 segments each, joined at 2 "kissing points."** Res 181 split each state into **three disconnected blocks** — Jewish: Eastern Galilee, the coastal plain (Haifa–Tel Aviv), the Negev/Beersheba area; Arab: Western Galilee, the Samaria/Judea hill country, the Gaza coastal plain + the Jaffa enclave. They interlocked at **two crossing/"kissing" points**: the northern one on the Haifa–Afula railway between Sarid and El-Mujeidil, the southern one *"midway between… Yasur and Batani Sharqi."* [P] T1, NLM `e18813a6`. ✅
- *On-screen value:* trace the map — the two states cross over each other; neither is a single connected territory.

**C5 — The Economic Union: the two states were bound together by design.** Res 181 Part I §D ("Economic Union and Transit") required a **customs union, a joint currency with a single exchange rate, joint non-discriminatory operation of railways/highways/posts/ports, joint development (irrigation/land reclamation), and shared access to water and power.** A **Joint Economic Board** (3 + 3 + 3 UN-appointed foreigners) was to be the **sole currency-issuing authority.** If the two Provisional Councils hadn't signed the undertaking **by 1 April 1948, the Commission would impose it.** [P] T1, NLM `e18813a6`. ✅

**C6 — Both committees said the Arab state could not survive without the union — UNSCOP called economic unity "indispensable."** UNSCOP "cardinal principle": *"the preservation of the economic unity of Palestine as a whole is indispensable to the life and development of the country and its peoples."* Sub-Cttee 2: *"The proposed Jewish State is to be allotted the best parts of the country and might, therefore, be viable, but the Arab State would be so lacking in resources as to be definitely not viable"* — it *"would have no other economic resources, no raw materials, no industries, no trade, and would have to subsist on subsidies or loans,"* and UNSCOP's own working notes conceded the Arab state could be viable only via *"a subsidy from the Jewish State."* [P] T1, NLM `2ece8af6`. ✅ ⚠️ OCR — re-verify verbatim.
- *Spine link:* the plan's own architects admitted partition-without-union was unworkable, then bolted on a union that required the consent of two parties, one of which rejected the whole plan. Sub-Cttee 2: *"it is impossible to combine economic union with political division. Economic unity implies consent."*

### C. RECOMMENDED, BUT COULD IT BE ENFORCED? (the throughline)

**C7 — The UN Palestine Commission had no army — enforcement was punted to a Security Council that never acted.** Res 181 Part I §B created a **5-member Commission** to which Palestine would be *"progressively turned over"* as Britain withdrew, *"under the guidance of the Security Council."* The Commission had authority to *"issue necessary regulations"* and **"political and military control" over locally-recruited militias only** — no international force of its own. Res 181 merely *requested* that the **Security Council** "take the necessary measures" and consider, under Charter **Articles 39 and 41**, empowering the Commission *if* it found a threat to peace. [P] T1, NLM `e18813a6`. ✅
- *Throughline:* the General Assembly could only **recommend**; the only body that could enforce (the SC) was never made to act.

**C8 — Britain refused to hand over piecemeal, killing the transition mechanism.** In Jan 1948 consultations, the UK's Sir Alexander Cadogan told the Commission Britain would terminate the Mandate by **15 May 1948** but *"cannot agree to relinquish it piecemeal"* — so, the Commission concluded, *"there could be no progressive assumption of authority in Palestine by the Commission prior to the termination of the Mandate."* Britain also held that it *"could not alone implement any plan not accepted by both sides."* [P] T1, NLM `bd1325df`. ✅
- *The progressive-handover machine in Part I §B depended on cooperation Britain explicitly withheld.*

**C9 — The Commission itself predicted collapse.** First Monthly Progress Report to the SC (29 Jan 1948): *"The Commission envisages the possibility of collapse of security and administrative services on the termination of the Mandate unless adequate means are made available to the Commission for the exercise of its authority"* — and it was still only *"devoting most serious attention to… the possible need for an international force."* No such force was ever provided. [P] T1, NLM `bd1325df`. ✅

**C10 — The Arab Higher Committee refused to recognize the resolution or even meet the Commission.** Per the Commission's report, the AHC cabled: *"ARAB HIGHER COMMITTEE IS DETERMINED [TO] PERSIST IN REJECTION [OF] PARTITION AND IN REFUSAL [TO] RECOGNIZE UNO RESOLUTION… AND ANYTHING DERIVING THEREFROM."* [P] T1, NLM `bd1325df`. ✅
- *Adjudicates the "they rejected it" claim against a primary document — rejection is real and documented; the open question (forks below) is whether it was intransigence or a principled response to an unenforceable, non-consensual plan.*

### D. WAS IT EVEN LEGAL? (recommendation vs binding + the ultra-vires near-miss)

**C11 — Res 181 calls itself a "recommendation," not a binding decision.** The operative text: the GA *"Recommends to the United Kingdom… and to all other Members… the adoption and implementation… of the Plan of Partition"* and merely *"Requests"* the Security Council to act. The mainstream legal reading (Kelsen, Goodrich & Hambro, Eagleton, Jessup, the UN Secretariat's own working paper) is that GA resolutions have *"no obligatory character whatsoever"*; the Assembly *"is not a legislature for the world"* (Charter Arts 10/11/14 → power of recommendation only). [P] text + [S] T1 (Quigley *Case for Palestine*, NLM `f141c666`; Kattan, NLM `94a0ed73`). ✅
- **CONTESTED (legal-status fork):** UN Sec-Gen **Trygve Lie**, the **Government of Israel**, Moshe Shertok, and Secretariat lawyer E. B. Sloane argued it WAS binding (UN as successor to the League's mandate authority / "agent of the international community" over territory of unclear sovereignty). Label both poles on screen.

**C12 — ⭐ THE GEM: the legality went to a vote — and the World Court referral lost by ONE vote.** Sub-Cttee 2 argued partition was *ultra vires*: the UN *"cannot make a disposition or alienation of territory nor can it deprive the majority of the people of Palestine of their territory and transfer it to the exclusive use of a minority."* Egypt and Syria moved to refer **eight legal questions to the International Court of Justice** for an advisory opinion. On **24 Nov 1947**, questions 1–7 were defeated 25–18; **Question 8 — whether the UN was "competent to enforce or recommend the enforcement of any… plan of partition which is contrary to the wishes… of the inhabitants of Palestine" — was defeated by a single vote.** Had the USSR or one Soviet-bloc state (which abstained on principle against ICJ referrals) voted yes, the legality of partition would have gone to The Hague before adoption. [S→P] T1 — Kattan citing the Ad Hoc Cttee record, NLM `94a0ed73`. ✅
- *This is the strongest shareable "you've never heard this" beat in the dossier. The map was adopted five days later, the legal question never answered.*

**C13 — Scholars split on whether it was lawful (acceptance of the throughline cuts across the fork).** Both legal monographs agree it was a non-binding recommendation, but divide on competence:
- **Kattan** (`94a0ed73`, T1): the GA had **"implied powers"** to recommend partition of a territory for which the UN bore *"special responsibility,"* and — *"acting with the consent of the mandatory"* — could lawfully modify a mandate's status; partition was *"impracticable"* and *"manifestly unjust"* but not clearly illegal as a recommendation. He stresses enforcement against the inhabitants' will would be the real legal violation.
- **Quigley** (`f141c666`, T1): the GA had *"no power over territory… broader than its general power of recommendation"* and no *"capacity to convey title"*; recommending partition against the majority *"violated"* the Arabs' self-determination and became a *"dead letter"* once the parties rejected it.
- → **[FLAG: DIRECTION NEEDED candidate — HELD]** This is a genuine scholarly fork on framing, but it does NOT change the *throughline* (both confirm: non-binding recommendation the UN couldn't enforce). Resolve as a split-verdict on-screen beat, not a thesis pivot. Flagged for user awareness, not halting.

**C14 — Even the pro-partition powers concluded it was unenforceable.** Kattan: once it was clear partition *"could not be secured by the necessary majority in the Security Council… partly because Britain would not enforce it, and partly because the Arabs and Jews were preparing for war,"* the **US reversed course** — Warren Austin told the SC the US now favored a **temporary UN trusteeship** instead. Britain, too, considered the plan unfair and would not implement a plan *"not accepted by both sides."* [S→P] T1, NLM `94a0ed73`. ✅
- *Confirms the throughline from the pro-partition side: the recommend-but-can't-enforce gap was visible to its own backers within months.*

### E. THE VOTE — WHAT WAS ACTUALLY SAID ON 29 NOV 1947 (A/PV.128)

**C15 — The result: 33 in favour, 13 against, 10 abstaining.** Adopted by roll-call at the 128th plenary, Flushing Meadow, 29 Nov 1947. Against: Afghanistan, Cuba, Egypt, Greece, India, Iran, Iraq, Lebanon, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Turkey, Yemen. Abstaining incl. the UK, China, Argentina, Chile, Mexico, Ethiopia, Colombia, Yugoslavia, El Salvador, Honduras. [P] T1, NLM `013c47e2`. ✅

**C16 — The rejection, in their own words, was framed as rejecting an ILLEGAL and FORCE-IMPOSED plan — not simply "no to a Jewish state."** Verbatim from the floor:
- **Iraq (al-Jamali):** the decision is *"anti-democratic, illegal, impractical and contrary to the Charter… Iraq does not recognize the validity of this decision."*
- **Lebanon (Chamoun):** the UN's task is to recommend *"not solutions which can be applied only by force or the threat of force, but solutions which by reason of their objective and equitable character command universal acceptance."* ← **the throughline, stated on the floor in 1947.**
- **Syria (Arslan):** *"Gentlemen, the Charter is dead. But it did not die a natural death; it was murdered, and you all know who is guilty."*
- **Pakistan (Zafrullah Khan):** the plan *"totally lacks legal validity."*
- **Saudi Arabia (Faisal):** *"today's resolution has destroyed the Charter… [Saudi Arabia] does not consider itself bound by the resolution."*
- [P] T1, NLM `013c47e2`. ✅
- *Adjudication: the "they rejected a fair deal" claim is TRUE that they rejected it — but the record shows the stated grounds were legality / consent / enforceability, the same grounds the documents (C7–C14) substantiate. The fork (C-forks below) is whether those grounds were principled or a cover for refusing any Jewish state.*

**C17 — There WAS a counter-offer on the table: a federal state, plus a motion to delay and study it.** At the "eleventh hour" Lebanon tabled a **federal State of Palestine** (federal government + Arab and Jewish cantons; a constituent assembly by *"direct universal suffrage"*; expressly modeled on *"the Constitution of the United States of America"*; Holy Places protected). Iran moved to **adjourn until 15 January 1948** so the Ad Hoc Committee could study it. The President ruled the partition resolution (A/516) had procedural priority and **voted it first**, so the federal alternative was never put to a substantive vote. [P] T1, NLM `013c47e2`. ✅
- *Complicates the pure-intransigence reading: a concrete (if late and self-serving) alternative existed and a formal delay-to-negotiate motion was made and procedurally sidelined.*

**C18 — Multiple delegations alleged great-power pressure on small states to vote yes (TESTIMONY, label as such).** Saudi Arabia: *"we have felt… the pressure exerted on various representatives… by some of the big Powers in order that the vote should be in favour of partition."* Pakistan expressed *"sympathy"* for delegates *"compelled, under heavy pressure, to change sides."* Iraq blamed *"great pressure and great influence… through UNSCOP… the Ad Hoc Committee… the General Assembly."* [P] T1 (as recorded statements), NLM `013c47e2`. ⚠️
- **[FLAG note]:** these are *allegations made by the losing side on the floor*, not adjudicated fact. The well-documented US lobbying of Liberia/Haiti/Philippines/etc. should be corroborated from a scholarly source (Cohen, FRUS `6911ab48`) before stating it as fact on screen. → pending corroboration query.

### F. THESIS PRESSURE-TEST + MIRROR VERDICT (the auditor's edge)

**C20 — ⚠️ THROUGHLINE CORRECTION: "the UN had no power to enforce" is too loose. The precise truth is sharper.** The General Assembly could only *recommend* (C11). But the **Security Council DID hold Chapter VII enforcement power** (Charter Arts 39/41/**42**) and **chose not to use it** — a failure of political *will*, not legal *capacity*:
- **Kattan** (`8e430c47`/`94a0ed73`, T1): *"only the UN Security Council… can make legally binding decisions… and enforce them by invoking its enforcement powers… provided for in Chapter VII."* Res 181 expressly *requested* the Council to act.
- **Cohen** (`d7ba048e`, T1): the US State Department's **own legal adviser, Ernest Gross**, refuted Warren Austin (5 Mar 1948) — it was *"a distortion of the UN Charter to hold that the Security Council was without authority to take measures to restore or maintain the international peace"* even if those measures facilitated partition.
- **Barr** (`edbfba54`, T1): *"No one, however, was willing to send troops."* Austin's legal argument was cover; the real fear was domestic unpopularity + inviting Soviet troops in.
- **→ Revised throughline (USE THIS):** *"The UN recommended a partition it gave itself no army to impose — and the one body that could have enforced it chose not to."* Still HOW>WHY, more precise, and it pre-empts the "but the UN had no legal power" pedantry.

**C21 — STRONGEST COUNTER-THESIS (must be aired for honesty): rejection + war, not design, is what killed it.** [P]-adjacent + [S] T1.
- **Morris** (`234bb4fe`): the Arabs *"launched hostilities to scupper the resolution's implementation,"* aiming to *"abort the emergence of a Jewish state… at inception."*
- **Ben-Dror** (`8e430c47`) quoting **Paul Mohn** (the Swede who literally drew the partition borders): *"If both sides had wanted to live in peace, it could have been realized in my partition plan… If they wanted war, it didn't matter how the border was drawn."*
- **Viability rebuttal — Galnoor** (`97984187`): the same 1937 territory dismissed as a non-viable "finger state" *"currently affords a livelihood, more than fifty years later, for over 3 million residents… The optimists… were correct."* Louis/Crossman: by 1948–49 the Yishuv already had *"the whole equipment of a Western European Socialist State."*
- ⚠️ *Auditor's note:* this rebuts the "Jewish state not viable" claim — but Sub-Cttee 2's viability critique (C6) was about the **Arab** state. Don't let the counter-thesis quietly swap which state was at issue.
- **Economic-union nuance — Flapan/Galnoor** (`607cb0e2`): the union wasn't impossible, but the Jewish Agency *"did nothing to prepare plans for the economic union"* and its own economists warned against tying a modern economy to a *"semifeudal, backward, agricultural periphery."* → the union's failure was also a matter of intent on both sides, not pure structural impossibility.

**C22 — ⭐ THE MIRROR VERDICT (split, as the format demands): the cleanest finding is that the "offer" was never actually imposed at all.**
- **"Arabs rejected a fair deal"** → *Survives:* the rejection was categorical and documented (C10, C16), and a workable-on-paper compromise existed (C21/Mohn). *Breaks:* on **"fair"** — ~55% of land to a third of the population owning ~7% (C19, conceded by Morris), a barely-majority "Jewish state" (C1), and a counter-offer (federal state) that was procedurally sidelined (C17). Acceptance would have meant ratifying their own minority status on their own land.
- **"Colonial theft imposed"** → *Survives:* the disproportion (C19), the non-consent of the majority (C12, C16), and the one-vote-from-The-Hague legality question (C12). *Breaks:* hard on **"imposed"** — **nothing was imposed.** Res 181 was a recommendation (C11) with no UN army (C7), refused by Britain (C8), declined by the Security Council (C20), and abandoned by the UN within four months (Quigley, C21). The actual 1949 borders were drawn by **war**, not by the map.
- **→ SYNTHESIS / throughline payoff:** *Both weaponized claims smuggle in the same false premise — that "the offer" was a real, enforceable thing that was accepted or refused into existence. It wasn't. It was a non-binding recommendation nobody would enforce. The map everyone argues about was never the law; it was a proposal that died, and the line on the ground was written by the war that followed.* ← this is the episode's verdict.

### FORK 1 — Why did the Zionist leadership ACCEPT? (genuine compromise vs tactical stepping-stone)
- **Genuine/reluctant pole:**
  - **Morris** (T1, `234bb4fe`): the movement *"said 'yes'"* despite lamenting the loss of the historic heartland; saw 181 as a *"giant achievement"* and *"emphatic ethical statement."*
  - **Shlaim** (T1, `4bb34733`): *"The Jewish Agency officially accepted the UN partition plan, but most of its leaders did so with a heavy heart… grave doubts about the viability of the Jewish state within the UN borders"*; the resolution gave an *"invaluable charter of international legitimacy."*
- **Tactical stepping-stone pole:**
