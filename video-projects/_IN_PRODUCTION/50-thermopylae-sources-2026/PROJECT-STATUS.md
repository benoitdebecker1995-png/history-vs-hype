## Primary Sources (for NotebookLM)

### The Three Accounts

1. **Herodotus, *Histories* Book 7** — the primary account
   - Greek, writing ~40 years after the battle (c. 440 BCE)
   - Likely interviewed survivors/descendants
   - Pro-Spartan, pro-Greek bias
   - Key passages: 7.201-233 (the battle narrative)

2. **Diodorus Siculus, *Bibliotheca Historica* Book 11** — the later retelling
   - Writing ~400 years after the battle (c. 60-30 BCE)
   - Drawing on Ephorus (lost source, 4th century BCE)
   - Different casualty numbers from Herodotus
   - Key passages: 11.4-11.11

3. **Ctesias of Cnidus, *Persica*** — the Persian-adjacent view
   - Greek physician at the Persian court
   - Writing shortly after Herodotus but with access to Persian royal records
   - Only fragments survive (preserved by Photius)
   - Different numbers, different details from Herodotus
   - Possibly also: Plutarch's *Moralia* ("Sayings of the Spartans") for the famous quotes

### Supporting/Context

4. **Simonides of Ceos** — the epitaph inscription
   - "Go tell the Spartans..." — contemporary, carved at the site
   - The oldest surviving source, and it's propaganda

5. **Persian sources** — fragmentary
   - Daiva Inscription (Xerxes)
   - No Persian narrative of Thermopylae survives — itself a key point for the video

## Script Skeleton (Pilot Episode Format)

### Beat 0 — Hook (0:00-0:30)
**Type:** myth_contradiction
"You know the story. 300 Spartans held a narrow pass against a million Persians. Every one of them died. It's one of the most famous last stands in history. Three ancient writers described what happened. They don't agree on a single number."

### Act 1 — The Myth (0:30-2:00)
The pop culture version: Frank Miller, the movie, "this is Sparta." What most people think happened. Brief, vivid, sets up the expectation to dismantle.

### Act 2 — Source 1: Herodotus (2:00-4:00)
Introduce Herodotus. Who he was, when he wrote, his biases. READ key passages on screen. Flag his numbers (Persian army size, Greek force composition). Show the manuscript.

### Act 3 — Source 2: Diodorus (4:00-5:30)
Introduce Diodorus. 400 years later. Different numbers. Drawing on a LOST source (Ephorus). Where he agrees with Herodotus, where he doesn't. Read the diverging passage.

### Act 4 — Source 3: Ctesias / Persian Side (5:30-7:00)
Ctesias at the Persian court. Fragments only. Different again. Then the key point: NO Persian narrative survives. We have only Greek accounts of a Greek victory. What does that mean for what we "know"?

### Act 5 — Synthesis (7:00-8:30)
What do all three agree on? (The pass, the last stand, the betrayal via mountain path.) What do they disagree on? (Numbers, motivations, who was there.) What can we actually say happened? Source criticism as the payoff — not "the truth" but "how close we can get."

### Beat Z — Close (8:30-9:00)
"Want me to go deeper?" CTA (pilot format). Tease: if this works, there are hundreds of events where the sources disagree.
