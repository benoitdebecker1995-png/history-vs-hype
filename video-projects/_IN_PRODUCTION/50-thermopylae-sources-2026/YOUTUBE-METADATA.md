### Full Description

```
"Molon labe." The most famous quote from Thermopylae doesn't appear in the earliest account of the battle.
Three ancient writers described what happened at the pass. They disagree on the size of the armies, the identity of the betrayer, and how the last stand actually ended. I read all three.
Sources: Herodotus (Histories 7.201-233), Diodorus Siculus (Library 11.4-11), Ctesias of Cnidus (Persica, Fragment 27).

What the three accounts disagree on:
• The Persian army: 1.7 million (Herodotus) vs 800,000 (Diodorus) vs 800,000 (Ctesias)
• The Greek force: ~7,000 (Herodotus) vs ~4,000 (Diodorus) vs unclear (Ctesias)
• The betrayer: Ephialtes, named (Herodotus) vs "a Trachinian," anonymous (Diodorus) vs "two leaders of the Trachinians" (Ctesias)
• The last stand: daytime defence on a hillock (Herodotus) vs night raid on the Persian camp (Diodorus) vs not described (Ctesias)

Every quote is read on screen with book, chapter, and section numbers. No Persian narrative of Thermopylae survives — all three accounts are Greek.
```
