## Description

```
For decades, two versions of reality ran in parallel on the Bakassi Peninsula. On paper, Nigeria had conceded — in 1913, in 1961, in 1975. On the ground, the people never left. In 2002, the International Court of Justice was forced to pick one. The paper version won.

This video fact-checks the standard narrative on the Bakassi Peninsula dispute between Nigeria and Cameroon. I read the full 155-page ICJ judgment, three dissenting opinions, the 1884 Treaty of Protection, the 1913 Anglo-German Agreement, the 1975 Maroua Declaration, and the 2006 Greentree Agreement — using primary sources throughout.

The "colonial theft" framing omits what Nigeria itself did. The "law is the law" framing omits what the dissenting judges said. The real story is a collision between what was written down and what was lived — and which one a court decides is legally superior.
```
