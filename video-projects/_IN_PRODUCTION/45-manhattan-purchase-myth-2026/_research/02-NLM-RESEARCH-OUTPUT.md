## PROMPT 2: Brodhead's $24 — Exact Origin

- **Creator:** E.B. O'Callaghan, January 1844, *Northern Light* (Albany journal)
- **Exact quote from O'Callaghan:** "the purchase of the Island of Manhattans, two hundred and seventeen years ago, by the Dutch, from the Indians, for the sum of sixty guilders, or twenty-four dollars!"
- **Conversion methodology:** Not detailed in sources — described as arbitrary
- **Spread:** "The story spread rapidly, usually as brief mentions in newspapers and periodicals with headings like 'Cheap!'"
- **Cemented by 1909:** Hudson-Fulton Celebration — 1M+ spectators on Fifth Avenue watched a float depicting the purchase for "paltry sum of twenty-four dollars"
- **Challenged by:** Russell Shorto — applying 19th-century exchange rate to 17th-century transaction ignores inflation, buying power, and that payment was in goods not cash
- **Modern equivalent:** Howe equates 60 guilders to roughly $1,000 today
- **Context:** A WIC soldier earned ~100 guilders/year — Manhattan cost more than half a year's salary

## PROMPT 4: Lenape Land Tenure

### Usufruct — Scholarly Consensus
- **Definition:** "the lawful right to something so long as it is being utilized" — Grumet
- **NOT "free for all":** Entitlements reserved for people with customary rights; strangers needed gifts or forced surrender
- **Lenape view:** "Land to us was like the air, sunlight, and water" — Hitakonanu'laxk (Treebeard), modern Lenape elder

### Fee Simple vs. Usufruct
- **Dutch:** Private property, land sales final, fee simple ownership
- **Lenape:** Communal shared-use, no individual permanent ownership
- **Key difference:** "When the Lenapes made agreements with the Europeans... they had no intention of transferring all rights to the area in question" — Soderlund

### Clan Mothers & Authority
- **Matrilineal society:** Descent through mother's line
- **Clan mothers chose sachems**
- **"Squaw sachems"** like Mamanuchqua actively signed treaties and sold land
- **Male sachems could NOT sell unilaterally:** "No land can be sold without all the Indians round being made acquainted with the matter" — Nutimus (1737)

### Lenape Quotes (for script)
1. **Hitakonanu'laxk (Treebeard):** "We saw it as sharing with them and did not consider giving them use of the land permanently. We certainly had no intention of excluding ourselves from using it."
2. **Nutimus (1737):** "No land can be sold without all the Indians round being made acquainted with the matter... the chief always – with the Leave of the others – undertook to sell"
3. **Sehoppy (1717):** "if [the land] be taken from us where must the Indians go... he had lived amongst the English ever since they came into the country and... they had lived lovingly and like brothers together and that a little land would serve the Indians and that there was enough in the country for both"
4. **Teedyuscung (1756):** "I have sold great tracts of land at unreasonably low rates, to the English, far below what any person would allow to be their value... the proprietor has purchased the lands so cheap, yet he sells them again too dear to the poor people, that they do not use the Indians well and think they owe us nothing"

## PROMPT 5: Physical Document Descriptions

### Schagen Letter
- **Length:** Single page — greeting, one paragraph, cargo list, sign-off
- **Format:** Handwritten in "crabbed, loopy script of seventeenth-century Dutch"
- **Marks:** Oval stamp "'S RYKS ARCHIEF" (State Archives) — added later

- **On-screen:** Purchase buried in a cargo manifest of beaver pelts and timber — one sentence amid 7,246 beaver skins

### Staten Island Deed (1630)
- **Language:** Originally Dutch, recorded by provincial secretary
- **Signers:** 8 Indigenous sellers named: Krahorat, Tamekap, Tetemakwemama, Wieromies, Siearewach, Sackwewew, Wissipoock, Saheinsios
- **Structure:** Rigid deed format — time, place, parties, payments, tract boundaries
- **Marks:** Standard deeds "signed with signatures or marks" — other deeds show "This is the mark of Pomipahan, made himself"

### Ranney Painting (1853)
- **Depicts:** Dutchmen and Indians flanking a chest overflowing with beads, cloth, trinkets
- **Invented elements:** Beads (first appearance anywhere), tree on right (copied from West's Penn's Treaty elm), kneeling figure

- **Location mystery:** "Discovered" at Rutgers University (1923), hung unnoticed "for many years." Currently in private collection.
- **Significance:** First image of the event; model for ALL subsequent depictions; introduced beads myth

## PROMPT 7: Counter-Evidence — Steelman

### What the "fair deal" side gets RIGHT:
1. **Indigenous agency:** Lenape were shrewd diplomats who knowingly traded land use rights for European technology and military alliances
2. **Dutch good faith (relative):** WIC 1625 instructions explicitly ordered negotiation, not conquest: "induce them to give up ownership and possession to us, without however forcing them thereto in the least"
3. **Both sides cared about boundaries:** "Both Indians and colonists cared deeply about territorial integrity and paid close attention to land rights and boundaries" — Grumet
4. **Fair price in context:** 60 guilders ≈ more than half a WIC soldier's annual salary; "in its wilderness state New World land was dirt cheap" — Shorto; In 1638, 100 acres of Long Island sold Dutch-to-Dutch for 52 guilders
5. **Legal foundation:** Transaction created unbroken chain of title recognized by English (1664), still valid today
6. **Lenape adapted quickly:** "it did not take them long to catch on, and within a few years such transactions were taking place among the Native Americans themselves" — Howe

### Scholars who defend legitimacy:
- **Richard Howe:** Purchase as step forward in modern contract law, not identity-based legitimacy
- **Russell Shorto:** "a deal that would satisfy both sides, each of which had its ideas about how it would pan out"
- **Robert Grumet:** Sales were "gradual and orderly," not "confused hodgepodge of scattered tracts signed away by random Indians duped"
