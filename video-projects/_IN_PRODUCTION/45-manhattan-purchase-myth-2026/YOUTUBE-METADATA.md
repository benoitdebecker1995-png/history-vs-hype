## Description (UPDATED 2026-05-07 — reconciled with finished cut, fixed subs.srt)

```
A founding myth is a missing archive filled by people with a motive.
The 1626 Manhattan purchase survives in one sentence — one line in a
Dutch West India Company cargo manifest. Sixty guilders. No beads. No
Canarsee. No deed. Everything else was added later, by people who had
a reason to add it.

The $24 figure was invented by a journalist in 1844 — sixty guilders
converted at an 1844 exchange rate, slapped onto a 1626 economy. The
beads were painted into the story in 1853. The Canarsee trickster was
added by a magazine in 1959. The bronze plaque in Inwood Hill Park —
the one that calls the story a legend — was bolted down by the
American Legion in 1954.

The Lenape never sold Manhattan. Every piece of the myth was forged,
over three centuries.

This video traces the forgery element by element, using the 1626
Schagen Letter, the work of historians Russell Shorto and Robert
Grumet, and the words of Lenape elder Hitakonanu'laxk.
```
