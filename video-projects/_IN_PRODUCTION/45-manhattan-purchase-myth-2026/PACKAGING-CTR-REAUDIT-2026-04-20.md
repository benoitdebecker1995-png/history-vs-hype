## FINDING 1 — Description opener is leaking CTR AND retention

### The locked opener
```
In 1626, the Dutch West India Company sent one letter home about
Manhattan. One sentence. Sixty guilders. No beads. No Canarsee. No
deed.
```

### What three notebooks agree on
- **Packaging Intelligence** (Finding 7, 7f040438-f30e-4b84-b88c-ac41192837d0): "Never open with background context. 'This document proves X' beats 'In 1916, during World War I...'" Chronological setups correlate with 20%+ drop-offs at the 2% mark.
- **Packaging Intelligence** (rule 11 + 15): "First sentence must contain a specific number, a name, or a shocking contradiction."
- **Wave 2** (top-quartile openers, n=41): Top performers open with puzzle ("for most of the 20th century there existed a country") or date-anchor AS the specificity bomb ("in the summer of the year 46 BCE Julius"), not as context.

### The leak
"In 1626, the Dutch West India Company sent one letter home about Manhattan" = 13 words of contextual setup before the shocking reveal. This is exactly the pattern Packaging Intelligence Finding 1 flags as the 17.1% intro-drop driver.

### Proposed rewrite (REC — adopt)
```
One sentence. Sixty guilders. No beads. No Canarsee. No deed. That's
the entire 1626 record of the Manhattan purchase.

The $24 figure was invented by a journalist in 1844. The beads were
painted into the story in 1853. The Canarsee trickster was added by
a magazine in 1959. The bronze plaque in Inwood Hill Park — the one
that calls the story a legend — was bolted down by the American
Legion in 1954.

The Lenape never sold Manhattan. The myth was forged, piece by piece,
over three centuries.
```

**Change summary:** Lead with the 5-beat staccato reveal. Backfill the 1626/"one letter" context into the payoff sentence. Shocking contradiction is now in word 1.

## FINDING 2 — Hook's first 15 seconds violate the thumbnail-promise rule

### The locked hook (script lines 40-44)
> Thousands of people walk past a boulder at the northern tip of Manhattan every year. The bronze plaque attached to it tells a famous story, but it gives the game away in its first three words: ACCORDING TO LEGEND.

### What three notebooks agree on
- **Packaging Intelligence** (Finding 7): "The first 5 seconds must deliver on the thumbnail/title promise. If the title says 'The Document That...', show the document in the first 5 seconds. Never open with background context."
- **Packaging Intelligence** (Finding 1): 29 out of 31 channel videos have their #1 drop-off in the 0-6% zone. Median drop at position 2% = 16.7%. Flat Earth lost 25.6% at 3% because "first 30 seconds are background context."
- **Wave 2** (rule 1F, Visual/Object Anchor, 14528ecf-1ed1-455b-85c7-424e4f36fac8): Top performers open with "This is..." pointing DIRECTLY at the thumbnail artifact. "This is a $100 trillion bank note from Zimbabwe." "This is the map we're going to talk about today." "This is the island of Ireland."

### The leak
Sentence 1 ("Thousands of people walk past a boulder") is setup. The plaque — the exact thumbnail image — isn't named until sentence 2, mid-clause. Viewers who clicked the plaque+FORGED thumbnail don't see the plaque land verbally until ~8-10 seconds in. The bridge is broken.

### Proposed rewrite (REC — adopt)
```
[VISUAL: Cold-open directly on the Inwood Hill plaque, "ACCORDING TO
LEGEND" already visible]

This is a bronze plaque at the northern tip of Manhattan. Its first
three words give the game away: ACCORDING TO LEGEND.

Then it tells you Peter Minuit purchased Manhattan in 1626 for
trinkets and beads worth about sixty guilders.

[BEAT]

Calling it a legend implies the story drifted into folklore over
time. But the historical record shows people engineered this
narrative on purpose — and we can date every piece.
```

**Change summary:**
1. Drop the "Thousands of people walk past a boulder" opener — pure context.
2. Open with "This is..." demonstrative (Wave 2 rule 1F — 5.4x avg views ratio).
3. First noun is "plaque" — the thumbnail object — in word 2.
4. Add a promise-of-methodology line ("we can date every piece") by second 15 — matches Wave 2's #4 actionable rule ("Promise your methodology by second 20").
