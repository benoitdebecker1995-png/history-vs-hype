## [P] Primary Sources

- **[P1] Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) — Full text**
  - Available at: Doctrine of Discovery Project (doctrineofdiscovery.org)
  - Also: UNESCO Memory of the World registry
  - Key clause: "All lands, both islands and mainlands, found and discovered already, or to be found..."

- **[P2] Inter caetera papal bull (May 4, 1493) — Full text**
  - Available at: Doctrine of Discovery Project (PDF with translation by Modrow & Smith)
  - Key clause: 100-league demarcation line
  - Quote: "Among other works well pleasing to the Divine Majesty...that barbarous nations be overthrown and brought to the faith itself"

- **[P3] Dum diversas papal bull (June 18, 1452)**
  - Authorized Portugal to "invade, search out, capture, vanquish, and subdue" non-Christians
  - Available at: Doctrine of Discovery Project
  - The legal foundation for everything that followed

- **[P4] Romanus Pontifex (January 8, 1455)**
  - Granted Portugal monopoly on African trade and territory
  - Available at: Doctrine of Discovery Project

- **[P5] Vatican Statement on Doctrine of Discovery (March 30, 2023)**
  - Available at: vatican.va
  - Key quote: "did not adequately reflect the equal dignity and rights of Indigenous peoples"
  - CRITICAL: did NOT formally rescind the original bulls

## [A] Academic Sources — University Press Publications

### Core (Must-read)

- **[A1] Lauren Benton, *A Search for Sovereignty: Law and Geography in European Empires, 1400-1900* (Cambridge UP, 2010)**
  - How Europeans conceptualized territorial claims through law and geography
  - How the Tordesillas line functioned (or didn't) as actual sovereignty
  - Argues Europeans imagined imperial space as networks of corridors and enclaves
  - BUY: ~$35-45

- **[A2] Laudares & Valencia Caicedo, "Tordesillas, Slavery and the Origins of Brazilian Inequality" (CEPR DP 18245, 2023)**
  - Spatial Regression Discontinuity Design using the actual Tordesillas meridian
  - KEY DATA: 3% higher slave density on Portuguese side (1872 census), 0.04 Gini points higher inequality today (8% of national average)
  - Mechanisms: wider racial income gap, education gaps, prejudice against Black Brazilians
  - FREE: cdn.dal.ca, insper.edu.br
  - Presented at AEA Conference 2023

- **[A3] Patricia Seed, *Ceremonies of Possession in Europe's Conquest of the New World, 1492-1640* (Cambridge UP, 1995)**
  - How different European powers legally "claimed" territory through distinct rituals
  - Spain: reading the Requerimiento (legal document), Portugal: planting stone markers (padroes), England: building fences, France: theatrical ceremonies
  - Why the FORM of possession mattered as much as the act
  - BUY: ~$30-40

- **[A4] Anthony Pagden, *Lords of All the World: Ideologies of Empire in Spain, Britain and France, c. 1500-c. 1800* (Yale UP, 1995)**
  - WHY they believed they had the right — the theological and legal justifications
  - Comparative imperial ideology across three empires
  - BUY: ~$25-35

- **[A5] Anthony Anghie, *Imperialism, Sovereignty and the Making of International Law* (Cambridge UP, 2005)**
  - Francisco de Vitoria's role: "the colonial origins of international law"
  - How modern international law was built ON colonial frameworks, not despite them
  - Already in channel library (used for Berlin Conference)
  - Chapter 1 directly covers Vitoria and the Spanish conquest

### Supporting (Context + Depth)

- **[A6] Christopher R. Rossi, "Treaty of Tordesillas Syndrome: Sovereignty ad Absurdum and the South China Sea Arbitration" (Cornell International Law Journal, Vol 50:2, 2017)**
  - Argues modern maritime claims in the South China Sea echo the same logic as 1494
  - Connects Tordesillas to UNCLOS and modern law of the sea
  - FREE: scholarship.law.cornell.edu

- **[A7] Robert A. Williams Jr., *The American Indian in Western Legal Thought: The Discourses of Conquest* (Oxford UP, 1990)**
  - How legal frameworks from the Doctrine of Discovery persist in modern U.S. law
  - Traces papal bulls through Johnson v. McIntosh (1823) to present
  - BUY: ~$35-50

- **[A8] Robert J. Miller et al., *Discovering Indigenous Lands: The Doctrine of Discovery in the English Colonies* (Oxford UP, 2010)**
  - How the Doctrine functioned in Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the U.S.
  - Written by indigenous legal scholars (Eastern Shawnee, Maori, Indigenous Australian, Cree)
  - BUY: ~$40

- **[A9] James Muldoon, *Popes, Lawyers, and Infidels: The Church and the Non-Christian World, 1250-1550* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 1979)**
  - The canon law foundations: what legal authority did the Pope actually have?
  - Based on unpublished Vatican Register 62 (letters about infidel societies)
  - Intellectual background of Vitoria and Las Casas
  - BUY: ~$30

- **[A10] A.R. Disney, *A History of Portugal and the Portuguese Empire, Vol. 2: The Portuguese Empire* (Cambridge UP, 2009)**
  - Most accessible English-language history of the Portuguese Empire
  - Covers Ceuta through 1807
  - Why Portugal needed the 370-league line (vs 100 leagues)
  - BUY: ~$40

- **[A11] Stuart B. Schwartz, *Early Brazil: A Documentary Collection to 1700* (Cambridge UP, 2010)**
  - Primary sources on Portuguese colonization of Brazil, many first time in English
  - Documents the actual process of claiming and colonizing the Tordesillas zone
  - BUY: ~$35

- **[A12] C.R. Boxer, *The Portuguese Seaborne Empire 1415-1825* (Carcanet, 1969; reprint Penguin)**
  - Classic overview of the entire Portuguese empire
  - How Portugal's maritime strategy shaped the Tordesillas negotiation
  - BUY: ~$20

- **[A13] Stuart B. Schwartz, *Sovereignty and Society in Colonial Brazil: The High Court of Bahia* (UC Press, 1973)**
  - How colonial governance actually worked in Portuguese Brazil
  - Legal structures that persisted from Tordesillas-era sovereignty claims
  - BUY/FREE: UC Press open access
