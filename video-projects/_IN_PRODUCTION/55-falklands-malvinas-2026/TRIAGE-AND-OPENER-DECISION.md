## 1. THE TRIAGE RULE APPLIED

Every claim was scored on four tests, in this order:

1. **Does it carry the spine?** The spine is about *forum* — who was willing to be judged. A claim
   about who was *right* is not automatically a claim about who would *risk a verdict*.
2. **Is the document showable?** A held file, a free public text, or a verbatim reproduced in an
   owned book with its archive reference.
3. **Is it in the measured whitespace?** (C22: arbitration, prescription, the Foreign Office, the
   1850 Convention, Wellington, *uti possidetis*, Onslow — **zero mentions across 34.4M views**.)
4. **Earn-your-inclusion / baseline-before-exception.** Verified is not the same as required. Every
   "but actually" needs its baseline stated first, and the baseline costs words too.

Test 1 did most of the cutting. The dossier's finest research — the 1833 counting dispute — is
research about *what happened*. This video is about *what neither government would let a court
decide*. That is the whole cut list in one sentence.

## 2. COLD OPEN — DECISION: **THE ARBITRATION REFUSAL**
### ⚠ TEXT AND ATTRIBUTION REVISED 2026-07-27 — see C30. The quote survives; the label was wrong.

**Chosen text** (Foreign Office memorandum, *"The Falkland Islands and Dependencies"*, quoted whole
and unelided by Greig, p.66):

> "**So far back as 1844 the Argentine Government made a request for arbitration in the case of the
> Falkland Islands which was categorically refused for the reason that His Majesty's Government are
> not in any circumstances prepared to envisage the possibility of such an arbitration going against
> them.**"

**On-screen attribution (Rule-1 compliant, per the C19 model):**
> Foreign Office memorandum, "The Falkland Islands and Dependencies" — quoted in D. W. Greig,
> *Australian Year Book of International Law* 8 (1983), p.66.
> Original: PRO FO 371/22499, p.143.

**🛑 DO NOT put a personal name or the year 1930 on this card.** The earlier plan followed Beck 1988
p.161, which attributes the sentence to "Gerald Fitzmaurice, 1930", gives two mutually inconsistent
TNA references for it in the same book, reworders it, and joins it to a clause that is not in the
source paragraph via an unexplained ellipsis. Greig prints the paragraph whole from the Godwin
memorandum. **Whether Godwin was quoting a 1930 Fitzmaurice opinion cannot be settled without the
file — so the card names the document, not a man.** Full workings at C30.

**What the revision buys us:** the sentence is now **unelided** (which the earlier version was not —
and an ellipsis inside a quoted claim is the exact failure this project logged at C23b); it carries
its **own motive** in the next two sentences of the same paragraph; and it arrives with a **date, 1844**,
forty years earlier than any competitor or any other source in the corpus reaches.

### Why the arbitration refusal and not Wellington — four reasons

**(a) Fitzmaurice IS the spine; Wellington is adjacent to it.** The thesis is forum avoidance.
Fitzmaurice states it outright, in writing, as government reasoning: *we will not go to arbitration
because we will not contemplate losing.* Wellington says nothing about a court. Opening on
Wellington means opening on a claim the video is not actually about, then pivoting.

**(b) Wellington needs a guard in the first thirty seconds. Fitzmaurice needs none.**
Wellington asserts "his Majesty has claims" in the same letter (C15b, honesty guard 1). Open on him
and the second sentence out of the gate has to be *"—but he was not conceding anything."* That is a
limp start, and it spends the guard at the moment there are fewest words to spare. Fitzmaurice's
sentence means exactly what it appears to mean. Its one qualification — Britain *did* litigate the
Dependencies — arrives naturally at beat 3, where it belongs.

**(c) It protects the strongest card's position.** If Wellington opens, Fitzmaurice becomes the
turn — which spends the project's sharpest quote at 20% and leaves the opening to a quote that must
immediately be walled off. Reverse them and both land harder: Fitzmaurice hooks, and Wellington
becomes the escalation at beat 5 (*"and this is not a 1930s problem — a Prime Minister wrote the same
thing in 1829, and sent the protest anyway fourteen days later"*).

**(d) Channel fit.** Niche-wide, specificity_bomb outperforms (5.4x vs cold_fact 3.7x, n=85). An
unfamiliar name + an exact year + a sentence that says *we won't go to court because we might lose*
is a specificity bomb. Wellington trades that for name recognition — which is a **title/thumbnail**
lever, not a cold-open lever. Wellington's fame is worth more on the thumbnail than in the VO.

**What Wellington gains by moving:** at beat 5 the fourteen-days mechanism (PM privately doubts →
public protest goes out anyway, for fear of the French and Americans) is the strongest version of
itself, because the audience has already met the 1930 version and recognises the pattern repeating
backwards. That recognition is only available if Fitzmaurice went first.

**Reopen this decision only if:** packaging lands on a Wellington-forward title, in which case the
cold open should match the title's promise.

## 3. THE BEAT SHEET — RESTRUCTURED 2026-07-27 (12 beats, ~13:00)

### What changed and why

**(a) The standalone context beat is gone.** The one *confirmed* at-creation retention lever in the
channel's data (n=55) is that the beat immediately after the cold open loses a mean **12.7 points**
of audience, versus 4.7 for the next band — and the rule is: no welcome, no methodology, no recap,
no roadmap, no scene-setting. The old beat 1 ("both governments say the law is on their side") was
exactly that. It is now delivered as the **consequence** of the opening document, inside beat 0–1,
so the viewer meets the official position as something already contradicted rather than as homework.
*(This also withdraws my earlier suggestion to move the referendum in front of the document — it
would have put an even softer context beat in the same lethal window.)*

**(b) The strongest evidence moves to 70–85% runtime.** Universal across all nine videos in the
Wave 6 teardown of RealLifeLore / Wendover / Johnny Harris — no exceptions. The Foreign Office
explaining, in its own words, that it would litigate the Antarctic but not the Falklands (C30c/C30d)
was sitting at ~25%. It is now **beat 8**, at ~72%.

**(c) The myth now has a living target** (C32a). RealLifeLore stated the conflation to 497,000
viewers in **May 2026**. The correction beat moves up to the turn, because it no longer needs
setting up — the audience may literally have watched it.

**(d) Wendover's Bell Curve.** Low-density hook (state the paradox, few facts) → high-density body
(evidence avalanche) → slow verdict. Implicit transitions only; **no "to understand this, we need
to look at…"** — Wave 6 flags explicit instructional transitions as RealLifeLore's lecture-fatigue
failure mode and the one thing not to copy from it.

### Craft directives for the script writer (from Wave 6 + the 2026-07-27 teardown)
- **Let the document breathe.** 3–5 seconds of reduced narration when each primary source first
  appears, before reading it. This is the channel's actual advantage and must not be scripted as
  B-roll.
- **Breathing room after devastating evidence.** 2–3 sentences of interpretation before the next new
  fact — especially after beats 7 and 8.
- **Thesis deepening.** State the central idea three times with increasing specificity: beat 1
  (plain), beat 8 (mechanical), beat 11 (final). Each restatement reflects what the viewer now knows.
- **Podcast test.** Every on-screen document must also be read aloud. Wendover passes this; Johnny
  Harris does not. We follow Wendover.
- **CTA in the final 5% only** — hard gate, `voice_lint.py::scan_cta_position`.

## 4. THE CUT LIST — what does not make it, and why

### Cut A — the entire 1833 counting dispute (C8, C9a–C9f)
The best primary-source work in the project. It goes.
- **Fails test 1.** It settles *what happened in 1833*. The spine is about *who would face a court*.
- **It cannot be done honestly in under three minutes.** It needs Vernet, Pinedo, the *Lexington*,
  the mutiny and Mestivier's murder before the counting dispute even makes sense. Done in ninety
  seconds it becomes the crop it exists to expose.
- **The spine needs 1833 as a date, not as a scene** — and Britain's own 1946 memorandum does the
  beat better than we could, in eleven words: *"an act of unjustifiable aggression."* That line is
  already in beat 6.
- **This is the next video.** "One despatch, three incompatible readings" is a whole episode, the
  research is done, and the whitespace on Onslow is total (zero mentions, 34.4M views).

### Cut B — the 1850 Convention and the Palmerston sentence (C1–C6)
- **Fails test 1.** Acquiescence is an argument about whether the claim *lapsed*, not about whether
  either side would be judged. It is a parallel dispute with its own full structure.
- C6 (one sentence, two readings) is the crop-test in its purest form and it is genuinely
  undecidable — which is exactly why it is expensive: it needs Moreno's letter of 31 July 1849
  reconstructed before the ambiguity is even visible. Too fine-grained for VO at 140 words a beat.
- The ledger's own triage already flagged beat 6 as a cut candidate. Confirmed.
- **Salvage:** C3's silence figures (34 years, 38 years, 91 years) can survive as a single
  subordinate clause in beat 9 if the read-aloud leaves room. Not budgeted.

### Cut C — Britain's own 34-year silence and the secret agreement's archival trail (C11a, C11c)
- **Fails test 1**, same reason as Cut B — it is the symmetry argument on *acquiescence*.
- C11c is a genuinely beautiful mirror (Britain raised no objection for 34 years while Spain governed
  the whole group) but it belongs to the abandonment video, not the courtroom video.
- **Salvage:** Goebel's secret-promise material survives in compressed form at beat 7 as the *cause*
  of the FO panic, which is the only job it needs to do here.

### Cut D — the Foreign Office edited Chatham House in 1936 (C17c)
The most striking single continuity in the dossier, and it goes.
- **The ledger's own guard is decisive:** *"If this beat cannot be written without implying collusion,
  CUT IT."* At ~150 words a beat there is no room to build the guard properly, and a half-built guard
  reads as insinuation.
- It does not carry the spine. It is about how the case was *presented*, not whether it was *tested*.
- Cutting it removes the single most attackable thirty seconds in the video. That is worth more than
  the beat.
- **Salvage:** pinned comment / description, with the full guard stated at length.

### Cut E — Pascoe's supporting Argentine material (C21d)
- **Baring debt (1838/1842):** strong, but it is an acquiescence beat (Cut B's category) and it needs
  the "an unaccepted offer is not a renunciation" counter-read to be honest. Two sentences minimum
  for the beat, two more for the guard. Not affordable.
- **Gallardo 1927:** single-source, British-reported, second-hand. Fails on evidence quality.
- **Groussac / the "pro-Nazi youth movement" line:** already barred by standing guard. Stays off

### Cut G — deliberate scope exclusions, with the reason ready
