## Tier 1 — Primary Sources `[P]`

### [P1] The 1771 Anglo-Spanish Declaration and Counter-Declaration
- **Text:** Prince de Masserano's declaration + Rochford's acceptance, 22 Jan 1771; in *British and
  Foreign State Papers* and Parry's *Consolidated Treaty Series*.
- **Provenance:** Diplomatic instrument, contemporaneous. **Bias/proximity:** Both governments drafted
  to let each claim victory domestically. **Intent:** Defuse war without conceding sovereignty — the
  ambiguity is deliberate and is itself the evidence.
- **Access:** Public domain; archive.org / HathiTrust.

### [P2] Convention of Settlement (Arana–Southern), 24 Nov 1849 (ratified 1850)
- **Text:** Full bilingual EN/ES. The load-bearing question — *does it mention the islands at all?* —
  is answerable by direct read, no scholar required.
- **Provenance:** Bilateral treaty. **Bias:** Neither party wanted sovereignty formally settled.
  **Intent:** Normalise relations after the Anglo-French blockade of the Río de la Plata.
- **Access:** Public domain; Argentine Foreign Ministry + *BFSP*.

### [P3] UN General Assembly Resolution 2065 (XX), 16 Dec 1965
- **Provenance:** UN instrument. **Bias:** Decolonisation-era framing; invites negotiation, does not
  assign sovereignty. **Intent:** Route the dispute into the C24 process.
- **Access:** Free — `docs.un.org`. Also pull the subsequent C24 resolutions through 2025.

### [P4] Constitución de la Nación Argentina (1994), Disposición Transitoria Primera
- **Provenance:** Constitutional text (ES — creator-verifiable). **Bias:** Domestic-binding
  statement of irredentist policy. **Intent:** Entrench the claim beyond any single government.

### [P5] HMS *Clio* log + Onslow's despatch, Jan 1833
- **Provenance:** Royal Navy operational record, TNA (ADM series). **Bias:** Written by the officer
  whose conduct is the disputed act. **Intent:** Report compliance with Admiralty orders.
- **Why load-bearing:** the single most contested event; determines whether 1833 was an expulsion of
  civilians or the removal of a garrison. Pair with [P6] and [P9].

### [P6] Commander Silas Duncan's report on the USS *Lexington* raid, Dec 1831
- **Provenance:** US Navy record. **Bias:** Justifying a raid on a settlement over sealing seizures.
  **Intent:** Defend the action to the Navy Department.
- **Why load-bearing:** establishes what was physically standing at Puerto Luis before Britain
  arrived — the pivot of candidate angle #2. **Must be read against Argentine accounts, not alone.**

### [P7] Jewett's proclamation, Puerto Soledad, 6 Nov 1820
- **Provenance:** Act of possession by an American-born privateer in Buenos Aires service.
  **Bias/intent:** Assert United Provinces sovereignty; Jewett's own commission and nationality are
  part of the evidentiary problem.

### [P8] Luis Vernet papers / grant documents, 1826–1831
- **Provenance:** Merchant-governor's own records. **Bias:** Vernet sought protection from *both*
  Buenos Aires and the British consul — the ambiguity is the finding.
- **Access:** Vernet papers held partly at the Biblioteca Nacional (AR) and in UK collections.

### [P9] Argentine Foreign Ministry claim dossier — *Cuestión de las Islas Malvinas*
- **Provenance:** Official statement of the Argentine legal case (ES). **Bias:** Advocacy document.
  **Intent:** State the claim at its strongest — which is exactly what the referee standard needs.

### [P10] UK FCO/FCDO sovereignty memoranda + the 2013 referendum official result
- **Provenance:** Official British counter-statement; FIG returning-officer record.
  **Bias:** Advocacy. **Intent:** State the British case at its strongest. Pair directly with [P9].

### [P11] Shackleton Report (1976), *Economic Survey of the Falkland Islands*
- **Provenance:** UK Government commissioned survey. **Bias:** Economic-viability framing.
  **Intent:** Assess whether the islands could sustain themselves — the document that shifted British
  strategic calculus. Note the oil angle has **zero search demand**; this is body evidence only.

## Tier 2 — Academic Monographs `[A]`

### [A1] Goebel, Julius. *The Struggle for the Falkland Islands: A Study in Legal and Diplomatic History*. Yale University Press, 1927 (repr. 1982).
- **Provenance:** Yale UP; Columbia legal historian. **Bias/proximity:** Written 1927, ~95 yrs after
  1833; widely read as **sympathetic to the Argentine claim** and cited as such by Argentine
  advocates. **Intent:** Legal-historical reconstruction of pre-1833 title.
- **Priority: HIGHEST.** Four projected T2 claims route through it. Public-domain era → check
  archive.org first.

### [A2] Pascoe, Graham & Peter Pepper. *Getting it Right: The Real History of the Falklands/Malvinas*. 2008.
- **Provenance:** Independent researchers. **Bias:** Explicitly **pro-British**, written to rebut
  Goebel and Argentine historiography point-by-point. **Intent:** Advocacy with apparatus.
- **Why included:** the affirmative British defender. Its direct opposition to [A1] is the referee
  structure. Treat both as *positions*, not neutral authorities.

### [A3] Kohen, Marcelo G. & Facundo D. Rodríguez. *Las Malvinas entre el derecho y la historia* (2015) / *The Malvinas/Falklands Between History and Law* (2017).
- **Provenance:** Kohen is professor of international law, Graduate Institute Geneva — the most
  credentialed exponent of the Argentine legal case. **Bias:** Argentine-affirmative, but
  peer-credible. **Intent:** Rebut Pascoe & Pepper directly.
- **Why load-bearing:** this is *the* affirmative Argentine defender, and it exists in Spanish —
  creator-verifiable. The Spanish edition is the untranslated-evidence asset if that angle survives.

### [A4] Gustafson, Lowell S. *The Sovereignty Dispute over the Falkland (Malvinas) Islands*. Oxford University Press, 1988.
- **Provenance:** Oxford UP; political scientist. **Bias:** Comparatively even-handed; post-1982.
  **Intent:** Analyse the dispute's structure rather than adjudicate it.

### [A5] Beck, Peter J. *The Falkland Islands as an International Problem*. Routledge, 1988.
- **Provenance:** Routledge; Kingston historian of polar/South Atlantic politics. **Bias:** British
  academic, institutionally moderate. **Intent:** IR framing incl. UN and Antarctic dimensions.

### [A6] Laver, Roberto C. *The Falklands/Malvinas Case: Breaking the Deadlock in the Anglo-Argentine Sovereignty Dispute*. Martinus Nijhoff, 2001.
- **Provenance:** International-law publisher; Argentine-born lawyer writing for a resolution-focused
  audience. **Bias:** Deadlock-diagnostic rather than partisan. **Intent:** Identify why the dispute
  cannot resolve — directly serves candidate angle #6.

### [A7] Cawkell, Mary. *The History of the Falkland Islands*. Anthony Nelson, 2001.
- **Provenance:** Long-standing islander-perspective history. **Bias:** Sympathetic to the settler
  community. **Intent:** Social/settlement history — the 1835–1884 gap the brief flagged.

### [A8] Dolzer, Rudolf. *The Territorial Status of the Falkland Islands (Malvinas): Past and Present*. Oceana, 1993.
- **Provenance:** German international lawyer — **third-country**, neither camp. **Bias:** Lowest of
  the set. **Intent:** Neutral legal assessment. Valuable precisely because he is not British or
  Argentine.

### [A9] Reisman, W. Michael. "The Struggle for the Falklands." *Yale Law Journal* 93 (1983).
- **Provenance:** Yale Law; leading international-law scholar. **Intent:** Post-war legal analysis of
  competing title and the use of force.

### [A10] Escudé, Carlos & Andrés Cisneros. *Historia General de las Relaciones Exteriores de la República Argentina* (ES).
- **Provenance:** Standard Argentine diplomatic-history reference. **Bias:** National perspective, but
  Escudé is notably critical of Argentine irredentism — useful internal dissent.

### [A11] Freedman, Lawrence. *The Official History of the Falklands Campaign*, Vol. 1: *The Origins of the Falklands War*. Routledge, 2005 (2nd ed. 2007).
- **Provenance:** UK government-commissioned official history; Freedman (King's College London) is
  the standard authority on the conflict. **Bias:** Official-history access = closest to the British
  state's own record, so treat as authoritative-but-institutional, not neutral. **Intent:** Reconstruct
  the 1965–1982 diplomatic run-up with declassified access most other authors lack.
- **Why load-bearing:** covers exactly the pre-war negotiation period this video needs, with primary
  access most Tier-2 sources cite second-hand. (Vol. 2, *War and Diplomacy*, is war-narrative — lower
  priority, buy only if the video's scope extends into 1982 itself.)

### [A12] Hoffmann, Fritz L. & Olga Mingo Hoffmann. *Sovereignty in Dispute: The Falklands/Malvinas, 1493–1982*. Westview Press, 1984.
- **Provenance:** US academic press, US-based scholars — third-country adjacent. **Bias:** Written
  post-war but pre-modern-legal-scholarship wave; useful as an early synthetic account.
  **Intent:** Literally scoped to this project's exact question and exact timespan (1493–1982).

### [A13] Perl, Raphael. *The Falkland Islands Dispute in International Law and Politics: A Documentary Sourcebook*. Oceana, 1983.
- **Provenance:** Compiled documentary sourcebook, US Congressional Research Service background.
  **Bias:** Editorial selection is itself a choice, but the documents are primary. **Intent:** Assemble
  the era's key instruments in one place.
- **Why load-bearing:** may pre-page-cite several of the [P]-tier primary documents above, cutting the
  hunt-it-down-yourself time on the free primaries significantly.

### [A14] Calvert, Peter. *The Falklands Crisis: The Rights and Wrongs*. Frances Pinter, 1982.
- **Provenance:** UK academic press, published in the immediate aftermath of the war.
  **Bias:** Written before 40+ years of retrospective legal scholarship could soften or harden
  either case — a distinct temporal pole from Goebel (1927) and the 2000s–2010s monographs.
  **Intent:** Contemporary legal-rights assessment, unfiltered by hindsight.

### [A15] Tatham, David (ed.). *The Dictionary of Falklands Biography*. 2008.
- **Provenance:** Reference work, Falklands-community compiled. **Bias:** Islander-sympathetic by
  construction. **Intent:** Fills the 1835–1884 settlement-era biographical gap alongside Cawkell [A7].

### [A16] Gough, Barry. *The Falkland Islands/Malvinas: The Contest for Empire in the South Atlantic*. Athlone, 1992. ⚠️ verify exact citation on order.
- **Provenance:** Naval/imperial historian. **Bias:** Imperial-contest framing, not overtly partisan.
  **Intent:** The wider 18th–19th c. European contest for South Atlantic strategic control — context
  for why Britain, Spain and France all cared about the islands before Argentina existed as a state.

### [A17] Caillet-Bois, Ricardo. *Una tierra argentina: las Islas Malvinas*. Academia Nacional de la Historia (ES).
- **Provenance:** The classic Argentine historiographical statement — the text the modern legal claim
  is substantially built on. **Bias:** Foundational-nationalist. **Intent:** State the historical case
  at the root, before Kohen's modern legal gloss on it.

### [A18] Destéfani, Laurio H. *The Malvinas, the South Georgias and the South Sandwich Islands: The Conflict with Britain*. 1982.
- **Provenance:** Argentine naval historian; published in **both English and Spanish**.
  **Bias:** Argentine-affirmative. **Intent:** A rare chance to compare a state's own case as
  presented to two different-language audiences simultaneously.

---

### [A19] Mira, Guillermo & Fernando Pedrosa (eds.). *Revisiting the Falklands–Malvinas Question: Transnational and Interdisciplinary Perspectives*. University of London Press, 2020. ⭐ RECENCY
- **Provenance:** University of London Press (open-access, `uolpress.co.uk`); edited collection
  bringing together **English, Spanish and Argentine specialists in one volume**.
- **Bias:** Deliberately transnational — the editorial premise is that neither national
  historiography is sufficient alone. Structurally the closest thing to a referee volume that exists.
- **Intent:** Interrogate the conflict across history, political science, sociology, film and
  cultural studies; explicitly examines how **nationalist identities obstruct resolution** — which
  speaks straight to candidate angle #6.
- **Why it matters here:** the ONLY post-2017 source in this corpus, and it was found in a single
  search. Also contains a bibliographic chapter (Anderson & Osuna Alarcón, "Information resources on
  the Falkland–Malvinas conflict") — a literature survey that will surface further recent work.
- **Access:** open access via University of London Press / Manifold. **£0.**

#### [A20] Trinidad, Jamie. *Self-Determination in Disputed Colonial Territories*. Cambridge University Press, 2018. (Cambridge Studies in International and Comparative Law #134, 292pp.) ⭐⭐ **BIGGEST MISS OF THE ORIGINAL LIST**
- **Provenance:** Cambridge UP flagship international-law series. Trinidad = Fellow of Wolfson
  College, Cambridge and of the **Lauterpacht Centre for International Law**; called to the Bar of
  England & Wales (2001). Reviewed in both *Leiden Journal of International Law* and the
  *British Yearbook of International Law* — i.e. a recognised major work, not a minor monograph.
- **⚠ Bias note (material):** Trinidad was also **called to the Bar of Gibraltar (2005)**. Gibraltar
  is the British-side analogue case and appears in the book. Not disqualifying — but his professional
  connection to one of the comparator territories must be stated when his framing is load-bearing.
- **Intent:** Analyse the relationship between **self-determination and territorial integrity** in hard
  decolonisation cases — the exact doctrinal collision at the heart of this dispute.
- **Why it matters:** covers **Western Sahara, Gibraltar, the Falkland Islands and Chagos in one
  volume** — precisely the comparative set this project needs, and precisely the set the creator
  already owns notebooks on. Ch. 2, "Territorial Integrity and the Limits of Self-Determination,"
  is the doctrinal spine of the video. **This single book does what four of my earlier picks were
  collectively gesturing at.**

#### [A21] Kohen, Marcelo G. — editorial/commentary on the Chagos Advisory Opinion and its bearing on the Malvinas (post-Feb 2019).
- **Provenance:** Kohen writing in the immediate wake of the ICJ opinion. **Bias:** Argentine-affirmative.
- **Why load-bearing:** he argues **UNGA Res. 2065 deliberately omitted any reference to
  self-determination**, and reads that omission as the UNGA acknowledging the principle did not apply
  to the islands' British population. That is the sharpest form of the Argentine legal argument, made
  by its most credentialed exponent, *after* the newest relevant precedent. Directly rebuttable by
  Trinidad [A20] — **an ideal on-screen referee pairing.**

#### [A22] Kataev — "Unravelling the Falkland Dispute: Is the Sea Lion…" *Hofstra Journal of International Business and Law*, Vol. 23.2 (2024).
- **Provenance:** US law journal — third-country. **Intent:** Ties the sovereignty question to the
  **Sea Lion oilfield**, i.e. the 2024-26 economic stakes.
- **Note:** the oil angle has **zero search demand** (see `PROJECT-STATUS.md`) — so this is *body
  evidence and modern-relevance grounding only*, never a hook. Newest source in the corpus.

#### [A23] Filmus, Daniel, Ricci & Rodríguez (eds.). *Malvinas, Antártida y Atlántico Sur: Geopolítica, Soberanía y Desarrollo en el Siglo XXI*. Editorial FEDUN, 2021 (**Spanish**).
- **Provenance:** Filmus was **Argentina's Secretary for Malvinas, Antarctica and South Atlantic
  Affairs** — this is effectively the contemporary Argentine *state* position in scholarly dress.
- **Bias:** Officially Argentine-affirmative; treat as a primary statement of policy, not neutral
  scholarship. **Intent:** 21st-century geopolitical framing incl. Antarctica linkage.
- **Why included:** the current official Argentine case, in Spanish, creator-verifiable — the
  living counterpart to Caillet-Bois [A17]'s foundational version.

#### [A24] "Self-Determination, the Chagos Advisory Opinion and the Chagossians." *International & Comparative Law Quarterly* (Cambridge), Dec 2019.
- **Provenance:** Peer-reviewed, Cambridge. **Intent:** How the Chagos opinion reshapes
  self-determination doctrine — the precedent-side reading that any Falklands argument must now clear.

#### [A25] "Falklands/Malvinas Islands, Distant Sovereignty, and Territorial Rights." Springer (handbook chapter).
- **Provenance:** Springer reference handbook. **Intent:** Territorial-rights theory applied to
  distant/non-contiguous sovereignty — the conceptual problem of governing land 8,000 miles away.

**Also flagged, not yet retrieved:** Mársico (2024) on the UN Decolonization Committee's current
posture toward negotiation. Chase before Stage B locks.
