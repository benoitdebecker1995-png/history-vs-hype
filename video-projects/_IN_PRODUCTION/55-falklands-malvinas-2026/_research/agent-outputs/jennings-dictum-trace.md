## ADJACENT EVIDENCE IN THIS PROJECT'S OWN EXHIBITS (same Jennings book, related but NOT proven-identical passage — do not conflate)
Two other scholars already in the project's source set cite Jennings 1963 on a closely related doctrinal point, which is the best available lead for WHERE in the book to look once it's obtained — but is not confirmed to be the same sentence:
- **Beck 1988** (`Beck-1988-International-Problem-FULLTEXT.txt`, fn.62, lines 2070-2072): cites Jennings pp.6, 20-23 in support of "no legal argument in support of this prescriptive right has been formally advanced by the United Kingdom Government, perhaps because of the manner in which prescription suggests an originally weak, even illegal, title." About prescription and weak/illegal original title — not explicitly about refusal-to-litigate.
- **Dolzer 1993** (`Dolzer-1993-Territorial-Status-CLEANED.txt`, fn.379, line 12002-12007, at Dolzer's own p.149-150): cites Jennings **p.22** for the proposition — Dolzer's own paraphrase — that a sovereign whose title is under challenge "must make available... further means at its disposal, such as an offer to negotiate or the resort to judicial settlement, in order to preserve its rights." Dolzer explicitly groups Jennings with Brownlie, Blum, D.H.N. Johnson, Pinto, and Suy as independently holding this position — i.e., Dolzer frames it as a **shared, general doctrine among multiple scholars**, not a Falklands-specific or idiosyncratic Jennings-only claim, and not case-specific in Jennings's own writing (the 1963 book predates the 1982 war by 19 years and is a general treatise).
- Beck's range (pp.6, 20-23) and Dolzer's page (p.22) overlap — both point into the same short stretch of Jennings's chapter on prescription. This is a strong lead, not a confirmation: it establishes that Jennings pp.20-23 IS where he discusses judicial-settlement/adjudication in relation to preserving/defeating title, which is thematically identical to the "presumption against validity... Court of Law" dictum. But Gustafson's quote is sourced to Moore's letter, not directly to the book, so the exact page and exact wording Moore used cannot be assumed to be p.22 without reading either Moore's letter or the book directly.

## THE DECISIVE QUESTION — working answer, NOT YET CONFIRMED AT THE PRIMARY TEXT
**Working hypothesis: this is a GENERAL statement of doctrine about title and adjudication, not Falklands-specific.** Basis:
1. Jennings's 1963 book is a general treatise (Melland Schill Lectures on territorial acquisition broadly) published 19 years before the 1982 Falklands War — it cannot itself be Falklands-specific in origin.
2. Dolzer 1993 independently frames the adjacent Jennings material (pp.20-23/p.22) as one instance of a doctrine shared across five different scholars (Brownlie, Blum, Johnson, Pinto, Suy, Jennings), which is the signature of a GENERAL principle, not a one-off remark about a specific dispute.
3. No source checked in this trace shows Jennings addressing the Falklands by name anywhere.

**This is a working hypothesis, not a verified finding** — it rests on inference from adjacent material and secondary framing, not on having read the actual sentence in context. It should NOT be presented on screen as confirmed until either Moore's letter or Jennings's book is read directly. If Moore's letter turns out to frame the quote as Jennings COMMENTING ON THE FALKLANDS specifically (Moore was writing in 1983, post-war, about the Falklands — so it's possible Moore selected/framed a general Jennings passage for Falklands application, which is different from Jennings himself writing about the Falklands), that changes the correct on-screen framing from "Jennings on international law" to "a general legal principle applied to the Falklands by [Moore/Gustafson]."
