## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Falkland Islands/Malvinas sovereignty dispute is one of the longest-running territorial conflicts in the modern world, pitting British continuous-administration arguments against Argentine successor-state claims dating to Spanish colonial rule. The standard narrative reduces it to the 1982 war — Argentina invaded, Britain sent a task force, Britain won. That framing buries the genuinely complex legal and diplomatic history underneath a military story. The channel's angle: both sides deploy legally shaky arguments and the dispute is actually unresolvable under current international law frameworks because self-determination and *uti possidetis juris* are in direct contradiction. The April 2026 Trump leaked memo — floating withdrawal of US Falklands support as leverage against the UK over Iran — has reignited the dispute to maximum news-hook potency, making this a near-perfect timing window.
