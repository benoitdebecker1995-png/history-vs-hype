## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In June 1947, Britain announced it would leave India in ten weeks — collapsing a June 1948 deadline by almost a full year. To divide a subcontinent of 400 million people along religious lines, the British government appointed Cyril Radcliffe, a London barrister who had never set foot in India and had zero experience drawing borders. He was given five weeks, outdated census data, and imprecise maps. The resulting Radcliffe Line — published two days after independence, when mass displacement had already begun — triggered one of the largest forced migrations in recorded history and created the Kashmir dispute that nearly produced a nuclear war in May 2025.

The standard narrative treats this as an unfortunate but inevitable tragedy. The underexplored angle: this was a logistics failure of deliberate design. Britain accelerated the timeline to escape liability for violence it knew was coming, Mountbatten almost certainly intervened in the final award for strategic reasons, and Radcliffe burned every working paper before leaving — making it impossible to reconstruct his reasoning. The channel's angle is **the mechanism**: not the tragedy, but the specific bureaucratic choices that produced it.

## STANDARD NARRATIVE (What competitors will say)

Every other video on this topic tells the same story in the same order:

1. Britain ruled India for 200 years; religious tensions between Hindus and Muslims built up
2. After WWII, Britain was exhausted and decided to leave
3. The partition was announced; Cyril Radcliffe drew a line; millions fled; ~1 million died
4. It was tragic, chaotic, and unavoidable given the communal violence
5. Kashmir became disputed because both countries claimed it
6. India and Pakistan have fought multiple wars since

**The implicit frame:** A tragic but understandable outcome of imperial withdrawal under difficult circumstances. Britain was incompetent but not malicious. The violence was a product of religious hatred, not British choices.

**What every competitor misses:**
- The timeline acceleration was a deliberate choice by Mountbatten to minimize British liability for violence, not a geopolitical necessity
- The 2-2 deadlock structure of the commission was foreseeable — it guaranteed Radcliffe would decide alone
- The Aug 8 draft vs. Aug 17 final document: there is documentary evidence the line was changed after Radcliffe showed it to Mountbatten
- Radcliffe's destruction of his papers was not grief — it was the removal of evidence
- The Gurdaspur decision didn't just give India road access to Kashmir; it created the specific conditions for every Kashmir war that followed
