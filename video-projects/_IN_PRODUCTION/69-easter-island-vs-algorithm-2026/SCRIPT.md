## OPEN

This summer, five different YouTube channels published the same video about Easter Island. Between them they have about one and a third million views. Each one claims that an artificial intelligence was handed every measurement ever taken on the island and asked what human researchers had missed.

Two of those videos disagree about the most basic fact on the island. One says the roads across Easter Island are exactly where the statues walked. The other says the roads are too narrow for a statue to have ever passed along them.

They cannot both be right. So I went and read what the research actually says.

And the real answer is better than either video. It is a story about people who were very good at something difficult, who were then destroyed by something that arrived from outside, and who have been blamed for it ever since.

## WHO ACTUALLY LIVED HERE

Easter Island — Rapa Nui — is one of the most remote inhabited places on earth. Polynesian settlers arrived around the year twelve hundred, and they did not arrive by accident. They crossed thousands of miles of open ocean deliberately.

We know something about how. Rapanui astronomy has around twenty-five recorded star names. Antares, which they called Te Hau Vaero, passes almost exactly overhead at this latitude, which makes it a marker for the island's position. Navigation on this island was real, and it was in the sky and in oral knowledge.

The island itself is difficult. Poor soil, no rivers, badly exposed to wind and salt.

So they engineered the agriculture. They covered ground with broken rock to hold moisture and release minerals — what archaeologists call lithic mulching. In twenty twenty-four, Dylan Davis at Columbia trained a classifier on satellite infrared imagery and mapped those gardens across the whole island. They cover about three-quarters of a square kilometre.

And they read the water. In twenty nineteen, a team led by Robert DiNapoli showed that the great stone platforms, the ahu, sit where fresh groundwater seeps out at the coast. I should say that study restricts its analysis to the eastern portion of the island, where the survey coverage is near-complete. That limit is in the paper, and it matters later.

They were also not as isolated as the story says. In twenty twenty-four, a team sequenced fifteen ancient Rapanui genomes and found roughly ten per cent Indigenous American ancestry, from contact around the thirteen hundreds — centuries before any European arrived.

None of that tells us how they moved the statues. That is the part everyone actually wants explained.

## THE STATUES WALKED

There are nearly a thousand statues. The largest one ever moved to a platform weighs about eighty-two tons and stands over thirty feet high. There is no metal, no wheel and no draft animal anywhere in this story.

The old explanation was log rollers and wooden sledges. That explanation is where the whole idea of a self-destroying society comes from, because moving hundreds of statues that way would need an enormous number of trees.

The islanders always said something else. Oral tradition says the statues walked, and there is a working song about an ancestor who knew how to make them walk.

That was treated as folklore for most of a century. It turns out to be the mechanically better answer.

Statues that are still standing on the roads, abandoned in transit, are shaped differently from statues that reached a platform. They lean forward, often by more than ten degrees, which puts the centre of mass at or past the front edge of the base. They have wide, D-shaped bases — on road statues the base is about as wide as the shoulders, while on platform statues the shoulders are wider.

And here is the detail that settles it. The narrow base on a finished statue was cut that way afterwards, once it had arrived, to make it sit properly and look right. The shape for travelling and the shape for standing are two different shapes.

A statue built like that cannot stand up on its own. But it can be rocked from side to side and walked forward on its own edges. In twenty twenty-five, Carl Lipo and Terry Hunt built a four-and-a-third-ton replica and walked it a hundred metres in forty minutes with eighteen people.

The roads fit that. They run about four and a half metres wide, they hold that width across changing ground, and excavation shows they are dished in cross-section, with a concave profile matching the curve of a statue's base — which reads as engineering to guide it.

Which brings us back to the video that says the roads are too narrow.

That claim has been checkable for seventy years. In the nineteen fifties, Thor Heyerdahl excavated around a fallen statue on one of these roads and reported the roadway was slightly wider than the statue's base. In two thousand, the geologist Charlie Love mapped five miles of road and found it consistently about eighteen feet across. In twenty twenty-five, Lipo and Hunt give four and a half metres.

Three researchers, seventy years apart, who disagreed with each other about almost everything, all measured the road as wider than the statue.

So we know how the statues moved. We still have the question of what happened to the people who moved them.

## THE HATS

Many of the statues carried a cylinder of red stone on the head, called a pukao. They come from one quarry, Puna Pau. The largest are around two metres across and weigh something like twelve tons, and they had to be put on top of a statue that was already standing.

In twenty eighteen a team including a physicist built three-dimensional models of fifty of them and of thirteen more still lying at the quarry. Their conclusion is that the pukao were rolled from the quarry and then levered up ramps and onto the statues by small groups of people.

What the hats meant is a different question, and here is how the people who have studied them longest answer it. In their own book, Hunt and Lipo write that we don't know for sure what the pukao represented, and that their best guess is a headdress of red feathers.

The video says the hats are an acoustic notation system, encoding two categories of navigational information in two resonant frequencies.

I searched the actual study for the words acoustic, resonance, frequency, sound and vibration. All five appear zero times, in a paper with a physicist on the author list.

The researchers who did the physics say "our best guess." The video says it is solved.

That still leaves the biggest claim about this island, which is not about statues at all. It is about collapse.

## THEY DID NOT DESTROY THEMSELVES

The story most people know is that the islanders cut down every tree, wrecked their own environment and collapsed into famine and war before Europeans ever arrived.

The most careful version of that argument is Jared Diamond's, in his book Collapse, in two thousand and five. And I want to be fair to it, because the version that spread is not the version he wrote.

Diamond gives the range of population estimates in the literature — a low of six thousand, a high of thirty thousand — before saying he finds the higher figures unsurprising. He calls his own comparison between the island and the Earth imperfect, in those words. And on the possibility that climate played a part, he writes that there is no evidence at present, though it may emerge in the future.

What travelled was the line about the man who cut down the last palm tree. The hedges did not travel with it.

Since then, three things have happened.

The genomes rule out the crash. The twenty twenty-four study states that it rejects a scenario involving a severe population bottleneck during the sixteen hundreds.

The population ceiling is far lower than the story needs. Davis's rock-garden mapping models a maximum of about three thousand nine hundred people, with a working figure closer to three thousand — which matches what the first European visitors actually counted. There was no population of fifteen thousand, so there was no boom to crash from.

And the climate evidence Diamond said might emerge did emerge. In twenty twenty-five, a rainfall reconstruction found a severe drought beginning around fifteen fifty and lasting about a century. They lived through it and changed how they organised themselves.

There is one more thing. The transport method that supposedly consumed the forests never existed. The statues walked.

So the collapse did happen. It just happened much later, and it came from somewhere else.

## WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

In eighteen sixty-two, Peruvian slave ships came to Rapa Nui.

The best reconstruction of what they took is Henry Maude's, in a book published by Stanford, which works through the trade ship by ship. He counts fourteen hundred and seven people taken from Easter Island — about thirty-four per cent of the population.

I should say that other reputable sources give a much higher figure, over two thousand. I'm using Maude's because he reconstructs it vessel by vessel, and because it is the lower number, and I would rather understate this than inflate it. Maude spends part of that book knocking down exaggerated stories about the island himself.

Among the people taken were the king, his son, the priests, and the learned men — the people who could read the rongorongo tablets. Nobody has been able to read them since. It gets presented as an ancient mystery. It has a date.

Fifteen people came back, and they brought smallpox, which killed about a thousand more. In two years the population of the island fell by something like fifty-seven per cent.

And then it kept going, which is the part usually left out. A French adventurer named Dutrou-Bornier took the island over and ran it as a sheep ranch. Five hundred and seventy-one Rapanui left — some with the missionaries, some to plantations in Tahiti. A hundred and seventy-five stayed.

In eighteen seventy-seven a visitor counted a hundred and ten people living on Easter Island.

So it is not one catastrophe. It is slave ships, then disease, then fifteen years of somebody else owning the place.

The statues were standing through all of it. They were not pulled down by a civilisation destroying itself before contact. They came down later, and Europeans pulled them down.

And the Rapanui are still there. Gina Pakarati is a co-author on two of the studies I have used in this video. The research correcting the story about these people includes the people themselves.

So that is what the island actually shows. Which leaves the question I started with — why the version with a million views tells it the other way round.

## WHY YOU HEARD THE WRONG VERSION

Everything in this video was found by named people between twenty nineteen and twenty twenty-five, published in journals you can open, and argued about in public.

The first of the two videos I mentioned gets almost all of that right. Its facts check out. But it credits the findings to an artificial intelligence, and the scientists disappear. Bárbara Sousa da Mota becomes "Barbara Susa Damota." Robert DiNapoli becomes "De Napoli." Search those names as the video says them and you find nothing. It also drops the eastern-portion limit I mentioned earlier, and claims the freshwater finding covered every platform on the island.

The second video invents its findings. And when I went looking for where they came from, I did not find a laboratory. I found a blog post from twenty thirteen by an anonymous surveyor, proposing that lines drawn between the platforms point at Pacific destinations. Its own author reports that forty-two per cent of those bearings landed on something, which means most of them did not. The video says every single one does.

I want to be careful here. I cannot prove that video took it from that blog. What I can say is that the claim is thirteen years old, it was never reviewed, and somewhere between then and now the failure rate disappeared.

That is the actual pattern, and it is older than artificial intelligence. A people who were exceptionally good at something, wrecked by something from outside, and then blamed for it. Each retelling drops what the last one was careful about. This is just the fastest version of it, and this time it removes the researchers too.

So if you want to check the next one yourself: does it name a researcher, an institution and a year? Can you find that name written the way it is said? And listen to how it handles the hard words. A quarry called Puna Pau should not turn into a man called Paul.

The point is not that I know more than the machine. It is that I have left you a trail you can walk back. This video is the trail, and everything in it is in the description.
