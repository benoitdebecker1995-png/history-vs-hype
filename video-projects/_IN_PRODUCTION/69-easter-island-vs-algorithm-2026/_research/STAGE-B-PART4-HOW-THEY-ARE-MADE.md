## 2. THE PIPELINE — what the industry openly sells

This is not secret. It is a marketed product category with courses, tool stacks and profit
calculators. The published workflow:

1. **Script** — ChatGPT (or similar) writes it from a topic prompt. ~$20/month.
2. **Voice** — the script is pasted into **ElevenLabs** or equivalent; audio comes back in about
   thirty seconds. ~$5/month.
3. **Visuals** — Midjourney, Leonardo, or a video generator (Sora, Veo, Kling), plus stock footage.
   $10–35/month.
4. **Assembly** — CapCut.
5. **Thumbnail and title** — generated the same way.

**The cost claim the industry advertises: production has gone from $50–200 per video to under $5, and
cadence from one a week to one a day.** History and documentary content is quoted at **$5–15 RPM**.

⚠ **SOURCE QUALITY WARNING — do not put a revenue figure on screen.** The RPM and cost numbers above
come from SEO marketing blogs selling the tooling, not from any audited source. They establish that
*this is a marketed business with advertised economics*; they cannot support "this channel earned
$X." **The honest on-screen version is the facts we measured ourselves** — 1.7 million views in 28
days, a sponsorship read, and an industry that advertises sub-$5 production — and let the viewer do
the arithmetic. If a revenue number is wanted, it needs a real source first.

## 3. THE SEAMS — what the assembly leaves behind

Four artefacts visible in the material we already hold.

### a) The spliced opener

The Silent Clue's transcript begins, before one word about Easter Island:

> *"Musk just pulled the curtain back on what looks like Grock 5 and XAI reportedly trained it with
> massive amounts of cursor programming data."*

An unrelated trending-tech blurb welded to the front of an archaeology documentary. That is a
pipeline reaching for a hook, not an editor making a choice.

### b) The franchise pitch — in both specimens

The Silent Clue closes: *"Subscribe because this pattern… is appearing at multiple ancient sites as
Grock works through the data. **The next one goes deeper.**"*
Specimen A closes: *"Easter Island isn't the only site where Grok is finding this exact pattern… The
pyramids were the same story… **That video is right here. Go watch it now.**"*

**Both end by pointing at the next unit of the same product.** The Silent Clue also states the
template outright: *"The process was the same one it ran at **Stonehenge**."*

### c) The names nobody has ever said out loud

Across the transcripts: **Bárbara Sousa da Mota → "Barbara Susa Damota."** **DiNapoli → "De
Napoli."** And in The Silent Clue: **Puna Pau → "Puna Paul."** Rongorongo → "Rango Rango." Pukao →
"puka," "pukau," "paca." Marquesas → "the Marces." Rapa Nui → "Rapanoui," "Rapaoui."

> The failure mode is consistent: **a system converting text to speech that has never heard the words
> spoken and does not know they are names.** "Puna Paul" is the clearest — a quarry becomes a man.

### d) The disclaimer that does no work

Specimen B's description carries, verbatim: *"AI-generated interpretations are not archaeological
evidence or established historical conclusions. The video distinguishes between documented research,
expert interpretations, and speculation."*

It is real, and it should be shown. Then show where it sits — below the fold, in the description —
while the narration's second sentence is *"The statues are not monuments, they are instruments, and
the island is not a settlement, it is a machine."*

### 🔴 HARD GATE BEFORE ANY OF §3c GOES ON SCREEN

**Every transcript we hold came from YouTube's automatic captions.** An auto-caption error is not a
mispronunciation. "Stonehinge," "volcanic tough" and possibly others may be the *caption engine*
mishearing correct audio.

**Listen to the actual audio and confirm each one before it is used.** This is exactly the discipline
the video is about, and getting it wrong here would be fatal. Confirm at minimum: "Puna Paul,"
"Barbara Susa Damota," "De Napoli," "Rango Rango." Log the timestamp for each.

## 4. THE PLATFORM CONTEXT — and why it is not a solved problem

**YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025**, then clarified it
again on **16 July 2026**, breaking it into three ineligible categories. The first is *generic,
repetitive or template-based content — videos with little variation, made easily via AI, CGI or
templates, easily replicable at scale.*

**Matt Halprin, YouTube's trust and safety chief**, verbatim:

> *"AI can actually allow people to make a lot of videos… Sometimes those videos are great… But that
> exact same new tool can allow you to make lots of videos really quickly that are very similar… and
> that's the stuff that we don't want to have in YPP."*

Note carefully what the policy does and does not say: **AI-assisted content remains monetisable if it
is unique, significantly transformed, and adds human value.** The rule is not "no AI." It is
"template at scale."

> **Which is the video's own position, arrived at independently.** Our line is *bounded tool vs
> invented oracle* — Dylan Davis's trained classifier versus a machine handed an island. YouTube's
> own policy draws the line in the same place. That is worth thirty seconds: **the standard already
> exists, and these videos are on the wrong side of it while running a VPN sponsorship.**

There is precedent for enforcement: YouTube demonetised the fake AI movie-trailer channels **Screen
Culture** and **KH Studio** once the formula was named publicly.

## 6. WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE VIDEO

**A new closing movement, and it is the payoff the format promised — the tell the viewer can use.**

Not "trust me, I'm human." Five checks anyone can run in a minute:

1. **Does it name a researcher, an institution and a year?** If a finding has no name attached, there
   is nothing to check.
2. **Search the name exactly as it is said.** "Barbara Susa Damota" returns nothing. **Bárbara Sousa
   da Mota** returns a *Nature* paper. A name you cannot find is the break in the chain.
3. **Check the channel's upload rate.** A 20-minute documentary every other day is not research.
4. **Check the channel's age.** Four months old, sixteen videos, 1.7 million views.
5. **Listen to how it says the hard words.** A quarry called Puna Pau should not become a man called
   Paul.

> **And the honest closing symmetry:** every one of those five checks is something *you* can do,
> which is the actual answer to "what is a human channel for." Not that I am smarter than the model.
> That I leave behind a trail you can walk back — and this video is the trail.

**Runtime consequence:** this is a genuine new movement, not a line. Combined with the chain
material in part 3, the ~15-minute exception is now clearly insufficient. **Creator decision
required.**
