## 1. THE WIDTH FIGURE — resolved, and they were never in conflict

Read off the papers directly (PyMuPDF extraction of both 2025 versions).

| Figure | What it actually measures | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **2.83 m** | The **overall width** of road moai 12-220-01 | Lipo & Hunt 2025, §1.4: *"The original statue stands 7.35 m tall and 2.83 m wide."* |
| **2.64 m × 2.10 m** | The **base footprint** of that same statue | Same paper, Figure 4 caption (SSRN preprint): *"The base measures 2.64 m × 2.10 m, with the center of mass located 139 cm behind the front edge."* |

Same statue, two measurements, both correct. Nothing to reconcile — the notebook had the base, the
narrative text has the maximum width.

### Which one goes on screen: **2.83 m**

Use the **larger** number. It is the most favourable figure to Specimen B's case, and the claim still
dies:

> **Road ≈ 4.5 m ÷ widest point of the statue 2.83 m = 1.59× wider.**

If the road is still 1.6 times wider than the widest part of the statue, we have beaten the strongest
available version of B's argument. That is the steelman discipline, and it makes the beat
unassailable. (Against the base footprint it would be 1.70×, which is a better number and a weaker
argument. Take the weaker number.)

### The supporting fact that makes the comparison fair

Road moai are not shaped like the statues on platforms. Lipo & Hunt:

> *"road statues exhibit a shoulder-to-base width ratio approaching 1.0, meaning the **base width
> nearly equals the shoulder width**"* — while platform statues *"show ratios consistently exceeding
> 1.0, with shoulders wider than bases."*

And the reason: *"This modification occurred **after transport**, when carvers reduced the base width
to achieve the aesthetic proportions desired for display."*

So on a road moai the base **is** effectively the widest part of the statue. Comparing road width to
base width is the right comparison, and B's own framing ("the road widths were measured against the
base dimensions") is the correct test. It just returns the opposite answer.

## 2. ⚡ A THIRD ROAD MEASUREMENT — and it comes from Heyerdahl

This was not in the file. `HuntLipo-2011-StatuesThatWalked.txt` p. 77–78, on Thor Heyerdahl's
excavation around a fallen moai on a prehistoric roadway. Heyerdahl's team found a densely packed
crushed-lava surface, and reported the roadway was:

> *"**slightly wider than the [moai] base** [and] was as firm and even as if a steamroller had
> prepared the ground."*

**That is three independent measurements, across three eras, by researchers who disagreed with each
other about almost everything:**

| Who | When | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Thor Heyerdahl** (excavation) | 1955–65 | roadway *"slightly wider than the moai base"* |
| **Charlie Love** (mapped ~5 miles of road) | 2000 | *"quite consistent, measuring about eighteen feet across"* ≈ 5.5 m |
| **Lipo & Hunt** (survey + 3D) | 2025 | *"ca. 4.5 meters"*, *"consistent dimensions across varied terrain"* |

Heyerdahl believed in horizontal transport and South American contact; Love is a field geologist;
Lipo and Hunt argue the statues walked. **Three rival positions, sixty years apart, all reporting the
road is wider than the base.**

> **This is the beat.** Specimen B's flagship discovery is not an open question the machine settled.
> It is a question that was answered by direct excavation in the 1950s, re-answered in 2000, and
> re-answered again in 2025 — and every answer says the opposite of what the video says.

Also worth keeping: B claims *"several of the roads are narrower"* — a claim about **variation**.
Both Love and Lipo & Hunt describe the width as **consistent**. The variation is asserted too.

### ⚠ The honest limit — do not overreach on camera

The 1.59× ratio is for **road moai**, and specifically for the statue that was 3D-scanned and
replicated. It is **not** a claim about every moai ever carved.

The largest statues are much bigger: **Paro**, the tallest ever moved to a platform, is *"about 82
tons and about 32 feet high"* (~9.8 m); a Tongariki statue reached ~86 tons; an unfinished quarry
statue would have been ~270 tons. **We do not have base widths for those**, and a proportionally
scaled 9.8 m statue would narrow the margin considerably.

Two things protect the beat anyway, and both should be said rather than hidden:

1. B's own test is road width against the statues that were **on the roads**. Those are the road
   moai, and that is what we measured.
2. Lipo & Hunt document that the giants did not travel: *"Of nine statues exceeding 8 m in length,
   six (67%) failed within 1.5 km of the quarry"*, and none went beyond 4.27 km, while smaller
   statues reached nearly 7.7 km.

**On-screen wording:** state the ratio for the statue that was measured and replicated, and say
plainly that the very largest statues are a different case which did not make it far from the quarry.
Do not say "no moai was ever wider than a road."

## 3. ⚡⚡ THE DATABASE PASS — B's biggest claim has a source, and it is a blog

Beat 4 was written as *"I could not find this, and here is where I looked."* **That is no longer the
right beat.** The claim was found. It is thirteen years old and self-published.

### What exists

**"Rapa Nui (Easter Island) — A Compass of the Pacific"** — `rapanuimoai.blogspot.com`, with a
companion post at `rapanui-easterisland.blogspot.com` dated **17 May 2013**.

- **Author:** profile shows **"Unknown."** Self-described as a *"certified professional surveyor &
  mapper"* with *"over 25 years experience."*
- **Peer review:** none. Blogger platform, self-published.
- **Method as stated:** NOAA navigational tools software, Miller Cylindrical Projection, UTM WGS 84
  (NAD 83). Bearings protracted from **Volcano Terevaka** as a central apex, taken **from midway
  between pairs of ahu**, and extended out to sea.
- **Named bearings — four:**
  - Ahu Vai Mata + Maikati Te Moa → **Hawai'i**
  - Maikati Te Moa + Te Peu → **New Zealand**
  - Tara Kai Ua + Nau Nau → **Isla del Sol, Lake Titicaca**
  - A Tanga + Papa Tekena → **Tikal, Yucatán**
- **Its own reported hit rate, verbatim:** *"**42% of the protracted bearings** pointed towards
  significant centers of culture of the time."*
- **Its own framing, verbatim:** *"The Ahu and Moai can be used as directional beacons much like the
  modern lighthouses are used today."*

### The three things that matter for the video

**1. The idea is not a 2026 machine discovery. It is a 2013 blog post.**
The video presents "the statues are navigational instruments" as what fell out when an AI was handed
every measurement ever taken on the island. The claim was on the open web, self-published, before
that.

**2. The 42% became 100%.**
The blog's author reports that **42%** of the bearings landed on something — which means **58% landed
on open ocean**. Specimen B says: *"every single one of them corresponded to a navigational bearing
toward a significant destination in the Polynesian world."*
**The failure rate did not survive.** That is the same operation as Video A dropping DiNapoli's scope
limit, and the same operation as the popular retelling dropping Diamond's hedges — but here it turns
a weak claim into an impossible one.

**3. The methods are not even the same, and B's cannot work.**
The blog constructs a bearing from **two ahu plus a volcano** — a line between landmarks. B says the
bearing is *"the exact direction each individual moai faced… extending those lines outward."*
**The moai face inland.** A line extended from the direction a moai faces runs into the middle of the
island, not to Hawai'i. B's stated method cannot produce ocean bearings at all.

### ⚠ Fairness — what we can and cannot say

**We cannot say Specimen B copied this blog.** We have a correspondence, not a derivation:

| | Blog (2013) | Specimen B (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Destinations | Hawai'i, New Zealand, Titicaca, Tikal | Hawai'i, Marquesas, Society Islands, New Zealand |
| Count | 4 named pairs, "42%" of bearings | **"11 distinct convergence points"** |
| Method | pairs of ahu + Terevaka as apex | direction each individual moai faces |
| Americas | central to the thesis | absent |

Two destinations match exactly. The Marquesas is the standard Ahu Akivi tourist story. **The "11
convergence points" figure appears in neither the blog nor anywhere else I searched.**

**On-screen wording:** *"This claim exists. Here it is — published in 2013 by an anonymous surveyor
on Blogspot, using pairs of platforms and a volcano, and reporting that 42% of its own bearings
landed on something. I can't tell you the video took it from here. I can tell you it is not a
discovery, and that somewhere between 2013 and 2026 the 58% that pointed at empty ocean disappeared."*

Handle the blog's author the way we handle Erik Kiley: a real person, doing real work by their own
lights, self-published and unreviewed. **Not the target.** The target is a video selling their
hypothesis as a machine's finding with the error rate deleted.

## 4. THE 0.4° CLAIM ALSO HAS A REAL ANCHOR — and it is one site, not an island

Same pattern, and this one strengthens the steelman.

**A genuine sub-degree alignment exists.** **Ahu Huri A Urenga** — read by **Mulloy (1973)** as a
real solar observatory — faces **winter solstice sunrise**, and is aligned to it *"with a precision
of within a single degree."* The platform is **twisted 20° relative to its own plaza** so the moai
faces the solstice sunrise even though the plaza does not. It is aligned with two neighbouring hills
and two smaller ahu.

**Ahu Akivi's** equinox alignment is repeatedly described as *"seen only at this location on the
island."* Roughly **20 ahu** are described as deliberately astronomically oriented — out of hundreds.

So: real, deliberate, sub-degree astronomy — **at named individual sites**, on a minority of
platforms, established by fieldwork over fifty years.

> **What B does:** takes a one-site, roughly-one-degree alignment and reports it as an island-wide
> average angular error of **0.4°** across the ahu system, then says *"you do not accidentally build
> 900 statues on platforms aligned to celestial events with sub-degree accuracy."*
> **Twenty became nine hundred. One site became an island.**

And the constraint that makes island-wide sub-degree precision impossible, from Edwards 2018 —
already in the file, now doing more work: the field's stated obstacle is *"degradation and site
location due to **errors in the official mapping of the island**."*
