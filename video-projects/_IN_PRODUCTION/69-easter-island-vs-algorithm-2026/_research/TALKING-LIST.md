## BEAT 2 · Who these people actually were · ~2:30

**Question:** *who lands on the most remote inhabited place on earth, on purpose?*

- Arrived around **1200**. Thousands of miles of open ocean, deliberately.
- Real Polynesian wayfinding: **25 recorded Rapanui star names**; **Antares — *Te Hau Vaero*** — the
  zenith star that fixes the island's latitude, passing at **89°19′** overhead; *Nga Vaka* recorded
  as important for navigation. `Edwards-2018`
- The island is hard. Poor soil, no rivers, exposed.
- **They engineered the agriculture.** Rock gardens — lithic mulching — **0.76 km²**, found by Dylan
  Davis at Columbia using a classifier trained on satellite infrared. `Davis-2024, Sci Adv`
- **They read the water.** The platforms sit on **freshwater seeps**. `DiNapoli-2019, PLoS ONE`
  ⚠ say the scope limit out loud: the study restricts to *"the eastern portion of the island."*
- **They were not isolated.** 15 ancient genomes show **~10% Indigenous American ancestry**, contact
  by roughly the 1300s — before any European. `MorenoMayar-2024, Nature`

**Land:** *these were not people barely hanging on. They were extremely good at this.*

## BEAT 3 · The statues walked · ~3:00

**Question:** *how do you move a 74-tonne statue with no wheels, no metal, no animals?*

- The old answer: log rollers and sledges — which is where "they cut down every tree" comes from.
- **The real answer: they walked them, upright.**
  - Forward lean often **over 10°**, putting the centre of mass at or past the front edge — it can't
    stand on its own, but it rocks forward.
  - Road statues have **wide D-shaped bases**: base-to-shoulder ratio ≈ **1.0**, against platform
    statues over 1.0.
  - **The detail that proves it:** the base was cut *narrower afterwards*, once the statue arrived,
    for display. The transport shape and the display shape are different shapes.
- **The experiment:** 4.35 tonnes, **18 people, 100 metres in 40 minutes.** `LipoHunt-2025`
- **The roads.** ~**4.5 m** wide, consistent across terrain, with **concave cross-sections matching
  the curve of a moai base** — engineered to guide it. Charlie Love mapped five miles in 2000:
  *"about eighteen feet across"* ≈ 5.5 m. And **Heyerdahl**, excavating in the 1950s and believing
  something completely different, reported the roadway *"slightly wider than the moai base."*
- **The oral tradition said this all along** — the walking song, the ancestor Tu'u Ko Iho who knew
  how to make the statues walk.

⚡ **STING (keep to ~20 s):** the AI video says the roads are too narrow. Three researchers, sixty
years apart, who agreed on almost nothing, all measured the road as wider than the statue.

**Land:** *the islanders told everyone the statues walked. It took a century for anyone to test it.*

## BEAT 4 · The red hats · ~1:30

**Question:** *and the hats?*

- **Pukao.** Red scoria, quarried from one place — **Puna Pau**. Up to 2 m across, around 12 tonnes.
- Rolled from the quarry and **parbuckled up ramps** onto statues already standing. About 15 people.
  `Hixon-2018, JAS` — 3D models of **50 pukao** plus 13 cylinders still at the quarry.
- **What they mean:** *"We don't know for sure what the pukao represented; our best guess is a
  headdress of red birds' feathers."* — Hunt & Lipo, in their own book.

⚡ **STING (~20 s):** the AI video says the hats are an acoustic notation system, two resonant
frequencies encoding two categories of navigational information. The actual pukao paper has **a
physicist on the author list** and contains the words *acoustic, resonance, frequency, sound* and
*vibration* **zero times**.

**Land:** *the real researchers say "our best guess." The machine says it's solved. That's backwards.*

## BEAT 5 · They did not destroy themselves · ~2:30

**Question:** *but they cut down all the trees and collapsed — everyone knows that.*

**Steelman Diamond properly first — he is the most careful link in the chain:**
- He gives the literature's range: *"a low of 6,000 to a high of 30,000"* before leaning high.
- He calls his own Earth-in-space metaphor ***"imperfect."***
- And on climate: *"we have no evidence at present, **though it may emerge in the future**."*
- **The popular version kept the last-palm-tree line and dropped every hedge.**

**Then what the last six years found:**
- **No bottleneck.** *"we reject a scenario involving a severe population bottleneck during the
  1600s."* `MorenoMayar-2024`
- **No 15,000.** Carrying capacity modelled at **3,901** at the top, ~3,000 working — which matches
  what early European visitors counted. `Davis-2024`
- **The drought was real.** A century of it from around **1550** — `Drought-2025, Comms Earth Env` —
  **exactly the evidence Diamond said might emerge, twenty years later.** They adapted; the birdman
  tradition comes out of that period.
- And the transport method that supposedly caused the deforestation **never existed.**

**Land:** *the collapse story needed them to be stupid. Every piece of it has come apart, and Diamond
himself left the door open for the piece that replaced it.*

## BEAT 6 · What actually happened to them · ~2:00 · **THE CORE — slow down here**

**Question:** *so if they didn't destroy themselves, what happened?*

- **1862. Peruvian slave ships.** Roughly **1,500 people taken — close to half the island.**
- Smallpox followed. **By the 1870s, about 110 people were left alive.** `Maude-1981, Stanford UP`
- **Among the people taken were the last who could read rongorongo.** That is why nobody can read it
  now. The script isn't a mystery. It's a bereavement.
- **The statues stood through all of it.** They fell later — Europeans toppled them.
- **The Rapanui are still there, and they are doing this research.** **Gina Pakarati** is a co-author
  on two of the studies in this video. Name her.

**Land:** *the catastrophe came from outside, by ship, and we spent a hundred and fifty years blaming
the victims for it.*

⚠ No sensory dwelling. Numbers and names, said plainly. Let it sit.

## BEAT 7 · Why you heard it from a machine that got it wrong · ~2:00

**Question:** *why is the version with a million views the wrong one?*

- Everything in this video was found by **named people between 2019 and 2025**, published, and you
  can open every paper.
- **One AI video got the facts right and deleted the people.** Bárbara Sousa da Mota became
  *"Barbara Susa Damota."* Search it as spoken — you get nothing. DiNapoli became *"De Napoli."*
  The facts survived; the way to check them didn't.
- **The other invented findings** — and they trace back to a **self-published blog from 2013** whose
  own author says **42% of his bearings work.** The video says all of them do.
- The pattern for three hundred years: **a people who were exceptionally good at something, wrecked
  from outside, and then blamed for it.** The newest version deletes them one more time — and this
  time it deletes the scientists too.

**The tell — short, three checks, not five:**
1. Does it name a researcher, an institution and a year?
2. Can you find the name **as it was said**?
3. How does it say the hard words? A quarry called **Puna Pau** should not become a man called Paul.

**Land:** *the point isn't that I'm smarter than the model. It's that I leave a trail you can walk
back. This video is the trail.*
