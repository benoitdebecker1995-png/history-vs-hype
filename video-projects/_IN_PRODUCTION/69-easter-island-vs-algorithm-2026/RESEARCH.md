## 4. 🔴 1862 AND AFTER — FOUR CORRECTIONS TO SCRIPT V1

**The primary authority is Maude, *Slavers in Paradise* (Stanford UP, 1981), now read directly.** He
reconstructs the trade ship by ship. Every figure below is off the page.

### CORRECTION 1 — the number taken is **1,407**, not "around fifteen hundred"

> *"the grand total of all islanders taken amounts to **1407**, or **34 per cent** of the estimated
> population of the island."* — Maude, PDF p. 204 area; Table 7 gives Easter Island: est. pop. 1862
> **4,126**, recruited **1,407**, remaining **2,719**, **34.10%**.

### CORRECTION 2 — it is about **a third**, not "close to half"

Maude, in his own summary: *"**nearly 35 per cent** of the population was taken to Peru."*
**"Close to half" is wrong and must come out of the script.**

### CORRECTION 3 — the smallpox toll and the real 1862–64 figure

> *"though fifteen returned this merely served to aggravate the crisis since they brought smallpox
> which killed off, at the best estimate which can be made, **a further 1000**: this meant that the
> **population decline between 1862 and 1864 was somewhere in the region of 57 per cent**."*
> — Maude, PDF p. 204

⚠ And Maude actively **corrects an inflated toll**: Métraux's claim that the epidemic "carried off
thousands" is *"clearly wide of the mark: there were not thousands on the island."*

### CORRECTION 4 — "about 110 alive by the 1870s" is right but **causally wrong as written**

The 110 figure is real and now sourced: the traveller **Pinard recorded 110 inhabitants in 1877**,
cited by **Grant McCall (1996, p. 29)** via `RapaNui-IndigenousPeoplesChile-Overview` (footnote 66),
which calls 1877 *"el nivel más bajo de población, con solo 110 habitantes."*

**But that low point is not the direct result of the 1862 raids.** Between them came **Dutrou-Bornier**,
the French adventurer who ran the island as a ranch 1872–1877, and a mass departure: **571 Rapanui
left — 328 with the missionaries, 243 to Brander's plantations in Tahiti.** Only **175** stayed under
Dutrou-Bornier.

> **The script must not compress 1862 → smallpox → 110.** It is slave raids, then smallpox, then
> fifteen years of dispossession and forced emigration. Four causes, not one. Saying so is more
> damning, not less — and getting it right is the entire point of this video.

### The rongorongo claim — **supported**, and here is the wording that is defensible

Two independent sources:

- `RapaNui-IndigenousPeoplesChile-Overview`: *"Entre los cautivos, se encontraban el ariki, su hijo,
  los sacerdotes y **los sabios de la isla, quienes sabían interpretar las tablas de madera kohau
  rongorongo**."* — among the captives were the king, his son, the priests, and the island's learned
  men, who knew how to read the rongorongo tablets.
- **Maude, PDF p. 204:** the traders took *"not only the king with many members of his family, but a
  considerable number of **learned men (maori)**."* ⚠ Note: `rongorongo` appears **zero times** in
  Maude's entire 280 pages — he says "learned men," not "tablet readers."

**Defensible on-screen wording:** *"Among the people taken were the king, the priests, and the
learned men — the people who could read the tablets."* **Do not say "the last people who could read
rongorongo"** — that is a stronger claim than either source makes, and decipherment loss was
cumulative, not instantaneous.

### ⚠ AN OPEN HISTORIOGRAPHICAL CONFLICT — do not hide it

The Chilean survey states that in seven months *"se 'reclutaron' cerca de **2.225** rapanui"* (citing
Foerster et al. 2013). **Maude gives 1,407.** Two reputable sources, 800 people apart.

**Recommendation:** use **Maude's 1,407** — it is the specialist monograph on the trade itself,
published by Stanford, reconstructed ship by ship, and it is the *lower* figure, so we are not
inflating an atrocity to make a point. **Say on screen that higher estimates exist and why we took
the lower one.** This is a thirty-second beat and it is the most on-brand moment available: the
careful historian correcting the inflated version, on the same island, in 1981 — Maude also demolishes
*"the fable that the island was used as a human corral."*
