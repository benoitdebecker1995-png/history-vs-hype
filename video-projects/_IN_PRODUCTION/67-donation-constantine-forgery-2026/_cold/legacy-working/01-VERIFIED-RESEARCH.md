### ⭐⭐⭐ C15 — STEUCO, SCOPED. The defence of the Donation was a real argument, and it fails for a reason worth showing.

**Source:** Michele Zanobini, "Agostino Steuco and the Debate on the Donation of Constantine,"
*Studies in Medieval and Renaissance History*, 3rd ser., vol. 13 (2016), pp. 177–191. **Citation
corrected here** — the file previously carried this without the author's name.

Agostino Steuco, Vatican librarian, answered Valla in 1547, 107 years after the treatise.

#### ⭐⭐ C15a — Steuco's case, and why it is not stupid

- He found **Greek versions of the *Constitutum*** — *Graecos vetustissimos codices* — and argued the
  Greek was older than the Latin Valla attacked. He says four copies sat in a "Palatine library":
  *"quatuor autem ante oculos palatina bibliotheca sugesserat cum haec scriberentur."*
- Therefore **the Latin is a translation**, and Valla's barbarisms are translation damage, not
  forgery — *librorum communis calamitas*, the common calamity of books.
- Therefore Valla erred in method: he *"looked for a form of linguistic and rhetorical refinement
  (*Romanitas*) in a text that was merely a translation."*

**[CORROBORATED]** · locators: Zanobini pp. 186–188

⛔ **THE STRUCTURAL THREAT, STATED PLAINLY.** Zanobini's verdict is that *"Steuco ultimately does not
dispute Valla's linguistic analysis, as he concludes all observations would be easily confuted by
looking at the Greek text,"* and that *"within the field of linguistic philology, Steuco goes a step
further than his opponent."* Zanobini attributes the difference to **Poliziano**, who between 1440
and 1547 *"revolutionized the criteria"* — i.e. Steuco is doing stemmatics (find the oldest witness)
against Valla's lexical test (test the words). **On the 1547 state of the art, Steuco's is the better
move.**

#### ⭐⭐⭐ C15b — and it took 382 years to settle. This is the payoff.

Steuco's stemma was **inverted**. Zanobini, following **Roberto Cessi (1929)**: *"It seems now
reasonable to suppose that the original text was first edited in Latin (A′), but soon translated into
Greek, the language of the eastern empire (β′)."* Steuco's "oldest" Greek codices are downstream of
the Latin, not upstream of it.

**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Zanobini p. 187 + stemma diagram, citing Cessi, *La donazione*, p. 89

**The beat:** Valla tested the words and was right. Steuco applied a *better* method and was wrong,
because he had the family tree upside down and no way to know it. Settling it required a stemma
nobody could build until 1929. **A better method with worse inputs loses to a cruder method with
better inputs — and the field needed four centuries to prove which was which.** That is a
how-history-works beat with no equivalent anywhere in the competitive field (`COMPETITOR-GAP-ANALYSIS.md` §5).

### ⭐⭐ C16 — CAMPOREALE, SCOPED. The "it's just rhetoric" objection dissolves — but not in our favour by default.

**Source:** Camporeale, *Christianity, Latinity, and Culture: Two Studies on Lorenzo Valla* (Brill),
extracted text. `sources_used` = Camporeale alone.

Camporeale reads the treatise as an ***oratio***, and the evidence is Valla's own:

- Valla to Giovanni Aurispa: ***"nihil magis oratorium scripsi"*** — *"I have written nothing more
  rhetorical."*
- Full classical architecture: *exordium · narratio · probatio · refutatio · peroratio*.
- ***Prosopopoeia*** — Valla writes speeches for Constantine, his kinsmen, the Roman Senate and Pope
  Sylvester.
- Declaimed *"as if in an assembly of kings and princes."*
- Grounded in *loquendi libertas* taken from Quintilian: *"no one who knows how to speak well can be
  considered a true orator unless he also dares to speak out."*

#### ⭐⭐ C16a — the passage that settles the objection

Camporeale does **not** treat the oration reading as a demotion. For Valla, rhetoric **is** the
critical discipline:

> *"**Rhetoric rises… to the level of a critical and interpretive discipline that consists in the
> philological and historical analysis of a specific language (logos/sermo); it places particular
> emphasis on how that language signifies and makes references with respect to a distinct reality
> made up of 'res, i.e., deeds, things that have been done (pragmata).'**"*

He calls this Valla's ***"science (scientia/episteme) of language,"*** working the *res–verba*
relation to prove a document false by demonstrating *"the falsifiability of the specific language
that expresses… that thing."*

**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Camporeale, § *Introduction to a Reinterpretation of the De falso credita donatione* · scoped, `sources_used` = Camporeale alone

**What this licenses and what it forbids.** ✅ The script may say Valla built an argument in which
the words are tested against the world they claim to describe — Camporeale's *vis verborum* → *vis
rerum* is that exact structure, and it is the C13a distinction in a Renaissance scholar's own terms.
⛔ The script may **not** call the treatise disinterested, scientific, or a piece of neutral
scholarship. Camporeale's Valla is doing ***theologia rhetorica*** aimed at the *"de-Constantinization"*
of the Roman church — exposing the *"antinomy of imperium and evangelium,"* charging that a pope
claiming temporal power acts as *"vicar of Caesar"* rather than *"vicar of Christ alone."* It is a
weapon and it is also a method. Saying only the second is the hagiographical anachronism of C14e.

### ⭐⭐⭐ C19 — VALLA'S BEST ON-SCREEN EXHIBIT ISN'T THE LATIN. **It's the coins.**

**Source:** Valla, *De falso credita*, Coleman 1922, facing pp. **70 (Latin) / 71 (English)**. Read
directly in the held text.

Valla has just argued that no pope ever behaved like a ruler of Rome. Then he produces physical
evidence:

> *"**Eoque numismata aurea… circumferuntur, non Graecis sed Latinis litteris inscripta, Constantini
> iam Christiani et deinceps cunctorum ferme Imperatorum, quorum multa penes me sunt cum hac
> plerumque subscriptione subter imaginem crucis, 'Concordia orbis.'**"*
>
> — *"And there are gold coins in circulation, inscribed not in Greek but in Latin letters, of
> Constantine after he became a Christian and of almost all the Emperors in succession — **many of
> which are in my own possession**, mostly with this legend beneath the image of the cross:
> **'Concordia orbis'** [the concord of the world]."*

Then the hinge:

> *"Qualia infinita reperirentur summorum pontificum, si umquam Romae imperassetis! **Quae nulla
> reperiuntur, neque aurea, neque argentea, neque ab aliquo visa memorantur.** Et tamen necesse erat
> illo tempore proprium habere numisma quisquis imperium Romae teneret; saltem sub imagine Salvatoris
> aut Petri."*
>
> — *"An infinite number of such would be found for the supreme pontiffs, if you had ever ruled Rome!
> **None are found — not gold, not silver, nor is any recorded as seen by anyone.** And yet whoever
> held power at Rome in that age necessarily had his own coinage; at the least, under the image of the
> Saviour or of Peter."*

**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Coleman pp. 70–71 · Latin + English read directly · corroborated by Levine p. 140

⭐ **Why this outranks *satrapes* as the lead exhibit.** *Satrapes* asks the viewer to trust a claim
about fourth-century Latin usage — the audience has to take our word for it. **The coin does not.**
Constantine's Christian-era gold survives in quantity, is photographable and public-domain, and the
argument is complete the moment both facts are on screen: *here is the emperor's coinage; there is no
pope's.* It is a **rigorous** argument from absence, not a lazy one — Valla first states the
governing principle (*whoever rules, mints*), then shows the positive class is abundant and the
negative class is empty. That is the structure ADR-0020 asks for.

And **Valla owned specimens.** *Quorum multa penes me sunt.* He is not citing a report; he is holding

#### The situation

**Seven years after Valla wrote**, on **16 June 1447**, the Venetian humanist **Lauro Quirini** sent

Valla aus dem Latein der Urkunde abgeleitet hatte,"* are void — every argument Valla drew from the

### ⭐⭐⭐ C34a — SANGIORGI: the Donation was never a gift. It was a *restitutio*.

Giovanni Antonio Sangiorgi (d. 1509), canon-law professor at Pavia, bishop of Alessandria (hence
*Alexandrinus*), cardinal 1493, in his commentary on the *Decretum Gratiani* **dedicated to Alexander
VI**. Setz: *"Die einflußreichste Stellungnahme von kanonistischer Seite."* He engages Valla
*"verhältnismäßig ausführlich… auch auf solche, die sich nicht juristisch oder theologisch entkräften
lassen"* — including arguments that cannot be defused juridically or theologically.

The whole case turns on one reclassification:

> *"quia potius dicitur agnoscere bonam fidem et **relaxare quod indebite per eum et antecessores suos
> erat occupatum**."* — *"he is rather said to be acknowledging good faith and **releasing what had
> been unlawfully occupied by him and his predecessors**."*

**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Setz p. 130 (Alexandrinus, *Commentaria super decretum*, 1511, f. 219v)

**Constantine wasn't giving Rome away — he was handing back what Rome had wrongly taken.** Setz:
*"Mit dieser Auffassung der Schenkung als restitutio ist die Position zur Widerlegung Vallas
bezeichnet."* It answers the *alienatio damnosa* objection in advance, and it is a genuinely
sophisticated legal move — **not a smear, an argument.**

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ C34b — AND THE DEVICE UNDERNEATH IT: every absence becomes evidence of suppression

Three of Sangiorgi's eight answers are the same move. Quotable as a set:

| Valla's objection | Sangiorgi's answer |
|---|---|
| The histories say nothing | *"imperatores qui Constantino successerunt **curarunt, ut ista occultarentur et abolerentur, nec de eis mentio in historiis fieret**"* — the successor emperors saw to it that it was concealed and abolished, and no mention made in the histories |
| Eusebius contradicts it | *"nec mirandum esset, si **ab emulis romane ecclesie Eusebii liber in hoc corruptus et depravatus fuisset**"* — no wonder, if rivals of the Roman church corrupted Eusebius's book |
| The Latin is barbarous | *"**nemo enim dubitet, quod si ecclesia romana donationem istam fingere voluisset elegantius et cum maiori solemnitate omnia composuisset**"* — had the Roman church wanted to fake it, it would have written it more elegantly |

**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Setz pp. 132–133 (ff. 220v, 221r)

⭐ **The third one converts the forgery's own clumsiness into proof of authenticity** — the argument
cannot be falsified by any evidence, including the evidence against it. **This is the cleanest

## P11.1b — MECHANISM-WORD LOCK: **ANACHRONISM** · confidence HIGH · locked 2026-08-02

**Method:** open, non-leading query to *Forgers and Critics* asking what detection vocabulary the
book actually uses, with no candidate word supplied (the fabrication guard forbids feeding a word in

**Grafton's working vocabulary, in his words:** *external evidence / external signs of validity* ·
*internal, or textual* evidence · **anachronisms** · **factual anachronism** · *higher criticism* ·
*philological analysis*.

**Why "anachronism" wins:**

1. **It is native and primary.** *"Already, then, some writers possessed an aptitude for **detecting
   anachronisms** — an aptitude essential to anyone trying either to create a plausible document or to
   expose one."*
2. **Grafton subdivides it himself** — ***"the concept of factual anachronism was clearly familiar to
   Porphyry."*** A named sub-category implies siblings, which is exactly the structure this video
   needs: one mechanism, two species.
3. **It covers both men.** C13a established that Cusa uses anachronism too (Clement writing to a James
   already dead; a bishop/priest distinction Jerome dates later). "Anachronism" is the **genus** the
   video explains; Cusa-vs-Valla is the **two species** inside it. The word does not flatten the
   thesis, it frames it.
4. **A stranger parses it cold.** One word, no Latin, already in general English, and the thing it
   names is visually showable.

⚠ **Two honesty constraints on how it may be used.**

- Grafton names ***factual*** anachronism. He does **not** use "linguistic anachronism" as a paired
  term in anything returned — the language side he handles under *philological analysis* and *internal
  evidence*. **The script may coin the pairing but must not attribute the phrase to Grafton.**

### ⛔ The framing in C39 is WRONG and must not be scripted

C39 said Steuco *"printed the evidence that destroys his own argument and did not notice"* and called

1. **f. 38r** — ***before he prints a word of Greek***, Steuco states in Latin that the text was copied

   that the cardinal ordered **his own interpreter** to copy the decree from the book at Constantinople.
2. **f. 42v** — he prints the colophon **in full, including the parts Delph's footnote truncates**:

3. **f. 44v** — he **converts the Byzantine date himself, in print**: *"Secundum vero computum

   Latin reckoning, **the year 1220, more or less.** He then reckons the interval and identifies the
   scribe as Nicholas Hydruntinus, whose Filioque disputation *with that same Benedict* sat in Cardinal
   Grimani's library in Venice.

**His actual argument:** the age of the **source book** — Balsamon's *Nomocanon*, c. 1170s — is what
matters, not the age of the copy. **That is a real argument and it must be met, not mocked.**
