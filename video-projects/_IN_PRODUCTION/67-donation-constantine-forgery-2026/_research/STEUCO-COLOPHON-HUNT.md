## 2. ⚠ The framing in the spawn brief needs revising — read this before scripting

The brief said Steuco *"printed the evidence that destroys his own argument and did not notice."*
**That is not what the book shows.** Steuco noticed, printed it in Latin *before* the Greek, and
argued around it. Three separate places:

1. **f. 38r (PDF p. 80)** — *before* he prints a word of Greek, Steuco tells the reader in Latin that
   the text was copied in the time of Innocent III, when Cardinal Benedict was sent to the Emperor
   and the Greeks, and that the cardinal ordered his own interpreter to copy the decree out of the
   book at Constantinople.
2. **f. 42v (PDF p. 89)** — he prints the Greek colophon in full, *including* the parts Delph's
   footnote omits: Cardinal Benedict, the legate of Pope Innocent III, and the December date.
3. **f. 44v (PDF p. 93)** — he **converts the Byzantine date into the Latin calendar himself**, in
   print, in the first sentence on the page: *"Secundú uero cóputú latinorú ab incarnatione dñi,
   Anno millesimo, ducentesimo, ac uigesimo plus minus"* — "by the Latin reckoning from the
   Incarnation, **the year 1220, more or less**." He then reckons the elapsed interval, and
   identifies the scribe as Nicholas Hydruntinus, whose Greek/Latin disputation on the Filioque
   *with that same Benedict* is in Cardinal Domenico Grimani's library at Sant'Antonio in Venice.

So the honest exhibit is not "he blundered." It is: **Steuco's own evidence dates his supposedly
independent Greek witness to a copy made to order for a Roman cardinal's legation — and Steuco
printed that, translated the Greek date into "about 1220" himself, and argued that the age of the
*source book* (Balsamon's Nomocanon, c. 1170s) was what mattered.** That is a stronger, more
defensible on-screen beat than "he didn't notice," and it survives a hostile fact-check. See §6.

Notes on Delph's note:

- **Delph truncates.** He stops at *ὡς προείρηται* and closes the quotation. Steuco's colophon
  keeps going for four more lines — Benedict, Innocent III, Nicholas as interpreter, the year and
  the month. Everything that makes this a *dated* exhibit is in the part Delph left out.
- Delph's supporting body text (same page) adds: the codex "could be found in the library in the
  monastery of Sant'Antonio in Venice."

### ⚠ Delph's foliation is NOT our copy's foliation
Delph's ff. 12v–14v correspond to **ff. 38r–44v** in the BSB Bologna 1530 copy we hold.
Offset ≈ 25 folios; the proportional position in the book is nearly identical (≈47–52% through),
which is what you expect from a **denser folio-format reprint** — almost certainly the
*Opera omnia* (Venice, Nicolini, 1591), vol. III, which is also the edition Delph uses for
*Contra Laurentium Vallam* (he cites it at ff. 211v–226r, foliation impossible in the Lyon 1547
octavo we hold as A17). **Delph never states his edition.** If you cite the folio on screen, cite
**f. 42v of the Bologna 1530**, which is what the image shows — not Delph's f. 14r-v.

## 4. The colophon as Steuco actually printed it (f. 42v)

Transcribed from the rendered page image (`crop_colophon_2x.png`), Steuco's accentuation and
lower-case initials preserved; `[ ]` = editorial expansion of a printer's abbreviation:

> **Huc usq[ue] edictu[m]. In cuius fine haec habebantur.**
> Τέλος τῆς διαθήκης κ[αὶ] διατάξεως τοῦ μεγάλου κ[αὶ] ἐν ἁγίοις κωνσταντίνου, ἥτις ἐγράφη
> παρὰ νικολάου τοῦ ὑδρουντίνου, ἐν τῷ μεγάλῳ παλατίῳ ἐν κωνσταντίνου πόλει ἀπὸ τῆς
> βίβλου, ὡς προείρηται, τῇ προτροπῇ τοῦ κυρίου **Βενεδίκτου** τοῦ καρδιναλίου, κ[αὶ] τοῦ
> τοποτηρητοῦ **Ἰννοκεντίου τοῦ τρίτου πάπα Ῥώμης**, ἣν π̅ τότε ὁ προρηθεὶς
> νικόλαος, **ἐξελληνιστὴς** κ[αὶ] ἑρμηνεὺς αὐτοῦ τοῦ καρδιναλίου, κ[αὶ] τῶν γραικῶν ἐν ταῖς
> τῶν περὶ δογμάτων διαλέξεσι, ἔτ[ους] **ςψιε** ἰνδικτιῶνος, κ[αὶ] **μηνὸς δεκεμβρίου**.
> **Quorum omniu[m] latine haec est sententia.** [→ Steuco's Latin translation of the decree
> resumes, running over onto f. 43r]

Working English:

> Here the edict ends. At its end stood the following. "End of the testament and ordinance of the
> great and saintly Constantine, which was written by **Nicholas of Otranto**, in the Great Palace
> in the city of Constantine, from the book, as has been said, **at the instance of the lord
> cardinal Benedict, and of the representative of Pope Innocent III of Rome** — for which the
> aforesaid Nicholas was then the **Greek-translator and interpreter** for the cardinal himself
> and for the Greeks in the disputations concerning doctrine — **in the year 6715, of the
> indiction, and in the month of December**."

Transcription notes (checked at 4×–7× on the page image, not taken from OCR):
- **ἐξελληνιστής** is certain — the word breaks `ἐξελ|ληνιστὴς` across the line and both halves are
  clean. It is the load-bearing word: Nicholas was not a neutral Greek scribe who happened to own
  the book, he was *the cardinal's own translator*, doing the copying as part of the legation's
  business.
- `ἣν π̅` (line 7, after *Ῥώμης*) is the one genuinely unclear reading — a π with a macron,
  a compendium. It is a connective, not a name or a date. Do not put that clause on screen.
- Steuco's Greek font prints **terminal sigma as σ, never ς** (`Τέλοσ`, `τῆσ`, `διαθήκησ`,
  `ἥτισ`). That is the 1530 compositor, not an error — do not let anyone "fix" it in the
  on-screen card if you reproduce the type faithfully.

**On the date.** ςψιε = 6715 *anno mundi* (Byzantine reckoning). AM 6715 runs 1 Sept 1206 –
31 Aug 1207, so **December of AM 6715 = December 1206**. ✅ This confirms the December 1206 dating
independently, off the printed page.
⚠ *One caveat, stated honestly:* the compositor has spaced the numeral as `ς  ψ  ιε`, and the word
`ἰνδικτιῶνος` follows with **no indiction numeral of its own**. The two defensible readings are
(a) year = ςψιε (6715), indiction numeral omitted → Dec 1206 [preferred: matches Benedict's
legation, 1205–07]; (b) year = ςψ, indiction = ιε (15) → internally inconsistent (AM 6700 falls in
indiction 10), so reject. Do not put an indiction number on screen.

## 5. Two supporting pages worth pulling as exhibits

**f. 38r = PDF p. 80 (0-idx 79)** — Steuco's own Latin provenance statement, immediately before the
Greek begins. This is Delph's n. 19 + n. 20. Transcribed from the page image, not OCR:

> …qua hoc tépore utitur eé largitú. **Proferá uero decretú ipsius Cóstantini, Graece scriptú,
> exscriptú ex libro, qui erat in Bibliotheca palatina, Cóstantinopoli**, in quo libro oía iura
> ecclesiastica cótinebá[n]tur, et ordines ac gradus ecclesiarú erát digesti. **Descriptú est ex
> ipso libro, t[em]p[or]e Innocentii tertii, quo tépore, Benedictus Cardinalis missus est ad
> Imperatoré, et Graecos, ut de fide Catholica dissereret. Qui cú eét Constantinopoli iussit suo
> interpraeti ex eo libro ipsum exscriberet.** Hoc aút mó habet. Ἐκ τοῦ νομοκάνονος τῆς βίβλου,
> τῆς συνωψισθείσης ἐκ προτροπῆς τοῦ μακαριωτάτου πατριάρχου κωνσταντινουπόλεως κυρίου μιχαὴλ, κ[αὶ] τοῦ…

Printed marginal sidenotes on that page: *Libraria palatina Constant.*, *Innocentius 3*.
The page also carries an early reader's **manuscript annotations** — "Constantini Imp: testamentum
pro Sylvestro eo P[a]p[a]" across the head, "Vallae malitia" beside the Valla paragraph, and
"Constantini Decretum atq[ue] Donatio" at the foot. A 16th-c. reader marking exactly this passage
is itself a nice B-roll detail.

**f. 44v = PDF p. 93 (0-idx 92)** — Steuco converts the date and names his scribe. Transcribed from
the page image:

> **Secundú uero cóputú latinorú ab incarnatione dñi, Anno millesimo, ducentesimo, ac uigesimo
> plus minus.** Quare ab eo tépore, quo decretum exscriptum est, fluxerunt anni trecéti, ac uiginti
> quatuor ac paulo plures. Agitur enim nunc annus Millesimus, quingentesimus uigesimus octauus, cú
> haec scriberétur, eius qui scribebat trigesimo primo. **Huius Nicolai Hydruntini** habetur in
> bibliotheca D. D[omi]nici Grimani, quae in Coenobio canonicorum regularium Venetiis, apud aedes
> sancti Antonii erecta é, disputatio de p[ro]cessu sp[irit]us sancti ex filio, graece et latinae
> ab eo descripta, **qua graeci cum ipso Benedicto disseruerunt**, ut ibidem meminit ipse Nicolaus.
> Habetur autem hoc decretum etiam in praefata bibliotheca, **ad nos autem ex Apulia allatú é, ex
> libro uetustissimo exscriptum.**

Printed sidenote: *Decreti enarratio*. Early reader's marginalia: "Anno chr[ist]i 1220", "1528",
"Autoris 31".

Four things this page gives you for free:
- **Steuco read the Greek date and converted it himself: "about 1220."** He is off by ~14 years
  (the correct conversion of ςψιε is 1206/07) but he is openly in the early 13th century.
- He is writing in **1528**, aged **31** — published Bologna 1530. A datable authorial moment.
- ⚠ *Internal inconsistency in Steuco, worth knowing before someone else finds it:* he says
  "1220 more or less" and then "324 years and a little more" have elapsed since 1528 — which lands
  on 1204, not 1220. Steuco's own arithmetic does not close. Do not put the "324 years" figure on
  screen unless you address the discrepancy; the "1220 plus minus" sentence is the clean one.
- Steuco's copy of the decree "was brought to us **from Apulia**, copied from a very ancient book"
  — Apulia being exactly where Nicholas of Otranto's abbey of Casole was. Steuco's own chain of
  custody points back at Otranto.

## 6. Recommended on-screen beat (replaces "he didn't notice")

Three cards, in this order:

1. **f. 38r** — Steuco announcing his ancient independent Greek witness, with his own Latin
   admission that it was copied *tempore Innocentii tertii* for Cardinal Benedict's legation.
2. **f. 42v** (the colophon) — Νικολάου τοῦ Ὑδρουντίνου / Βενεδίκτου τοῦ καρδιναλίου /
   Ἰννοκεντίου τοῦ τρίτου / μηνὸς δεκεμβρίου, ςψιε → **December 1206**.
3. **f. 44v** — Steuco's own conversion, *"Anno millesimo, ducentesimo, ac vigesimo plus minus"* —
   he read it as about 1220 — and the Grimani-library disputation with "that same Benedict."

The line to write to: Steuco's independent Greek witness turns out to have been **commissioned by
the papacy's own envoy**, in the Great Palace, by an interpreter working *for* the cardinal — and
Steuco published all of that himself. His defence was that the *book* being copied from was old.
That is a real argument, and it deserves to be met rather than mocked.
