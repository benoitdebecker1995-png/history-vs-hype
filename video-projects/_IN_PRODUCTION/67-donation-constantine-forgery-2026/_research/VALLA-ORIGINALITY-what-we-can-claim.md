## 0. VERDICT UP FRONT

**The thesis survives, and it comes out of this pass stronger and narrower. Nothing here kills it.**
Option A′ — *Cusa proved it false with the registry; Valla proved it false with the sentence* — is
supported by all four sources, and by Grafton in 2018 in as many words. But three claims the project
could have drifted into must now be struck, and one of them was already half-drafted.

**What survives (ESTABLISHED):**
- Grafton, 2018: **"Valla's conclusions about the form and language of the document remain largely
  unshaken."** (*Forgers and Critics*, Afterword, printed p. 130). The steelman concedes the load-bearing point.
- Levine, the referee, calls Valla's philological attack **"systematic philological criticism"** and
  **"the coup de grâce"** (p. 142).
- Fubini's dependence thesis attacks the parts of Valla the project **already conceded to Cusa** (§§1–4)
  and does not touch §5, the linguistic movement.

**What must be struck (three):**
1. ❌ **"Valla invented the method of internal criticism."** Grafton kills it flat: *"More than a
   millennium before Valla and Casaubon, pagan and Christian scholars had created what remain the
   standard weapons against forgery"* (*Defenders*, p. 166). Julius Africanus ran style-difference,
   language-of-composition and content-anachronism arguments against the Susanna episode c. AD 250.
   **This is a bigger threat than Poliziano and the project had not flagged it.**
2. ❌ **"Valla founded systematic philology."** Grafton gives that to **Poliziano** — but see §1.3,
   because the domain is *manuscript genealogy*, not forgery detection, and the distinction is clean
   enough to hold on screen.
3. ⚠ **"The *palea* argument is Valla's."** Fubini (p. 82) says Valla took it from Cusa **and
   misunderstood it** — reading a technical canon-law term as a man's name. This directly damages the
   ⭐ exhibit starred in `RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md`. See §2.3; the exhibit is salvageable but only if
   reframed, and the reframe is *better* than the original.

**One flag resolved:** §C6's `[FLAG: ATTRIBUTION UNVERIFIED]` **can be lifted.** Valla makes the
vocabulary-dating argument in his own words, in the Latin already on disk. Verbatim in §5.

## 1. GRAFTON — the steelman, and what he actually says

### 1.1 The Poliziano claim is real, but it is NOT in *Forgers and Critics*

**Direct check, stated per ADR-0020.** I searched the full extracted text of *Forgers and Critics*
for `Poliziano`, `Politian`, `Politianus`, `Ambrogini`, `Miscellanea`.

- **`Poliziano`: exactly 1 occurrence in the whole book** — and it is in **Ann Blair's 2019 Foreword**
  (printed p. x), describing Grafton's *Scaliger* volumes. Not Grafton's voice, not about the Donation.
- **`Politian` / `Politianus` / `Ambrogini`: 0 occurrences.**
- **The index has no Poliziano entry at all.** Its Valla entry reads, in full: `Valla, Lorenzo, 28-30`.

**ESTABLISHED: the "Grafton gives systematic philology to Poliziano, not Valla" threat is
misattributed to the wrong book.** It belongs to *Defenders of the Text* (1991) and the Scaliger
volumes. `RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md` §"the real threat" and `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` §5.2 both cite it to
*Forgers and Critics* 1990. **Fix that citation upstream.**

### 1.2 What *Forgers and Critics* says about Valla and the Donation — and it is favourable

Valla appears at printed **pp. 28–30** (main text) and **pp. 130–131** (2018 Afterword). That is all.

**p. 28–30**, on the Renaissance demolition of forgeries:
> "One of the first triumphs of the new philology of the humanists was, of course, Lorenzo Valla's
> detailed demonstration that the Donation of Constantine contained profuse errors of fact and
> phrasing that proved its medieval origin beyond doubt."

Note three things the script can use and one it cannot. Grafton says **"errors of fact *and phrasing*"**
— he credits the linguistic half. He says **"beyond doubt."** He says **"one of the first triumphs"** —
not *the* first, and not *the* founding.

**Afterword, p. 130** (Grafton writing in August 2018, printed p. 138):
> "No demonstration of forgery is more famous, none captures the imagination of students and readers
> more powerfully, than Lorenzo Valla's demolition of the Donation of Constantine… **Valla's
> conclusions about the form and language of the document remain largely unshaken.**"

**This is the single most useful sentence in the four sources for the video's thesis**, and it is from
the hostile witness. It is a 2018 verdict, from the historian of forgery, on exactly the half of Valla
the video is about.

**But Grafton immediately complicates it, and the script should carry the complication:**
- Bowersock argues Valla was *also* right to remove Constantine from the building of Old St Peter's
  (Constantius II built it) — **and that Baronius forged evidence to prove Valla wrong** (p. 130, n. 2).
  ⚠ *Baronius, forging.* That is a live, filmable, on-thesis fact if it can be run down separately.
- Noel Lenski (*Constantine and the Cities*, 2016, pp. 182–83) argues Constantine **did** build it, on
  the evidence of a mosaic inscription on the triumphal arch **"known to Valla but not cited by him"**
  (p. 130, n. 3).
- Grafton's own criticism of Valla, p. 130: *"It does seem curious that Valla never addressed one
  salient point"* — the *Liber Pontificalis*, which itemises Constantine's gifts to the church. Valla's
  contemporaries took it as genuine and **"he did not address its contents directly"** (p. 131).
- p. 131: **"A final evaluation proves elusive"**, given that specialists still offer "powerful and
  contrasting interpretations." His note 5 lists exactly Camporeale 1996, Fubini 1996, Ginzburg 1999,
  Delph 1996 — the same spread `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` §5.2 assembled independently. That is a good sign.

### 1.3 What *Defenders of the Text* says — this is where the Poliziano claim lives

Grafton's own account of his discovery (printed p. 7):
> "Poliziano's work had marked a watershed in the history of classical scholarship: **a break between
> an older, rhetorical style of humanism and a newer, technical philology.**"

And the strong claims, in his own voice:
- **p. 55:** *"He was the first to compare and evaluate sources in a historical way — that is, in the
  way still employed today."*
- **p. 64:** *"Poliziano's genealogical method went far beyond the isolated insights of Lamola and
  Merula. In fact, he arrived at the same set of principles with which modern scholars still set about
  editing classical texts. … **No previous scholar had even come close to formulating the set of
  critical principles that Poliziano considered to be generally valid.**"*

**"No previous scholar" includes Valla** (d. 1457; Poliziano 1454–94). So the threat is real.

**But the domain is different, and the difference is defensible on screen.** Poliziano's achievement,
as Grafton describes it, is **stemmatics**: *eliminatio codicum descriptorum* — proving one surviving
manuscript is the parent of the rest, so the copies can be discarded. That answers *"which copy of a
genuine text do I trust?"* It is not forgery detection. Grafton himself lists them as **parallel and
distinct** achievements (p. 27):

> "Petrarch's exemplary work on assembling and correcting the text of Livy, **Valla's historical
> scrutiny of the Donation of Constantine** (and of the Latin language as a whole, which he saw as the
> fullest deposit of and truest key to Roman history), Poliziano's devising of the principle of
> *eliminatio codicum descriptorum*, and Erasmus's application of the principle of *lectio difficilior
> potior* — these triumphs of humanist philology…"

**Four names, four distinct instruments, listed at equal rank, Valla's named for the Donation.** That
is Grafton's own framing and the script may lean on it.

⚠ **The uncomfortable line the script should not pretend it hasn't read** (*Defenders*, p. 8). Grafton
is complaining about historians who do precisely what a YouTube video is tempted to do:
> "modern historians' attention was claimed by the few individuals like Valla, Erasmus, and Bodin, who
> came to **quotable general conclusions**… **Only broad and polemical theses — not the long and
> difficult journeys by which they were arrived at — seemed to matter.**"

The honest response is not to hide it: the video's answer is that it *is* about the journey — the
mechanism, sentence by sentence — which is the channel's whole HOW-over-WHY lane. But do not claim
Valla founded a discipline; that is exactly the move Grafton is naming.

### 1.4 Systematic, or brilliant-but-ad-hoc? Grafton's answer: **ad hoc, and systematisation came later**

This is the direct answer to the question as posed.

- *Forgers and Critics*, **p. 102**: the humanists of the early Renaissance "tested and rejected many
  forgeries"; the mid-sixteenth-century theorists — Melchior Cano, Jean Bodin — "devised more
  thoroughgoing solutions… **Accepting the humanists' isolated but valid insights**, Cano and Bodin
  tried to fuse them into an art of choosing and reading authorities about the past. **They provided
  not empirical case studies but universally applicable rules for evaluating sources.**"
- **p. 106**, Grafton in his own voice: these rules "certainly mark an effort to replace the
  **empirical, case-by-case practices of the early humanists** with a general theory."

**"Isolated but valid insights." "Empirical, case-by-case."** That is Grafton's characterisation of
Valla's generation, and it is the qualification the script has to absorb.

⚠ **But Grafton then demolishes the systematisers too**, and the script gains from saying so.
*Forgers and Critics* pp. 104–107: Cano's and Bodin's "modern" rules descend from **Annius of Viterbo —
a forger** — who inserted rules of source-evaluation into his own fakes, and Annius took them from
Josephus's *Contra Apionem*. Grafton, p. 118: *"the efforts of other scholars to locate in the
methodological speculations of the sixteenth century the rise of a new and effective method of source
criticism are surely wrong."* So Grafton denies the founding to Valla **and** to the people he says
outranked Valla on system. **There is no founder in Grafton's account. That is the actual finding, and
it is more interesting than a founder.**

### 1.5 The forgery↔criticism thesis — the genuine complication, and the video's best modern hook

This is the whole subject of *Forgers and Critics* and the script must have read it.

- **p. 5:** forgery has "stimulated vital innovations in the technical methods of scholars."
- **p. 6:** "**Forger and critic have been entangled through time like Laocoon and his serpents**; the
  changing nature of their continuous struggle forms a central theme in the development of historical
  and philological scholarship."
- **p. 125:** "Forgery and criticism also share a fundamental limitation. **The critic cannot escape
  time and place any more than the forger can.** … that is why at least some of his rejections of texts
  will be rejected in their turn."
- **p. 126:** criticism is "inevitably fallible in its conclusions and **deeply indebted to forgery for
  its methods.** It is often undertaken for partial and unscholarly motives."
- **Afterword, p. 129:** "In forgery and criticism… history moves in cycles. **The robbers were ahead
  of the cops in antiquity, and they still are.** Forgers still devise new ways to create texts that
  look authentic. Critics still stumble after, hunting for anachronisms and contradictions."

⚠ **This is a complication for a video celebrating detection — and it is also the strongest modern
bridge available.** "The robbers were ahead of the cops in antiquity, and they still are" is a 2018
sentence about forgery detection that maps onto synthetic media without a word of strain, from a
Princeton historian, on the record.

**Crucially, Grafton does not collapse the distinction, and the script must not either** (p. 126–127):
> "But it seeks not to protect but to expose… **The exercise of criticism is a sign of health and
> virtue in a civilization; the prevalence of forgery is a sign of illness and vice.**"

And the honesty rule, p. 127: *"We cannot carry on that tradition if we refuse to recognize how much it
owes to — and how often it has been implicated in — the activities of its criminal sibling."*

⚠ **The buried grenade — and it is the sharpest thing in the four sources.** *Defenders*, pp. 162–167.
Grafton sets up the received story ("in the ability to detect the corrupt and the spurious, the
humanists created a critical art without a literary precedent. **So we are told**," p. 162), then:
> "Yet this story is far too clear to be correct." (p. 163)
> "**More than a millennium before Valla and Casaubon, pagan and Christian scholars had created what
> remain the standard weapons against forgery and were vigorously debating how to apply them in
> practice.**" (p. 166)

His exhibit is **Julius Africanus to Origen, c. AD 250** (p. 165), on the Susanna episode in Daniel —
"a little masterpiece of the higher criticism":
1. the style of the episode differs from the rest of the book;
2. a book containing **Greek puns cannot be a translation from a Hebrew original** — "exactly as
   Casaubon would 1350 years later";
3. the content does not fit the historical situation it claims (Jews in Babylonian captivity could not
   have passed a death sentence on the king's wife);
4. literary inconsistency — Daniel prophesies by visions and dreams elsewhere, never by inspiration.

**That is substantially Valla's toolkit, twelve hundred years earlier.** Also in Grafton's inventory:
Galen on the Hippocratic corpus, Porphyry against the *Zoroaster* text and against Daniel, the
Aristotelian commentators, Diogenes Laertius.

**⚠ The one surviving distinction, and the script must state it precisely or not at all.** Africanus,
Porphyry and Galen are arguing about **literary and scriptural authorship**. Grafton's account of
**documentary/legal forgery** is different: in the Middle Ages canon lawyers *did* become "specialists
in detecting fakes," but by rules for "verifying the verbal form, physical appearance, and seals" —
**"standards chiefly of an external kind"** (*Forgers and Critics*, pp. 24–25). Nowhere in either book
does Grafton name anyone before Valla who convicted a **legal instrument** out of its **internal
Latinity**. That is the narrow ground left, and it is enough.

⚠ Stated per ADR-0020: I searched both Grafton volumes for `Cusa`, `Cusanus`, `Constitutum`,
`satrap`. **Grafton never discusses Nicholas of Cusa on the Donation.** *Forgers and Critics*: `Cusa`
0 hits, `Cusanus` 0 hits. *Defenders*: the sole `Nicholas of Cusa` hit is p. 64, about a **Plautus
manuscript Cusa brought to Rome in 1429** — irrelevant to the Donation. **Grafton is silent on the
priority question, not supportive of it.** He cannot be cited either way on Cusa.

## 2. FUBINI — what he actually claims, on what evidence

### 2.1 The claim, verbatim (p. 82)

This is stronger than "knew of it" and weaker than the project feared. Fubini:

> "Now the principal problem for the evaluation and understanding of the attack of Valla on the
> Donation of Constantine rests in our need to grasp its relation to the treatise of Cusanus, *De
> concordantia catholica*… Often doubted, or proposed only doubtfully, by Valla scholars, **such a
> direct relationship can, in my view, find ample demonstration — and for Cusanus's entire treatise,
> not just for the specific part regarding the Donation of Constantine.** In other words Valla's
> knowledge of the *De concordantia catholica* in 1440 **might have been the very reason** for his
> writing on the Donation of Constantine. Actually, Valla makes use of its arguments for his own
> polemical purposes, and in so doing **he figures as a rival to Cusanus himself.**"

**Parse it precisely — the two halves have different strengths:**

| Fubini's claim | Strength | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Valla **knew and used** *De concordantia catholica* | Asserted as demonstrable ("ample demonstration"), with four textual parallels adduced | **CONTESTED but well-evidenced.** Fubini is the specialist and he shows his working. |
| Knowing it **caused** Valla to write | "**might have been**" — Fubini's own hedge | **SPECULATION, by his own wording.** Do not report this as his finding. |

### 2.2 The evidence — four parallels, and the fourth is the load-bearing one

Fubini says the full evidence is in **Fubini 1992** (Macerata, 385–431, specifically 403–16) and that
here he discusses "only the most meaningful and relevant of the similarities" (p. 82, n. 8).

**⚠ This resolves `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` §C4's open question: the 1992 and 1996 items are the same
argument in two venues.** The 1996 JHI paper is the English précis of the 1992 Italian one. **The
project does not need to chase the Italian 1992 volume to state Fubini's position honestly** — though
it would still be needed to *test* it.

1. **The *palea* passage** (p. 82, n. 9). Cusa, *DCC* III.2.300: *"Unde qui postea addidit, pro palea
   illam confictam scripturam posuit."* Valla (Setz 95 = Coleman 75): *"Nonnulli eum, qui hoc capitulum
   adiecit, aiunt vocatum Paleam."* Fubini: "one notes in the *nonnulli* an actual citation of Cusanus."
2. **The "I have read all the histories" parallel** (n. 10). Cusa III.2.295: *"Relegi omnes quas potui
   historias, Augustini, Ambrosii ac aliorum opuscula peritissimorum… et nullam invenio concordantiam
   ad ea quae de illa donatione leguntur"*; Valla (Setz 89 = Coleman 67): *"Evolvantur omnes Latinae
   Greciaeque historiae, citentur ceteri auctores…"* Fubini: "the concepts and terms are in practice
   the same, and owe much more to Cusanus than to Valla, who adduces only Eutropius."
3. **The "Legend of Sylvester" / apocrypha exchange** (p. 83, n. 11) — which Fubini calls **"the most
   important correspondence between the two texts."** Cusa III.2.304 asks who would not believe Jerome
   over an anonymous writing "called apocryphal precisely because the author is not known"; Valla
   answers, *"Ego vero, ut ingenue feram sententiam, gesta Silvestri **nego** esse apocrypha… sed falsa
   et indigna quae legantur existimo"* (Setz 152 = Coleman 153). Fubini reads Valla's *Ego vero* as a
   direct rebuttal of that sentence.
4. **⭐ The "Cephas" etymology** (n. 10). Cusa *DCC* **II.34.252** vs Valla (Setz 153 = Coleman 155),
   both arguing *Cephas* is not Greek but Hebrew-or-rather-Syriac, **and both carrying the same
   hesitation** (Cusa's "*ut quidam dicunt*", Valla's "*seu potius*"). **This is Fubini's strongest
   evidence because it lies outside Cusa's Donation section entirely** — a shared argument in Book II
   cannot be explained by both men reading the same canon-law sources about the Donation. It is what
   licenses his claim to dependence "for Cusanus's entire treatise."

**Assessment: this is real philological evidence, not assertion.** Four parallels, three with paired
Latin, one structurally decisive. It should be treated as the leading specialist position, not as a
suspicion. It remains CONTESTED only in that no one has published a refutation the project has seen —
and note that Fubini is arguing against **Setz**, the standard monograph, which is also on disk (A5).

### 2.3 ⚠ WHAT THIS DOES TO THE *PALEA* EXHIBIT — read this before the script uses it

`RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md` stars ***palea*** as "⭐ THE EXHIBIT THIS FOUND." **Fubini damages it twice
over** (p. 82):

> "Most important in this regard is the demonstration that originally the supposed privilege had not
> been included by Gratian in the *Decretum* but rather placed among the so-called *paleae*, or
> additional interpolations of his pupil Paucapalea. **But here Valla misunderstands Cusanus's text,
> reading 'Palea' as the name of author of the fraudulent interpolation.**"

So on Fubini's reading: (a) the argument is **Cusa's**, not Valla's; and (b) Valla **got it wrong** —
*palea* is a technical term for an interpolation into Gratian (from Paucapalea), and Valla turned it
into a person's name. Coleman's text does hedge it as *"Some say* that the man who added this chapter
was called Palea" — and Fubini reads that *nonnulli* as the tell that Valla is quoting Cusa.

**Consequence, and it is not fatal — the reframe is better than the original.** Do **not** put on
screen "the medieval Church's own lawyers wrote *straw* in the margin and Valla caught it." Do put on
screen the honest version, which is more on-thesis: **the Donation was not in the original *Decretum*;
the canon lawyers had already filed it as an interpolation; Cusa found that; Valla repeated it and
garbled the term into a man's name — because canon law was not his subject.** Fubini says exactly that
in the same paragraph: *"From Cusanus, Valla extracts the substance of his arguments from canon law —
**a subject in which he had no first-hand knowledge**."*

That sentence is a gift. It draws the line the video is already drawing, from the hostile witness:
**where Valla is working in Cusa's domain he is derivative and sloppy; the Latin is the domain where
he is neither.**

### 2.4 What survives of "Valla did something new" if he worked from Cusa: **all of it**

Fubini's dependence claim is confined to the **canon-law and documentary-silence arguments** — which
are precisely Valla's §§1–4, the movements `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` **already assigned to Cusa**. Fubini
nowhere claims Valla got the linguistic argument from Cusa, and he could not: the project has verified
across all 859,314 characters of Sigmund's translation (§C0) that Cusa makes no linguistic argument.

**Fubini's own account of what makes Valla different is a *stronger* version of the video's thesis,
not a weaker one** (pp. 84, 86):
> "That which for Cusanus… was a distinction between 'apocryphal' and 'approved' texts, was instead for
> Valla a **direct opposition between 'true' and 'false.' This was clearly not a matter of vocabulary.**"
> "The distinction between 'true' and 'false' was instead a kind of decision that was **strictly
> individual and rational, independent of institutions and of hierarchy.**"
> "Valla… through the **unheard-of accusation of falsehood**, had turned the denunciation of the
> 'authenticity' of the *Constitutum* into a **truly revolutionary instrument.**"

**Cusa's category is institutional — *apocryphal* means the Church has not received it. Valla's is
evidential — *false* means it is not what it says it is, and anyone with the text can check.** That is
the registry-versus-sentence distinction restated in the idiom of the man who says Valla copied Cusa.
It is the most quotable thing in the paper for the video's actual argument.

### 2.5 Two incidental finds from Fubini worth keeping

- **A contemporary reader identified Valla's linguistic argument as the distinctive thing, in the
  fifteenth century** (p. 85). A Bologna law professor, **Andrea Barbazza**, "was struck by Valla's
  **linguistic investigation**, which he thought a *subtilis inductio*, useful for whoever wished to
  proceed to the denial of the 'validity' of the *Constitutum*." Barbazza consulted an unnamed senior
  prelate, who replied: *"Vos layci estis decepti, nihilominus ex quo Deus tolerat talem donationem,
  multo ergo fortius nos illam tolerare debemus."* — "You laymen have been deceived. Nonetheless, since
  God tolerates such a donation, we ourselves have all the more reason to tolerate it." Source given as
  **Maffei, *La Donazione di Costantino nei giuristi medievali* (Milan, 1969), 316f.** ⚠ Fubini dates
  Maffei 1969; the project's acquisition list has 1964 Giuffrè. Resolve the edition before citing.
  **This is a filmable, dated, first-generation reaction and it is on-thesis: the Church's answer to
  the proof was not a rebuttal.**
- **Composition vs circulation.** Fubini dates the treatise to 1440 but says it "first circulated" in
  **1443** (p. 80), evidenced by Filippo Maria Visconti's echo of it in a diplomatic overture to
  Florence at the end of 1443. Useful precision for the script: **written 1440, in circulation 1443.**

## 3. LEVINE — the referee, and he is closer to Grafton than expected

### 3.1 Does he address Cusa? Yes — but in one footnote, and it does not decide priority

Levine, p. 118, on the received story: *"Others had long suspected it and, on one occasion at least,
carefully examined and rejected it."* His **note 1** identifies that occasion as **"Nicholas of Cusa in
his *De Concordantia Catholica* (1432–1435), Book III, chap. ii."**

So Levine (1973) knows Cusa, places him **before** Pecock and Valla, and credits him with having
"carefully examined and rejected" the Donation. He does **not** discuss whether Valla read Cusa, and he
does not compare their methods. ⚠ Note his dating: **1432–1435**, a range, against the project's flat
"1433." Script-safe form: *"in the early 1430s, at the Council of Basel"* — or keep 1433 and be ready
to defend it from Rojas Donat & de Toro.

### 3.2 On independence — Levine asserts it, and the grounding is thinner than it looks

**This answers `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` §9 open question 3 directly.**

- p. 119: *"the co-incidence of the criticism of Pecock and Valla was **merely fortuitous**."*
- p. 119, **n. 3** — the entire evidential basis: *"There is, for example, **no reason to suppose** that
  Pecock had read Valla as H. Maynard Smith alleges in his *Pre-Reformation England* (London, 1938),
  p. 422."*

**⚠ That is a rebuttal of a specific allegation, not a demonstration of independence.** Levine's real
argument is indirect and structural — pp. 119, 133, 135: the two men "inhabited essentially different
intellectual worlds"; Pecock had "the same ignorance of Latin and Greek literature," and "inability to
read any Greek at all"; his education was "impossible to obtain in fifteenth-century England." The
independence follows from incapacity, not from documents.

**Status: Pecock's independence from Valla is ESTABLISHED-by-inference, not demonstrated.** It is
sound inference — Pecock could not have used Valla's method had he read it — but the script should say
"independently" rather than "provably independently," and must not extend it to Valla-and-Cusa, on
which Levine is silent. He does **not** simply inherit it from Babington: Babington appears in Levine's
notes only for Pecock's life and for the *Repressor* text.

### 3.3 On method — Levine's verdict, and it CONTESTS Grafton

The money passage, **p. 142**:
> "But it is Valla's 'grammatical' argument that is in the end **the most significant and persuasive
> and provides the *coup de grâce* to the Donation. He has gone back to the texts and subjected them
> to a systematic philological criticism.** By considering the language of his sources and by setting
> them into their contemporary context, he has recaptured their meaning and intention at least
> sufficiently to declare the Donation conclusively anachronistic. **Renaissance grammar, it turns
> out, is more efficacious for the solution of a historical problem than scholastic logic**, and the
> humanist much the superior historian to his rival the professional theologian."

⚠ **Levine uses the word "systematic" of exactly the thing Grafton calls "isolated insights" and
"empirical, case-by-case."** That is a genuine specialist disagreement and the script may not paper
over it. **CONTESTED — Levine (1973) vs Grafton (1990/91), and both are on disk.**

**But Levine himself narrows it in the next two pages, and the narrowing dissolves most of the
conflict:**
- p. 142: *"Yet once again there was more to be done and Valla does not do it… The Donation is not what
  it purports to be; what is it then? When was it written and why? It is easy to see why Valla stops
  short. **He is not writing history; he is writing polemic.**"*
- p. 143: *"**It was a very long time before historians would use the techniques of humanist philology
  systematically to reconstruct the past.**"*
- p. 143, closing: *"**how it came to pass that Valla's technique was transformed from philology and
  polemic into a systematic and constructive historiographical method is another and still not very
  well known story. In any case, and of this we may be sure, it did not occur until long after the
  exposure of the Donation of Constantine.**"*

**Reconciled: Levine says the *attack on this document* is systematic; the *discipline* was not built
until much later. Grafton says the same thing from the other end.** The two specialists are describing
one fact from two directions, and the script can state it without choosing sides.

Also from Levine, p. 119: *"it is **Pecock** with his syllogistic logic who proves the more alien to
modernity, while **Valla**, with his new philological method, though promising much for the future,
**only begins to point the way**."* And p. 143: *"of the two, it is clear that **we stand closer to
Valla**; and it is to renaissance philology, rather than to medieval logic, that the future of
historical method belonged."*

### 3.4 Levine's independent corroboration of a correction the project already made

**p. 143, n. 62**, citing Kelley's *Foundations of Modern Historical Scholarship*:
> "Valla was thus disinclined to attempt either to date or to discuss the Donation in any way except
> **simply to show its non-classical character**."

**That confirms `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` V1 and `01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` §C6 from a second, independent
direction: Valla showed the Latin was not classical; he did not date it.** The century-dating is
modern. Levine, **n. 60**, names who actually did it: **Scheffer-Boichorst (1889)**, "through close
examination of the style, vocabulary, and ideas," placing the forgery in the papal chancery of Stephen
II or Paul I, **A.D. 752–767, "a conclusion generally held today."**

⚠ **That is a clean, honest, four-century-long arc for the video: Valla proved the Latin was wrong for
Constantine's century (1440); it took until 1889 for the same method, refined, to say *which* century.**
Same instrument, better calibration. That is mechanism-forward and it is true.

### 3.5 ⭐ Levine's best gift to the script: Valla cited a forgery to expose a forgery

**p. 140, n. 55**, on Valla's use of Pope Melchiades as a witness that Constantine was Christian before
Sylvester: *"Here Valla succumbs to a forgery every bit as obvious as the Donation; cf. Coleman 73 n."*

**This is the single most on-thesis intellectual-honesty beat available to the video.** It is Grafton's
entanglement thesis instantiated in the video's own hero, it costs nothing, and it is the difference
between this video and the shelf. Verify against Coleman p. 73 n. before filming.

Also worth carrying: Levine, p. 141, notes Valla's evidence is **not only linguistic** — he adduces
"the gold coins of Constantine minted after his conversion with inscriptions in Latin (not Greek)" and
asks *"Where are the coins for the Popes?"* — plus inscriptions. **Numismatic and epigraphic evidence,
in 1440, filmable.** And p. 141, the standing caveat: *"The Declamation… was written by Valla to
support the King of Naples in a territorial dispute with the Pope."* Döllinger's dismissal is in n. 61:
"an artistic production, an eloquent declamation, [rather] than a calm historical investigation" — and
Döllinger "even preferred Pecock."

## 4. THE VERDICT — the sentence the script can defend

### ✅ THE DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE

> **"Cusa proved the document false from the record. Valla was the first to convict it out of its own
> Latin — and that is the test we still run."**

**All four sources carry this.** Grafton concedes the linguistic conclusions stand (FC p. 130) and
credits Valla with "errors of fact *and phrasing*" that proved medieval origin "beyond doubt" (FC
p. 30), listing the Donation scrutiny as one of four distinct triumphs of humanist philology (DOT
p. 27). Levine calls it the *coup de grâce* and "systematic philological criticism" (p. 142). Fubini
concedes the "unheard-of accusation of falsehood" and the true/false-versus-apocryphal break (pp. 84,
86). Cusa's silence on language is verified across the whole of Sigmund (§C0).

**Note the two words doing the work: "the document," and "Latin."** The claim is about **this
instrument**, not about the invention of a method — which is what makes it survive Grafton.

### ⚠ SAFE VARIANTS, in descending strength

- ✅ *"His contemporaries proved it with the archive. He proved it with the sentence."* — carries.
- ✅ *"He didn't need the archive. The document convicted itself."* — carries, and is the mechanism.
- ✅ *"Cusa was first, by seven years, and he was right. Valla did something different."* — carries;
  Sigmund's editorial note (§C0) states it, Levine's n. 1 concedes it, Coleman's introduction concedes it.

### ❌ THE OVERCLAIMS — each with the source that kills it

| Overclaim | Killed by |
|---|---|
| "Valla invented historical criticism / source criticism." | Grafton, *Defenders* p. 166 — the standard weapons predate him by a millennium. |
| "Valla founded systematic philology." | Grafton, *Defenders* pp. 55, 64 — that is Poliziano, on his own axis; and *Forgers and Critics* pp. 102, 106 — the early humanists worked case-by-case. |
| "Valla was the first to prove the Donation false." | Cusa, 1433. Sigmund; Levine n. 1; Coleman's introduction. |
| "Valla was the first to use internal evidence against a forgery." | Julius Africanus, c. AD 250 (Grafton, *Defenders* pp. 165–66). |
| "Valla dated the Latin to the eighth century." | Levine n. 62 via Kelley; Coleman's apparatus is the source of the dating. Scheffer-Boichorst 1889 did it. |
| "Nobody had questioned it for 700 years." | Otto III's chancery, c. 1001 — already struck. |
| "The *palea* argument is Valla's discovery." | Fubini p. 82 — it is Cusa's, and Valla misread the term. |

### What choosing the defensible sentence costs, plainly

It costs the **founder narrative** — no "the man who invented fact-checking," no origin myth. The
video cannot say Valla began anything; it can only say he was first to do **this** to **this document**,
with an instrument that had existed for literary texts and had never been turned on a legal one.

**What it buys is larger.** It buys a video that survives its own comment section, because every
correction a viewer can bring — *Cusa was first*, *Africanus did it in antiquity*, *Poliziano was the
real philologist*, *Valla was a paid polemicist for the King of Naples*, *Valla cited a forgery
himself* — is already **in** the video, sourced, on screen. That is the channel's actual product. The
founder story would get one of those corrections in the first hour.

⚠ **One structural requirement, unchanged from `CUSA-VS-VALLA…md` §6.3 and now better evidenced.** The
script must state once, clearly, that (a) Cusa was first and Valla probably read him, and (b) Valla was
being paid to write it. Two sentences. Fubini and Levine both supply the wording.

## 5. ⚠ THE §C6 FLAG — RESOLVED. Valla makes the argument himself.

`01-VERIFIED-RESEARCH.md` §C6 flags that the list of datable tells is Coleman's apparatus, not
demonstrably Valla's argument, and asks whether any source shows **Valla himself** making the
vocabulary argument.

**Yes. Two sources, and the primary text on disk confirms it. The `[FLAG: ATTRIBUTION UNVERIFIED]` can
be lifted for the general argument and for three specific words.**

### 5.1 Levine attributes it to Valla, quoting him, with locators (pp. 140–141)

Levine reads the philological movement item by item and quotes Valla's own words. On **satrap**
(p. 140): *"At once we hear of imperial 'satraps'. Whoever heard them mentioned in the councils of the
Romans? Valla can remember neither Roman nor provincial 'satraps' in any of his reading."* On the
**tiara** (p. 141), quoting Valla directly: *"How great barbarousness is there in these words: who ever
heard this word Phrigium used in the Latin tongue?"*

And the dating inference, **in Valla's own voice**, Levine p. 141 quoting him:
> *"doth not the very barbarousness of the manner of speaking witness openly: that this carol hath been
> feigned not in the time of Constantine but in a time more late?"*

⚠ **Provenance caution.** Levine states (n. 53) that he uses the **1534 William Marshall English
translation** of Hutten's edition, *not* Coleman. **Do not put Levine's English on a quote card as
Valla's words.** Use the Latin, or Coleman, and cite Levine for the attribution.

### 5.2 ⭐ The sentence in Valla's own Latin — located in the file already on disk

Searched `Valla-DeFalsoCredita-Coleman-1922-YaleUP-LatinEnglish.txt` directly. Latin side, at Coleman's
English page marker **[121]** (i.e. Coleman English **pp. 120–121**):

> *"Quid? **Illa loquendi barbaries nonne testatur non saeculo Constantini, sed posteriori, cantilenam
> hanc esse confictam?** 'Decernimus quod … uti debeant' pro eo quod est decernimus ut utantur: sic
> nunc barbari homines vulgo loquuntur et scribunt…"*

Coleman's facing English, same page:
> **"What? Does not that barbarous way of talking show that the rigmarole was composed, not in the age
> of Constantine, but later;** 'decernimus quod uti debeant' for the correct form 'decernimus ut
> utantur'? Boors commonly speak and write that way now…"

**That is Valla stating the inference explicitly: barbarous language ⇒ composed after Constantine's
age — and grounding it on a specific syntactic tell (*quod* + subjunctive for *ut*).** It is on disk,
in Latin and English, with a page. **This is the C6 quote card.** It is also *better* for the video
than a word-list, because the reasoning is visible in the sentence itself.

A second instance, at English page marker **[92]**: *"**Omitto hic barbariem sermonis**, quod 'princeps
sacerdotibus' pro 'sacerdotum' dixit, et quod in eodem loco posuit 'extiterit' et 'existat'…"*
