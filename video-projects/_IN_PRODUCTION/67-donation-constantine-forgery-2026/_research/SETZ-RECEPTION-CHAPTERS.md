# PART 1 — PICCOLOMINI → PIUS II

## S1. Setz's opening verdict: Enea contributed nothing of his own

> „Enea Silvio Piccolomini, der spätere Papst Pius II. (1458-64), hat keinen eigenen Beitrag zur
> Zerstörung der Legende von der Konstantinischen Schenkung geleistet, er gehört nicht mit
> Nikolaus v. Kues, Valla und Pecock in eine Reihe. Eneas Stellungnahmen spiegeln vielmehr die
> Diskussion seiner Zeit."

*"Enea Silvio Piccolomini, the later Pope Pius II (1458–64), made no contribution of his own to the
destruction of the legend of the Donation of Constantine; he does not belong in a line with Nicholas
of Cusa, Valla and Pecock. Enea's statements rather mirror the discussion of his own time."*

**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 101

*Script:* Do not sell Piccolomini as a fourth debunker. He is the **transmission** beat — the man who
absorbed the critique and carried it into the papacy — not a fourth proof.

## S2. 1436: Enea argues FOR the Donation at Basel

At the Council of Basel he defended church rule by invoking the Donation:

> „dicere vero ineptos fore ad regimen sacerdotes, nihil est aliud quam vos omnes contemnere
> Constantinumque Caesarem reprehendere, cuius munere civitatum dominia suscepistis. Sileant ergo,
> qui talia submurmurant."

*"To say that priests are unfit to rule is nothing other than to despise you all and to reproach
Constantine Caesar, by whose gift you received lordship over cities. Let those who mutter such
things be silent."*

Setz adds, in his own hedged register: „scheint Enea die Darlegungen des Nikolaus v. Kues noch nicht
gekannt zu haben" — *"Enea seems not yet to have known Nicholas of Cusa's arguments."* That is a
**seems**, not a finding. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 102 (Mansi, *Orationes* I p. 25)

*Script:* The arc has a starting point on the wrong side. Useful — he changes his mind on evidence.

## S3. 1443 *Pentalogus*: three doubts, all from Cusa, none from Valla

In the *Pentalogus* (early 1443) Bishop Silvester of Chiemsee asks that a general council settle the
Donation, „quod de Constantini donatione astruitur, et multorum mentes disturbat" (*"what is alleged
about Constantine's donation, and which disturbs many minds"*); Chancellor Kaspar Schlick answers
„Ah quid tu fictam donationem adducis? Nunquam id somniatum fuit" (*"Ah, why do you bring up a
fabricated donation? That was never even dreamt of"*).

Setz isolates three grounds for doubt: not attested in the historians; the emperors kept ruling the
West after Constantine; the *Liber pontificalis* records gifts of property and revenue but not of
lands and lordship. His conclusion: „Diese Argumente sind genau die, die von Nikolaus von Kues
angeführt worden waren… **Von Vallas Schrift finden sich hier noch keine Spuren**" — *"These
arguments are exactly those advanced by Nicholas of Cusa… Of Valla's treatise there is as yet no
trace here."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 102–103 (Pentalogus, ed. Pez, *Thesaurus* IV.3, col. 679)

*Script:* Three years after Valla wrote, a future pope has the Donation in doubt from **Cusa alone**.
This dates how slowly Valla travelled — and it independently supports the project's Cusa-priority line.

## S4. 1453 *Dialogus*: Valla's fingerprints, two of them specific

In the *Dialogus* (1453), Enea reproduces Valla's *palea* judgement almost point for point:

> „neque scriptum illud, quod paleam vocant, eo stilo compositum est, ut Constantini videri possit:
> commentitia res est atque, ut arbitror, ignorante Gratiano inter eius conserta labores,
> inemendate, inculte, barbare dictata."

*"Nor is that writing which they call the palea composed in such a style that it could seem
Constantine's: it is a fabricated thing and, as I judge, was inserted among Gratian's works without
his knowledge — dictated uncorrectedly, uncouthly, barbarously."*

Setz calls the second borrowing clearer still — Valla's rule that authors reporting earlier events
deserve belief only if they can cite witnesses from the time (Valla 37,11), and his mockery of
Accursius's *plus quam stulta narratio* (37,17). Enea has Bernardino answer:

> „quid tu mihi iure consultos nostri temporis affers, cum de historia dubium est? qui, cum nihil
> liceat contra textum legis hiscere, Accursio glosatori fidem habeant…"

*"Why do you bring me the jurists of our own time, when the doubt is about history? — men who,
since they may not open their mouths against the text of the law, put their faith in the glossator
Accursius…"*

**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 104 (Dialogus, ed. Mansi, pp. 256–257)

*Script:* This is the concrete, quotable evidence that Valla's method — cross-examine your witness's
sources — reached a man who became pope five years later.

## S5. But Enea takes the forgery and keeps the power — Setz's own emphasis

> „Wichtiger allerdings als die Aufnahme dieser Argumente sind die Unterschiede in der
> Interpretation. Für Valla war der Nachweis der Fälschung der Ausgangspunkt, um die weltliche
> Herrschaft der Päpste in Frage zu stellen. Hier kann Enea Valla nicht folgen."

*"More important, however, than his adoption of these arguments are the differences in
interpretation. For Valla the demonstration of the forgery was the starting point for calling the
worldly rule of the popes into question. Here Enea cannot follow Valla."*

And the payoff: „Die Konstantinische Schenkung spielt dabei keine Rolle mehr, die Erkenntnis, daß es
sich dabei um eine Fälschung handelt, hat deshalb keine weiteren Konsequenzen."
*"The Donation of Constantine plays no further role in this; the recognition that it is a forgery
therefore has no further consequences."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 105–106

*Script:* **This complicates the project's framing and should be said out loud.** Proving the
document false did not dissolve the power it was invented to justify. Enea simply re-founded papal
temporal rule on the binding-and-loosing power and the *translatio imperii*. A forgery exposed is
not a claim withdrawn.

## S6. Setz refuses the stronger claim about Enea's arguments

Against Paparelli's „La falsità del documento è dimostrata con argomenti anche più convincenti di
quelli adoperati dal Valla", Setz writes: „Es kann keine Rede davon sein, daß er gar mit
überzeugenderen Argumenten die Fälschung nachgewiesen habe."
*"There can be no question of his having demonstrated the forgery with even more convincing
arguments."* **[CONTESTED]** · locator: Setz p. 106 and n. 17 (Paparelli pp. 159f.)

*Script:* If any source says Pius II out-argued Valla, that is Paparelli, and Setz rejects it.

## S7. Setz's own attribution is hedged — carry the hedge

> „Da er aber nach den Folgerungen fragt, die sich aus dem Nachweis der Fälschung ergeben, **darf
> man** seine Stellungnahme im Dialogus **wohl** als eine bewußte Antwort auf die Schrift Vallas
> werten."

*"But since he asks after the consequences that follow from the demonstration of the forgery, one
may **probably** read his position in the Dialogus as a deliberate answer to Valla's treatise."*

**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 106

*Script:* "Reads as a deliberate answer to Valla" is Setz's inference, flagged by *darf man wohl*.
Do not write "Pius II wrote a reply to Valla." Write "scholars read it as an answer."

## S8. Did he change position as pope? The contemporary evidence, both ways

**(a) He never retracted.** Mario Salamonio, *De principatu* (Rome 1544), dedicated to Leo X:

> „Laurentius Valla sine controversia vir doctissimus, quod Constantinianam negaverit donationem,
> parum abfuit, quin inter hereticos relatus fuerit, Sylvium vero Aeneam post eum, qui fortissime
> contra eandem donationem scripsit **neque unquam retractavit**, Pium II. inter Pontifices Maximos
> omnium consensu approbatum vidimus: nos latet, quae sors futura sit nobis, taceamus ergo, ne in
> aliquod discrimen incidamus."

*"Lorenzo Valla, beyond dispute a most learned man, came within a little of being counted among the
heretics for denying the Constantinian donation; yet Aeneas Silvius after him, who wrote most
forcefully against that same donation **and never retracted it**, we have seen approved by universal
consent as Pius II among the supreme pontiffs. What our own fate would be is hidden from us — so
let us keep silent, lest we run into some danger."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 107

**(b) His defenders wished he had.** Felino Sandei (d. 1503), bishop of Lucca, explained Enea's view
by his earlier curial company and added the counterfactual: „at si factus pontifex iterum in his
scripsisset, rectificatum ostendisset cor… Constantini munera non denigranda cognovisset" — *"but if
as pope he had written on these matters again, he would have shown a corrected heart… he would have
recognised that Constantine's gifts are not to be blackened."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 107
(Antivalla postscript, Cod. Lucca, Bibl. Capitolare 582)

**(c) The *in minoribus* dodge.** Tiresio Foscarari, *Donatio Constantini… iuris civilis auctoritate
comprobata* (Bologna 1549): „non obstat auctoritas summi antistitis Pii Papae **in minoribus**
existentis… quando erat parvulus loquebatur ut parvulus… at cum ad summi apostolatus apicem assumptus
fuit, credendum est mentem in melius mutasse" — *"the authority of the supreme prelate Pope Pius,
when he was in lesser orders, is no obstacle… when he was a child he spoke as a child… but when he
was raised to the summit of the apostolate, it must be believed his mind was changed for the
better."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 107 n. 19

**(d) Setz rejects the one text used to show a change.** Of the *Germania* (1457/58) passages,
Adolf Schmidt argued Enea had become „einer früheren Meinung untreu". Setz: „Es ist verfehlt, diese
Äußerungen denen im Pentalogus und Dialogus gegenüberzustellen" — *"It is mistaken to set these
statements against those in the Pentalogus and Dialogus"* — because the *Germania* passages are about
church **property**, not a transfer of **rule**. **[CONTESTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 106–107 n. 18

*Script:* The honest line is: **no evidence he recanted; his own defenders had to argue he would have.**
"He never retracted it" is a near-contemporary Roman jurist's phrase, on the record, printed in Rome
in 1544. That is a strong, safe beat.

## S9. Pius II was publicly counted an opponent of the Donation — into the 1540s

Enrico Boccella, *In Constantini Imperatoris Donationem Iuris Utriusque Praxis* (Lucca 1539),
"attempts a reckoning with all opponents of the Donation of Constantine from Dante, through Pius II,
to Hutten": „Er versucht eine Abrechnung mit allen Gegnern der Konstantinischen Schenkung von Dante
über Pius II. bis zu Hutten." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 133

Also, Gironi Pau's chancery handbook appendix (1493) — reprinted by **Hutten** — directs the reader:
„Quod donatio Constantini etiam de facto non fuerit, lege Laurentium Vallam **et Papam Pium in
Dialogo**" (*"That the Donation of Constantine did not in fact occur, read Lorenzo Valla and Pope
Pius in the Dialogue"*). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 142

*Script:* Two independent curial-side witnesses file a pope on the sceptics' side of the ledger.
Hutten later prints one of them **inside his Valla edition** — the papal-office name is being used as
a shield.

## S11. Antoninus, Platina, Zeno — three churchmen, three postures

- **Antoninus of Florence** (d. 1459), *Chronicon*: „Quid ergo et quantum donaverit (Constantinus),
  non est bene certum" — *"What and how much he gave is not well certain."* For him the Donation was
  not a *simplex donatio* but a *restitutio ecclesiae facta iuris sui*. **Hutten reprinted this
  judgement in his editions.** **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 108
- **Platina** (d. 1481), Vatican librarian, *Liber de vita Christi ac omnium pontificum* (finished
  1479, commissioned by Sixtus IV): calls the leprosy story a *conficta fabula*, keeps the Roman
  baptism, and on the extent of the gift lists diadem, vestments, churches — „doch von einer
  Abtretung der Herrschaft ist nicht die Rede" (*"but of a cession of rule there is no mention"*).
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 108
- **Jacopo Zeno** (d. 1481), bishop of Padua, *De vitis pontificum*, names Valla directly and
  answers him — the earliest named engagement in this group. On the anachronistic Constantinople he
  pleads a later deliberate insertion; on *satrapes* he says the word had long entered Latin. Setz:
  „Nur ein Argument Vallas weist er eingehend, **aber wenig überzeugend** zurück" (*"Only one of
  Valla's arguments does he rebut at length, but not very convincingly"*).
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 108–109 (Cod. Vat. lat. 5942, ff. 26r–31r)

*Script:* The papal librarian, writing for the pope, quietly drops the transfer of rule from his own
account of the gift (Platina) — a good "the institution edits itself" beat, parallel to Quirini.

# PART 2 — THE CURIAL COUNTER-ATTACKS (§ V, pp. 137–151)

## S12. Setz's framing of the three

> „Von Antonio Cortese, Mitglied der päpstlichen Kanzlei, einem unbekannten Prediger aus der Umgebung
> des Papstes und dem gelehrten Priester Pietro Edo… hat **nur Edo eine ausführliche
> Auseinandersetzung mit Valla versucht**. Alle aber haben in besonderer Weise die Persönlichkeit
> Vallas mit in den Blick genommen."

*"Of Antonio Cortese, member of the papal chancery, an unknown preacher from the pope's circle, and
the learned priest Pietro Edo… only Edo attempted a detailed engagement with Valla. But all of them
took particular aim at Valla's person."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 137

*Script:* One line carries the whole section: **two of the three curial answers argue about the man,
not the document.**

## S13. Cortese's *Antivalla*: explicitly declines to argue the evidence

Setz: „beschränkt sich auf persönliche Vorwürfe. **Auf Vallas juristische und theologische
Argumentation geht Cortese nicht ein**; denn die Frage, ob Konstantin die behauptete Schenkung getan
habe oder nicht, ist für ihn nicht von Bedeutung."
*"…confines itself to personal reproaches. Cortese does not engage Valla's juridical and theological
argument at all; for the question whether Constantine made the alleged donation or not is of no
importance to him."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 137

His actual grounds, in his own words:
- The thing needs no defence, being „tum maximis suffulta rationibus, tum pontificum decretis
  confirmata, tum diuturna possessione stabilita" — *"supported by the greatest reasons, confirmed by
  the decrees of the popes, and stabilised by long possession."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 138
- Valla broke rank with himself: „Quod tibi tam iratum subito numen fuit, aut quod te insaniae ac
  furoris genus invasit, ut repente **ex grammaticorum critico** tibi regum, imperatorum, pontificum
  maximorum **censor, iudex, arbiter** factus viderere?" — *"What deity turned so suddenly angry with
  you, or what kind of madness and frenzy invaded you, that all at once, from a critic among
  grammarians, you should appear to have made yourself censor, judge and arbiter of kings, emperors
  and supreme pontiffs?"* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 139
- The office argument: „Nam sicut cadaver humanum diutius ferre mare non potest, ita perditis
  hominem moribus dignitas non patitur pontificia" — *"For as the sea cannot long bear a human
  corpse, so the papal dignity does not tolerate a man of ruined morals."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 140
- A rhetoric charge that doubles as a trap: „Quid igitur persuades? Si rem non dubiam, non intelligis
  quam sis ineptus, qui tot uteris argumentationibus…? Si dubiam, cogita an sit conveniens homini
  praesertim eloquentiam professo suum initio dolorem praeseferre." — *"What then are you persuading?
  If the matter is not in doubt, you do not see how foolish you are, using so many arguments…; if it
  is in doubt, consider whether it becomes a man — especially one who professes eloquence — to parade
  his grievance at the outset."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 139

**Cortese also supplies the biographical smear that stuck for 300 years**: that Eugene IV, on
learning of the *libellus*, took it to the cardinals and „decretum est ut quaestio haberetur
postulareturque de crimine laesae maiestatis Laurentius" (*"it was decreed that an inquiry be held
and that Lorenzo be charged with the crime of lèse-majesté"*), and that Valla fled, thereby confessing.
Setz: „Mit diesen **fehlerhaften** biographischen Angaben hat Antonio Cortese noch nach 300 Jahren
Glauben gefunden" — *"With these erroneous biographical details Antonio Cortese was still believed
300 years later"* (Tiraboschi, 1784). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 141

Contemporary verdict on the *Antivalla*, from Sandei, who copied it: „Antonius Cortesius hucusque
miles fuit contra Vallam rorarius, proelium non inivit; **hominis petulantiam non ignorantiam
confundit**." — *"So far Antonius Cortesius has been a skirmisher against Valla; he never joined
battle; he confounds the man's insolence, not his ignorance."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 141

*Script:* Sandei's sentence is the usable quote-card here — a curial insider, who copied the
*Antivalla* himself, saying the curial answer never joined battle. Note authorship is not fully settled: both manuscripts name
*A. Cortesius*, the colophon reads *alex(andri) cortesii*, and Miglio floats the son Alessandro
(Setz p. 137 n. 2).

## S14. The anonymous oration *Quod papa praesit temporalibus*: Valla as a heretic label

Delivered to the pope and cardinals; argues from scripture alone, „sanctorum autem patrum decreta
tanquam domestica testimonia vel suspecta silentio pertransibo" (*"the decrees of the holy fathers,
as domestic and therefore suspect testimony, I shall pass over in silence"*). It groups Valla with
the Waldensians, cites him as „nescio quis Laurentius Valla" (*"one Lorenzo Valla, whoever he is"*),
and produces the pun: „**Valdensis potius quam Vallensis appellandus sit**" — *"he should be called
Valdensis rather than Vallensis."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 143–144 (ed. Gaeta, *Una polemica*, pp. 390–398)

It answers six "errors" drawn from a **single page** of Valla — the speech Setz identifies as Valla
13,26–14,5 — and then generalises: „Isti sunt errores sex… quos ex tam brevi pagella libellique de
ficta seu ementita Constantini donatione Laurentius Vallensis instituit. Quid si tantum otii daretur,
ut totum illud opusculum liceret corrigere omni ex parte corruptum…" — *"These are the six errors…
which Lorenzo Vallensis set up out of so brief a little page of the pamphlet on the feigned or
falsified donation of Constantine. What if leisure enough were given, to correct that whole little
work, corrupt in every part…"* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 144

Setz's summary: „Valla ist dabei nicht mehr als ein Beispiel für die Gegenposition, Inhalt und Form
seiner Darlegungen interessieren nicht näher." — *"Valla is here no more than an example of the
opposing position; the content and form of his arguments are of no closer interest."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 144–145

*Script:* Six errors extracted from one page, and a rebuttal of the whole treatise promised for when
someone has time. That is the shape of the answer.

## S15. Pietro Edo (1496): the one critic who answered every sentence

Two works, months apart, both 1496: the *Antidotum* (dedicated to bishop Leonello Chieregato) and the
*Apologia* (addressed to Alexander VI himself), which quotes and comments on Valla passage by passage.
His stated aim: „ei mox antidotum esse apponendum censui, quod omnem prorsus tolleret contagionis
periculi et veneni vim" — *"I judged that an antidote must at once be applied to it, one that would
wholly remove the danger of contagion and the force of the poison."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 145–146 (Cod. Vind. 4917, f. 118r)

His three motives for Valla: general lay hatred of clerics; that Valla was a Roman resentful of papal
rule over his city; and that the rich dislike taxes. He also floats, as suspicion: „suspicor
maledicum hunc hominem cuiuspiam forsitan sacerdotis alumnum ne dicam filium fuisse" — *"I suspect
this abusive man was perhaps the ward, not to say the son, of some priest."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 146

**Edo hands off the document question:** „Ego vero non hoc loco (ut multi iam fecere) quaerendum esse
censeo Silvestrone Constantinus quicquam largitus sit, iure ne potuerit elargiri, nam iureconsultis
id relictum esse velim." — *"I do not think this the place (as many have already done) to ask whether
Constantine granted anything to Silvester, or could lawfully grant it — I would leave that to the
jurists."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 147

**Edo's constructive move — worth the script's attention.** Because he cannot defend the Latin, he
invents a transmission story: the original perished on Peter's tomb, „cum omnia consumat ac deleat
vetustas"; *Palea* was a person, Gratian's pupil and a monk, who reconstructed the text from the
*Gesta Silvestri* from memory — „non omnino verba inter scribendum prosecutus sed sensum" (*"not
following the words as he wrote, but the sense"*). Hence Constantinople (Palea knew what the emperor
already intended), hence the biblical cadences (a monk trained in scripture, not in the orators),
hence the bad Latin (the *negligentia* of copyists). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 149–150

**Edo concedes on the glossators.** „nullam a glosatoribus decretorum mencionem factam esse miror,
cum tamen in eiusmodi capitulis multa contineantur quae… contraria eamque ob rem exposicione
indigerent clariore." — *"I am astonished that no mention was made by the glossators of the decrees,
since such chapters contain much that is contradictory and would therefore need clearer exposition."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 148

Setz's verdict: „Von allen Stellungnahmen zu Valla ist die des Edo sicherlich die **originellste**…
Steuchus hat sich ein halbes Jahrhundert später geweigert, Valla wörtlich zu zitieren, Pietro Edo
stellt sich sozusagen jedem Satz." — *"Of all the responses to Valla, Edo's is certainly the most
original… Steuchus half a century later refused to quote Valla verbatim; Pietro Edo faces, so to
speak, every sentence."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 151

*Script:* Edo is the honest opponent — the one who reads every line, concedes what he cannot answer,
and then builds an elaborate scribal fable to save the text. That contrast (Edo quotes Valla; Steuco
refuses to) is a clean beat.

## S16. Do the curial critics anticipate Steuco's Greek argument? No — that line runs elsewhere

Direct check of all three: **Cortese** argues person and possession only; **the anonymous oration**
argues scripture only; **Edo** works exclusively from the *palea* in Gratian — „Wie Valla befaßt er
sich nur mit der Palea im Dekret Gratians, **die sonstige Überlieferung wird nicht angesprochen**"
(*"Like Valla he deals only with the palea in Gratian's Decretum; the rest of the transmission is not
addressed"*). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 149

Setz assigns the Greek line to a different set of people, in his Rückblick: „Es ist aber sicher kein
Zufall, daß **wiederholt griechische Versionen des Constitutum angeführt wurden**, nachdem Valla den
lateinischen Text der im Dekret Gratians überlieferten Donatio in den Mittelpunkt seiner Kritik
gestellt hatte. Neben **Quirini** sind **Galateo** und vor allem **Bartholomaeus Pi(n)cernus** zu
nennen." — *"It is surely no accident that Greek versions of the Constitutum were repeatedly adduced,
after Valla had put the Latin text transmitted in Gratian's Decretum at the centre of his critique.
Beside Quirini, Galateo and above all Bartholomaeus Pi(n)cernus are to be named."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 192

*Script:* The Greek counter-move is a **response to Valla's choice of target**, and it runs
Quirini (1447) → Pincernus → Steuco, not through the curial polemicists. This is a mechanism beat:
attack the Latin text, and the defence produces a different text.

# PART 3 — HUTTEN, LUTHER, COCHLÄUS, THE TRANSLATIONS, STEUCO (§ VI, pp. 151–188)

## S17. Hutten was not the first editor, and he already had a copy

Setz's opening: „Das entscheidende Ereignis für die Wirkungsgeschichte von Vallas Schrift bleibt die
Neuherausgabe durch Ulrich von Hutten, auch wenn er **nicht mehr als Erstherausgeber angesprochen
werden kann**. Die editio princeps von 1506 des Anonymus de Aloysio blieb ohne erkennbare Wirkung."
*"The decisive event for the reception history of Valla's treatise remains the re-edition by Ulrich
von Hutten, even though he can no longer be described as first editor. The editio princeps of 1506
by the Anonymus de Aloysio remained without discernible effect."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 151

The evidence that Hutten already intended to print it before meeting Cochläus is Cochläus's own
letter to Willibald Pirckheimer, 5 July 1517:

> „Pridie quam recederet (Huttenus) apud me vidit Laurentii Vallae libellum contra Constantini
> donationem, quem ego ad modicum tempus videndum ab alio commodatum acceperam; vult homo eum
> libellum in Germania **rursus** impressioni mandare, petiit, ut libellus iste, **quia correctior
> esset**, transscriberetur, non potui ei id denegare."

*"The day before he left, Hutten saw at my house Lorenzo Valla's little book against the Donation of
Constantine, which I had borrowed from another to look at for a short time; the man wants to commit
that book to the press in Germany **again**; he asked that this copy, **because it was more correct**,
be transcribed, and I could not refuse him."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 152 (Hutten, *Schriften* I p. 142,12)

*Script:* *rursus* and *correctior* do the work. He already had a text and already knew it was worse.

## S18. Hutten's editorial achievement, established by collation

Setz identifies **two exemplars**: (1) the 1506 editio princeps — shown by shared omissions, shared
additions and shared corruptions against every other witness, including readings unique to the print
(4,38 *ruinis*/*minis*; 47,34 *bonus*/*Bonifacius*; 25,15 where Hutten mis-expands an abbreviation as
*Atqui* for *a qua*); and (2) Cochläus's transcript, which by collation belongs to the
**Padua A 44 / Lüneburg Misc. D 4° 33** group and sits nearer the **better** of the two, Lüneburg.
Setz: „Diese Feststellung deckt sich mit Huttens eigenem Urteil, daß diese zweite Vorlage *correctior*
gewesen sei" — *"This finding agrees with Hutten's own judgement that this second exemplar was more
correct."* He also isolates Hutten's own smoothing (e.g. 30,14 *pontificem* → *summum pontificem*;
19,22 *editionibus* → *codicibus*), and notes Hutten corrected errors for the second edition
(25,34 *India* → *Iudea*). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 152–155

*Script:* Hutten collated two witnesses and preferred the better one. If the video wants to say the
Reformation's most famous pamphleteer did real editorial work, this is the page that supports it.

## S19. Hutten's preface: what he says he is doing

Addressed to **Leo X**. The frame is that Leo's pontificate is the break between Julius II's tyranny
and a restored freedom, so that suppressed truth may now be printed:

> „proinde in lucem prodeat siquid diu latuit, et eo maiori cum fiducia prodeat, quanto quidque
> verius, quantoque syncere magis scriptum est; qualis hic est liber, quem alii idcirco non tulerunt
> pontifices, quia verum audire noluerunt, tu idcirco amabis, quia veritatis iam ante poculum nobis
> propinasti."

*"Therefore let whatever has long lain hidden come to light, and let it come forth with the greater
confidence the truer and more sincerely it is written — such as this book is, which other popes did
not tolerate because they did not wish to hear the truth, and which you will love because you have
already offered us the cup of truth."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 155 (Hutten, *Schriften* I p. 157,7)

He blames the popes for the forgery itself — „neque fuerunt pontifices illi qui finxerunt Constantini
donationem, quia pastores non fuerunt" (*"nor were those men popes who invented the Donation of
Constantine, because they were not shepherds"*) — and defends Valla's character: „**Ergo non
maledicit pontificibus Valla, sed in tyrannos vera dicit.**… qui maximo etiam proposito, ut illa
tempestate, periculo veritatem deserere noluit." — *"Therefore Valla does not slander the popes; he
speaks the truth against tyrants… who, even at the greatest risk for that time, would not abandon the
truth."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 156 (*Schriften* I pp. 157,10 and 159,3/9)

Closing dedication: „tibi vero gratissimum facere me confidam, qui **reiectum nuper et damnatum** hac
de re Vallae libellum veluti ex tenebris in lucem, ab interitu ad vitam revocem. Quinetiam ipsum tibi
illum dedico, ut testatum sit quam te pontifice **renata libertate verum dicere licuerit omnibus**."
*"I trust I do you a most welcome service in recalling Valla's recently rejected and condemned book
on this matter, as it were out of darkness into light, from destruction to life. Indeed I dedicate it
to you, that it may be attested how, under your pontificate, with liberty reborn, all have been
permitted to speak the truth."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 157 (*Schriften* I p. 161,29)

*Script:* Hutten dedicates the demolition of papal title deeds **to the pope**, and frames it as a
compliment. That is the beat. But see S20 — the *reiectum nuper et damnatum* is Hutten's assertion,
not an established fact.

## S20. ⚠ The "suppression" claim: Setz says nothing documents it — reinforces the project's C10a

> „Einige Autoren wollen wissen, daß Vallas Schrift von der Kirche unterdrückt worden sei. Hutten
> spricht vom (libellum) *reiectum nuper et damnatum*, der unbekannte Autor [in] Paris, BN, lat.
> 3187 A… wirft den Päpsten vor: *…vetant et prohibent ne veniat ad lucem*. **Es läßt sich nicht
> sagen, ob es sich hier um mehr als nur um Vermutungen handelt. Bis heute ist kein Zeugnis bekannt,
> das eine kirchliche Intervention belegt.**"

*"Some authors claim to know that Valla's treatise was suppressed by the Church. Hutten speaks of the
book as 'recently rejected and condemned'; the unknown author [in the Paris manuscript] charges the
popes: '…they forbid and prohibit it from coming to light.' **It cannot be said whether this is more
than conjecture. To this day no testimony is known that documents a church intervention.**"*

Setz then dates the actual, documented turn: „Das änderte sich erst mit der **Reformationszeit**… 1546
in Venedig den päpstlichen Nuntius auf den Plan rief. **1559** wurden dann zum erstenmal einzelne
Werke Vallas auf den Index gesetzt, zunächst *De libero arbitrio*, *De falsa donatione Constantini*
und *Annotationes in Novum Testamentum*." **[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Setz p. 101 (corroborated at Setz pp. 193 and 178 — the Czech translator Gelenius makes the same suppression claim in 1513 and, in Setz's words, „Einen Zeugen für diese Ansicht nennt er allerdings nicht" / *"he names no witness for this view"*)

*Script:* **The censorship happened, but it happened in 1559, a century after Valla died** — and the
"they buried it" story is what Valla's *publishers* said, not what the record shows. This is the
single most script-relevant correction in the whole reading, because it changes the causal claim: the
Church did not suppress the treatise into obscurity; it banned it only after the Reformation had
weaponised it.

## S21. The dating of the preface: Setz overturns Kalkoff and Benzing

The preface is dated „Ex arce Steckelberg, Calen. Decemb. Anno post millesimum et quingentesimum
decimo septimo" — **1 December 1517, from castle Steckelberg**. Kalkoff (1925) called it a deliberate
back-dating; Benzing's bibliography (1956) put the preface in winter 1519, on the "higher-criticism"
ground that „1517 war Hutten noch gar nicht imstande, eine so anklagende Praefatio wie die vorliegende
zu formulieren" (*"in 1517 Hutten was not yet capable of formulating so accusatory a preface"*).
Setz's reply: „**Diese Interpretation ist kaum haltbar.**" **[CONTESTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 159–160

The evidence Setz turns on is Hutten's own *Exhortatorium* of December 1518 attached to the Turkish
oration, explaining that he no longer fears Leo X: „…quem tantum abest ut in hac re metuam, ut etiam
Laurentii Vallae adversus ementitam Constantini donationem libellum in lucem iterum, praefatione ad
ipsum facta, **edere nuper ausus sim**." — *"…whom I am so far from fearing in this matter that I have
even lately dared to bring Lorenzo Valla's book against the falsified Donation of Constantine to light
again, with a preface addressed to him."* Setz: „Diese Sätze bedürfen keiner Interpretation. Sie
besagen eindeutig, daß Vallas Schrift **im Jahre 1518** erschienen ist."
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 162 (Hutten, *Schriften* I p. 241,21)

**The place, though, is fiction.** Hutten entered Albrecht of Brandenburg-Mainz's service in September
1517 and was on embassy in France, returning only after 20 December 1517. „Damit ist ausgeschlossen,
daß er am 1. Dezember auf seiner Burg war. **Die Ortsangabe erweist sich so als Fiktion.**" Setz's
reconstruction — that Hutten finished the edition in France and gave his home address instead — is
his own inference, marked *offensichtlich*. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 163

**Resulting chronology** (Setz, pp. 163–166): first edition **1518** — and, against Benzing, probably
from **Johannes Schöffer in Mainz**, not Cratander, since Cratander had no Basel press before
September 1518; second edition **November/December 1519**, from **Andreas Cratander in Basel**,
evidenced by Albert Burer to Beatus Rhenanus, 12 Nov 1519: „mitto quantum de donatione impressum est,
pulchre convolutum" (*"I send as much of the Donation as has been printed, nicely folded"*).
Setz's Mainz-to-Basel explanation is flagged as speculation: „Hierzu kann nicht mehr als eine
Vermutung geäußert werden" — possibly the tightened censorship under Albrecht.
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 165–166

*Script:* Safe formulations: preface **dated** 1 Dec 1517; place-line **fictional**; first edition
**1518**; second **late 1519**. Do not say "Hutten back-dated it" — Setz argues against that, and it
is the claim in the older literature.

## S22. Luther on first reading it — the letter, verbatim

Luther met the treatise in **Hutten's second edition**, lent to him by Dominicus Schleupner. To Georg
Spalatin, **24 February 1520**:

> „Habeo in manibus officio Dominici Schleupner Donationem Constantini a Laurentio Vallensi confutatam
> per Huttenum editam. Deus bone, quantae seu tenebrae seu nequitiae Romanensium, et quod in Dei
> iudicio mireris per tot secula non modo durasse, sed etiam prevaluisse ac inter decretales relata
> esse tam impura tam crassa tam impudentia mendacia inque fidei articulorum (nequid monstrosissimi
> monstri desit) vicem successisse. **Ego sic angor, ut prope non dubitem papam esse proprie
> Antichristum illum, quem vulgata opinione expectat mundus**; adeo conveniunt omnia, quae vivit,
> facit, loquitur, statuit."

*"I have in my hands, by the good office of Dominicus Schleupner, the Donation of Constantine refuted
by Lorenzo Valla, published by Hutten. Good God, what darkness and wickedness of the Romanists — and
what one must wonder at, under God's judgement, is that for so many centuries such impure, such
gross, such shameless lies not only endured but prevailed, and were entered among the decretals and
took the place of articles of faith (lest anything be missing from this most monstrous monstrosity).
**I am so distressed that I almost do not doubt that the pope is properly that Antichrist whom the
world by common opinion expects**; so completely does everything agree — what he lives, does, says,
decrees."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 167 (D. Martin Luthers Werke, Briefe 2, Nr. 257, pp. 48f.)

Setz's reading: „Luther zeigte sich überrascht und bestätigt zugleich. Überrascht hat ihn, daß eine
Fälschung über Jahrhunderte wirken konnte, in das kirchliche Rechtsbuch aufgenommen und so zu einem
*articulus fidei* werden konnte." — *"Luther showed himself surprised and confirmed at once.
What surprised him was that a forgery could work over centuries, be taken into the church's law book,
and so become an article of faith."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 167

*Script:* The most usable single sentence for the deployment beat: reading a philologist's
demolition of a charter is what tips Luther into naming the pope Antichrist — **and he says it in
a private letter, six months before *An den christlichen Adel***.

## S23. Luther afterwards: 1520, the table talk, and the 1537 translation

- **1520, *An den christlichen Adel***: „Es musz ein besundere plage von got gewesen sein, das szoviel
  vorstendige leut sich haben lassen bereden, solch lugen aufftzunehmen, so sie doch szo gar grob und
  unbehend sein, das mich dunckt, es solt ein trunckenn bawr behender und geschickter liegen kunden."
  *"It must have been a special plague from God that so many understanding people let themselves be
  talked into accepting such lies, since they are so gross and clumsy that it seems to me a drunken
  peasant could lie more deftly and skilfully."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 168 (WA 6)
- **Table talk, undated**: „Magnum igitur mendacium est Donatio Constantini, qua papa dimidium de
  imperio Romano sibi arrogat. Etiamsi factum esset, tamen non fuisset in potestate imperatoris,
  neque convenit papae iuxta dictum Christi: Vos autem non sic." — *"The Donation of Constantine is
  therefore a great lie, by which the pope arrogates half the Roman empire to himself. Even had it
  been done, it would not have been in the emperor's power, nor does it befit the pope, according to
  Christ's word: But you, not so."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 169 (WA Tischreden 5, Nr. 6043)
- **1532, on Valla the man**: „ein frommer man… *purus, simplex, dexter, candidus… is coniunxit
  pietatem cum literis*." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 167 (WA Tischreden 2, Nr. 1470, p. 107)
- **1537, the annotated German translation** of the *palea*, titled *Einer aus den hohen Artikeln des
  päpstlichen Glaubens, genannt Donatio Constantini*, written for the projected council at Mantua and
  sent straight to Rome by nuncio Giovanni Morone. On Valla: „Es hat ein gelerter trefflicher man,
  genant Laurentius Valla, ein Roemer… sich da wider gelegt und **der erst schrifftlich angefochten**,
  wie wol er fast seuberlich mit der braut auff dem stein weg thet und die Rote mordische verdampte
  hure zu Rom nicht so ungewasschen angreiff, wie der Luther gethan hat."
  *"A learned, excellent man named Lorenzo Valla, a Roman… set himself against it and was the first to
  attack it in writing — though he went fairly daintily with the bride on the stone path, and did not
  take hold of the red, murderous, damned whore at Rome as unwashed as Luther has done."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 169 (WA 50, p. 73)

Setz's summary: „**Luthers Einschätzung der Konstantinischen Schenkung hat sich über die Jahre nicht
geändert.** Der Brief von 1520 hat die Stichworte gegeben, die in dem Kommentar von 1537 zu
Leitthesen eines Pamphlets geworden sind." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 170

*Script:* Luther credits Valla with priority in writing (**der erst schrifftlich angefochten**) and in
the same breath says he was too polite. Note this is factually wrong on priority (Cusa, Pecock) — a
useful reminder that the Reformation's picture of the discovery was already simplified.

## S24. Cochläus: the Catholic controversialist who never withdrew his agreement

In the same 5 July 1517 letter: „**Credo equidem verissima esse quae scripsit Laurentius**; vereor
tamen ne tuto edi queant. At Huttenus anathema non formidat, et indignum mihi videtur, ut veritas a
veritatis gladio prohibeatur." — *"I do indeed believe that what Lorenzo wrote is entirely true; yet I
fear they cannot safely be published. But Hutten does not dread the anathema, and it seems unworthy to
me that truth should be barred by the sword of truth."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 173 (Hutten, *Schriften* I p. 142,18)

Setz: „Von einem späteren Bedauern über die Drucklegung von Vallas Schrift durch Hutten kann keine
Rede sein. Das Urteil von 1517… hat Cochläus später **nicht widerrufen, sondern lediglich
differenziert**." — *"There can be no question of later regret over Hutten's printing of Valla's
treatise. Cochläus did not later revoke the judgement of 1517; he merely qualified it."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 175

In 1537 (*Von der Donation des Kaysers Constantini*) he argues the *palea* was never authoritative:
it „sei freylich noch nie von eynigem Babst Cardinal oder Bischoff fur **Autentica** und
glaubwirdig… approbirt oder angenommen worden biß auff diese stund" (*"has indeed never to this hour
been approved or accepted as authentic and trustworthy by any pope, cardinal or bishop"*). He also
defends Valla against the Lutherans — „daß Lau. Valla viel einer anderen meynung vom Bapstumb gewest
ist denn Luther und seiner rott" — and against the heresy charge, noting Valla lies honourably buried
in the Lateran. His distinction: „quotiens enim Valla Petri sedem, Petri successores, Petri
principatum et primatum in Donationis libro fatetur, probat, asserit, confirmat" — *"how often in the
Donation book Valla acknowledges, approves, asserts and confirms Peter's see, Peter's successors,
Peter's principate and primacy."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 175–176 (*De Petro et Roma*, pp. 12–13, 46, 94)

*Script:* Cochläus is the intellectual-honesty beat, and he cuts against a simple
Catholic-vs-Protestant framing: **Luther's fiercest Catholic opponent had already conceded the
document, in writing, in 1517** — and separated the forgery from the primacy. Setz endorses this as
the discriminating reading (p. 177).

## S25. William Marshall's English translation, 1534 — the deployment beat

> „Die englische Übersetzung gehört zu den Werken, mit denen die in der **Suprematsakte vom November
> 1534** erhobenen Ansprüche des englischen Königs auf Unabhängigkeit von Rom gestützt werden sollten."

*"The English translation belongs among the works with which the claims to independence from Rome
raised in the Act of Supremacy of November 1534 were to be supported."*

Facts as Setz gives them: Thomas Cromwell in Henry VIII's service from 1530, principal secretary
1534; from 1532 he gathered a circle of writers; **Marshall joined it in 1533**, his first task the
translation of Marsilius of Padua's *Defensor pacis*. Marshall names the Valla translation in a
**letter to Cromwell of 1 April 1534**:

> „Surely I thinke there was never better boke made and sett forthe for the defasing of the Pope of
> Rome than this."

It appeared the same year from **Thomas Godfray in London**, titled *A treatyse of the donation or
gyfte and endowment of possessyones gyven and presented unto Sylvester pope of Rome by Constantyne
emperor of Rome*. **„Der Übersetzer ist nicht genannt."** The copy-text was **Hutten's first edition**.
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 181–182

Setz adds the ideological fit: Henry increasingly grounded his imperial dignity in early British
legend, in which King Arthur was Constantine's successor uniting Roman empire and English kingship —
„Wenn nun die Herrschaftsabtretung Konstantins hinfällig wurde, konnte Konstantin mit größerem
Nachdruck als früher **Vorfahre des Königs von England** herausgestellt werden."
*"Once Constantine's cession of rule fell away, Constantine could be presented with greater emphasis
than before as an ancestor of the king of England."* And, hedged: „Der König hatte Vallas Widerlegung
**vielleicht** schon 1533 kennengelernt." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 182 (Scarisbrick, *Henry VIII*, pp. 270ff., 309 n. 3)

**Confirming the Levine link.** Levine's early-modern English renderings — "Callest thou the donation
of the world a privilege?" and "Did the scribes of the emperors (because I will not say the
horsekeepers) speak after this manner and fashion in thy time?" — carry footnotes reading
"Marshall trans., sig. f ii" and "Sig. J ii", and Levine's own note states the 1534 Godfray printing
(STC 5641) "appears to be by William Marshall" and cites *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII*, 1 April
1534, VII, nos. 422–423. **The project's Levine quotes are Marshall's 1534 English.**
**[CORROBORATED]** · locator: Setz pp. 181–182 + Levine, *Studies in the Renaissance* 20 (1973), pp. 140–142 and nn. 54, 56, 58

⚠ **One discrepancy to carry.** Levine's note says Hutten published Valla "in 1519 and this edition
was employed for" the English translation; **Setz says the copy-text was Hutten's *first* edition**
(1518, per S21). **[CONTESTED]** · locator: Setz p. 182 vs Levine p. 143 n. 53

*Script:* This is the clean deployment beat: a fifteenth-century Italian philologist's Latin
declamation, translated to order inside Cromwell's propaganda shop, printed in London in the year of
the Act of Supremacy, with a covering note calling it the best book ever made for defacing the pope.
The translator's name is on the letter, not on the book.

## S26. The other translations — five languages, three different uses

- **Czech, Gregor Hruby (Gelenius), 1513** — the earliest known translation and the only one never
  printed (Prague UB Cod. 168 / XVII D 38). His preface calls the Donation an expression of the
  church's worldliness, invents reading marks to signal questions, pauses and *ironia*, and appends a
  glossary of Roman magistracies. He also asserts the church blocked copying and printing of Valla —
  „Einen Zeugen für diese Ansicht nennt er allerdings nicht" (*"he names no witness for this view"*).
  Omitted: the passages on the charter's vocabulary and etymologies (e.g. *optimates* 23,1;
  *phrygium* and *lorum* 28). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 177–179
- **French, probably Lyon, c. 1522** — a reworking, not a translation: based on the 1520 reprint of
  the editio princeps, it cuts the philological passages, reorders the argument, adds a life of
  Constantine and a narrative from Constantine to Charlemagne, and a section headed *Translation
  (ou mieulx division) de lempire Grec et Latin*. Setz: „Aus Vallas kritischer Untersuchung einer
  Urkunde ist so **eine historische Abhandlung** geworden." (*"Valla's critical examination of a
  charter has thus become a historical treatise."*) **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 179–180
- **German, *Des Edlen Römers Laurentii Vallensis Clagrede…*, preface signed H.S.** — ordered "vorlängst
  gedruckt" by Duke Albrecht of Prussia from Lucas Cranach in 1526, so printed by then; copy-text
  Hutten's first edition. Explicitly reformational: the pope is Antichrist, and the translator
  advertises a word-for-word method — „die warheyt dem Latein gemaeß, von wort zu wort… in dz schlecht
  einfaltig deutsch verdolmetschen… Dann die schlechte eynfaltige rede ist eyn freündin der warheyt."
  (*"to translate the truth in accordance with the Latin, word for word… into plain simple German…
  For plain simple speech is a friend of truth."*) **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 180–181
- **Italian, 1546**, *Trattato utile et degno d'esser letto da ogni persona…* — no translator, no
  preface; copy-text Hutten's **second** edition, from which it also renders Pincernus's Constitutum
  and **Hutten's preface**. Setz's only emphasis is the year: it coincides with Steuco writing his
  reply. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 182–183

*Script:* One tight formulation covers this: the same treatise becomes a devotional-philological
exercise in Prague, a political history in Lyon, a Reformation tract in Germany, and a state paper in
London. The text does not change; the use does.

## S27. Steuco (1547): the defence that declines to prove the Donation

Augustinus Steuchus — papal service from 1536, bishop of Kissamos, prefect of the Vatican Library —
wrote *Contra Laurentium Vallam De falsa Donatione Constantini Libri duo*, printed **Lyon 1547**.
Setz suspects a commission: „**liegt die Vermutung nahe**, daß es sich dabei um eine bestellte Antwort
handelt, **vielleicht** gedacht als Antwort auf Luthers Schrift *Wider das Papsttum zu Rom, vom Teufel
gestiftet* (1545)." — explicitly a conjecture. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 184

**His stated aim, which is the finding worth the script:**

> „hoc primum praefabor, non me quidem velle **probare** atque omni ratione contendere, donationem
> esse veram; sed **concesso eam dubiam esse**, clariores ac plures extare rationes, quae veram esse
> probent, quam rationes Laurentii valere ad probandum non esse veram."

*"I shall say this first: that I do not wish to prove, and to contend by every means, that the
donation is true; but rather — granting that it is doubtful — that there exist clearer and more
numerous reasons proving it true than Lorenzo's reasons avail to prove it untrue."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 184 (Steuchus, *Contra Vallam*, p. 86)

**He refuses to quote Valla**, and says why, before the dedication (which is addressed to St Peter):
„Laurentius Vallensis contra donationem Constantini libellum conscribens, eum blasphemiis et impiis
irrisionibus refersit. Visum igitur est praestare, eum ex hoc opere subducere, sensibus duntaxat in
redarguendo prolatis." — *"Lorenzo Vallensis, writing his book against the Donation of Constantine,
stuffed it with blasphemies and impious mockeries. It therefore seemed better to remove him from this
work, and to bring forward only the substance in refuting him."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 184

**The Greek argument.** Steuco had already used the Greek transmission against Valla in *Pro religione
christiana adversus Lutheranos* (1530). In 1547 the *palea* is for him merely a corrupt witness; he
argues from Latin and Greek *antiquiora exemplaria*, weighting Greek manuscripts he says he found in
the Vaticana. Setz: „Die griechische Version des Constitutum gilt ihm **zwar nicht als die ältere**,
sie bezeuge aber allein schon durch ihr **Vorhandensein** die Echtheit der Schenkung."
*"The Greek version of the Constitutum does not count for him as the older one, but by its mere
existence it attests the genuineness of the donation."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 186

**His construction to save the text**: the charter was drafted not at the baptism but *insecutis…
non multis annis*, by Christian bishops at Constantine's order in the orbit of Nicaea — which for him
explains the poor Latin („dixi quoque in desuetudinem eo tempore sermonem Latinum abiisse"), the
*satrapes*, the biblical echoes („conscriptum etiam a Christianis episcopis edictum fuit, Constantino
iubente, ob eam causam sunt pleraque ex sacris literis admista"), and the anachronistic
Constantinople. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 187

**⚠ Steuco may have privately abandoned the Donation.** Setz reports, on Freudenberger, that in 1541
Steuco told Johann Albrecht Widmannstetter „…dixit etiam tum mihi, se in longe diversissima sententia
nunc esse, quod ad Constantini donationem attinet, quam olim fuerit, cum scriberet de Constantini
donatione" — *"he also said to me then that he is now of a far different opinion as regards
Constantine's donation than he once was, when he wrote about the donation of Constantine."* Setz
frames this with „**er soll** … geäußert haben" — reported speech, not established.
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 183 and n. 4 (Freudenberger pp. 124f., 127 n. 38)

Setz's closing verdict on Steuco: „**In ihren Hauptmerkmalen… sind die Ausführungen des Steuchus in
keiner Weise originell.** So war es vor allem die Fülle der durch den Druck leicht zugänglichen
Argumente, die auf den empfänglichen Leser nicht ohne Eindruck blieben und bis ins 17. Jahrhundert
hinein fortwirkten." — *"In their main features Steuco's arguments are in no way original. It was
above all the sheer quantity of arguments, made easily accessible by print, that did not fail to
impress the receptive reader and went on working into the seventeenth century."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 188

*Script:* The Vatican librarian's official answer opens by conceding the document is doubtful and
proposes to win on the count of reasons rather than on proof — and Setz's judgement is that it worked
by volume, not by argument. Handle the 1541 remark carefully: it is *soll geäußert haben*.

## S28. Also in this section: Flacius rates Cusa's refutation above Valla's

Matthias Flacius Illyricus put excerpts of Valla into his *Catalogus testium veritatis* (1556),
compared Valla's critique with Cusa's, and concluded: „**omnium tamen firmissima est Cusani
refutatio**" — *"yet of all of them the firmest is Cusa's refutation."* Setz's explanation is
that Cusa marshals more evidence. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 172 (Catalogus, Basel 1566, pp. 839, 959)

Related: the Magdeburg Centuries, Centuria IV (1560), reprint the Donation from Gratian plus the
*Confessio* from Pincernus's translation, and their commentary is „weitgehend eine bloße
Zusammenfassung der Argumente Vallas" (*"largely a mere summary of Valla's arguments"*) — while
naming Valla only once, in connection with the baptism and leprosy.
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 173

*Script:* A Protestant polemicist in 1556 ranks the **cardinal's** refutation above the humanist's.
That is an external check on the project's Cusa-priority thread from a hostile-to-Rome source with no
incentive to credit a cardinal.

# PART 4 — THE RÜCKBLICK (pp. 189–194): Setz's own summary verdict

## S29. Setz overturns the received reading of the treatise itself

> „Es ist an der Zeit, auch hier die tradierte *communis opinio* aufzugeben… Danach handelte es sich
> um eine antikirchliche Streitschrift zur Unterstützung des Kampfes König Alfonsos V. (I.) von
> Neapel (1435-58) gegen Papst Eugen IV. (1431-47), um den „kühnsten Angriff gegen die weltliche
> Papstgewalt". … **In allen Punkten kann diese Interpretation korrigiert werden.**"

*"It is time to abandon here too the received communis opinio… According to it, this was an
anti-church polemic in support of King Alfonso V (I) of Naples's struggle against Pope Eugene IV, the
'boldest attack on the worldly papal power'. … In every point this interpretation can be corrected."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 189–190 (the quoted phrase from Gregorovius III p. 259)

His grounds, stated flatly: the treatise contains no indication that it was written on Alfonso's
commission or even in his support; the political situation of 1440 is absent; Valla presents himself
as a representative of the Roman people, not as Alfonso's partisan; there is no sign the king used
the work; and the criticism is aimed „gerade nicht gegen den *pontificatus* als Institution, sondern
gegen einzelne *pontifices*" (*"precisely not against the pontificate as an institution, but against
individual popes"*). Setz classes the work by Valla's own words for it: *oratio, qua nihil magis
oratorium scripsi*, plus *res canonici iuris* and *res theologiae*.
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 190

*Script:* **This cuts against the project's likely framing and must be said.** Setz's book argues the
treatise is a **rhetorical** work about truth and false authority, not a political pamphlet, and that
Valla never attacked the papacy as such. If the video calls it a political weapon, that is true of
what **later people did with it** (S25, S22), not of what it was.

## S30. Setz's verdict on the reception, in his own order

- **No debate in Valla's lifetime.** „Die Schrift hat weder sofort nach ihrer Veröffentlichung noch zu
  Lebzeiten des Verfassers eine für uns erkennbare Diskussion ausgelöst." — *"The treatise provoked no
  discussion discernible to us, either immediately after publication or in the author's lifetime."*
  The only critical response known from his lifetime is Quirini's 1447 dedication to Nicholas V, and
  it „blieb zudem ohne Wirkung und Echo, selbst Valla hat sie offenbar nicht kennengelernt."
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 191–192
- **Criticism sharpens only when the popes re-invoke the Donation politically**, and it comes first
  from **jurists** — Sangiorgi as the opinion-former, Bolognini treating it at length, the latter
  provoked while defending **another forgery**, the *Privilegium Theodosianum* of the University of Bologna.
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 192
- **What all the critics share**: „Allen Äußerungen ist gemein, daß sie die Handlungsweise Konstantins
  unter heilsgeschichtlichem Aspekt beurteilten, nicht wie Valla nach Wahrscheinlichkeit und
  Belegbarkeit des angeblich historischen Geschehens fragten **oder gar den vorliegenden Text der
  Urkunde kritisch prüften**." — *"Common to all the responses is that they judged Constantine's
  conduct under the aspect of salvation history, and did not, as Valla did, ask after the probability
  and documentability of the alleged historical events, let alone critically examine the text of the
  charter before them."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 192
- **Spread**: over two dozen manuscripts known; before the 1506 Strasbourg first printing the work was
  known from France to Bohemia — to chancellor Jean de Ganay, to the Utraquist leader Wenzeslaus
  Koranda the Younger, to Conrad Celtis. „Aus Gründen, die im Dunkeln liegen, ist die Erstausgabe fast
  ohne Widerhall geblieben." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 192–193
- **Hutten**: first edition **1518**; second required already in **1519**; „Er hat jedenfalls Vallas
  Werk nicht erst, wie man bisher meinte, bei Johannes Cochläus in Bologna kennengelernt."
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 193
- **The institutional turn, dated**: „**Erst in der Auseinandersetzung mit der Reformation ging dann
  auch die Kirche als Institution gegen Vallas Werk vor.** Als quasi offizielle Stellungnahme darf die
  Argumentation des Steuchus gelten, die 1547 im Druck erschien. Wenige Jahre später wurde Vallas
  Schrift auf den *Index librorum prohibitorum* gesetzt." — *"Only in the confrontation with the
  Reformation did the Church as an institution proceed against Valla's work. Steuco's argument, printed
  1547, may count as the quasi-official response. A few years later Valla's treatise was placed on the
  Index."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 193
- **The one-line summary of the whole reception**: „**Valla wurde zu einem Wahrheitszeugen für die
  Reformation und den nationalen Kampf gegen Rom.**" — *"Valla became a witness to truth for the
  Reformation and for the national struggle against Rome."* — with the immediate qualifier, credited
  to Cochläus alone at the time, that Valla criticised individual popes but did not question the
  papacy. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 193

*Script:* The reception arc in Setz's own shape, and it is a better structure than "he proved it and
the truth won": **written 1440 → no debate at all for ~50 years → jurists attack when the popes need
the Donation again → printed 1506 to silence → Hutten 1518 → weaponised across five languages in
sixteen years → and only THEN does the Church answer officially (1547) and ban it (1559).** The
institution's response is a century late and comes as a reaction to the Reformation, not to Valla.

# PART 5 — THE JURISTS (§ IV, "Juristen gegen Valla", printed pp. 123–136)

from the PDF text layer, printed page = PDF index − 23.*

## S31. Setz's framing: the qualified opponent never appeared

Valla himself classed the treatise as „res canonici iuris et theologiae, sed contra omnes canonistas
atque omnes theologos" (*"a matter of canon law and theology, but against all canonists and all
theologians"*), and Felino Sandei set the bar for any refutation: „rationes autem, quibus Valla non
certe omnino indocte motus est, …nemo unquam medulitus evacuabit, nisi pontificium ius et veram
theologie cognitionem adeptus fuerit" — *"the reasons by which Valla was moved, certainly not
altogether ignorantly, no one will ever gut to the marrow unless he has acquired papal law and a true
knowledge of theology."*

Setz's verdict on whether anyone cleared it:

> „Überblickt man die Äußerungen der Juristen zu Vallas Schrift, muß man feststellen, daß es diesen
> kanonistisch und theologisch geschulten Herausforderer **nicht gegeben hat**. Auch Sandei selbst hat
> diese Aufgabe nicht erfüllt, das von ihm versprochene *acuratissimum opusculum*… blieb
> offensichtlich ungeschrieben."

*"Surveying the jurists' statements on Valla's treatise, one must conclude that this
canonistically and theologically trained challenger did not exist. Sandei himself did not discharge
the task either; the most careful little work he promised evidently remained unwritten."*

**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 123–124

Setz also dates the jurists' reaction: „**Freilich haben sich die Juristen, sieht man einmal von Lauro
Quirini ab, auch erst relativ spät überhaupt herausgefordert gefühlt**; bei den Zeitgenossen Vallas
finden sich höchstens einige Anspielungen." — *"To be sure, the jurists — Lauro Quirini aside — only
felt challenged relatively late; among Valla's contemporaries there are at most a few allusions."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 124

*Script:* The section's own thesis, and it is usable as-is: the legal profession's answer to Valla was
late, and the man competent to give it never wrote.

## S32. The pre-Bolognini jurists: three postures worth one line each

- **Nicolaus de Tudeschis / Panormitanus** (d. 1445), the leading canonist of the age, and — like
  Valla — in King Alfonso's service. He argues from parallel passages of canon law against those
  „qui dicunt quod de illa donatione non reperiunt scripturam authenticam" (*"who say they find no
  authentic writing about that donation"*), and rules doubt itself near-heretical:
  „**nec parum ab errore distat revocare in dubium validitatem illius donationis**" — *"to call the
  validity of that donation into doubt is not far from error."* Setz notes this judgement was
  „später vielfach zitiert und nachgesprochen". He also denies the Roman people any constitutive role:
  „hodie Imperator non recognoscit Imperium a populo Romano, … sed ab ecclesia, a qua confirmatur et
  coronatur et deponitur" — *"today the Emperor does not acknowledge the Empire from the Roman people,
  but from the Church, by which he is confirmed, crowned and deposed."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 124 (Panormitanus, *Consilia*, Venice 1578, f. 57v; *Commentaria*, f. 52v)
- **Antonio Roselli** (d. 1466), *Monarchia sive Tractatus de potestate Imperatoris et Papae*: does
  not doubt the Donation but confines its effect to the Papal State. Setz's use of him is the reverse
  of the expected one — Roselli's inherited stock of arguments contains Valla's own points (transfer
  of rule by the *populus Romanus*, indivisibility of the empire, the emperor's limited power of
  disposal), which „zeigt damit auch, wie sehr **Valla seinerseits** mit der juristischen Diskussion um
  die Schenkung vertraut gewesen sein muß" (*"thus also shows how well Valla for his part must have
  known the juristic discussion of the donation"*). Roselli reads the imperial oath as
  *reverentiae iuramentum, non fidelitatis* and holds it cannot be extorted — „extorqueri non potest
  huiusmodi iuramentum" — the same *extorquere* Valla threw at the popes (Valla 18,7).
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 125–126 (Roselli, *Monarchia*, pp. 291ff., 298–300)
- **Andrea Barbazza** (d. 1479) — the interesting one, because he **concedes the method and then
  declines the conclusion**. On the stylistic critique: „superioribus annis fuit quidam poeta, quem
  aliqui mordent ut hereticum… conatus est ostendere quod verba apposita in d.c. constantinus non
  congruunt stilo, quod vigebat tunc temporis, et per consequens est aliena a stilo communi et
  usitato, et per consequens est suspecta de falso, **et pro certo est subtilis inductio**." — *"In
  recent years there was a certain poet, whom some bite at as a heretic… he tried to show that the
  words set down in the chapter 'Constantinus' do not agree with the style that flourished at that
  time, and consequently it is foreign to the common and customary style, and consequently is
  suspected of falsity — and this is certainly a subtle inference."* He even supplies a parallel: a
  Venetian merchant's forgery in his debt book, likewise exposed by a *subtilis investigator*; and he
  himself once lectured through every argument against the Donation („demonstravi vivis rationibus
  urgentibus et demonstrativis quod talis donatio non tenuit"). He still lands on
  „ego teneo illud quod tenet sacrosancta ecclesia" — *"I hold what the holy Church holds."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 126–127 (Maffei, *La Donazione*, pp. 313ff.)

*Script:* Barbazza is the beat of this trio: a professor of law grants that the argument from style is
a *subtilis inductio*, teaches it in a lecture, and then holds what the Church holds. Method accepted,
conclusion refused — that is a different failure from Cortese's refusal to read.

## S33. Bolognini: the longest legal answer, written to protect his own university's forgery

The facts, as Setz gives them (this is the coordinator's question 2):

> „Bolognini veröffentlichte 1491 einen Kommentar zu dem angeblichen Gründungsprivileg für die
> Universität Bologna, ausgestellt von Theodosius im Jahre 423. **Diese Urkunde wurde in den Jahren
> zwischen 1226 und 1234 gefälscht und fand 1437 Eingang in die offizielle Universitätsgeschichte.
> Bolognini hat selbst an dieser Universität studiert und gelehrt.**"

*"Bolognini published in 1491 a commentary on the supposed foundation privilege for the University of
Bologna, issued by Theodosius in the year 423. This charter was forged in the years between 1226 and
1234 and entered the official university history in 1437. Bolognini himself had studied and taught at
this university."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 127 (crit. ed. of the *Privilegium Theodosianum*: Fasoli–Pighi pp. 60–64; on its origin and afterlife pp. 79ff. and Borst pp. 24ff.)

**The hinge is a single word in the forged charter.** The *Privilegium Theodosianum* reads
*In romana nostra excellentissima civitate*; the **nostra** makes Theodosius lord of Rome, which
cannot stand if Constantine had already ceded the West. Bolognini lists escape routes for the *nostra*
— derivation from the title *Imperator Romanorum*, imperial *protectio* over city and church — and
then reaches the one he will not take:

> „hec ultima responsio mihi non placet, nam **ego tenui semper et teneo quod donatio illa omnino
> valuerit** et ostendam apertissime conclusionem hanc esse verissimam iuribus et rationibus."

*"This last answer does not please me, for I have always held and do hold that that donation was
wholly valid, and I shall show most plainly that this conclusion is entirely true by laws and by
reasons."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 127 (Bolognini, *Privilegium*, f. 14vb)

Of the Donation's critics he names only Valla, „qui fuit unicus et singularis in hac sua opinione"
— as the only one who questioned not merely the validity but the fact. He then quotes or paraphrases
most of Valla's treatise and answers point by point. His pupil Angelo Uggeri, who compiled the index,
scored it: „per quadraginta columnas reperies istud examinatum copiosissime et ibi habebis **tria et
centum argumenta Laurentii vallensis**, que omnia domini Ludovici ingenio et accurata diligentia
prostrata sunt" — *"through forty columns you will find this examined most copiously, and there you
will have Lorenzo Vallensis's hundred and three arguments, all of which have been laid flat by
Lord Lodovico's talent and careful diligence."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 127 (Bolognini, *Privilegium*, register s.v. *donatio imperatoris*)

## S34. ⚠ The irony is Setz's, not ours — but he marks it with a word, not a sentence

The coordinator asked whether the "attacked Valla while defending another forgery" observation is
Setz's or our inference. **It is Setz's**, and he marks it three times, though he never uses the word
*Ironie*:

1. He puts the point structurally: „Bologninis Auseinandersetzung mit Valla ist zwar die
   ausführlichste von juristischer Seite überhaupt, doch **verdient sie weniger wegen der
   Argumentation Beachtung als wegen des Zusammenhangs, in dem sie steht**." — *"Bolognini's
   engagement with Valla is indeed the most detailed from the legal side, but it deserves attention
   less for its argument than for the context in which it stands."*
   **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 127
2. He uses the loaded adverb **ausgerechnet** ("of all things") in the Rückblick: „Lodovico
   Bologninis, der sich **ausgerechnet** im Zusammenhang mit der Verteidigung einer anderen Fälschung,
   des sog. *Privilegium Theodosianum* für die Universität Bologna, zu einer Auseinandersetzung mit
   Valla herausgefordert fühlte." — *"Lodovico Bolognini, who felt challenged into an engagement with
   Valla, of all things, in connection with the defence of another forgery, the so-called Privilegium
   Theodosianum for the University of Bologna."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 192
3. He lets a contemporary say it. The Bolognese chronicler Hyeronimus de Bursellis (d. 1497), within
   a few years: „**Et mirum quippe, ut tantus vir non perpendeat huius exemplaris aut transcripti
   commentitiam falsitatem.**" — *"And it is indeed astonishing that so great a man should not weigh
   the fabricated falsity of this exemplar or transcript."*
   **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 130 (Bursellis, *Cronica*, RIS n.e. 23,2, pp. 9f.)

**What Setz does NOT say, and the script must not put in his mouth:** he does not claim Bolognini
knew the Theodosian charter was forged, and he does not claim the Donation defence was *motivated* by
that knowledge. The chain he does establish is: Bolognini's own university's founding charter was
forged (a modern datation, 1226–1234); Bolognini commented on it as genuine; a word in it conflicted
with the Donation; so he had to defend the Donation; and a contemporary remarked on his failure to
see the forgery. That chain is enough — do not upgrade it to conscious bad faith.

**[CONTESTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 127, 130, 192 — the forgery datation is modern scholarship (Fasoli–Pighi, Borst), against Bolognini's and the university's own 1437 acceptance

*Script:* This is the beat of the section, and it is a mechanism beat: **the defence of one forgery
required the defence of another.** Forged documents form a load-bearing structure — pull one and the
next one moves. Say it that way, and attribute the datation to modern editors, not to Bolognini's
contemporaries.

## S35. Bolognini's actual arguments — and the two places Valla beat him

His method is to devalue the philology by appealing to sense: „iste auctor nimis scrupulose agit circa
has subtilitates verborum et sillabarum et non bene, **quia sensum ipsum considerare debemus et non
tantum ornatum**" — *"this author proceeds too scrupulously about these subtleties of words and
syllables, and not well, because we must consider the sense itself and not only the ornament."*
Alongside runs a personal charge — Valla wrote „satis impudenter… sub quadam specie veritatis ac
iustitie defensande" (*"impudently enough… under a certain appearance of defending truth and
justice"*) — and repeated appeals to his hatred of the popes, which Setz says Bolognini invokes to
explain why Valla cannot grasp the charter's meaning. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 128 (Bolognini, *Privilegium*, ff. 14vb, 18rb)

The individual replies, in Setz's account:
- **Eutropius** (Valla's chief contemporary witness) is devalued: „multa alia non scripsit, que alibi
  reperiuntur" — *"he did not write many other things which are found elsewhere."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 128 (f. 16va)
- **Constantinople** gets a double answer: the city already existed as Byzantium, and Constantine had
  in those very days resolved it should bear his name. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 129 (f. 18va)
- **The charter on Peter's tomb**: Bolognini is convinced it was still there in Valla's day, then
  hedges — „si non reperiretur hodie non esset admiratione dignum propter temporis longitudinem"
  (*"if it were not found today, that would not be worth wondering at, on account of the length of
  time"*) — and closes with the popes' present rule as proof enough. Setz calls this and the next a
  **Zirkelschluß**: against Valla's point that no transfer of rule is attested, Bolognini cites the
  charter's own word *tradimus*. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 129 (ff. 18va, 22va)

**Where Bolognini scores.** Setz credits him with one hit: „daß er Valla **mit Recht** entgegenhält, er
habe dem Senat und Volk von Rom eine Rolle zuerkannt, die diese zur Zeit Konstantins nicht mehr
gespielt hätten." — *"that he rightly objects to Valla that he assigned to the Senate and people of
Rome a role they no longer played in Constantine's time."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 129 (f. 17va)

**Where Valla taught him.** Bolognini accepts Valla's correction that *Augustus* is not from *augere*,
dismissing the *imperitorum ethimologia*; his own etymology (from *avium gustu*, with the Greek
σεβαστός) tracks Valla 44,5. Setz: „Die Anlehnung an Valla ist unverkennbar, doch an keiner Stelle
wird deutlich, woher Bolognini seine Kenntnisse hat." — *"The dependence on Valla is unmistakable, but
nowhere is it made clear where Bolognini got his knowledge."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 129 (ff. 25ra, 4r)

He closes by submitting to authority, and Setz flags the oddity of the name he invokes:
„remittens me tamen in hoc ultimo… decisioni sacrosancte romane ecclesie, **in cuius terris sumus ut
dicebat ipse Bartolus**" — *"submitting myself however in this last matter to the decision of the
holy Roman Church, in whose lands we are, as Bartolus himself used to say."* Setz: „Die ausdrückliche
Nennung des Bartolus **muß überraschen**, hatte er doch mit dieser Wendung seine Zweifel an der
Gültigkeit der Schenkung verborgen" — *"The explicit naming of Bartolus must surprise, since it was
with this very phrase that he had concealed his doubts about the validity of the donation."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 130 (f. 25va)

**Setz's verdict on its effect:** „Bologninis Kritik an Valla ist zwar später noch verschiedentlich
zitiert worden, **hat aber letztlich keine Wirkung gehabt**." — *"Bolognini's criticism of Valla was
indeed cited variously later, but ultimately had no effect."*
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 130

*Script:* Usable pair: the longest legal refutation ever written against Valla submits, at its close,
to the Church "in whose lands we are" — quoting the very jurist who had used that formula to hide his
own doubts. And Setz's own tally of the exchange is that Bolognini won one point (the Senate) and lost
one (the *Augustus* etymology), which he then adopted.

## S36. Sangiorgi / Alexandrinus: what the legal counter-case actually is

Setz: „**Die einflußreichste Stellungnahme von kanonistischer Seite** stammt von Giovanni Antonio
Sangiorgi (d. 1509)." Professor of canon law at Pavia from 1474; bishop of Alessandria 1483 — hence
"Alexandrinus"; cardinal 1493. The text is his **commentary on the *Decretum Gratiani*, dedicated to
Alexander VI**. Setz notes he engages Valla „verhältnismäßig ausführlich… **auch auf solche, die sich
nicht juristisch oder theologisch entkräften lassen**" (*"relatively fully… including those that
cannot be defused juridically or theologically"*). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 130 (Alexandrinus, *Commentaria super decretum*, 1511, ff. 219v–221r)

**The load-bearing legal move is that the Donation was not a gift at all.** The whole case rests on
*restitutio*:

> „quia potius dicitur agnoscere bonam fidem et relaxare quod indebite per eum et antecessores suos
> erat occupatum."

*"because he is rather said to be acknowledging good faith and releasing what had been unlawfully
occupied by him and his predecessors."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 130 (f. 219v)

Setz spells out why this settles the legal question in advance: „Mit dieser Auffassung der Schenkung
als *restitutio* ist die Position zur Widerlegung Vallas bezeichnet." — *"With this conception of the
donation as a restitution, the position for refuting Valla is fixed."* Constantine had no divine title
to rule, only „consensus populi et principum ac universi senatus urbis Rome"; because the donation was
made with that same consent, it is irrevocable — „Damit ist allen geantwortet, die die Schenkung als
*alienatio damnosa* beurteilen." **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 131 (f. 220r)

**The eight answers, and the devices they use.** Sangiorgi singles Valla out by name:
„et quia inter huiusmodi obtrectatores romane ecclesie pre ceteris liberius in verba blasphemie
Laurentius valla prorupit, **ne simplicium corda eius falsis dogmatibus seducantur**, ad illa
respondebo" — *"and because among such disparagers of the Roman church Lorenzo Valla above the rest
burst out too freely into words of blasphemy, lest the hearts of the simple be seduced by his false
doctrines, I shall answer them."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 131 (f. 220r)

- **Why the emperors kept ruling after the gift**: „ex tacita permissione Silvestri" — *"by Silvester's
  tacit permission."* Setz calls the solution „so überraschend wie einfach."
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 131 (f. 220r)
- **Why Silvester could not refuse**: it was not a *nova acquisitio* but a *restitutio*.
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 131
- **Why no formal taking of possession was needed**: „nam clarum est quod favore ecclesie et pie cause
  valet donatio etiam non data presentia, quando deo dirigitur" — *"for it is clear that in favour of
  the church and a pious cause a donation is valid even without delivery of possession, when it is
  directed to God."* Valla's insistence on formal *traditio* is turned into proof of ignorance:
  „adducit… nonnulla fundamenta legalia et **clare demonstrat se ignarum iuris**" — *"he adduces some
  legal foundations and clearly shows himself ignorant of law."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 131 (f. 220r)
- **Why the histories are silent** — the conspiracy answer, and the sentence with the longest afterlife:
  „quod mihi non videtur mirum, nam in ultimis annis imperii Constantini ista gesta fuerunt et
  imperatores qui Constantino successerunt **curarunt, ut ista occultarentur et abolerentur, nec de eis
  mentio in historiis fieret**." — *"which does not seem to me surprising, for these things were done
  in the last years of Constantine's reign, and the emperors who succeeded Constantine took care that
  they should be concealed and abolished, and that no mention of them should be made in the
  histories."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 132 (f. 220v)
- **Why Eusebius contradicts the story** — same device, applied to a manuscript: „nec mirandum esset,
  si ab emulis romane ecclesie Eusebii liber in hoc corruptus et depravatus fuisset" — *"nor would it
  be surprising if Eusebius's book had been corrupted and depraved in this by rivals of the Roman
  church."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 132
- **The *palea* status** is no ground for doubt: other texts *magne autoritatis* are also called *paleae*.
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 132
- **The Latin** is conceded — „Es wird zugegeben, daß der Text *rudis aliquo modo* ist" — and turned
  around into the forger's-competence argument: „**nemo enim dubitet, quod si ecclesia romana
  donationem istam fingere voluisset elegantius et cum maiori solemnitate omnia composuisset.**" —
  *"for let no one doubt that if the Roman church had wanted to fabricate that donation, it would have
  composed it all more elegantly and with greater solemnity."*
  **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 133 (f. 221r)

*Script:* The three devices are quotable as a set, because they are all the same move —
**every absence of evidence is converted into evidence of suppression**: the histories are silent
because the successor emperors suppressed it; Eusebius contradicts it because enemies corrupted his
book; the Latin is bad because a real forger would have done better. That is the legal counter-case
the project lacked, and it is an argument, not a smear.

## S37. Why Setz calls Sangiorgi opinion-forming — and who follows him

> „Die Ausführungen des Alexandrinus haben einen großen Einfluß gehabt. **Der Hinweis darauf hat
> manchem eine eigene Argumentation erspart.**"

*"Alexandrinus's arguments had a great influence. A reference to them spared many people an argument
of their own."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 133

The named followers, in order:
1. **Bartholomaeus Pincernus** cites him expressly as *iuris utriusque consultissimum* — and Pincernus
   is the man „der mit seiner Übersetzung des Constitutum Constantini auf der Grundlage der
   griechischen Fassung eine Hauptstütze für die nachfolgende Diskussion lieferte" (*"who with his
   translation of the Constitutum Constantini on the basis of the Greek version supplied a mainstay
   for the subsequent discussion"*). **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 133
2. **Enrico Boccella**, *In Constantini Imperatoris Donationem Iuris Utriusque Praxis* (Lucca 1539) —
   Setz prints a **two-column collation** showing Boccella's text running almost word for word into
   Sangiorgi's suppression sentence, and concludes: „Boccellas umfangreiche Ausführungen erweisen sich
   so als eine **wertlose Kompilation**." (*"Boccella's extensive arguments thus prove to be a
   worthless compilation."*) Boccella also reprints Pincernus's translation without naming him, and —
   Setz's find — quotes several sentences of **Valla** as if they were **Cusa's**.
   **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 133–134 (collation at p. 134; the Valla-as-Cusa slip at p. 134, Valla 4,35–4,41)
3. **Tiresio Foscarari** (1549) repeats the same misattribution of Valla to Cusa, „Die gleiche
   Verwechslung findet sich in ähnlicher Weise bei Tiresio Foscarari." Setz notes the reading
   *ruinis/minis* in his Valla text points to a **Hutten** edition — i.e. the defenders were reading
   Valla in the Lutheran knight's edition. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 135 and n. 18
4. **Francesco Borsati**, *Consilia* (1572), by which point the argument has become a filing system:
   he lists all authorities „qui baptismum, apostolorum visionem, donationem immensam Constantini
   Magni… comprobant", sorted into *Pontifices, Theologi, Imperatores, Germani Imperatores,
   Historiographi, Leges Pontificum et Imperatorum, Auctoritates Canonistarum* and *Auctoritates
   scribentium in iure civili*. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 136

Setz's summary of what the legal debate became: „Im Laufe der Jahrzehnte scheint die Diskussion um die
Konstantinische Schenkung bei den Juristen nur zu einer noch umfangreicheren **Auseinandersetzung mit
Autoritäten und Meinungen** geworden zu sein. **Valla war dabei nur einer aus der Reihe der Gegner.**"
*"Over the decades the discussion of the Donation among the jurists seems only to have become an ever
more extensive engagement with authorities and opinions. Valla was in it merely one of a row of
opponents."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 136

*Script:* Two beats here. (a) Sangiorgi is opinion-forming because citing him **replaced** arguing —
Setz's own sentence. (b) The two independent misattributions of Valla to Cusa (Boccella 1539,
Foscarari 1549) show the defenders were working from compilations, not from the treatises — a concrete
illustration of the copy-chain the channel likes.

## S38. Zasius: the jurist who read it twice and refused to care

Bonifacius Amerbach gave Ulrich Zasius a copy of Hutten's second edition in December 1520, unsure what
to make of it: „habet, ni fallar, aliqua noster libellus, si non veritatem, certe veritatis imaginem
preseferentia" — *"our little book has, if I am not mistaken, some things displaying, if not the
truth, certainly the appearance of truth."* Zasius already knew it, reread it, and answered:

> „Lectum (Valle libellum) olim hoc momento perfunctorie relegi videturque michi, si locus sit
> probabilibus argumentis, utroque fune urgere, maxime quod et ceteri viri melioris note eadem sunt in
> opinione. Sed super huiusmodi vanis rebus, cum vite nostre tempora pro eorum compendio vix utilibus
> sufficiunt, **conflictari semper duxi supervacaneum, qui odi, quidquid plus ostentationis prestat
> quam rei**."

*"Having read Valla's book long ago, I have at this moment reread it cursorily, and it seems to me
that, if there is room for probable arguments, it presses from both ends — chiefly because other men
of better note are of the same opinion. But over such empty matters, since the times of our life
scarcely suffice for useful things, I have always thought it superfluous to fight — I who hate
whatever offers more display than substance."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 136 (Amerbachkorrespondenz II, Nr. 709, pp. 220f.)

Setz adds from Zasius's Digest commentary that he held the Donation damaged the *imperium* but thought
further discussion unwarranted, *quia est inutile*. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 137 n. 27 (Zasius, *Opera omnia* 1550, col. 393)

*Script:* Optional closing note on the jurists: the one who concedes Valla is probably right
(„ceteri viri melioris note eadem sunt in opinione") is the one who declines to write about it,
because it is *inutile*. Silence here is not suppression; it is a professional judgement that the
question no longer pays.

## S40. The Greek argument: the previous pass's conclusion holds, with one correction

Direct check of the Greek vocabulary inside §§ on Bolognini and Sangiorgi proper (PDF 150–157):
three hits only, and the matched forms are — `grecos` (p. 129, **Bolognini's Augustus etymology,
borrowed from Valla**, not a Greek-Constitutum argument), `griechischen` (p. 133, **Pincernus's**
translation), `Graeco` (p. 134, **Boccella** reprinting Pincernus). **Neither Bolognini nor Sangiorgi
argues from a Greek version of the Constitutum in Setz's account.**
**[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz pp. 129, 133, 134 (string search over pp. 127–134)

**The correction to the earlier pass (S16) is one of direction.** The Greek line and the legal line do
not merely run in parallel — they join, and Sangiorgi is upstream. Pincernus, who supplied the
Greek-based translation that became „eine Hauptstütze für die nachfolgende Diskussion", cites
**Sangiorgi** as his legal authority, *iuris utriusque consultissimum*. So the canonist's *restitutio*
case and the philologist-proof Greek text arrive in later hands as a single package — visible in
Boccella (1539), who prints Pincernus's translation and copies Sangiorgi's sentences, and in Foscarari
(1549), who does both again. **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 133; package visible at pp. 133–135

Setz's own placement of the Greek argument's development is unchanged and remains at p. 123:
„Pincernus und Galateo haben wie Quirini mit ihren Hinweisen auf die griechische Überlieferung **kaum
mehr als Material bereitgestellt**… Ausführlicher verwertet hat die griechische Version erst
Augustinus Steuchus." — *"Pincernus and Galateo, like Quirini, with their references to the Greek
transmission supplied hardly more than material… Only Augustinus Steuchus exploited the Greek version
more fully."* **[INSPECTED]** · locator: Setz p. 123

*Script:* Corrected formulation: **Quirini (1447) and Pincernus produce the Greek material; Sangiorgi
produces the legal doctrine; Boccella and Foscarari transmit them as one bundle; Steuco (1547) is the
first to work the Greek properly.** The canonist and the Greek text are not rival defences — they are
the two halves of the defence the sixteenth century inherited.
