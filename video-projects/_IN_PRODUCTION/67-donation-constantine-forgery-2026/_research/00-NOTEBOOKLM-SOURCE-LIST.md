## TIER 1 — PRIMARY SOURCES

### [P1] *Constitutum Constantini* — the forged document itself
- **Critical edition:** Horst Fuhrmann (ed.), *Das Constitutum Constantini*, MGH Fontes iuris
  Germanici antiqui in usum scholarum 10 (Hannover, 1968)
- **Access:** ⭐ **FREE** — Monumenta Germaniae Historica digital: `dmgh.de` (`mgh_fontes_iuris_10`)
- **Language:** Latin — **the creator verifies Latin.** The forged text can be read in the original.
- **Why:** This is the object under examination. Every proof in the video is a claim about *this text*.
  The video cannot show a translation and call it evidence.
- **Provenance:** Fuhrmann is the standard modern editor; the MGH is the reference series for medieval
  Latin documents. Bias/proximity: none material — a critical edition, apparatus visible.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free]`

### [P2] Lorenzo Valla, *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione* (1439–40)
- **Latin original:** the 1520 Hutten print is digitised — Bayerische Staatsbibliothek,
  `daten.digitale-sammlungen.de` (bsb00088524)
- **Modern bilingual:** *On the Donation of Constantine*, trans. G. W. Bowersock, I Tatti Renaissance
  Library (Harvard University Press, 2007/2008) — **facing Latin and English**
- **Access:** Latin original ⭐ FREE; Harvard edition 🔴 buy
- **Why:** ⭐⭐⭐ **This IS the argument.** Everything else is commentary on it. The facing-page edition
  lets the creator check the English against the Latin himself — which is the channel's whole product.
- **Provenance:** Valla wrote it in 1440 while employed by **Alfonso V of Aragon, who was in armed
  dispute with the Pope.** That is a live conflict-of-interest and the video must state it. Intent:
  polemical as well as scholarly.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free Latin + buy Harvard]`

### [P3] Nicholas of Cusa, *De Concordantia Catholica* (1433), Book III
- **English:** *The Catholic Concordance*, ed./trans. Paul E. Sigmund, Cambridge Texts in the History
  of Political Thought (Cambridge University Press, 1991)
- **Why:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The priority question lives here.** Six years before Valla. Whether Cusa's argument
  is philological or historical decides whether this video has one protagonist or three.
- **Provenance:** Written for the Council of Basel — Cusa was arguing a conciliarist case against papal
  monarchy. Same conflict-of-interest problem as Valla, from the opposite direction.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [P4] Reginald Pecock, *The Repressor of Over Much Blaming of the Clergy* (c. 1449–55)
- **Edition:** ed. Churchill Babington, Rolls Series (London, 1860), 2 vols
- **Access:** ⭐ **FREE** — public domain, archive.org / HathiTrust
- **Why:** The third independent proof, in Middle English rather than Latin. Tests whether the
  convergence is real or an artifact of one scholarly network.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free]`

### [P5] Caesar Baronius, *Annales Ecclesiastici* (1588–1607) — the concession volume
- **Why:** The first public admission by a Church official, reportedly ~150 years after Valla. If the
  video claims the Church conceded, it must show where.
- **Access:** early modern prints digitised; ⭐ likely free
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free if locatable]`

### [P6] Jean Mabillon, *De re diplomatica* (Paris, 1681)
- **Why:** The founding text of **diplomatics** — the discipline that formalised what Valla did ad hoc,
  241 years later. This is the closing beat of the video made showable: the method becoming a science.
- **Access:** digitised, ⭐ likely free
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free if locatable]`

## TIER 2 — ACADEMIC

### [A1] ⭐ Anthony Grafton, *Forgers and Critics: Creativity and Duplicity in Western Scholarship*
- **Publisher:** Princeton University Press, 1990 (rev. ed. 2019)
- **Why:** ⭐⭐⭐ **The single most important acquisition on this list, and the Phase 1 brief missed it.**
  Grafton is the leading authority on forgery and its detection in Western scholarship. This book *is*
  the video's thesis, examined by the person best placed to examine it — including the uncomfortable
  half: that forgery and criticism grew up together, each driving the other.
- **Provenance:** Grafton, Princeton, historian of scholarship. Peer-reviewed university press.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A2] Anthony Grafton, *Defenders of the Text: The Traditions of Scholarship in an Age of Science, 1450–1800*
- **Publisher:** Harvard University Press, 1991
- **Why:** The longer arc — what humanist textual criticism became. Supports the descent line to
  diplomatics and palaeography.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A3] ⭐ Joseph M. Levine, "Reginald Pecock and Lorenzo Valla on the Donation of Constantine"
- **In:** *Studies in the Renaissance* 20 (1973)
- **Why:** ⭐⭐⭐ **Directly compares the independent proofs.** The priority question's most likely
  single answer. JSTOR.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A4] Johannes Fried, *"Donation of Constantine" and "Constitutum Constantini"*
- **Publisher:** Walter de Gruyter, 2007
- **Why:** The modern reassessment — including whether the Donation and the Constitutum are one
  document or two, and when it was actually made. Bears directly on the "700 years" claim in the title.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A5] Wolfram Setz, *Lorenzo Vallas Schrift gegen die Konstantinische Schenkung*
- **Publisher:** De Gruyter / Niemeyer, 1975
- **Language:** **German — the creator verifies German.**
- **Why:** The detailed textual study, and the likeliest source on the publication delay (1440 → print).
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A6] Salvatore I. Camporeale, "Lorenzo Valla's *Oratio* on the Pseudo-Donation of Constantine"
- **In:** *Journal of the History of Ideas* 57:1 (1996)
- **Why:** Argues the treatise as rhetoric as well as philology — a corrective to reading it purely as
  proto-science. Steelmans against our own thesis, which is why it matters.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A7] Lodi Nauta, *In Defense of Common Sense: Lorenzo Valla's Humanist Critique of Scholastic Philosophy*
- **Publisher:** Harvard University Press, 2009
- **Why:** How Valla actually thought. Guards against turning him into a modern forensic scientist.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A8] Christopher B. Coleman, *The Treatise of Lorenzo Valla on the Donation of Constantine*
- **Publisher:** Yale University Press, 1922
- **Access:** ⭐ **FREE** — public domain, archive.org
- **Why:** The classic English edition with facing Latin. Superseded by Bowersock but free, and useful
  as a cross-check on translation choices.
- **Provenance:** 1922 — a century old. Use for the Latin and as a second translation, not for
  interpretation.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE — free]`

### [A9] Carlo Ginzburg — on proof, evidence and the historian's method
- **Candidates:** *Threads and Traces: True False Fictive* (California, 2012); "Checking the Evidence:
  The Judge and the Historian" (*Critical Inquiry*, 1991)
- **Why:** The philosophical frame — what counts as proof in history, by a historian who writes about
  exactly that. Feeds the closing beat.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]` ⚠ confirm which volume carries the Valla material before buying

### [A10] Riccardo Fubini, "Humanism and Truth: Valla Writes Against the Donation of Constantine"
- **In:** *Journal of the History of Ideas* 57:1 (1996)
- **Why:** Companion to Camporeale in the same issue; contests the motive and the dating.
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`

### [A11] Paul E. Sigmund, *Nicholas of Cusa and Medieval Political Thought*
- **Publisher:** Harvard University Press, 1963
- **Why:** Context for what Cusa was doing in 1433 and why. Pairs with [P3].
- **Status:** `[ACQUIRE]`
