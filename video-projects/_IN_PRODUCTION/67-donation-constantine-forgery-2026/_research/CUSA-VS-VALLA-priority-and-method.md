# Cusa vs Valla — Priority and Method

**Written:** 2026-08-01 · **Stage:** Phase 1.5 (primary texts read directly; Phase 2 NLM verification still required)
**Brief:** `RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md` §"THE OPEN QUESTION THAT SHAPES THE VIDEO"
**Question:** did Nicholas of Cusa use the same method Valla did, or a different one?

---

## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

**Different in kind — verified against both primary texts.** Cusa's case against the Donation is
an argument from the documentary record: he searched the conciliar acts, the papal correspondence
and the patristic corpus, found no trace of it, found a later papal decree that enumerated the
Roman church's holdings and did *not* mention it, and concluded the Constantine material was
apocryphal. He never examines the document's Latin. Valla runs the same historical arguments **and
then does something no one had done: he convicts the document out of its own sentences** — its
vocabulary, its tenses, its self-contradictions, its impossible geography. Reginald Pecock, working
independently in England in the 1450s, used Cusa's method, not Valla's.

**But the framing correction the project needs is not the one it expected.** The threat to "Valla
invented source criticism" is not Cusa's priority — it is that (a) Riccardo Fubini argues Valla was
probably *working from* Cusa, which damages the "three independent discoveries" premise of Option B
at least as much as it damages Option A; and (b) Anthony Grafton's account gives the founding of
systematic philology to **Poliziano**, not Valla. Both are handled in §5 and §6.

**Status ladder used throughout:** ESTABLISHED (read in the primary text or asserted by a named
specialist with a locator) · CONTESTED (named scholars disagree) · UNKNOWN (nobody has it) ·
UNCHECKED (I did not test it — named as such).

## 1. WHAT CUSA ACTUALLY ARGUED

### 1.1 The text, located

*De concordantia catholica*, **Book III, chapter II**. I read the Latin critical text directly via
the **Cusanus-Portal** (`https://cusanus-portal.de/content/suche.php`, POST search over the Kallen
*Opera omnia* text, queried 2026-08-01). Citation form for the script:
**DCC III.2, § 307** (Kallen ed., *Nicolai de Cusa Opera omnia* XIV/3; the portal marks the printed
page as `[p.335]`).

⚠ **Citation caution.** Rojas Donat & de Toro (2023, n. 64) cite this as "*De concordantia
catholica* 14, ed. Kallen, *Opera Omnia*, vol. 3, **307**." Their "307" is the **paragraph** number,
not a page — the portal shows § 307 sitting on printed page 335. Do not render it as "p. 307."

### 1.2 The chapter heading — Cusa states his own thesis

Verbatim Latin, DCC III, chapter list (portal, `[p.19]`):

> *Cap. II. Quod imperialis potestas ordinate ex dono Constantini in occiduis a papa non dependet,
> et quod ipsa famosa donatio apocrypha sit, sicut quaedam alia, et ibi historiae quaedam verae de
> Pippino et Carolo breviter connexae ad sciendum, quomodo temporalia primum ad Romanam ecclesiam
> devenerunt.*

("That imperial power in the West does not depend on the pope by Constantine's gift, and that that
famous donation is apocryphal, like certain others; and there some true histories of Pippin and
Charles are briefly joined on, so that it may be known how temporalities first came to the Roman
church.")

**Two things follow immediately.** (i) The chapter is *about* imperial independence — the Donation
is the obstacle he is clearing, not the object of a textual study. (ii) He signals his replacement
account in the same breath: the papacy's temporalities actually came from **Pippin and
Charlemagne**. That is a source-substitution argument, not a language argument.

### 1.3 The verdict sentence — verbatim

DCC III.2, § 307 (portal text, whitespace normalised):

> *Vidi etiam decretum Leonis papae in synodo Romana cum subscriptione episcoporum et clericorum et
> civium Romanorum, ubi Leo papa Ottoni I. restituit omnia loca per Pippinum et Carolum et Robertum
> reges sancto Petro data. Et nominantur in eodem decreto omnia loca, et **nullam facit de donatione
> Constantini mentionem. Sunt meo iudicio illa de Constantino apocrypha**, sicut fortassis etiam
> quaedam alia longa et magna scripta sanctis Clementi et Anacleto papae attributa, in quibus
> volentes Romanam sedem omni laude dignam plus, quam ecclesiae sanctae expedit et decet, exaltare
> se penitus aut quasi fundant.*

("I have also seen a decree of Pope Leo in a Roman synod, subscribed by bishops, clergy and Roman
citizens, in which Pope Leo restored to Otto I all the places given to St Peter by kings Pippin,
Charles and Robert. And all the places are named in that same decree, **and it makes no mention
whatever of the donation of Constantine. Those things about Constantine are in my judgement
apocryphal** — as perhaps also are certain other long and large writings attributed to the holy
popes Clement and Anacletus, in which men wishing to exalt the Roman see, worthy of all praise,
further than is expedient or fitting for the holy church, plant or all but plant themselves.")

**Note the analogy he reaches for**: the Pseudo-Clementine and Pseudo-Anacletan decretals. Cusa is
classifying the Donation as one item in a known genre of over-enthusiastic papal pseudepigrapha.
That is a **canon-law / transmission** judgement.

### 1.4 The next paragraph — his stated method

§ 308 opens the programme explicitly:

> *Si quis illas omnes scripturas illis sanctis attributas diligenter perlegeret et eorum tempora
> ad illa scripta applicaret ac deinde in opusculis omnium sanctorum patrum, qui usque ad
> Augustinum, Hieronymum et Ambrosium fuere, ac etiam de gestis conciliorum...*

("If anyone were to read through carefully all those writings attributed to those saints, and apply
their *dates* to those writings, and then [look] in the works of all the holy fathers down to
Augustine, Jerome and Ambrose, and also in the acts of the councils...")

⚠ *ac eorum tempora ad illa scripta applicaret* — "apply their dates to those writings" — is the
closest Cusa comes to an internal-dating criterion, and it is **chronological**, not linguistic: does
the ascribed author's lifetime fit the document's content? It is the same test Pecock runs. It is
not Valla's test.

### 1.5 Cusa's grounds, itemised

| # | Ground | Type | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | No testimony to the donation in the **acts of the councils** | historical / argument from silence in the official record | ESTABLISHED (§308; Rojas Donat & de Toro 2023, p. 18) |
| C2 | No testimony in the **papal correspondence** | historical / silence | ESTABLISHED (same) |
| C3 | No testimony in the **Fathers down to Augustine, Jerome, Ambrose** | historical / silence | ESTABLISHED (§308) |
| C4 | **Pope Leo's synodal decree restoring holdings to Otto I enumerates every place — and never mentions Constantine's donation** | documentary / silence in the *decisive* record | ESTABLISHED (§307, verbatim above) — this is his single strongest and most concrete proof |
| C5 | It belongs to a **known genre of forged papal decretals** (Pseudo-Clement, Pseudo-Anacletus) | canonical / transmission | ESTABLISHED (§307) |
| C6 | The papacy's temporalities in fact came from **Pippin, Charlemagne** (and Robert) | historical / positive counter-account | ESTABLISHED (chapter heading + §307) |

| — | Anything about vocabulary, syntax, style, or Latinity | **ABSENT** | ESTABLISHED by direct check — see below |

**Direct absence check (per ADR-0020).** I searched the Cusanus-Portal Latin index for `apocrypha`,
`apocryphus`, `donatio`, `Constantinus`, `Silvester`, `Pippinus`, `gesta Silvestri`. The Donation
material surfaces only at DCC III chapter-heading and §§ 306–308. Nothing in that stretch discusses
the *language* of the document. **Caveat:** the portal returns a maximum of 3 hits per query and
several lemma queries returned nothing at all (indexing behaviour, not proof of absence in the
work). A page-by-page read of DCC III.2 in Kallen or Sigmund is the Phase 2 confirmation. Treat
"Cusa makes no linguistic argument" as **ESTABLISHED-pending-confirmation**, corroborated
independently by Rojas Donat & de Toro (2023) and by Coleman (1922).

### 1.6 The date: 1433

**ESTABLISHED, with a wobble worth one sentence of care.** *De concordantia catholica* was written
for and presented to the Council of Basel, dated 1433 by Rojas Donat & de Toro (2023, p. 18) and by
the standard literature. Renna (2014) writes "sometime after 1433" when describing when *Valla* met
the text, not when Cusa wrote it. Script-safe phrasing: **"in 1433, at the Council of Basel."** Do
not say "published" — it circulated in manuscript; print came much later.

## 2. WHAT VALLA ACTUALLY ARGUED

Verified against the **Latin and English of the Coleman edition** (Christopher B. Coleman, *The
Treatise of Lorenzo Valla on the Donation of Constantine*, Yale University Press, 1922 — Latin and
English on facing pages; public domain, Project Gutenberg #70092). Page numbers below are Coleman's
English pagination, which appears in the file as bracketed page marks. **Bowersock's I Tatti edition
(Harvard 2007) numbers the text by § 1–97**; the script should carry both.

### 2.1 The brief's six claims, checked one by one

| # | Claim in `00-PRELIMINARY-BRIEF.md` | Verdict | Locator + what the text actually says |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | **8th-century Latin, not 4th** | **CONFIRMED, but reframe** | Coleman pp. 93–95, 105–107, 115–117. Valla's own phrasing is *barbarisms* and stylistic incompetence — "I will not speak here of the barbarisms in [the forger's] language" (p. 93). He does **not** state a dated periodisation of Latin ("this is 8th-century Latin"). The century-dating is **Coleman's editorial apparatus**, e.g. his note that *seu* meaning "and" is an eighth-century usage "for dating the forgery." ⚠ Do not put "Valla dated the Latin to the eighth century" in a quote card. What Valla says is that no Roman emperor or chancery wrote like this. |
| V2 | **"Satrap"** | **CONFIRMED — the strongest single item** | Coleman pp. 85–87. Verbatim: *"Whoever heard of satraps being mentioned in the councils of the Romans? I do not remember ever to have read of any Roman satrap being mentioned, or even of a satrap in any of the Roman provinces. But this fellow speaks of the Emperor's satraps, and puts them in before the Senate…"* He then contrasts the real formulae — **S.C.** (*senatus consulto*) and **S.P.Q.R.** — as what genuine Roman instruments carry. That contrast is the on-screen gold: a wrong word *and* the right word it should have been. |
| V3 | **Tense inconsistency** | **CONFIRMED** | Coleman p. 115. *"And what is being done he wishes understood as already done; as, 'we have proclaimed' for 'we proclaim'… And he puts the same act in the present and in the past tense; as, 'we decree,' and 'we have decreed.'"* Same page: *extat* for *est*, *nempe* for *scilicet*, *concubitores* for *contubernales*. Also p. 93: *extiterit* and *existat* in one sentence. |
| V4 | **Poor vocabulary / bad Latin** | **CONFIRMED** | Coleman pp. 93, 105–109. Best legible instance: **"phrygium"** for the papal tiara — *"Whoever ever heard 'tiara' [phrygium] used in Latin? You talk like a barbarian… but what does 'phrygium' mean? You do not explain this."* (pp. 107–109). |
| V5 | **The Constantinople impossibility** | **CONFIRMED — and it is better than the brief says** | Coleman pp. 95–97. The document makes Constantine grant primacy over "Constantinople" as one of four patriarchal sees three days after his baptism — *"when it was not yet a patriarchate, nor a see, nor a Christian city, nor named Constantinople, nor founded, nor planned!"* Valla then catches the forger contradicting himself **inside the same document**: the closing section has Constantine *announcing an intention* to build a city there. *"But if he was intending to transfer the empire, he had not yet transferred it… if he was planning to build a city, he had not yet built it."* He caps it: *"Liars need good memories."* He adds a geography error at pp. 97–101: the forger writes "the province of Byzantia" when Byzantium was a **town**, and places Thrace in the East when it lies to the north. **This is the sequence to build the video's centrepiece around** — it needs no Latin at all. |
| V6 | **Gallicanus consulate error** | **CONFIRMED, with a correction that must be made** | Coleman pp. 137–139. Valla: *"'Constantine consul for the fourth time, and Gallicanus consul for the fourth time.' Strange if each had been consul thrice, and they were colleagues in a fourth consulship!"* ⚠ **Coleman's note 500 undercuts Valla here:** *"In the best text of the Donation this is not called the fourth consulship of Gallicanus. In any case, however, the date is impossible; no such consulship as this is known."* **Valla was attacking a corrupted reading.** The conclusion survives; his specific gotcha does not. This is exactly the kind of thing the channel should say out loud — it costs nothing and it is the difference between this video and the shelf. |

### 2.2 The "fief" claim — the vidIQ insertion

**FABRICATED, or at minimum unsupported. Do not use it.**

Direct check, both sides of the Coleman edition (Latin and English), case-insensitive:
`fief` — **0 hits**. `feud` / `feudal` / `feudum` — **0 hits**. `vassal` / `vassall` — **0 hits**.
The only near-miss is *beneficiarios/beneficiarius* at Coleman p. 47 (Latin ~"Maluit ergo sibi
principes ac reges beneficiarios facere, quam ipse beneficiarius illorum esse"), which is about
**patronage — who obliges whom** — in an argument about Sylvester's character, not a philological
dating proof and not feudal-law vocabulary.

**Verdict: the word "fief" does not appear in Valla's argument in the edition I read. It should be
struck from every downstream artefact.** (Residual caveat: I checked Coleman, not Setz's critical
edition; but Coleman collates the Vatican MS against Hutten, and a word that carried a philological
argument could not vanish from both.)

### 2.3 Two arguments the brief MISSES, and both are better than "satrap"

**V7 — The manuscript/canonical argument. ESTABLISHED. Coleman pp. 75–77.**
Before he touches the Latin, Valla attacks the document's *transmission*:

> *"…nor is it found in any of the oldest copies of the Decretum. And if Gratian had mentioned it
> anywhere, he would have mentioned it [where] he treats of Louis' agreement… Some say that the man
> who added this chapter was called **Palea**, either because that was his name, or because what he
> added of his own, compared with Gratian, is as **straw [palea] beside grain**."*

This matters for three reasons. (1) It is the **same species of argument as Cusa's** — the document
is absent from the older manuscript tradition — which means Valla did not replace the historical
method, he *stacked* the linguistic method on top of it. (2) It gives the video a physical,
filmable object: the word *palea* written in the margin of a canon-law manuscript, the medieval
Church's own flag on the text. (3) It is a genuine 700-year-old tell that the Church's own lawyers
had already logged.

**V8 — The argument from institutional conduct. ESTABLISHED. Coleman pp. 79–81 and following.**
Valla asks where the *consequences* of a grant that size are. No historian records the handover, no
coinage marks it, no successor acts as if it happened. This is Cusa's C1–C4 in a rhetorical register.

### 2.4 Valla's actual architecture — corrected

The brief's "three-part structure" (from Wikipedia) is not what the treatise does. The text as
Coleman prints it runs roughly:

1. Constantine would not and could not have given (pp. ~27–47) — probability + Roman public law.
2. Sylvester would not have accepted (pp. ~47–67) — character + gospel poverty.
3. No transfer of possession ever occurred; no source records it (pp. ~67–79) — **Cusa's ground**.
4. The document is not in the old Decretum; it is a *palea* (pp. 75–77) — **canonical ground**.
5. **The document convicts itself, line by line** (pp. ~79–155) — **Valla's own ground**. This is the
   long central movement and it is where the video lives.
6. What actually happened, and the papacy's later conduct.

**Read against Cusa, the finding is clean: Valla's §§ 1–4 are Cusa's argument. Valla's § 5 is new.**

## 3. THE PRIORITY VERDICT

### 3.1 Who was first

| | Date | Method family | Independent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nicholas of Cusa**, *De concordantia catholica* III.2 | **1433** | Historical / canonical / documentary silence | Prior work exists — see §3.3 |
| **Lorenzo Valla**, *De falso credita…* | **1439–40** | Cusa's family **plus** systematic internal-linguistic proof | **CONTESTED** — see §3.2 |
| **Reginald Pecock**, *Repressor* | **c. 1449–55** | Cusa's family (silence of Damasus, of the chronicles; contradiction by the *Historia Tripartita*) | Asserted independent (Babington 1860) |

**Cusa was first by six to seven years. ESTABLISHED and not seriously disputed.** The channel should
say so plainly and early.

**But priority is the wrong axis, and the video should say why.** Cusa's conclusion and Valla's
conclusion are the same; their *instruments* are not. Cusa proved the Donation false the way a
lawyer proves a will false — by going to the registry and finding no entry. That method works only
where you have the registry. Valla's method works on the document alone. **That is the difference
that survives to 2026** and it is precisely the channel's HOW-over-WHY lane.

### 3.2 Did Valla know Cusa's argument? — CONTESTED, and this is load-bearing

| Position | Scholar | What they say |
|---|---|---|
| Valla was substantially indebted to Cusa | **Riccardo Fubini** | "Valla's small but incisive work has a great debt to Nicholas of Cusa's treatise; if he did not know the whole of the Cusan's work, at least a high probability can be deduced that he knew its principal points." (Fubini 1992, pp. 391–393; summarised in Rojas Donat & de Toro 2023, p. 19 with n. 67) |
| Shares the suspicion, but leaves independence open | **Mario Fois** | Fois 1969, p. 329 (via Rojas Donat & de Toro 2023, n. 68) |
| Valla read DCC Book III, chapters 1–4 | **Thomas Renna** | Renna 2014, pp. 7 and 15: "Sometime after 1433, Valla came upon a version of Nicholas of Cusa's *Catholic Concordance*… Valla was jealous that he himself had not written a similar deconstruction." ⚠ **Renna asserts this in his body text but hedges in his own footnote 49** ("It is possible that Valla had a *vague idea*…"). Treat Renna as a hypothesis, not a finding. |
| Perfect independence (of Pecock and Valla from each other) | **Churchill Babington** | Repressor ed. 1860, vol. 1 introduction, n.: "There is, however, a perfect independence of each other in the two treatises." Note: this is about **Pecock and Valla**, not Cusa. |

**Did Valla acknowledge Cusa? No — direct check.** I searched Coleman's full Latin and English text
for `Cusa`, `Cusanus`, `Nicholas`, `Nicolaus`. **Cusa is named nowhere in Valla's treatise.** The
only occurrence of the name in the volume is Coleman's own introduction. Valla names contemporaries
freely elsewhere in the work (Eugenius IV repeatedly, Sigismund once) — so this is silence, not a
convention against naming the living.

**What this does to the video's options.** It cuts *against* Option B harder than against Option A.
"Three unconnected people caught it at once" requires independence; the leading specialist on
exactly this question says Valla probably wasn't independent of Cusa. Only **Pecock** is a
well-attested independent discovery, and Pecock used Cusa's method — so the "convergence" reduces
to *one* method found twice and *one* method found once.

### 3.3 The prior history the video must not erase

Neither Cusa nor Valla was the first to *doubt* it. **ESTABLISHED:**

- **Otto III's chancery, c. 1001** — a diploma granting eight counties to St Peter contains the line
  *"Hec sunt enim commenta ab illis ipsis inventa quibus Iohannes diaconus cognomento Digitorum
  mutilus preceptum aureis litteris scripsit et sub titulo magni Constantini longi mendacii tempora
  finxit"* — naming a deacon John "the finger-mutilated" as having written the thing in gold letters
  and invented "long times of lying" under the name of great Constantine. Source: *MGH DD O III*
  n. 389, p. 820, quoted at Rojas Donat & de Toro 2023, p. 17 n. 61. **This is a filmable document
  and it is 430 years before Valla.** Use it — honestly, it strengthens the video.
- **Critical voices from the late 12th century onward**, without secure proof: Menozzi 1980;
  Conetti 2004, pp. 245–289 (via Rojas Donat & de Toro 2023, p. 18 n. 62).

**Consequence for the throughline.** The claim "the Latin gave it away" is safe. The claim "nobody
noticed for 700 years" is **false** and would be the video's most likely correction-magnet. Correct
framing: *people had suspected it for four centuries; what changed in the 1430s–40s was that
suspicion became proof.*

## 4. PECOCK — same question, short answer

**Method: Cusa's, not Valla's. ESTABLISHED by direct reading.**

Text read: *The Repressor of Over Much Blaming of the Clergy*, ed. Churchill Babington, Rolls Series
19, 2 vols, London 1860 (public domain; archive.org `repressorofoverm01peco`, `…02peco`). The
Donation discussion is **Part III, ch. XIII, vol. 2, pp. 357–366**.

Pecock's "manie euydencis," itemised:

1. **Silence of Damasus.** Jerome asked Damasus to write everything he could find about what was
   done at Rome from Peter to his own day; Damasus did so; Damasus records Constantine's *small*
   endowments to Roman churches and says nothing of a great one. "Wherfore treuthe is, that thilk
   greet endewing so born an bond was not maad." (pp. 359–360)
2. **Damasus would have been in possession of it** had it existed — he succeeded to whatever
   Sylvester held, and a man knows best what he holds. (p. 360)
3. **Silence of every credible chronicle.** No story or chronicle mentions the endowment "saue the
   legeude or storie of Siluestris gestis" and the epistle ascribed to Constantine — and neither is
   "credible neithir worthi be allowid." (pp. 360–361)
4. **Positive contradiction from the *Historia Tripartita*** ("the worthi and famose and credible
   stori clepid Thre departid storie") on Constantine's will and the disposition of his territories.
   (p. 361)
5. **The endowment demonstrably came later** — from "Pipyn king of France… Charles… Lodowic… and
   bi Matilde," citing Gratian, Dist. LXIII c. *Ego Lodowicus*. (pp. 358–359) — **the same
   substitution Cusa makes.**

**No linguistic argument anywhere in the chapter.** Pecock is writing in Middle English about a
Latin document; the philological move is not available to him and he does not attempt it.

**Priority note:** Babington (1860) asserts Pecock and Valla are perfectly independent of each
other. Levine (1973) is the specialist treatment and should be read in Phase 2 before the script
asserts independence.

## 5. "VALLA INVENTED HISTORICAL CRITICISM" — WHAT THE SPECIALISTS ACTUALLY SAY

**Verdict: it is a defensible, heavily qualified scholarly claim that has hardened into a popular
absolute. The video can make it if it makes it precisely. It cannot make it as a slogan.**

### 5.1 Where the English-language claim comes from

**Coleman 1922, editor's introduction** — this is the fountainhead, and it is more careful than its
descendants:

> "Among the achievements of modern historical criticism Valla's work was a conspicuous pioneer.
> **Its quality and its importance have often been exaggerated, and as often underestimated.** …
> A critical appraisal would have to take into account that **Nicholas Cusanus some seven years
> earlier in his *De concordantia catholica* covered part of the same ground even better than Valla
> did, and anticipated some of his arguments.** But Valla's treatise is more exhaustive, is in more
> finished and effective literary form, and in effect established for the world generally the proof
> of the falsity of the Donation. Moreover, **for the first time, he used effectively the method of
> studying the usage of words in the variations of their meaning and application, and other devices
> of internal criticism which are the tools of historical criticism to-day.**"

Two usable findings sit in that one paragraph. Coleman **concedes Cusa's priority and says Cusa did
parts of it better** — and still lands on the method distinction. That is the video's thesis,
stated by the man who edited the text, in 1922.

### 5.3 The formulation the script can defend

- ❌ "Valla invented historical criticism." Contested; Grafton and the SEP will not carry it.
- ❌ "Valla was the first to prove the Donation false." **False.** Cusa, 1433.
- ❌ "Nobody had questioned it for 700 years." **False.** Otto III's chancery, c. 1001.
- ✅ **"Valla was the first to prove a document false out of its own language — and that is the test
  we still run."** Supported by Coleman explicitly; consistent with Fubini, Camporeale and Grafton,
  none of whom dispute the *novelty of the instrument*, only its solitude and its scope.
- ✅ **"His contemporaries proved it with the archive. He proved it with the sentence."**

## 6. THE RECOMMENDATION

### 6.1 Option B at its strongest — the honest case for the convergence video

Three men in one generation, on two sides of the Alps, in three languages of scholarship, all
concluded the same 700-year-old instrument was false. That is a real historical fact and it demands
an explanation, and the explanation is available and interesting: the Council of Basel had put the
foundations of papal temporal power under adversarial examination for the first time in centuries;
Cusa was writing *for* the Council; Valla was writing for a king at war with the Council's enemy;
the incentive to audit the papacy's title deeds had never been higher. That is a genuine
mechanism — **institutions produce scrutiny, not geniuses** — and it is the exact inverse of the
lone-genius myth this channel exists to dismantle. It would also insulate the video against the
correction the comments will otherwise bring ("Cusa did it first"), by making that fact the subject
rather than the footnote. It is the more intellectually honest instinct, and `RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md`
was right to flag it.

### 6.2 Why it loses anyway

1. **Its premise is contested by the specialist who owns the question.** Fubini's finding is that
   Valla probably worked from Cusa. A video whose spine is "three unconnected people" is one
   comment away from a correction it cannot answer, and the correction is in Italian in a 1992
   Macerata conference volume. That is the worst kind of exposure.
2. **The convergence isn't three of anything.** Cusa and Pecock ran the same method. Valla ran that
   method *plus* one more. Structurally there are two methods, not three discoveries.
3. **Its answer is politics.** "Why did three people catch it at once?" resolves to the Basel
   schism, Eugenius IV, Alfonso V and Neapolitan investiture. That is WHY, not HOW — the channel's
   own data says mechanism outperforms politics with this audience, and the Correctionist segment
   (2.31% conversion, the channel's best) is being recruited on *method*.
4. **It has no on-screen object.** The convergence is an abstraction. The Latin is a thing you can
   put on a card.

### 6.3 The recommendation: **Option A, in a corrected form — call it A′**

**Do not make Valla the hero. Make the two methods the subject, and let Valla win on the merits in
front of the viewer.**

The spine:

> A document said the Emperor gave the West to the Pope. In 1433 a German cardinal proved it false —
> by going to the archives and finding nothing there. Six years later an Italian in Naples proved it
> false again, and he never left his desk, because he did it out of the document's own sentences.
> Only one of those two methods still works when the archive is gone. Here is how it runs.

Why A′ is the right call:

- **It is true at every point**, including the points the brief got wrong. Cusa is first, and named
  in the first ninety seconds. Otto III's chancery doubted it in 1001. Valla's Gallicanus gotcha was
  aimed at a bad reading. None of that weakens the video; all of it is the video.
- **It converts the project's biggest liability into its cold open.** The Cusa priority stops being
  a fact that must be managed and becomes the control condition in an experiment.
- **It preserves the packaging lock unchanged.** "The Vatican Ruled Europe for 700 Years. The Latin
  Gave It Away." — A′ *is* that sentence. No person claim, and the tell is the Latin.
- **It is mechanism-forward.** The comparison "registry vs. sentence" is the HOW the audience buys,
  and it generalises to the modern hook without strain: C2PA and provenance metadata are Cusa's
  registry; forensic linguistics and stylometry are Valla's sentence; and when the registry is
  compromised, only the second one is left.
- **It gives the strongest on-screen sequence a natural home**: the Constantinople self-contradiction
  (Coleman pp. 95–97), which needs no Latin, no expertise, and no trust in the narrator.

**One structural warning.** A′ still requires the script to state, once and clearly, that Valla may
have been building on Cusa (Fubini) and that specialists disagree about how good his arguments were
(Fois vs Antonazzi). Say it in two sentences and move on. That single admission is what makes the
rest of the video unattackable.

**Do not use** the "Pope Pius II proved it in 1453 and never published" line (`RESEARCH-VIABILITY.md`
Check B, item 5) until it is verified. I searched for it and found only Aeneas Sylvius
Piccolomini's ***De ortu et auctoritate imperii Romani* (1446)**, which is a different work, a
different year, and was not suppressed. **Status: UNVERIFIED, likely garbled.**
