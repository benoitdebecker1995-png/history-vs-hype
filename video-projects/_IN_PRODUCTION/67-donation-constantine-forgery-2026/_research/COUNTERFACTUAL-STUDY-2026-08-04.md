# Same-channel counterfactual study — is the Donation ceiling demand or supply?

**Run 2026-08-04.** Method proposed by the GPT-5.6 Sol pass; data collected here via the vidIQ MCP,
because the same pass demonstrated it will fabricate numbers. **Long-form only** — Shorts excluded,
which is the correction that prompted this.

**The question:** absolute views and views-per-subscriber cannot tell a *demand* ceiling from a
*supply-quality* ceiling. Comparing each treatment against **its own channel's baseline** can.

---

## ⛔ VERDICT — REVISED 2026-08-04 AFTER THE DECISIVE MEASUREMENT. THE FIRST VERDICT WAS WRONG.

**The demand-ceiling conclusion is RETRACTED.** It was built on one video whose title changed four
variables at once, and on ratios I had no basis to call robust. Running the measurement that controls
for creator, language, audience, genre and accumulation time reverses it.

> ### THE RESULT
>
> Stephan's History of the World made this video **twice**, and each is measurable against its own
> contemporaneous surrounding window:
>
> | Published | Framing | Views | Window median | **Ratio** |
> |---|---|---:|---:|---:|
> | 2024-05-12 | **entity-led** — *"The Church's Most Famous Forgery: **The Donation of Constantine**"* | 4,309 | 2,134 | **2.02× — overperformed** |
> | 2026-06-09 | **scholar-led** — *"**Lorenzo Valla** exposed forged Church documents…"* | 1,172 | 1,841 | **0.64× — underperformed** |
>
> **A 3.17× within-creator swing.** Same channel, same language, same audience, same genre, each
> compared only against uploads from its own period.

**An English Donation video beat its channel's baseline by 2×.** There is no demonstrated English
demand ceiling. The 0.64× result measured a package, not a topic.

### What the study actually establishes

1. ⭐ **Packaging is the operative variable, and the evidence is now within-creator.** The entity-led
   framing doubled its channel baseline; the scholar-led framing came in a third lower. This is the
   strongest support the project has for the title decision — and the title changed *before* this ran.
2. ⛔ **"English-specific weakness" was never identified** and is withdrawn. Language was fully
   confounded with creator reputation, channel genre, audience expectation, packaging and year.
   Static in the Attic (English, 1.10×) already showed an English audience responding when the subject
   fit its channel.
3. ⚠ **The farm evidence is narrower than "void."** It establishes that the topic name plus generic
   forgery framing did not lift weak channels above their ordinary performance. It cannot distinguish
   "never served" from "served and failed" — impressions and test-batch CTR are not public.
4. ⛔ **Section B is temporally unidentified and carries no inference.** See the LIMITS section.

**What this changes for #67:** the breakout is unproven, not foreclosed. **Do not plan around 30,000
views** — that operational caution survives. But the topic is not capped, the packaging is the lever,
and the entity-led title is the one that has demonstrably worked on this subject in English.

⚠ **The sibling does not follow from this study** and never did. Its case rests on the independent
Suggested-adjacency result (4.15% vs 1.12%, n=24, confounded with fame) and must justify itself there.

## B. Versus current channel median — biased, and the bias direction is known

For videos published years ago the baseline is the channel's *current* performance, on a channel that
has since grown. **The ratio is therefore biased downward.** A video that still overperforms here is
robust; one that underperforms is ambiguous.

| Channel | Segment | Donation video | Channel median | Ratio | Read |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Massimo Polidoro** (IT, 342K) | mainstream skeptic/debunker | 40,432 (2021) | 22,686 | **1.78×** | ⭐ **Robust overperformance** — survives a baseline stacked against it |
| **Point of View** (TH, 3.95M) | large general history | 255,510 (2024) | 221,648 | **1.15×** | Mild overperformance, probably real given the bias |
| **Static in the Attic** (EN, 94.4K) | conspiracy-adjacent | 15,142 (2025) | 13,798 | 1.10× | At baseline |
| **Varus PrimaRenatus** (RU, 142K) | academic history | 30,370 (2021) | 45,958 | 0.66× | Ambiguous (bias) |

**Point of View was the study's biggest open threat and it does not overturn the read.** At 3.95M
subscribers its Donation episode is the largest treatment in any language, and it lands at **1.15×** —
the topic travels *fine* on a big general-history channel, in Thai. That is consistent with, not
contrary to, an **English-specific** weakness.

📌 **Didascale (FR, 12.5K) excluded.** Its 2,350-view Donation video would score ~19× — but the
channel's baseline is a dump of ~100-view audio-Bible readings, so the denominator is meaningless.
This is exactly the segmentation trap the method was designed to avoid, and it is why the raw
`outlierScore` figures in a keyword search cannot be read as over-performance.

## D. ⭐ The finding that was not the question — same creator, same topic, two framings

Stephan's History of the World covered this subject **twice**:

| Published | Title | Views |
|---|---|---:|
| 2024-05-12 | *"The Church's Most Famous Forgery: **The Donation of Constantine**"* | **4,309** |
| 2026-06-09 | *"**Lorenzo Valla** exposed forged Church documents and corrected the Bible!"* | **1,172** |

**3.7× in favour of the entity-led title.** Same creator, same research base, same register — the
variable that moved is whether the recognisable *thing* or the unrecognisable *scholar* is in the
title.

⚠ **Confounded and stated as such:** the 2024 video has had two extra years to accumulate, and the
channel was smaller then. Both confounds have effects in opposite directions, and neither is
quantified here. **Suggestive, not proof.** But it points the same way as the channel's own
`Crusades 5.47% vs Medieval Literacy Boom 1.11%` split, and the same way as the ANGLE-LOCK's title
decision — which was made before this study ran.

## E. ⭐ THE DECISIVE MEASUREMENT — same creator, both framings, both windows

Added 2026-08-04 after the Sol pass identified it as the one comparison that would move the verdict.
It controls **creator, language, audience, channel genre, publication period and accumulation time**
— everything sections A and B could not.

**2024-05-12 — entity-led — *"The Church's Most Famous Forgery: The Donation of Constantine"*, 4,309 views**

| | views |
|---|---|
| Five before (2024-03-13 → 05-08) | 649 · 6,384 · 2,765 · 4,246 · 971 |
| Five after (05-24 → 07-03) | 1,504 · 2,764 · 615 · 3,021 · 659 |
| Window median | **2,134** |
| **Ratio** | **2.02× — overperformed** |

Set against the 2026 scholar-led video's **0.64×** (section A), that is a **3.17× within-creator
swing** attributable to how the same subject was packaged.

📌 vidIQ's own independent over-performance metric agrees in direction: the 2024 entity-led video
carries `outlierScore` **3.87**; the 2026 scholar-led video returns **null**.

⚠ **What this still does not prove.** Two videos, one creator. The 2024 title differs from the 2026
one in at least four ways at once — famous entity present, subject named, single promise, no scholar —
so it identifies *"the entity-led package"* as a bundle, not which element carries it. And the
subjects are not identical: the 2026 video is a Valla biography that covers the Donation, not a
Donation video. **It refutes the ceiling claim; it does not isolate a mechanism.**

## LIMITS

- ⛔ **Section B carries no inference — corrected 2026-08-04.** I originally claimed those ratios were
  *biased downward* by channel growth and used that to call two of them robust. **The bias direction is
  not knowable from lifetime views.** Channel growth pushes one way; five years of accumulation on the
  old video against weeks on the baseline uploads pushes the other, as do evergreen search,
  back-catalogue rediscovery, retitling, and changes in upload cadence. None separate from lifetime
  totals, and the accumulation effect is plausibly the larger. Section B is **descriptive only**.
  Logged in `video-projects/_CORRECTIONS-LOG.md`.
- **True surrounding windows exist for two videos, both on the same channel** (§A and §E). vidIQ's
  channel endpoint returns "recent" or "popular" uploads, but `vidiq_youtube_search` scoped by
  `channelId` + `publishedAfter/Before` + `order=date` reconstructs an arbitrary past window — that is
  how §E was obtained, and it is the method to reuse.
- Segment labels (neutral / skeptic / conspiracy-adjacent / farm) are my judgement from channel content,
  not a measured taxonomy.
- Polish, Spanish and French treatments were **not** baselined. Thai (Point of View) was, and came
  back 1.15× — the largest open threat to section A, now closed and consistent with it.
- Nothing here bears on whether the *history* is right. It is a distribution study only.
