### 1.1 The grant clause — what is actually conveyed (§17, **ll. 261–270**)

> **261** Unde ut non pontificalis apex
> **262** vilescat, sed magis amplius quam terreni imperii dignitas et gloriae
> **263** potentia decoretur, ecce **tam palatium nostrum**, ut praelatum est, quam-
> **264** que **Romae urbis et omnes Italiae seu occidentalium regionum pro-**
> **265** **vincias, loca et civitates** saepefato beatissimo pontifici, patri nostro Sil-
> **266** vestrio, universali papae, **contradentes atque relinquentes** eius vel
> **267** successorum ipsius pontificum **potestati et ditioni** firma imperiali cen-
> **268** sura per hanc nostram divalem sacram et pragmaticum constitutum
> **269** decernimus disponenda atque **iuri sanctae Romanae ecclesiae** con-
> **270** cedimus permanenda.

*Literal:* "…behold, both our palace, as aforesaid, and the city of Rome and all the provinces,
places and cities of Italy or of the western regions — handing over and relinquishing them to the
power and rule of him and his successor pontiffs, by this firm imperial censure, through this our
divine sacred and pragmatic *constitutum* we decree they be disposed of, and we concede that they
remain under the law of the holy Roman Church."

**⭐ This vindicates the C7 title correction and sharpens it.** Fried's reading of these very lines
(2007, p. 34): *"The emperor did not grant any new authority, but assigned the places and regions
mentioned to the existing authority of the bishop of Rome."* The verbs are `contradentes atque
relinquentes` — handing over and leaving behind — into a `potestas et dicio` treated as already
existing. So even "Claimed" is generous to the document: the forger writes as if the authority were
already the pope's. **The escalation to "power over the entire Western Roman Empire" is later
readers' work, not the text's** (already filed as C8b).

⚠ **Do not confuse this with the earlier grant at ll. 202–208.** That one lists Iudaea, Graecia,
Asia, Thracia, Africa, Italia "vel diversis insulis" — but it is `nostram largitatem … pro
concinnatione luminariorum possessionum praedia`, **endowments for church lighting and upkeep**, not
sovereignty. A script that merges the two lists overstates the grant. `[GUARD: two different grants]`

**Also note l. 219** — the Lateran palace is contradicted *twice*, at 219 and again at 263
("ut praelatum est", "as aforesaid"). The forger is repeating himself.

### 1.2 The Constantinople passage — the line Valla convicts it on

**Two lines, ~100 apart, and the contradiction is between them.** This is the exhibit.

**(a) §12, l. 172** — Sylvester is given primacy over Constantinople *as an existing patriarchal see*:

> **171** Atque decernentes sancimus, ut principatum teneat tam super
> **172** quattuor praecipuas sedes Antiochenam, Alexandrinam, **Constantinopoli-**
> **173** **tanam** et Hierosolymitanam, quamque etiam super omnes in universo orbe
> **174** terrarum dei ecclesias…

**(b) §18, ll. 271–276** — Constantine says he has *not yet built the city*:

> **271** Unde congruum prospeximus, nostrum imperium et regni pote-
> **272** statem orientalibus transferri ac transmutari regionibus et **in Byzantiae**
> **273** **provincia in optimo loco nomini nostro civitatem aedificari** et nostrum
> **274** illic constitui imperium; quoniam, ubi principatus sacerdotum et
> **275** christianae religionis caput ab imperatore caelesti constitutum est,
> **276** iustum non est, ut illic imperator terrenus habeat potestatem.

*Literal (b):* "Whence we have judged it fitting that our empire and the power of our reign be
transferred and removed to the eastern regions, and that in the province of Byzantia, in the best
place, a city be built to our name, and our empire be established there…"

**The tell, in one sentence a viewer can hold:** the same document names Constantinople as one of the
four great patriarchal sees (l. 172) and then, at l. 273, has Constantine announcing he intends to
found a city there and name it after himself. **A see that does not exist yet cannot be one of the
four.** And the whole thing is dated to the third day after the baptism (Datum, l. 304–306).

✅ **This is exactly the proof C5 records in Valla's Latin** — "non Romae, sed Byzantii… **Quis ergo
non videt, qui privilegium composuit, eum diu post tempora Constantini fuisse**". C5 and this section
are now locked to each other: Valla's sentence, and the two lines of the document it convicts.

⚠ **Order-of-sees variant, note for on-screen work.** Fuhrmann's critical text reads
"Antiochenam, Alexandrinam, Constantinopolitanam et Hierosolymitanam". **Gratian's *Decretum* — the
text Valla actually read — reads "Alexandrinam, Antiocenam, Ierosolimitanam, Constantinopolitanam"**
(Fried 2007, Appendix B.IV, from Friedberg, D.96 c.14 Palea). Fuhrmann's introduction (printed p. 16)
notes that in one recension "Konstantinopel ist ans Ende nach dem Kleinpatriarchat gestellt". If the
card shows Gratian's wording, the Constantinople-last order is *itself* a transmission fingerprint —
but the two must not be silently mixed. `[GUARD: which recension is on the card]`

### 1.3 The word `satrapis` — Fuhrmann's line numbers

### 1.4 The leprosy/baptism prelude — extent confirmed, and improved on

**Fried's citation checks out.** Fried 2007, p. 47: the reform-era canon lawyers (Anselm of Lucca,
Deusdedit, Ivo of Chartres) quoted the deed in short — *"The prelude of leprosy and baptism
(**l. 79-155**) was excluded, as well as the introductory declaration of faith by the newly baptised
emperor (**l. 27-78**)"*.

**Fuhrmann is more precise, and gives a single cut point.** Printed p. 18:

> *"…und die gregorianische Kanonistik begann aufs Wort genau da, wo die Anselmo dedicata ihren
> Auszug hat beschließen müssen: **nach `comperi` mit `Utile iudicavimus` (Zeile 157)**. In Zukunft
> findet fast ausschließlich nur die Donatio Beachtung."*

*("…and Gregorian canon law began word-for-word exactly where the Collectio Anselmo dedicata's
excerpt had had to end: after `comperi`, with `Utile iudicavimus` (line 157). Henceforth almost
exclusively only the Donatio received attention.")*

**So the canon lawyers' Constitutum begins mid-line at l. 157.** Everything before it — the address,
the creed, the leprosy, the dream, the baptism, the Petrine texts — is gone. **Lines 1–157a dropped;
157b–306 kept.**

**⭐ And the document was quarried in two halves by two different constituencies.** Fuhrmann, same
page: the *Collectio Anselmo dedicata* (c. 890) *"hat … nur die Confessio exzerpiert"* — took only
the creed. Burchard of Worms (†1025) skipped those chapters entirely. The Gregorian reformers took
only the *Donatio*. **Nobody used it whole.**

**Independent confirmation, and it lands on the same line.** The 1506 *editio princeps* of Valla
printed Pincernus's translation alongside "ut veritas eius appareat manifestius"; Setz notes (p. 94
n. 24) *"das Zitat beginnt mit `Dignum indicavimus cum omnibus nostris satrapis` (**entspricht
Constitutum Constantini, Zeile 157**)"*.

**Why this matters for the script.** Filed already as C8c ("they kept the grant and dropped the
magic"). It is now **exact**: the cut is at l. 157, and the first words the lawyers kept are the
satraps clause. The single most datable word in the document sits in the first line the canon lawyers
chose to keep. That is a beat.

### 2.1 Camporeale — the treatise is an **oration**, and Valla said so himself

**Source:** Salvatore I. Camporeale, "Lorenzo Valla and the *De falso credita donatione*: Rhetoric,
Freedom, and Ecclesiology in the Fifteenth Century", in *Christianity, Latinity, and Culture: Two
Studies on Lorenzo Valla*, tr. Patrick Baker, ed. Baker & Celenza (Brill, 2014). Printed pages below.

**The spawn brief's framing was slightly off, and the truth is more useful.** Camporeale does *not*
say the treatise is rhetoric *instead of* philology. He says philology **is** a mode of rhetoric, for
Valla, and that this is what Valla thought he was doing.

**(a) The method is real and Camporeale credits Valla with it.** p. 57: sections III–VI are *"the
part of his discourse in which Valla fully elaborates the philological procedure that he pioneered"*;
p. 61: they *"reveal here the full measure of the original argumentation that he pioneered"*;
p. 58: *"His rhetorical argumentation (as a critical analysis of language) therefore takes on a
strictly philological character."*

**(b) But the genealogy is Quintilian, not proto-science.** p. 58: the procedure is *"an `enarratio`,
a form of morphological and semantic criticism that Valla derived from Quintilian's `Institutio
oratoria` and fully re-elaborated himself."* p. 27: the res–verba procedure is *"properly speaking a
rhetorical mode of argument — in both Quintilian's and Valla's understanding of rhetoric… rhetoric
rises — first in Quintilian and then above all in Valla — to the level of a critical and interpretive
discipline."*

**(c) ⭐ The killer quote, and it is Valla's own.** Camporeale, p. 27, quoting Valla's letter to
Giovanni Aurispa (*Epistole*, 249–252, at 252.9):

> **"nihil magis oratorium scripsi"** — *"I have written nothing more rhetorical."*

**Valla's own description of this work is that it is his most rhetorical piece of writing.** Any
script sentence of the form "Valla approached it like a scientist" is contradicted by the author.

**(d) The purpose is political, and specific.** p. 22: the Oration is a speech to the whole Church,
*"as if in an assembly of kings and princes"* (`quasi in contione regum et principum`, *De falso*
62.9 §6). p. 25: it opens with `dissentio` — "I dissent". pp. 24–25: the immediate context is the
war between Alfonso of Aragon's Naples and Eugenius IV. p. 26: the peroration (*De falso* 172–176)
*"is an explicit plea to Eugenius IV"* — the pope should *"definitively reject and abrogate the
Constitutum Constantini"* and mark his pontificate with the *"de-Constantinization"* of the Roman
Church.

**How much of "Valla invented a method" survives?** Most of it, but not the framing.
✅ **Survives:** he did it first, in this form, on this document, sentence by sentence, and
Camporeale calls the procedure pioneered and original.
❌ **Does not survive:** that it was disinterested, that it was proto-scientific, that the goal was
authentication. It was an oration, aimed at a named pope, in the middle of a war, by the propagandist
of one belligerent, and its author called it his most rhetorical work.

**Script consequence:** the honest line is *"the method is real; the motive was not curiosity."*
That is a better story than the hagiography, and it is the channel's register.

### 2.2 ⭐ Steuco (1547) — the strongest single argument against Valla, and it is a *good* one

**Source:** Michele Zanobini, "Agostino Steuco and the Debate on the Donation of Constantine",
*Studies in Medieval and Renaissance History*, 3rd ser. 13 (2016), pp. 177–191. Steuco's own text:
*Contra Laurentium Vallam, de falsa donatione Constantini libri duo* (Lyon: Sebastian Gryphius,
1547).

#### The setup: a century of bad defences first

Zanobini pp. 181–184. Cortesi (†1474, *Folia Antivallae*) and Pietro Del Zochol (*Antidotum* and
*Apologia*, 1496) attacked Valla for **impiety, not philology**. Zanobini, p. 181: they were *"less
interested in disproving Valla's philological approach, but favored instead an explicit accusation
of impiety."* Del Zochol even concedes the point — *"Sive Constantinus donaverit sive non"*
("whether Constantine donated or not", *Antidotum* fol. 130) — and argues the papacy's right holds
either way.

**But Del Zochol lands one real philological blow** (Zanobini p. 184 n. 16, quoting *Antidotum* fol.
104v): not everything has a name in the ancient poets and orators; for new things one must go to the
vernacular — *"ut campana, ut bombarda, ut molendinum, ut oculare"* (bell, bombard, mill, eyeglass).
**Translation of the argument: a non-classical word may signal a new object, not a late forger.**
This is the germ Steuco develops, and it is a genuine limit on the method.

#### Steuco's argument, and why it is serious

**(a) The charge.** Zanobini p. 186 n. 21, quoting *Contra Vallam*, p. 49:

> *"Totumque hoc calumniarum genus contra donationem cognoscitur esse inane, propterea quod profert
> verba ex donatione, **ut apud Canonistas haberentur**: nec curavit, aviditate calumniandi provectus,
> **proferre ex Graecis, ut vetustis Latinis codicibus** verba ista donationis."*

*("And this whole class of calumnies against the donation is recognised to be empty, because he
produces the words of the donation as they are held among the canonists; nor did he trouble, carried
away by his eagerness to calumniate, to produce those words of the donation from the Greek, or from
ancient Latin manuscripts.")*

**(b) ⛔ THE CHARGE IS FACTUALLY CORRECT, AND FUHRMANN CONFIRMS IT INDEPENDENTLY.** Fuhrmann,
printed p. 19: *"Die hoch- und spätmittelalterliche Diskussion um das Constitutum Constantini hielt
sich meist an das **Palea-Stück in Gratians Dekret** und griff **selten auf die Vollform** zurück."*
("The high and late medieval discussion mostly kept to the Palea piece in Gratian's Decretum and
seldom went back to the full form.") Zanobini, p. 187: *"It seems plausible that Valla worked on A,
a Latin document included in the twelfth-century Decretum Gratiani, a text that ignores the previous
versions of the document."* **Valla attacked a canon-law excerpt. He did not collate manuscripts.**

**(c) What Steuco found.** Zanobini pp. 186–187. Steuco located Greek versions in an unnamed
"Palatine" (probably Vatican) library — *"quatuor autem ante oculos palatina bibliotheca sugesserat
cum haec scriberentur"* (*Contra Vallam*, p. 57): four copies of older versions in front of his eyes
as he wrote. He argued the Greek was **older than Valla's Latin**, and that the Latin was itself a
**translation** of it.

**(d) ⭐ THE ARGUMENT.** Zanobini p. 187–188:

> *"Being unaware of the existence of Greek texts presenting an older version of the Constitutum,
> Valla looked for a form of linguistic and rhetorical refinement (Romanitas) **in a text that was
> merely a translation**. Thus, Steuco ultimately does not dispute Valla's linguistic analysis as, he
> concludes, all observations would be easily confuted by looking at the Greek text."*

**This is the steelman, and it is methodologically sound in form.** If a text is a translation, its
Latin dates the *translator*, not the *composition*. Barbarous Latin then proves nothing about the
fourth century. Valla's entire central proof would collapse — not because the reasoning is bad, but
because it was pointed at the wrong object.

**(e) The reason the defender got there: he had a better method.** Zanobini p. 188 — Steuco inherited
**Poliziano's `collatio codicum`**, the systematic comparison of all surviving manuscripts, developed
after Valla's death. Zanobini p. 190: Steuco *"offered to modern philology the first draft of a
stemma codicum of the Constitutum."*

> **⭐⭐ THE BEAT THE VIDEO SHOULD CARRY.** The man defending the forgery in 1547 was, on the narrow
> question of technique, **ahead of the man who exposed it in 1440** — because a hundred years of
> philology had happened in between, and the defender had read it. He built the first family tree of
> the document's manuscripts *in order to defend it*. This is the single best answer to "why wasn't
> the proof obvious?" and it is also the video's own thesis in miniature: methods improve, and the
> improvement is available to whoever picks it up.

#### Why Steuco is nevertheless wrong — state this too, or the video is unbalanced

#### ⭐ So why the delay? Three answers, and none of them is suppression

**(i) It was late to print, but not late to circulate.** 66 years in manuscript, widely, including in
hostile hands. Setz p. 91 is explicit that the manuscript numbers make the suppression hypothesis
groundless.

**(ii) When it *was* printed, nothing happened.** Setz, p. 94: *"Die editio princeps, die erst seit
wenigen Jahren bekannt ist, **blieb bei ihrem Erscheinen ohne erkennbare Wirkung.**"* ("The editio
princeps, known only for a few years, remained without discernible effect when it appeared.") The
only reader Setz can even place near it is Michael Köchlin, and probably only because his own book
was going through the same shop that month.

**(iii) ⭐ The reception problem was the tone, not the thesis.** The 1506 editor bound a
**criticism of Valla** into his own edition, taken over from his manuscript exemplar (Setz p. 94):

> *"**Vera dixit Valla** de donatione Constantini ficta et simulata, **sed nimium proterve, nimium
> arroganter et, ut verius dicam, nimium bestialiter invectus est in romanum pontificem.** Quod si
> modestius loquutus fuisset, posset istud opusculum legis iure censeri."*

*("Valla spoke the truth about the donation of Constantine being feigned and counterfeit — but he
inveighed against the Roman pontiff too impudently, too arrogantly, and, to speak more truly, too
bestially. Had he spoken more modestly, this little work could be reckoned as of legal standing.")*

**The first man to print it thought the argument was right and the delivery disqualifying.** He is
the perfect witness for the video's actual thesis, and he is on the same page as the argument.

**And a second, from 1511** (Setz p. 92): Jean Lemaire de Belges, *De la difference des schismes et
des Conciles*, concedes Valla proved it with *"raisons presque invincibles"* — and then declines,
preferring *"la commune opinion et la possession dont les Papes iouyssent"* as *"la plus saine
opinion"*. **A reader who says the proof is nearly irrefutable and then follows common opinion
anyway.** That is a whole segment on why proof does not move institutions.

**Verdict: DULLER THAN SUPPRESSION, AND MORE USEFUL.** The argument was available, read, copied,
conceded — and ignored, because it was a polemic by a notoriously abrasive man against an institution
whose possession nobody proposed to disturb. It became consequential only when **Hutten weaponised it
for the Reformation in 1518**, at which point the same text suddenly mattered. **The variable was not
access to the evidence. It was who needed it.** Which is precisely this video's payload.

### 2.4 Nauta — how Valla actually thought, and the guard against modernising him

**Source:** Lodi Nauta, *In Defense of Common Sense: Lorenzo Valla's Humanist Critique of Scholastic
Philosophy* (Harvard UP, 2009). Printed pages = PDF − 12.

**(a) Nauta issues the warning himself,** p. 7: *"it is important to proceed carefully here and to
avoid anachronistic interpretations that ascribe positions to Valla that he did not hold or could not
hold."*

**(b) ⭐ The decisive point for us.** Nauta, p. 7, listing the assumptions Valla's critique rests on:

> *"…that language can present a reliable picture of reality, **that only classical Latin can
> properly fulfill this role**, that ordinary linguistic practice establishes the meaning of words,
> that the use of ancient classical style is not just a matter of aesthetic preference but the only
> way to present things in their truest style."*

**And, p. 7:** *"Valla's 'ordinary language' was hardly ordinary; it was the classical Latin of the
great authors, not the Roman volgare of Valla's own time."*

**⚠⚠ THIS IS THE GUARD, AND IT IS SHARP.** Valla's yardstick is **normative**, not **descriptive**.
His test is *"does this match how the best authors wrote?"* — Cicero, Quintilian, Livy — **not**
*"does this match how people wrote in 315 AD?"* He is not comparing the text against a reconstructed
fourth-century corpus, because no such thing existed. He is comparing it against excellence.

**Why it works anyway on this document:** the *Constitutum* is both bad Latin and late Latin, so the
two tests give the same verdict. **On a well-written forgery it would not.** A competent classicising
forger passes Valla's test and fails a modern one. That is the real distance between Valla's move
and the discipline that descends from it — and it is exactly the nuance that makes the video honest
rather than triumphalist.

**(c) The tool is Quintilian's triad.** Nauta, p. 121: Valla *"often bases his arguments on
Quintilian's triad of reason, linguistic usage, and authority (`ratio, usus, auctoritas`)"*; the
criterion is *`consuetudo`* / *`usus loquendi`* (p. 2). Nauta adds a corrective at p. 121: *"Valla did
not uphold the classical Latin of the best authors as the only truly authoritative `consuetudo`."*
So: a rhetorical grammarian's toolkit, applied hard.

**(d) ⛔ Nauta on this treatise specifically — blunt.** p. 205: Valla *"had lent active support to his
patron by writing treatises, such as the oration on the Donation of Constantine and the `De
professione religiosorum`, against various claims made on behalf of the pope… As political
allegiances had shifted and the king no longer needed **the polemical propaganda of his militant
secretary**, Valla's room for antipapal and antireligious writing had shrunk."*

**A Harvard-published monographer calls it polemical propaganda.** The script must not pretend
otherwise.

**(e) No unified method.** p. 208: *"He approached theological issues in his characteristically
eclectic way, sometimes making use of philological arguments, sometimes giving rhetorical expression
to what he considered the authentic faith… **It is difficult to discern a comprehensive or consistent
method** in his works."* And p. 207: *"even his philological work, as some scholars have argued, did
not always live up to its high ambitions."*

**(f) But Nauta lands where we do.** Same sentence, p. 208 — the strength of Valla's scholarship lies
*"in his humanist study of words and texts, a knowledge that he put to good use in studying the New
Testament and **in exploding forgeries, legends, and false ascriptions**."*

**Net:** Valla did not have a method in the sense of a repeatable procedure he theorised. He had a
trained ear and a norm, and he used them on a document that could not survive them. **"He invented
the test" is an overclaim. "He ran the test first, and in public" is not.**

### 2.5 ⚠ BONUS COUNTER-CASE (unrequested, but it hits an exhibit): Brandes on the satraps

**Source:** Wolfram Brandes, "The Satraps of Constantine", Appendix A in Fried 2007, pp. 115–128.

Brandes's stated purpose (p. 115): *"to find passages in the text of the 'Constitutum Constantini'
which **do not necessarily** (as is still nearly continuously claimed by researchers) **indicate a

He traces *satrapa* through Justinianic legislation, Procopius, the "Letter of the Eastern

**Consequences for us, both ways:**
- ❌ **Against Exhibit 2 as a dating tell.** The satrap anomaly is real but its inference is contested:
  Fried and Brandes read it as evidence *against* Roman origin (pointing to Frankish/Carolingian
  circles), not as a clean mid-8th-century Roman fingerprint.
- ✅ **For Valla.** Brandes, p. 118: *"no-one has yet attempted a special study of this blatant
  violation of Roman-Byzantine administrative reality (**with the exception of the above-mentioned

**Also note Fried's broader position** (p. 63, ch. V opening): *"There is no evidence whatsoever that
Stephen II, Paul I, Hadrian I or Leo III ever knew of the 'Constitutum Constantini'."* The
