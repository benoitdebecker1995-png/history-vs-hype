# Donation of Constantine — active research

This is the creator-readable synthesis and the evidence that can carry the current script. It is not
the research diary. Claims that were corrected are replaced here, not left beside their replacement.
The preserved dossier is under `_cold/legacy-working/`; source PDFs remain under
`_research/sources/` and are opened by exact locator.

## What the evidence supports

The Donation's exposure did not produce one clean institutional reversal. Critics tested the text
against language, records and objects. Defenders answered in several different ways: translating a
Greek witness so that vulnerable words disappeared; treating the Donation as restitution rather than
a gift; turning missing or hostile evidence into proof of suppression or corruption; protecting a
second charter whose claims collided with the Donation; and finally accepting the forgery while
restating papal territorial power on other grounds.

The strongest counterpoint is that these were not all cynical evasions. Sangiorgi's restitution
argument is a real legal distinction. Steuco disclosed and dated the medieval copy he used and made a
reasonable argument about the age of its source. Quirini never confessed to doctoring anything, and
Setz phrases intent as an inference. Bolognini treated his university charter as genuine; the evidence
does not prove he knowingly defended either forgery. Valla himself wrote polemic, omitted evidence
that weakened one part of his case, and used strained philology when the Apostles' Creed was at stake.

The safe conclusion is narrower and better: evidence can defeat a document without defeating every
interest, legal claim or institution that relied on it.

## Where historians actually disagree

- **Why Valla wrote.** Political service to Alfonso of Aragon is real context, but Setz resists
  reducing the treatise to that motive. By late 1443 Alfonso's Naples dispute with the pope had been
  settled and Valla still refused to retract. Motive does not change the validity of his textual case.
- **When and where the Donation was composed.** Recent historiography still contains several models
  spanning roughly 750–850, including Roman/Frankish and Byzantine-oriented accounts. The current
  script does not need to choose one.
- **What Quirini intended.** His departures cluster at exactly the points Valla attacked. Setz says
  deliberate accommodation “seems permitted”; that is strong evidence for an inference, not direct
  evidence of Quirini's state of mind.
- **How modern Valla was.** His philology was decisive, but Levine warns that he was writing polemic,
  not a systematic history of when, where and why the forgery was made. Do not turn him into the
  inventor of modern historical method.

## Script-bearing evidence

### E01 — Valla's object test: imperial coins, no papal coinage

**Safe narration.** Valla argued that anyone exercising imperial rule in Rome would mint money. He
could point to surviving Christian-era coins of Constantine and later emperors—he said he owned many
himself—and asked where the corresponding papal coins were.

**Exact text.** “An infinite number of such would be found for the supreme pontiffs, if you had ever
ruled Rome! None are found—not gold, not silver, nor is any recorded as seen by anyone.” Later he
wrote: “We tell counterfeit coins apart, separate them out, throw them away—and shall we not tell
counterfeit doctrine apart?”

**Locator.** Lorenzo Valla, *De falso credita et ementita Constantini donatione*, Coleman edition,
Latin/English facing pp. 70–71; counterfeit-doctrine passage in the p. 152 region. Directly read;
the first argument is independently discussed by Levine p. 140. Held source:
`_research/sources/P2-Valla-OnTheDonationOfConstantine-Bowersock-ITatti-HarvardUP.pdf` is the preferred
modern translation for final quotation checks; retain Coleman as the directly checked bilingual
locator until the I Tatti page is confirmed.

**Exhibit.** Constantine coin imagery in `_assets/exhibits/` plus the source page. Do not imply that
absence alone proves the whole forgery; present Valla's governing premise and positive comparison.

### E02 — Cusa's independent record test

**Safe narration.** Seven years before Valla, Nicholas of Cusa asked why later popes needed Frankish
kings to recover territory if Constantine had already transferred the West.

**Locator.** Nicholas of Cusa, *The Catholic Concordance*, trans. Paul Sigmund, §298. Held source:
`_research/sources/P3-Cusa-CatholicConcordance-Sigmund-1991-CambridgeUP.pdf`.

**Limit.** The external/internal evidence framing is our explanatory shorthand, not Cusa's or
Grafton's terminology. Do not attribute Valla's linguistic argument to Cusa.

### E03 — Quirini's translation removes the vulnerable terms

**Safe narration.** On 16 June 1447 Lauro Quirini sent Nicholas V a Latin translation presented as
an ancient Greek witness. Setz found that the translation normally tracks the Greek closely but
departs where Valla's criticism would bite: *satrapes* becomes *provinciarum prefecti*; *lorum* and
its embarrassing relative clause become *munile*; other mocked details are softened or absent.

**Exact scholarly verdict.** Setz writes that the inference “seems permitted” that Quirini
deliberately took Valla's linguistic criticism into account at several points, and that the rendering
*provinciarum prefecti* appears chosen “to remove the basis of Valla's criticism.”

**Locators.** Wolfram Setz, *Lorenzo Vallas Schrift gegen die Konstantinische Schenkung* (1975),
pp. 117–120. Source Greek/Latin comparison: Fuhrmann, *Constitutum Constantini*, p. 80 line 158
(*satrapibus*) and p. 87 lines 220–222 (*frygium*, *lorum*); Quirini text in Setz p. 119.

**Exhibits.** `_assets/exhibits/EXHIBIT-Fuhrmann-p80-satrapibus.png`,
`EXHIBIT-Fuhrmann-p87-frygium-lorum.png`, and `EXHIBIT-Setz-p119-Quirini-Sanctio.png`.

**Limit.** Say “Setz infers” or “the departures land where Valla attacked.” Never say Quirini
confessed or that intent is certain.

### E04 — The supposed second witness was a textual round trip

**Safe narration.** The Greek tradition was not independent confirmation of the Donation. It
descended from the medieval Latin tradition and was later translated back into Latin. Quirini's
witness therefore belonged to the same textual family as the canon-law form Valla attacked.

**Locators.** Fuhrmann pp. 15–17 for the Latin recension and its legal transmission; Setz
pp. 106–107 and 116–117 for the Greek tradition and back-translation. Angelov pp. 91–157 confirms
the Greek transmission and the Balsamon–Blastares connection.

**Limit.** The complete chain is a synthesis across these sources, not a diagram printed by one
historian. Label it as reconstruction. Do not call every individual link independently proven by
Setz.

### E05 — Steuco saw and dated the medieval copy; his stemma was wrong

**Safe narration.** In 1530 Agostino Steuco disclosed that the Greek text had been copied for
Cardinal Benedict under Innocent III, printed the colophon, converted its date and explained why a
late copy did not settle the age of the source book. His error was not failure to notice the date. He
reversed the direction of descent, treating the Greek as ancestral when it derived from the medieval
Latin tradition. Cessi resolved that stemmatic question in 1929.

**Primary locators.** Steuco, *Adversus Lutheranos* (Bologna, 1530), held scan
`_research/sources/A19-Steuco-AdversusLutheranos-BSB11227550-SCAN-NOTEXT.pdf`:

- fol. 38r: provenance stated before the Greek text;
- fol. 42v = PDF p. 89: full colophon, including Cardinal Benedict, Innocent III and December 1206;
- fol. 44v: Steuco's conversion, “Anno millesimo, ducentesimo, ac vigesimo plus minus.”

**Secondary control.** Delph, “Valla Grammaticus, Agostino Steuco, and the Donation of Constantine,”
printed pp. 62–63; Setz pp. 106–107; Cessi 1929 for the stemma conclusion.

**Exhibit.** Prefer the three Steuco pages. The held scan is about 150 dpi: legible, but obtain a
better copy if viewers must read the Greek. The old `EXHIBIT-Delph-p60...` card is retired: the note
is on printed p. 62, and Delph uses another edition's foliation.

### E06 — Sangiorgi's serious legal move: restitution

**Safe narration.** Canon lawyer Giovanni Antonio Sangiorgi argued that Constantine was not donating
territory but restoring property Rome and its predecessors had wrongfully occupied. Proving a charter
forged would then remove that charter as evidence without itself settling ownership.

**Exact text.** “...releasing what had been unlawfully occupied by him and his predecessors.”

**Locator.** Setz p. 130, quoting Sangiorgi, *Commentaria super decretum* (1511), fol. 219v.

**Limit.** Treat this as a genuine legal distinction, not wordplay. The current sources establish its
importance in the canon-law response, not whether the underlying title was historically valid.

### E07 — Sangiorgi makes contrary evidence confirm the claim

**Safe narration.** Sangiorgi accepted three pieces of adverse evidence and reassigned their meaning:
histories were silent because emperors suppressed the story; Eusebius contradicted it because enemies
corrupted his text; the Latin was poor because Rome would have forged it more elegantly.

**Exact text for the clearest card.** “If the Roman Church had wanted to fake this donation, it would
have composed it all more elegantly and with greater ceremony.”

**Locator.** Setz pp. 132–133, quoting Sangiorgi fols. 220v and 221r.

**Interpretive limit.** “This made the claim resistant to disproof” is our analysis. Do not pretend
Sangiorgi announced a general theory of unfalsifiability.

### E08 — Bolognini and the collision between two charters

**Safe narration.** In 1491 Lodovico Bolognini defended both the Donation and Bologna's supposed 423
foundation charter, now dated to 1226–1234. The word *nostra* in the Bologna charter made Theodosius
lord of Rome, creating a conflict with Constantine's supposed transfer. Bolognini rejected the answer
that weakened the Donation. His pupil's index claims 103 replies to Valla; a contemporary Bolognese
chronicler remarked that so learned a man had failed to detect the charter's falsity.

**Exact text.** “This last answer does not please me, for I have always held and do hold that that
donation was wholly valid.”

**Locator.** Setz p. 127, quoting Bolognini, *Privilegium*, fol. 14vb; modern critical edition
Fasoli–Pighi pp. 60–64. The 103 count is the pupil's index, not an independent modern count.

**Limit.** The collision and his institutional connection are facts. The evidence does not show that
Bolognini knew either charter was forged or consciously lied.

### E09 — Piccolomini accepts the forgery and preserves the power

**Safe narration.** Enea Silvio Piccolomini defended the Donation in 1436, raised Cusa-derived doubts
in 1443 and by 1453 used two Valla-like objections. He nevertheless grounded papal temporal power in
spiritual authority and *translatio imperii*. He later became Pius II.

**Locator.** Setz pp. 105–106. Setz's conclusion: once the power was grounded elsewhere, “the
Donation plays no further role” and recognizing it as a forgery had no further consequences.

**Limit.** Setz does not examine Piccolomini's pontificate here. Do not say Pius II recanted or later
invoked the Donation as pope.

### E10 — Circulation, not early suppression

**Safe narration.** At least twenty-five manuscripts of Valla's treatise are known. It first appeared
in print in 1506. The surviving record does not document Church suppression before the Reformation;
the first documented institutional ban was the 1559 Index listing.

**Exact text.** Setz: “Es sind heute nicht weniger als 25 Handschriften bekannt...” and “Bis heute
ist kein Zeugnis bekannt, das eine kirchliche Intervention belegt.”

**Locators.** Setz pp. 90–91 (PDF pp. 114–115) for the manuscript count and refutation of the
suppression conjecture; p. 101, corroborated at pp. 178 and 193, for the absence of testimony and the
1559 turn.

**Limit.** Do not say the treatise was continuously popular. Bowersock notes little trace of interest
until the 1480s, while Setz records one 1451 manuscript. Safe: it attracted little attention for a
generation, then circulated broadly; there is no evidence of the early suppression story.

### E11 — Valla's method had motivated limits

**Safe narration.** Valla's argument was philologically powerful and rhetorically interested. He
omitted an ancient Saint Peter's mosaic inscription that weakened part of his case. In a 1444 dispute
about the Apostles' Creed, Camporeale finds that he forced a textual emendation inconsistent with a
disinterested application of the method.

**Locators.** Camporeale, *Christianity, Latinity, and Culture*, held PDF
`_research/sources/A6-Camporeale-ChristianityLatinityCulture-TwoStudiesOnValla-Brill.pdf`; use the
relevant direct page before final narration. Grafton discusses the mosaic omission; Bowersock
corrects the associated imperial son to Constans.

**Limit.** The current final paragraph is load-bearing and must receive a fresh direct-page check
after creator wording. “Declined to do so where the answer would have cost him” is an interpretation,
not a quotation from Camporeale.

## Corrections that must stay out of active retrieval

- **Not 13 manuscripts.** Notebook-generated “13” and its shelfmarks were fabricated. Direct Setz
  reading gives twenty-five.
- **Not “Steuco failed to notice.”** He disclosed, dated and argued from the colophon. His textual
  family tree ran backwards.
- **No Quirini confession.** Intent is Setz's cautious inference from a collation.
- **No conscious Bolognini fraud.** The institutional collision is documented; knowledge is not.
- **No early Church burial story.** No testimony documents intervention before the Reformation.
- **No straight line from Valla to Mabillon.** Direct search of *De re diplomatica* found no Valla or
  Donation lineage; do not revive the abandoned closing.
