# Project 62 — research

Creator-readable synthesis plus the evidence that can bear script claims. Not the research diary.
Superseded interpretations are not preserved beside the current ones; the older working files are in
`_cold/legacy-working/` and Git holds the history.

---

## The argument, in one paragraph

OUN-B and UPA structures carried out an organised campaign against Polish communities in Volhynia in
1943. The record strongly supports regional command responsibility. It does not supply an
authenticated signed master order and it does not establish Bandera's personal operational role. The
UPA also fought a real, sustained insurgency against Soviet power. Ukrainian state memory can
therefore draw on a genuine independence struggle while acknowledging Volhynia without letting it
define the organisation. Poland's objection is exactly that this separation becomes untenable when
the state honours the organisation itself.

## The three propositions the video tests

1. The anti-Polish campaign was organised, not a spontaneous village uprising. **Strong.**
2. The exact originating command is less certain than the campaign itself. **Unresolved, and stays
   unresolved in narration.**
3. The anti-Soviet insurgency was real. **Strong, including from hostile Soviet records.**

## Where the paper trail ends

Say this plainly rather than writing around it. Several contemporary records show anti-Polish actions
being assigned, implemented, discussed and evaluated through OUN/UPA structures — which makes a purely
spontaneous explanation difficult to sustain. The evidence does not reconstruct the first decision and
does not establish Bandera's direction. Regional responsibility for Dmytro Kliachkivsky is strongly
supported; central-versus-regional initiation is open.

## Evidence

Format: claim · status · evidence · exact location · limits · planned use.

### E01 — The textbook double claim *(the opening rests on this)*

**Claim.** A current Ukrainian textbook states that the Volhynian OUN-B leadership decided to expel
the Polish population and that UPA units attacked Polish settlements in July 1943 — and the same
textbook describes the UPA's later fight against Soviet security forces as a "heroic struggle."

**Status.** Verified in the claim ledger of 17 August, at ceiling *"quote 'heroic struggle' with page."*

**Open before filming.** An older synthesis of 16 August marks the same-textbook version of the second
half as *"unsafe claim now"* pending direct page inspection. The newer ledger supersedes it, but the
page number and checked translation must be visible on screen. **Do not film the opening until the
exact page is in hand.** If it cannot be produced, the safe fallback is: *a current Ukrainian textbook
acknowledges the expulsion decision and the attacks; separate official educational and memory material
presents the UPA struggle as heroic* — which is supported, and which changes the opening.

**Limits.** One Ministry-listed edition. Say "this textbook" or "at least one official textbook."
Never "Ukrainian schools" as a universal claim.

**Use.** Narration and on-screen exhibit. Whole-page identity, the anti-Polish passage, the heroic
passage, checked translation, exact pages.

### E02 — The 2026 honour and the Polish response

**Claim.** In May 2026 a Ukrainian presidential decree gave a special-forces unit the honorary name
"Heroes of the UPA." Poland's president subsequently withdrew Zelensky's Order of the White Eagle.

**Status.** Verified. Decree 440/2026, 26 May 2026; Polish revocation 19 June 2026.

**Limits.** State the action. Do not add an interval between the two events unless it is needed.

**Use.** Cold open, both as narration and as rendered document cards. Re-verify the decree page in the
week of upload — the current card carries a warning that the number is not yet on screen.

### E03 — What OUN-B wanted, and why Polish settlement was a problem

**Claim.** OUN-B's objective was an independent Ukrainian state. Volhynia and Eastern Galicia were
inside interwar Poland with a large Polish population, which nationalists treated as a territorial
obstacle to that state after the war.

**Status.** Verified.

**Use.** Narration. Plain organisational aim; no Bandera operational claim attached.

### E04 — Kolodzinskyi and the pre-war doctrine

**Claim.** Mykhailo Kolodzinskyi advocated removing what he called the "Polish element."

**Status.** Verified via edition; on-screen doctrine card available.

**Limits.** Pre-war idea. **Explicitly not a 1943 order.** The card must carry that label.

**Use.** Narration and exhibit.

### E05 — Interwar Polish policy

**Claim.** Polish policies helped radicalise part of the nationalist movement.

**Status.** Verified.

**Limits.** Ukrainians could vote and had legal political parties; there was also repression,
restriction and unkept autonomy promises. Repression explains a grievance. It does not cause or
justify the massacre, and it does not make what followed inevitable. Most Ukrainians were never in the
OUN.

**Use.** Narration, with the concession stated before the limitation.

### E06 — The police desertions: capacity, not policy

**Claim.** Around five thousand Ukrainian auxiliary policemen deserted German service in spring 1943,
bringing weapons, training and combat experience. OUN-B moved to absorb them before rivals could.

**Status.** Verified. Snyder printed p.167 / PDF p.183; Rossoliński-Liebe PDF p.233; McBride.

**Limits.** Say the influx substantially expanded capacity. Do **not** claim the earlier UPA was tiny
or incapable. Joining paths varied — some willing, some pressured, some following their units. This
establishes capacity, not collective perpetration and not policy.

**Use.** Narration. This is the mechanism the draft kept skipping.

### E07 — 11 July 1943

**Claim.** Nearly a hundred localities were attacked on 11 July, some during Mass. At Poryck the first
shots came during the Gloria.

**Status.** Verified. Motyka / McBride; the Poryck detail is Motyka reproducing Jadwiga Krajewska's
testimony.

**Limits.** "Nearly a hundred" — do not present 167 as uncontested. Attribute Poryck to the survivor
and paraphrase rather than quote.

**Use.** Narration; Motyka p.122 as exhibit, plus a restrained map.

### E08 — Scale

**Claim.** Fifty to sixty thousand Polish civilians were killed in Volhynia in 1943.

**Status.** Verified.

**Limits.** Keep the geography and the year attached to the number every time it is spoken.

**Use.** Narration; Siemaszko named-victim registry as exhibit.

### E09 — Document 149, the main narrated exhibit

**Claim.** An internal Security Service report from the Zahrava military region calls it an "action of
destroying the Poles," and records that local SB punishment decisions were often not effectively
controlled from above.

**Status.** Verified. *Litopys UPA*, New Series vol. 2 (Kyiv–Toronto 1999), Document 149. TsDAVO
f. 3838, op. 1, spr. 55, ark. 1–5. Copy, signed "Tretiy." Covers 15 September – 15 October 1943.

**Limits.** Use the reporting period, not the erroneous 3 September date on the document. Translate
`нищення` conservatively as **"destroying"**, never "extermination," until a native-Ukrainian review
says otherwise. The document does not by itself prove a precise extermination objective, and the weak
local control applies to local SB control, not to the whole campaign. Do not speak the archive
locator; show it.

**Use.** Main narrated exhibit. Original page, Ukrainian text preserved, labelled translation,
provenance visible.

### E10 — Organisational convergence

**Claim.** Several independent contemporary records show anti-Polish actions being assigned,
implemented and evaluated through OUN/UPA structures.

**Status.** Verified. Huta Stepańska action report, Document 100, 27 July 1943 (UPA First Group).
Volodymyr–Horokhiv district report, July 1943 — which records weak or absent execution in some
localities. Document 149. Krai Order No. 1/43, 27 October 1943.

**Limits.** Krai Order No. 1/43 is logistical and regulatory. **It is not evidence of an extermination
policy and must never be shown as one.** No single source may be made to serve as a general order.

**Use.** Under the hood, with Documents 100 and 149 as possible visuals.

### E11 — Stelmashchuk, and why it is not the proof

**Claim.** Yurii Stelmashchuk said in Soviet custody in 1945 that Kliachkivsky relayed an oral
directive.

**Status.** Verified as testimony, with serious caveats.

**Limits.** Soviet custody. Coercive context. A surviving typed copy, not an accessible original.
Retrospective. **Not needed in narration.** If shown at all, it must carry `COPY, TYPESCRIPT` and
`SOVIET CUSTODY` on screen, and the published English translation should be used rather than an
unchecked one. Note the tension: the script's justification for trusting Soviet records is
admission-against-interest, and an interrogation protocol is the opposite of that.

**Use.** Optional. The section is stronger built on records the movement produced about itself.

### E12 — The real historiographical disagreement

**Claim.** There is a genuine dispute over how much explanatory weight belongs to local social
conflict and wartime collapse versus organisation through OUN/UPA structures.

**Status.** Verified with attribution. Motyka, *Zbrodnia wołyńska 1943 roku i mit buntu ludowego*,
*Dzieje Najnowsze* 48(1) 2016, pp. 53–66 — who identifies the spontaneous-revenge hypothesis and
rejects it. Hud & Muravskyi 2024 give greater weight to long-term social conflict. McBride shows
organisation and local agency coexisting.

**Limits.** Do not caricature the alternative position as claiming the UPA merely happened to be
present. Do not use "Jacquerie" without attributing it to Motyka's description of the appearance he is
rebutting.

**Use.** Narration. This is what stops the video being a prosecution.

### E13 — The anti-Soviet insurgency

**Claim.** The UPA/OUN underground conducted real armed resistance against Soviet power, documented in
Soviet-produced records. Large formal formations declined; a smaller OUN/SB underground continued for
years.

**Status.** Verified, including from adversarial Soviet operational records.

**Limits.** The movement also coerced and killed Ukrainian civilians and suspected collaborators. Real
resistance does not sanitise Volhynia; Volhynia does not make the resistance imaginary. Do not carry
insecure battlefield numbers, including the disputed Hurby figures. Do not invent a decisive
battlefield achievement — duration and persistence supply the significance.

**Use.** Narration; two Soviet operational pages with producer and date as exhibits, matched to the
narrated examples.

### E14 — Germany

**Claim.** OUN-B cooperation with Nazi Germany in 1941 was real, including the proclamation's
cooperation language. German repression of OUN-B leadership was also real.

**Status.** Verified.

**Limits.** Neither "German puppet" nor "major anti-Nazi resistance" describes the record. Keep this
concise — it is a Russia-beat, not a chapter.

**Use.** Brief narration; the 1941 proclamation plus a Rossoliński-Liebe attribution card.

### E15 — The memory mechanism

**Claim.** Regional rehabilitation after 1991, mostly in the west → 2010 presidential decree naming
OUN and UPA members among twentieth-century independence fighters → 2014 as the hinge → 2015 Law
No. 314-VIII recognising OUN and UPA as organisations that fought for independence and making
commemoration state policy → 2022 → the 2026 decree, which states its own reason.

**Status.** Verified. Kasianov; the law itself; the decree.

**Limits.** This is increasing relevance and state recognition. It is **not** mass ideological
conversion, and it is not "nationalist memory replaced Soviet memory" — the textbook beat disproves
that. The 2015 law recognises; it does not create a penalty.

**Use.** Narration; the enacted Law 314-VIII capture and the decree as exhibits.

### E16 — Polling

**Claim.** Rating Group, 27 April 2022: 81/10, n=1000 CATI outside occupied Crimea and Donbas.
KIIS 2025, on a WWII-activity question: 37/8/50.

**Status.** Verified.

**Limits.** **Different questions. Not a trend.** Say so explicitly. Omit the spoken 5% from the KIIS
figures. Exact question wording, sample and fieldwork must be on the card before any number is spoken.

**Use.** Narration and separate poll cards.

### E17 — The fairness beat

**Claim.** In February 2024 Poland's Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy stopped official
commemorative events for Józef Kuraś "Ogień" and the Świętokrzyska Brigade over violence against
civilians. The IPN objected publicly, acknowledging that killings of unarmed civilians cannot be
justified but arguing that critically judged actions should not erase a contribution to the struggle
for a free Poland.

**Status.** Verified. Ministry decision 16 February 2024; IPN response 20 February 2024.

**Limits.** Fifteen to twenty-five seconds. **State explicitly that the cases are not equivalent.**
The comparison is between competing rules for public honour, not between crimes. Do not call it Polish
hypocrisy and do not say Poland "does the same thing."

**Use.** Narration, placed after joint mourning and before the close. Both statements shown as two
sides of an institutional dispute.

### E18 — Joint mourning

**Claim.** In 2023 the two presidents jointly mourned the victims.

**Status.** Verified.

**Limits.** Joint mourning only. It does not mean the dispute is resolved — and it is also why "neither
side can retreat from its position" is false and must not be said.

**Use.** Narration.

## Claims deliberately excluded

Do not reintroduce these. Each was considered and rejected on the evidence.

- A precise extermination objective inferred from `нищення` alone.
- A signed central order, or a settled answer on central-versus-regional initiation.
- Bandera's personal operational direction of the campaign. Absence of evidence is also not proof he
  opposed it.
- Equivalence between Kuraś and the UPA, or between the relevant crimes.
- A polling trend between 2022 and 2025.
- Symmetry between Volhynia 1943 and Sahryń or Operation Vistula. Both are real and belong in the
  account; neither makes the campaigns symmetrical.
- "Nationalist memory replaced Soviet memory." The textbook refutes it.
- "Ukraine buries the massacre." This was narrowed away from on 14 August and has since been walked
  back into drafts twice. It is closed.
