## Evidentiary ceilings

### Document 100 can safely establish

- A contemporary UPA First Group operational report describes a planned action around Huta Stepańska.
- It assigns two groups to named settlement/colony “liquidation” tasks.
- It narrates operations on 17–19 July and reports named settlements/colonies “liquidated” in its conclusions.
- It supports the narration's core point that UPA operational involvement is visible in the movement's own reporting.

### Document 100 cannot safely establish

- That the report itself explicitly says “26 Polish settlements.” It does not.
- A count of exactly 26 without an externally stated counting rule. The mission, assignment and conclusions lists differ, and some entries combine or separate villages, colonies and hamlets.
- Local Ukrainian participation, unless supported with another exact passage/source.
- The origin of a general command, a Central Provid decision, or campaign-wide command structure.
- Civilian status for every person or settlement described by the report's hostile terminology.

### Document 149 can safely establish

- Internal discussion and evaluation of an anti-Polish “action.”
- The immediate explanatory context that the “active Polish element” was largely preserved.
- The narrow observation that higher control over local SB structures and punishment decisions was weak or ineffective in the ways the report describes.

### Document 149 cannot safely establish

- A signed Central Provid master order.
- The exact intended final result from `нищення` alone.
- Bandera's personal direction.
- Perfect centralized control.
- Complete decentralization of the anti-Polish campaign.
