## ATTRIBUTION MISMATCHES (the reason this claim is dangerous)
- **MIS-CREDIT / UNPINNABLE SPEAKER:** the claim as parked implies "an OUN/UPA figure" that a card would want to name or treat as a leadership statement. The sources support **only "one of the UPA commanders" — explicitly UNNAMED, in both RL and Motyka.** The single *named* actor in the chain is the **Polish infiltration agent** (Łada), not the UPA speaker. → **CANNOT PIN a speaker. Do not name one; do not imply a specific meeting or resolution.**
- **PREDICATE-DRIFT (policy vs individual):** this is **NOT official OUN/UPA policy** and **NOT a signed OUN/UPA document.** It is one commander's remark, captured by an enemy agent. It must never drift into "official OUN policy" / "the OUN declared." RL frames it as an individual leader's statement relayed by Polish intelligence — keep it there.
- **SOURCE-CLASS caveat (adversarial + second-hand):** the root is a **Polish underground/AK intelligence report** — the hostile side's account of an enemy's words, the same evidentiary class the project already caveats for the NKVD Stelmashchuk testimony. A Ukrainian-camp critic (e.g. Viatrovych) would challenge exactly this kind of Polish-spy report of an unnamed enemy. Frame with the "as reported by Polish underground intelligence" caveat spoken, or don't use it.
- **DATE:** the statement is anchored to the **turn of 1942/43** (Łada's report) and internally to the **1 March 1943** uprising. There is **no exact date** for when the UPA commander spoke. Say "reported in early 1943," not a precise date.
