## Finding in one sentence

The old conclusion "archivally restricted / unreachable" is obsolete: the Sectoral State Archive of the SBU (HDA SBU) currently accepts e-mail requests while its reading room is closed under martial law. But the request must first establish whether `spr. 11315, t.1, ch.2, ark.16` exists in Kyiv or in the Volyn SBU archive, whether it has a part 2 at all, and whether it contains a contemporary Klymchak text rather than a later Soviet interrogation, a transcription, or nothing of the kind.

## What remains verified

- The rendered owned copy of McBride p.648, `genealogy/mcbride-p648.png`, accurately reads: “I liquidated all Poles from young to old. I burned all the buildings and took the possessions and livestock for the needs of the battalion.” His fn.102 gives `HDA SBU, spr.11315, t.1, ch.2, ark.16`, then Filar p.34 and Piotrowski, *Genocide and Rescue in Wolyn*, p.180.
- That page is a **scholarly English reproduction**, not an image of the document. It is therefore showable only as McBride's rendering, not as a verified original-language primary.
- The original-language version circulating in the literature varies: “from young to old” / “from the youngest ones,” and 29 August / 30 August / 29 September. Do not merge those variants or back-translate McBride as a Ukrainian original.

## The new access result (current, official)

1. [HDA SBU's official archive page](https://ssu.gov.ua/haluzevyi-derzhavnyi-arkhiv) says that Soviet-period records have been open since 2015, including UPA materials; it says many documents are digitized and that the remainder can be consulted through archive requests.
2. The same page says the **reading room is temporarily closed because of martial law**, but requests for archival information are accepted by e-mail or post. Its current archive contact is **arhivsbu@ssu.gov.ua**.
3. This is operational, not merely theoretical: an [SBU report of 10 June 2024](https://ssu.gov.ua/novyny/inozemni-doslidnyky-vyvchaiut-metody-roboty-rosiiskykh-spetssluzhb-za-materialamy-arkhivu-sbu-dyrektor-arkhivu) says the archive supplied nearly **5 TB** of copies in the preceding year. It also explicitly discusses foreign researchers using the archive.

**Retrieval verdict:** **REQUESTABLE NOW; SCAN NOT YET ACQUIRED.** Send the bounded request below. An affirmative archive response with colour scans is the only legitimate route that can upgrade this exhibit to primary-grounded.

## Fees and rights — what is verified, and what still needs the archive's answer

- The SBU's own [information-request FAQ](https://ssu.gov.ua/en/dostup-do-publichnoi-informatsii) says information is free; reimbursement applies when copying exceeds ten pages, with an exception for information of public interest. This request is initially five folios (14–18), so no charge is stated for the requested scope.
- If the archive treats the scan as a chargeable reproduction rather than its usual information response, the nationwide maximum in [Cabinet Resolution No. 4](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/4-2020-%D0%BF) is 0.1% of the subsistence minimum per scanned page. The [2026 Budget Act](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/4695-20) sets that minimum for working-age people at UAH 3,328: **at most UAH 3.33 per scanned page** (A4 printing at most UAH 6.66; A3 at most UAH 9.98). SBU has not published an archive-specific tariff or invoice procedure for this request, so ask for a quote and payment instructions before work begins.
- The current [National Archival Fund Act, art. 8](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/3814-12) permits reproduction, incorporation into other copyright objects, and publication without the economic-rights holder's consent for intellectual-property objects contained in archives of repressive organs or Ukrainian liberation-movement structures. That is a strong legal basis for a documentary use of a confirmed scan. Still ask HDA SBU for its required credit line and whether it places any file-specific condition on supplying the image.
- **Do not infer the scan's licence from the SBU website footer.** Its CC BY 4.0 statement licenses site content, not automatically every archival reproduction.

## The locator and provenance problem (why the request is framed as a verification request)

The existing quotation chain is not a provenance chain to a viewed document:

1. **McBride p.648, fn.102** → same `spr.11315, t.1, ch.2, ark.16` + Filar p.34 + Piotrowski p.180.
2. **McBride p.647, fn.63** uses the *identical locator* for a different item: a 24 June 1943 Stelmashchuk→Lebed letter. He says he and other authors had not been able to review the file directly.
3. **Ihor Iliushyn, _Volynska trahediia 1943–1944_** describes the Klymchak item as appearing **in the interrogation protocol of Yurii Stelmashchuk**, and dates the alleged report 29 August 1943 — not as a verified standalone captured UPA original. The relevant passage is publicly indexed by the [Institute of History of Ukraine](https://history.org.ua/LiberUA/966-02-2872-4/966-02-2872-4.pdf).
4. **Serhii Riabenko's 2016 critique** says he inspected the Volyn SBU criminal case to which Nakonechnyi referred; he reports that it has a single undivided volume, no “part 2,” and contains no Klymchak report or Ostrivky/Wola Ostrowiecka action. This is a specific counterclaim, not a reason to declare the quote false without an archive response. Read it here: [Istorychna Pravda, 22 Oct. 2016](https://www.istpravda.com.ua/articles/2016/10/22/149259/).

**Resulting flags:**

- **[FLAG: PROVENANCE — SECONDARY-ONLY].** The original's existence, form, and custody are not verified.
- **[FLAG: ATTRIBUTION MISMATCH].** “Signed archival document” and “original Klymchak after-action report” are unsupported. If the folio proves to be a Soviet interrogation or later synopsis, the correct tier is **PRIMARY-VIA-TESTIMONY**, not a UPA original.
- **SAME-FOLIO / REPOSITORY ANOMALY.** One exact folio cannot be asserted to be three distinct documents without the file cover, internal inventory, and image. The citation alternates between central HDA SBU and the Volyn SBU archive. The archive must resolve both questions.

### What the sweep did establish

1. **The citation has unstable surface details.** Public repetitions give both **29** and **30 August 1943**; vary the date of the action, the addressee (OUN leadership/UPA), and render the property clause as `mienie i chudobę`, `mienie i bydło`, or English “possessions and livestock.” These are transcription variants, not independent witnesses. The date/wording must remain unpinned until the requested folio is supplied.
2. **Iliushyn strengthens the need for custody verification, not the report's authenticity.** His openly accessible study describes the Klymchak material as in Stelmashchuk's interrogation protocol, rather than presenting a stand-alone captured UPA report. That is compatible with a Soviet case-file transcription, and inconsistent with confidently showing an original Klymchak sheet.
3. **Litopys UPA New Series vol. 9 does not contain a Klymchak report.** It lawfully reproduces Stelmashchuk's Soviet interrogation material. Its pp.441–442 are a useful replacement evidence object, but a different item: p.441 says Savur gave an organisational directive; p.442 attributes the 29–30 August action to military-district commander “Oleh,” not to Klymchak and not to Savur.
4. **GARF f.9478 op.1 d.399 is a different route to the February 1945 Stelmashchuk interrogation, not an alternate Klymchak original.** McBride's fn.62 identifies its fol.18 as the February 28 protocol containing the exact “physical destruction” testimony. No public GARF scan was found.
5. **The IPN and USHMM searches did not produce the item.** IPN confirms a long-running source-publication collaboration with HDA SBU, but its public series list gives no 11315/Klymchak item. USHMM RG-31.026M remains a Communist Party/TsDAHO collection, not the HDA SBU case. Neither is a legitimate substitute for the archive request.
