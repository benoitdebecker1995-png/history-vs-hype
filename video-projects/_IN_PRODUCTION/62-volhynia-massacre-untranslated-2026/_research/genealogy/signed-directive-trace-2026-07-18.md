We should take advantage of the occasion, before the German forces withdraw, to liquidate the entire Polish population from 16 to 60. ... We cannot lose this battle and, without counting the cost, we should diminish the Polish strength. Forest villages and those near forests should disappear from the face of the earth.'"* Its **footnote 499** is the next rung. TIER: secondary, stated as if directly sourced.

## THE PRIMARY (bedrock — reached, and it's a locked door)

- **Document:** HDA SBU (Branch Archive of the Security Service of Ukraine) / Archiwum SBU Obwodu Wołyńskiego, **spr. 11315, t.1, ch.2, ark.16** (= "d. no. 11315, I, pt. II, p.16" in Piotrowski's notation).
- **What it is claimed to contain, per the three different historians who cite it:** (a) the 24 June 1943 Stelmashchuk→Lebed letter (McBride fn.63); (b) a Klymchak-adjacent report/confession (McBride fn.102, also cited by Filar and Piotrowski's *Genocide and Rescue*); (c) Piotrowski's "secret directive... signed by Klachkivskyi" (*Poland's Holocaust* fn.499). **A single archival folio holding a compiled transcription of several short items is entirely plausible for an NKVD-era case file** — which is exactly why three quoters extract three different fragments and characterize the whole thing three different ways.
- **Custody chain:** the sole researcher of record who ever had access and published transcriptions from it is **Vladyslav Nakonechnyi**, a former Soviet Communist Party regional archivist in Lutsk (2006 book, pp.104–5, 116–7). Everyone else — Filar (1999), Piotrowski (1998, 2000), Siemaszko & Siemaszko, Motyka (2011/2016) — draws on Nakonechnyi's transcription or on each other, **never on the folio itself.**
- **Access status:** **UNREACHED, archival-only, now inaccessible.** McBride (2016), the most rigorous scholar in this corpus, states plainly: *"I and other authors have not been able to review this file directly due to recent archival restrictions."* Katchanovski (2020), the one scholar who physically tried, reports (personal communication, SBU Volyn archive) the file *"either became classified again or [was] burned during the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991."*
- **Showable file:** **none exists.** No photo, scan, or facsimile of this folio appears anywhere in the notebook corpus or the open literature. This is the honest opposite of Row 4 (Order No. 11), where a T1 scan was retrieved.
- **Rung reached + route:** owned-library raw-read (Piotrowski, Siemaszko, Motyka, McBride, Katchanovski — all in-notebook) → the citation trail converges on a single archival locator that is independently confirmed unreachable by the two scholars (McBride, Katchanovski) who actually tried. **Already at the wall** — no further access route exists short of a future HDA SBU Kyiv declassification.
- **How verified:** char-exact grep of `source_get_content` raw text for all four sources (Piotrowski, Siemaszko & Siemaszko, McBride, Motyka), cross-referencing footnote numbers and archival locators. `notebook_query` was not used for any evidentiary claim.

## ATTRIBUTION MISMATCHES

1. **SOURCE-DRIFT / footnote-laundering — Piotrowski (*Poland's Holocaust* p.247, fn.499).** Piotrowski cites the archive directly ("Archiwum SBU Obwodu Wołynskiego, d. no. 11315...") as if he personally consulted it. He did not: no Western or Ukrainian scholar with institutional access (McBride) has been able to review this file, and Piotrowski — a US-based sociologist writing in 1998, a year *before* Filar's own monograph reproducing this material was even published (Filar 1999) — has no plausible independent path to the Volyn SBU archive. Every *other* archival citation in this same paragraph-cluster of Piotrowski's book is explicitly routed **"as quoted by Filar"** (fn.494, fn.500) — this one, fn.499, is not, which is itself the tell. Almost certainly Filar-derived (via Filar's 1995 *Semper Fidelis* article or private correspondence/advance draft), presented without the "as quoted by" disclosure.

2. **PREDICATE-DRIFT — the quoted text itself may be misdated/mistranslated (Katchanovski's independent finding).** Piotrowski's exact wording ("liquidate the entire Polish population from 16 to 60," "before the German forces withdraw," "disappear from the face of the earth") matches, almost verbatim in structure, the document Katchanovski separately analyzes as **likely 1944, concerning Galicia** (post-German-retreat), not June 1943 Volhynia — and as a Polish-translation **distortion** of an original that specified "Polish MEN 16–60" (a targeted military-age-male scope), rendered by Filar-lineage translations as "Polish population" (implying total-population genocide). If Katchanovski is right, Piotrowski's headline "signed directive" quote is not only unauthenticated as "signed," it may be the **wrong document, wrong year, wrong region, and mistranslated** in its most quoted clause.

3. **MIS-CREDIT / echoed characterization — Siemaszko & Siemaszko.** They use "podpisanej przez 'Kłyma Sawura'" (signed by Klym Savur) but **explicitly attribute this to Filar's edited collective volume** — i.e. they are repeating Filar's characterization, not independently verifying a signature. This is the second "leading historian" reference the script line needs, and it is now raw-confirmed.

4. **No drift — Motyka.** Motyka's account ("przekazał," relayed/passed, evidenced by testimony + Stelmashchuk's own signed letter) is the accurate baseline against which Piotrowski's and Siemaszko's "signed" language is shown to be loose.

## PROVENANCE VERDICT + TIER

- **"A signed Klym Savur kill-order exists":** **FALSE as stated.** No such examinable document exists anywhere in the corpus or the open literature. **SECONDARY-ONLY** for the "signed" characterization — it traces to a single unreachable archival folio (spr.11315 t.1 ch.2 ark.16) that no scholar (including the two who tried, McBride and Katchanovski) has been able to examine, and whose contents reach us only through a Soviet-era Communist Party archivist's (Nakonechnyi's) 2006 transcription.
- **"Klyachkivsky signed other, non-kill orders":** **PRIMARY-GROUNDED, T1.** Order No. 11 (4 Sept 1943) is retrieved, rendered, and authenticated (Row 4).
- **"The oral-directive-via-testimony chain is real":** **PRIMARY-VIA-TESTIMONY,** per Row 1/5 of the ledger (McBride p.642, Litopys Vol.9 pp.441–442) — the duress-caveat NKVD-interrogation tier, not a document tier.
