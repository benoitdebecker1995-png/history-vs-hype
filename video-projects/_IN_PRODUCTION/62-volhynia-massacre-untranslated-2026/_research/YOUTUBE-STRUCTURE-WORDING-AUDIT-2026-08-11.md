# YouTube Structure and Wording Audit — Volhynia / UPA

**Script audited:** `SCRIPT.md` (168 lines; 1,932 parsed spoken words)  
**Target:** 12-minute educational-history video  
**Timing basis:** creator's measured 163 wpm → approximately **11:51** before any unplanned pause or ad-lib  
**Voice authority:** T8 ad-lib, `09-ADLIB-HARVEST-2026-08-11.md`, `VOICE-CORPUS-FOR-MODEL-PASSES.md`, and canonical `VOICE-PROFILE.md`  
**Audit scope:** viewer-facing structure, open-question continuity, evidence/visual presentation, comprehension for the ear, spoken naturalness, and narrowly separated platform risk. This is not a fact-check.

## Headline finding

The script has a strong, intelligible spine and a real YouTube payoff: a live 2026 dispute opens the video, the chronology turns an idea into an army, Poryck turns the army into a human event, the paper trail tests organization without inventing a signed order, and the modern-memory section explains why the same organization is still useful to Ukraine. It meets the channel's academic-source twist better than a normal narrative-history script.

It is **not ready to film unchanged**, for three viewer-facing reasons:

1. The opening uses `UPA` and `OUN` before either is explained, delays the exact subject **Volhynia massacre**, and spends 18 seconds on method before the viewer knows who the men were (`SCRIPT.md` lines 12–20).
2. The central label dispute reaches `ethnic cleansing`, `genocide`, and `genocidal ethnic cleansing` without defining the difference (`lines 86–90`). That is the video's intellectual hinge, so the viewer cannot be asked to infer it.
3. The intended final question at line 166 is followed by administrative outro copy at line 168. The creator explicitly chose line 166 as the one unanswered closing question; line 168 removes that effect and weakens the finish.

The other major risk is presentation rather than wording: document and map cues are bundled at the ends of long sections. If filmed literally, the viewer hears the explanation before seeing the evidence. The visual cue needs to sit beside the sentence it proves, especially in lines 54, 68–72, 104–112, and 132–144.

## Verdict

**NEEDS ONE LOCAL REVISION PASS BEFORE FILMING.** The architecture is worth keeping. No re-outline is needed. Fix the three blocking items, pair the documents to the exact sentences, and preserve the creator-protected lines. With those changes, this is strong educational-history YouTube: accessible chronology, human evidence, honest source limits, and a modern payoff that only works because the historical case was earned first.

## BLOCKING BEFORE FILMING

### B1. The hook fulfills the title but does not orient an unfamiliar viewer fast enough

**Lines:** 12–20 (`~0:00–0:59`)  
**Viewer experience:** The decree and Polish retaliation create immediate stakes, but the listener hears `UPA` at line 12 and both `OUN` and `UPA` at line 14 without knowing what either group was. `These men` at line 16 therefore has no stable referent beyond the title's scare-quoted “Heroes.” The script finally expands UPA at line 28, about 59 seconds in. The exact search/topic phrase `Volhynia massacre` is also not spoken as a unit in the first minute.

**Why this matters:** This is an open-question-led video. The viewer can hold “why did Poland react?” only after they know what body Zelensky honoured. Right now the method declaration in line 18 delays the answer the viewer is waiting for. The current primary-source cue also appears only after the whole opening, rather than beside the decree and Polish statement.

**Voice check:** Line 18 grows directly out of the T8 ad-lib and is natural creator reasoning, but it is not one of the protected, do-not-rewrite lines in the voice corpus. The proposed version keeps the creator's plain first-person inquiry and “what the sources can and cannot prove,” without the early research-process paragraph.

**Proposed rewrite (lines 12–20):**

> On May 26th, 2026, President Zelensky named an elite Ukrainian special-forces unit after the “Heroes of the UPA” — the Ukrainian Insurgent Army.
>
> Three weeks later, Poland's president stripped Zelensky of the Order of the White Eagle, Poland's highest honour. The official Polish statement called crimes committed by the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists and the UPA genocide. Zelensky sent the medal back.
>
> When I first saw this story, I wanted to understand why Ukraine would honour the UPA despite the Volhynia massacre, and why that could create a dispute with one of its most important allies in the middle of a war.
>
> So I looked at the academic work and followed it back to the primary sources where I could, because this dispute turns on what those sources can and cannot prove. First, who were the UPA?

**Presentation fix:** Put the decree on screen during line 12, the official Polish statement during line 14, and the title question during the final sentence. Do not wait until line 22 to begin the evidence sequence.

### B2. The label dispute is not explained in plain language

**Lines:** 86–90 (`~5:52–6:16`)  
**Viewer experience:** The script correctly says the death toll is not the argument and the framing is. It then gives three academic labels in twelve seconds. A listener who does not already know the legal/historical distinction hears scholars sorting the same event into near-synonyms and cannot tell what the disagreement changes.

**Why this matters:** This is the setup for the command-evidence question at line 92. Without the definitions, “was it organized?” sounds like a new topic rather than the reason the genocide label turns on intent and command.

**Voice check:** “The numbers aren't the main point of contention. What they dispute is the framing” is creator-protected and must remain. The rewrite begins after that line. It uses plain definitions, not a legal lecture.

**Proposed rewrite (lines 88–90):**

> Ethnic cleansing means an organized campaign to drive a population from a territory. Genocide requires an intent to destroy that group, at least in part. McBride calls Volhynia ethnic cleansing. Katchanovski rejects the genocide label, but still calls it a one-sided ethnic cleansing organized by the OUN-B and UPA. Motyka calls it genocidal ethnic cleansing.
>
> So they disagree about the final label, but they still describe an organized campaign to remove the Polish population.

**Verification note:** The plain-language definition should be checked against the project's already verified genocide-definition material before insertion. The structural need for a definition is blocking even if the final wording changes.

### B3. The final payoff is not allowed to be final

**Lines:** 164–168 (`~11:25–11:51`)  
**Viewer experience:** Lines 164–166 produce the strongest ending in the script: a personal moral limit, then the creator's chosen unanswered question. Line 168 then reopens the administrative layer—thanks, sources below, make up your own mind—and turns the question into a penultimate thought.

**Why this matters:** The voice corpus is explicit that line 166 is meant to stand unanswered. It is also the cleanest possible universal payoff to the Zelensky/Poland opening. Every sentence after it lowers the ending's recall value.

**Voice check:** Lines 164 and 166 are creator-protected. Do not rewrite, reorder, hedge, or turn the question into a statement.

**Fix:** Cut line 168 from spoken VO. Put **“Sources linked below”** on the end card after the final question. Do not add a spoken replacement.

## Voice-authority and platform distinction

The creator's ad-lib/read-aloud material is the authority wherever it conflicts with generic script advice. This audit therefore does **not** replace the protected lines at 32, 40, 58, 62, 64, 82, 84, 86, 92, 102, 114, 116, 118, 134, 146, 148, 158, 162, 164 or 166. Where a protected line creates a structural concern, the fix is an insertion, a visual cue or delivery choreography. The proposed rewrites use the creator's dominant patterns: flowing causal sentences, plain definitions, `so`/`because` connectors, and short verdicts only after explanation.

There is no material YouTube-policy blocker in the written script. It is educational, document-led and non-graphic; Nazi racial language is attributed and rejected. Graphic massacre imagery would be a separate visual-policy and monetization risk, so the edit should stay with maps, documents, names, memorials and non-gory site photography. The wording risks identified above are chiefly comprehension, fairness and retention risks—not prohibited-content risks.
