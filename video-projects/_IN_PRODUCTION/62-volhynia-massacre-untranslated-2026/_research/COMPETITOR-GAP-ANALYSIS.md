## 8. AGENT ASSESSMENT

### What ALL competitors get wrong or oversimplify

**The binary trap.** Every video falls into one of two camps: "UPA = Nazi/genocidal" (A1, A3/A4, B1/B2 with Polish bias) or "it was complicated" (A5, partial B1/B2). None of them do what the evidence actually allows: show that **the units committed ethnic cleansing that is well-documented**, AND that **Russia's use of that fact to justify the 2022 invasion is a logical non-sequitur the documents themselves do not support**. The first claim and the second claim are NOT the same thing, and no competitor separates them.

**The Klyachkivsky omission.** The person who most likely issued the operational order is absent from every competitor. Competitors discuss "Bandera" and "the UPA" as if they are one continuous actor. In fact, Bandera was in Sachsenhausen during the 1943 massacres; Klyachkivsky was the field commander. This imprecision is not a small error — it is the error that allows both the Ukrainian denial ("Bandera didn't personally order it") and the Russian over-claim ("Bandera ordered genocide") to persist unchallenged. Naming Klyachkivsky and interrogating the order is what the referee video does.

**The document problem.** Every competitor uses narration to describe documents that actually exist. The RT article references the June 30, 1941 Act — but RT is doing so to prove a propaganda point, not to submit it to forensic examination. The competitor gap is not just "show the document" — it is "show the document and then ask what it actually establishes and what it does not." That is the distinctive move.

### The #1 question viewers have that nobody answers

> "Why is Poland — a NATO ally actively supporting Ukraine against Russia — the country enraged right now? If this is Russian propaganda, why is Poland agreeing with it?"

The 2026 news clips (France 24) describe the diplomatic incident but do not answer this. The WWII history videos predate the incident. No competitor walks through the logical chain: Poland's anger is real and evidence-based (the Volhynia record is documented); it is NOT the same as Russia's "Nazi Ukraine" claim (which falsely extends 1943 to 2022); and the failure to distinguish these two things is why the controversy has no resolution. Our referee video answers this question directly.

### Recommended angle for History vs Hype

**The Klyachkivsky order as the spine.** Open with the 2026 news hook (Zelensky decree + medal-stripping — no competitor has this AND historical depth). Pivot immediately to the document that nobody shows: what is the Klyachkivsky June 1943 order, was it written or oral, what does it actually say, and what does that establish about command responsibility? Then: what does the June 30, 1941 Act establish about collaboration-intent — and what happened ten weeks later? Then: what does Russia add that the documents do not support? Close: what the 2026 diplomatic crisis is actually about (the real history vs. the weaponized history).

The primary source strategy: the June 30, 1941 Act is the easiest document to obtain and display (it's a political proclamation, in Ukrainian/German, with the German text creator-verifiable). The Klyachkivsky order is harder (oral vs. written dispute) but that *difficulty* is the story — the video is about why this order is so fought over, not about displaying a clean signed exhibit. Both are viable on-screen exhibits in different ways.

**The specific moat:** No English-language YouTube video has displayed the June 30, 1941 Act on screen, walked through the Sachsenhausen episode as the "both/and" twist, named Klyachkivsky as the operational actor, interrogated the oral-vs-written order question, identified where Russian propaganda diverges from the actual documents, AND connected all of this to the live 2026 diplomatic crisis. That combination — in one 8-10 minute referee video — is currently unclaimed territory.
