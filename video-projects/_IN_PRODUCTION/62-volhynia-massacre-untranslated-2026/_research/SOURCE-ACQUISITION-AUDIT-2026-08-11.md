## Findings

### 1. Grzegorz Motyka and Jurij Szapował (scientific eds.), *Polacy i Ukraińcy pomiędzy dwoma systemami totalitarnymi 1942–1945*, parts 1–2

- **Bibliographic identity:** Warsaw–Kyiv, 2005; vol. 4 of *Polska i Ukraina w latach trzydziestych–czterdziestych XX wieku. Nieznane dokumenty z archiwów służb specjalnych*; ISBN 83-89078-77-5. The official IPN catalogue gives **1,512 printed pages** and **194 Polish, Ukrainian and Russian documents**. Scientific editors: Grzegorz Motyka and Jurij Szapował; the title page records additional institutional editors.
- **Canonical legal access:** [IPN digital-library landing page](https://przystanekhistoria.pl/pa2/biblioteka-cyfrowa/polska-i-ukraina-w-latach-1/85985%2CPolacy-i-Ukraincy-pomiedzy-dwoma-systemami-totalitarnymi-19421945-cz-12.html). Direct official PDFs: [part 1](https://przystanekhistoria.pl/download/166/147694/Polska-i-Ukraina-w-latach-trzydziestychczterdziestych-XX-wieku-t4-cz1.pdf) (6.62 MB; PDF 876 pages) and [part 2](https://przystanekhistoria.pl/download/166/147691/Polska-i-Ukraina-w-latach-trzydziestychczterdziestych-XX-wieku-t4-cz2.pdf) (4.66 MB; PDF 636 pages). Expected filenames are the final URL segments.
- **Exact project use:** part 1, section II “Na Wołyniu,” document 36, printed Polish pp. 443–445 (PDF page-index needs checking after acquisition): excerpt of Yurii Stelmashchuk’s 28 February 1945 interrogation protocol. This is a high-value bilingual publication locator for the already-scripted oral-directive passage.
- **Tier/provenance:** **S→P reproduced / primary-via-testimony, not T1.** This is an edited publication of a Soviet interrogation extract, not the 1945 original; it does not cure coercion, copying, selection, translation, or custody problems. It must never be presented as Klyachkivsky’s own signed order. The existing 1963 protocol-copy image remains the stronger visual exhibit; the edition is best as an independent publication/translation check and full citation.
- **Does it materially change the script?** **No rewrite, but acquire.** It upgrades sourcing discipline and offers additional contemporaneous Polish, Ukrainian and Soviet records if a beat needs replacing. It does not close the signed-order gap and does not alter the script’s required Soviet-interrogation caveat.

### 2. Georgiy Kasianov, *Memory Crash: Politics of History in and around Ukraine, 1980s–2010s*

- **Bibliographic identity:** Central European University Press, 2022; English edition, xiii + 407 printed pages (digital platforms list 424 PDF pages including front/back matter); print ISBN 978-963-386-380-0; PDF eISBN 978-963-386-381-7.
- **Canonical legal access:** [CEU Press / BibliOpen record](https://www.bibliopen.org/p/bopen/9789633866856), which provides “Download PDF” and “Download ePub.” The record identifies the license as **CC BY-NC-ND**. Expected local filename is not exposed by the landing page; retain the publisher metadata rather than invent one.
- **Evidence role:** modern historiography of how state institutions, political groups and historians use the past in national identity, political competition, war and international relations, including Ukraine’s relationships with Poland and Russia.
- **Tier/provenance:** **T2 scholarly interpretation, not evidence of what occurred in Volhynia in 1943.** The book’s endpoint is 2020, so it cannot support the 2025–26 decree, medal or exhumation facts; those continue to require official contemporary sources. It should also not be used to infer present motives for any individual leader.
- **Does it materially change the script?** **Potentially, but only in the final interpretation.** It can make the closing mechanism (“how nations manufacture heroes by choosing what to remember”) more precise and less accusatory. It should replace no primary card and must not expand the runtime.

### 3. Andrii Portnov, *Poland and Ukraine: Entangled Histories, Asymmetric Memories*

- **Bibliographic identity:** Essay No. 7, Forum Transregionale Studien / PRISMA Ukraïna, 2020. This is the English essay, not the distinct 2023 Polish-language book *Polska i Ukraina: wspólna historia, asymetryczna pamięć*.
- **Canonical legal access:** [PRISMA Ukraïna publication page](https://www.prisma-ukraina.de/publikationen/detail/poland-and-ukraine-entangled-histories-asymmetric-memories-1), which links to the [institutional PDF/read-online record](https://perspectivia.net/receive/pnet_mods_00003867). The PDF’s visible pagination runs to **99 pages**. Filename as exposed by the institutional PDF route: `PORTNOV_Essay_7_2020.pdf`.
- **Evidence role:** concise comparative frame for Polish and Ukrainian historical narratives, explicitly including the 1943 ethnic cleansing of Volhynian Poles, post-Soviet memory conflict and reconciliation.
- **Tier/provenance:** **T2 scholarly interpretation/essay.** It is a persuasive analytic synthesis, not a documentary edition and not a casualty or command-chain authority. “Asymmetry” describes relative salience in the public debates it studies; do not turn it into a claim that Ukrainians as a people do not care about Polish victims.
- **Does it materially change the script?** **No.** It is a useful cross-check for one or two lines in the Act 4–5 transition, especially to prevent the ending becoming a simplistic “Ukraine hides it / Poland remembers it” binary. Kasianov is the higher-priority modern-memory acquisition.

### 4. Romuald Niedzielko, *The Book of the Righteous of the Eastern Borderlands 1939–1945*

- **Bibliographic identity:** English edition of *Kresowa księga sprawiedliwych 1939–1945*, published by the Polish Institute of National Remembrance (IPN). The official page presents Polish, Ukrainian and English editions.
- **Canonical legal access:** [IPN English download landing page](https://przystanekhistoria.pl/pa2/tematy/zbrodnia-wolynska/36892%2CKresowa-ksiega-sprawiedliwych-19391945.html) and [direct English PDF](https://przystanekhistoria.pl/download/166/72550/KresowaksiegasprawiedliwychANG.pdf). Expected filename: `KresowaksiegasprawiedliwychANG.pdf`; 3.75 MB; **221 PDF pages**. EPUB and MOBI are also officially offered.
- **Evidence role:** a named registry of people who warned, hid or otherwise aided Poles under threat; the IPN description says its entries concern over 1,300 Ukrainians (often anonymous), with actions saving more than 2,500 people. It can supply one particular, properly sourced rescue case.
- **Tier/provenance:** **T2 curated memorial/testimonial compilation.** It is very useful against collective-blame framing, but its editorial premise uses IPN’s legal/moral language and its individual stories should be treated as documented testimony, not a representative statistical sample. Do not use it to quantify the total scale of rescue or violence beyond what the edition explicitly documents.
- **Does it materially change the script?** **Only if we choose a named case.** The current rescuer beat already has Szabłowski. This source gives a stronger English, official documentary footing but is not a reason to add a second rescuer sequence. One 10–15 second name-and-action beat is the ceiling.

### 5. Oxana Shevel, “Decommunization in Post-Euromaidan Ukraine: Law and Practice”

- **Bibliographic identity:** PONARS Eurasia Policy Memo **No. 411**, January 2016; author affiliation listed as Tufts University. It is a policy memo, not a peer-reviewed monograph.
- **Canonical legal access:** [PONARS memo landing page](https://www.ponarseurasia.org/decommunization-in-post-euromaidan-ukraine-law-and-practice/) and [publisher-hosted PDF](https://ponarseurasia.org/wp-content/uploads/attachments/Pepm411_Shevel_Jan2016.pdf). Expected filename: `Pepm411_Shevel_Jan2016.pdf`; **5 PDF pages**.
- **Evidence role:** contemporaneous scholarly context for the four May 2015 laws, including Law No. 2538-1, their critics’ concerns, and early implementation. It usefully distinguishes the legal package from the popular shorthand “decommunization.”
- **Tier/provenance:** **T2 policy analysis.** The Rada statute is still the sole T1 source for legal wording, scope and penalties. The memo is early (January 2016), so it cannot establish downstream court practice, later policy, or the 2026 hero decree. It must not be used to say the law made historical criticism criminal without a precise statutory analysis.
- **Does it materially change the script?** **No rewrite.** It is a clean corroborating source for the already corrected 2015-law explanation, and a guard against overstating the law. It is optional if the official statute plus the script’s present wording are retained.
