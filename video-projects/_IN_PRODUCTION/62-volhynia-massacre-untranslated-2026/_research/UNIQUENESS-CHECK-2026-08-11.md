# Uniqueness Check — Comments × Competitor Videos

**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**Verdict:** **Differentiated, with one condition.** The topic itself is not unique. The video becomes unique only if it answers the audience's high-demand question—*why Ukraine still honors the UPA*—through a visible evidence ladder and a causal account of modern memory politics. If it becomes mainly a Bandera biography, atrocity overview, or genocide-label debate, it collapses into existing coverage.

## The part nobody in the inspected set combines

1. The 2026 decree and Polish medal rupture as the opening problem.
2. A chronological explanation of how an independence movement acquired a removal doctrine, command structure, trained manpower, and operational capacity.
3. Poryck as one restrained human case, widening to the coordinated campaign and named-victim record.
4. An evidence ladder that distinguishes doctrine, capacity, coordination, command testimony, provenance limitations, and the missing signed extermination order.
5. A real surviving Order No. 11 shown precisely because it **does not** contain the extermination language viewers may expect.
6. Stelmashchuk's statement shown with its typed-copy and Soviet-custody limitations rather than hidden or treated as a clean confession.
7. One causal modern-memory chain: 1991 regional rehabilitation → post-2014 resistance need → decommunization → 2015 state recognition → 2026 military honor.
8. Concrete tests of selective memory: official framing, the museum omission, and the exhumation dispute.
9. A short boundary between the real historical crime and Russia's unsupported conclusion about modern Ukraine.

That combination is the moat. “Two memories of the same army” by itself is already standard. “Bandera was complicated” is already standard. “Volhynia was genocide” is already owned by larger Polish and sensationalist treatments.

## Failure conditions

The rebuild stops being unique if it:

- gives Bandera and Russia separate large chapters;
- treats Bandera's imprisonment as the video's central correction;
- settles into a scholar-by-scholar genocide-label debate;
- lists sources without letting the viewer see what each source proves;
- front-loads so many names and institutions that Poryck arrives after momentum is lost;
- speculates that the missing order was destroyed;
- says only that Ukraine remembers anti-Soviet resistance without explaining how that memory became state policy;
- uses “both sides” language without sequence, objective, geography, and scale;
- ends as a generic moral appeal instead of answering the 2026 honor question.

## Recommended unique promise

**Viewer-facing question:** Why does Ukraine honor the UPA when the same organization carried out the Volhynia campaign?

**History vs Hype method:** Show the evidence in ascending order and tell the viewer exactly what each rung proves, supports, and cannot prove.

**Payoff:** Ukraine is not honoring the massacre; it is honoring a usable anti-Soviet independence tradition. But state memory separates that tradition from crimes committed by the same organization, and the documents do not allow that separation to erase organizational responsibility.

## Rebuild guardrails

- Keep the evidence ladder from the proposed outline.
- Keep the modern-memory progression as the answer, not an epilogue.
- Keep one developed victim/place and one brief Ukrainian-rescuer example.
- Fold Russia into Bandera's introduction and the closing callback; no standalone Russia segment.
- Contextualize spoken scholars by role or put them on screen.
- Preserve the difficult-heroes question as the final spoken thought.
- Decide the CTA with the creator; do not remove it automatically.

## Bottom line

**Yes, this can be a genuinely distinctive video.** Its uniqueness is not “we found a fact nobody knows.” It is the viewing experience: the audience begins with an emotionally polarized modern dispute, follows a chronological human story, sees the actual evidentiary chain and its limits, and receives a mechanism-level answer for why the honor exists today. No inspected close competitor combines those four functions.
