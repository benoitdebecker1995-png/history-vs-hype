## THE CHAIN (12 belief-beats mapped to the 5 acts)

### B1 — Cold open (ACT 1, 0:00) — the recognition hook
- **Believes:** "Nazi Ukraine" is pure Russian propaganda — *or* the inverse, honoring the UPA proves Ukraine is Nazi.
- **What breaks it:** Ukraine names an army unit "Heroes of the UPA"; then **Poland — an ally, not Russia — strips Zelensky's medal, and Israel condemns too.** (Decree No. 440/2026; medal saga.)
- **Now believes:** This isn't Russia being Russia. A close ally and Israel are angry — there's something real here.
- **Question it opens:** *Why is Poland, in the middle of a war it's fighting on Ukraine's side, this angry?*

### B2 — The two stories (ACT 1, ~0:45) — frame-set (softest beat; see flags)
- **Believes:** One side must simply be right.
- **What breaks it:** Two coherent, steelmanned stories — 🇺🇦 fought Hitler AND Stalin, Nazis jailed Bandera, resistance symbol · 🇵🇱 ~100K Poles killed 1943–45, coordinated, graves still closed.
- **Now believes:** Both are told as settled history, and they can't both be whole.
- **Question it opens:** *What happens when you test each one against the actual documents?*

### B3 — The 1941 Act (ACT 2, ~2:15) — **THE TURN** (belief-flip #1, at ~18% runtime ✅)
- **Believes:** The UPA were anti-Nazi resistance (Ukraine's clean story).
- **What breaks it:** The 30 June 1941 Act — Stetsko pledges to *"collaborate closely with National Socialist Greater Germany… under the leadership of Adolf Hitler."*
- **Now believes:** They opened by offering to work *with* the Nazis; the anti-Nazi fight was the smaller half.
- **Question it opens:** *So were they just Nazi collaborators after all — is Poland's/Russia's story the true one?*

### B4 — The Sachsenhausen twist + 1944 re-collaboration (ACT 2, ~2:45)
- **Believes:** So they were Nazi collaborators, plain and simple.
- **What breaks it:** Within a week the Germans jail Bandera; Sept 1941 formal arrest; Sachsenhausen special wing; brothers die in Auschwitz. Then 1944 — some units take German weapons *again* and hide it.
- **Now believes:** Collaboration AND victimhood AND resistance — all true at once; and the burying started with the men themselves.
- **Question it opens:** *Then what actually happened in Volhynia — and who is responsible for it?*

### B5 — The forensic why (ACT 3, ~3:30) — belief-flip #2
- **Believes:** If it happened, it was spontaneous ethnic hatred / the chaos of war.
- **What breaks it:** A 20-year doctrine (Dontsov's amorality → Kolodzinskyi's "cleanse the western lands") + the 1943 trigger (~5,000 auxiliary police desert with Holocaust "experience in the mass killing of civilians") + **Lebed, 9 Apr 1943: "cleanse the entire revolutionary territory of the Polish population."**
- **Now believes:** It was doctrine + trained manpower + a directive — not spontaneous.
- **Question it opens:** *But was there an actual order — can you prove it came from the top?*

### B6 — The scale (ACT 3, ~5:00) — the atrocity lands at full weight
- **Believes:** "Tens of thousands" is a vague, inflated, round number.
- **What breaks it:** 11 July 1943 — ~100 villages hit in one morning, ~4,000 dead; the **Siemaszko registry names 36,000+** of an estimated 50–60K dead, one by one.
- **Now believes:** It's precisely, individually documented — real and organized.
- **Question it opens:** *So who signed the order?*

### B7 — The crux (ACT 3, ~6:00) — belief-flip #3, the double-rebuttal (load-bearing)
- **Believes:** There must be a signed extermination order (Russia) — *or* there's no order, so it proves nothing (Ukraine).
- **What breaks it:** No signed top order survives (only Stelmashchuk's duress NKVD testimony) — **but** the signed Order No. 11 shows Klyachkivsky signed logistics, tribunals, land reform, never the kill-order; Motyka (Polish genocide-scholar) concedes no signed order yet confirms organized character indirectly; Klymchak's after-action report: *"I liquidated all Poles from young to old."*
- **Now believes:** The kill-order was kept off paper **on purpose** while everything around it was signed and filed — both extremes break on the same evidence.
- **Question it opens:** *If it's all this damning, why is Ukraine building monuments to these men now?*

### B8 — Acknowledgment → reversal (ACT 4, ~7:30) — belief-flip #4 (thesis in miniature)
- **Believes:** Ukraine must be ignorant of this / has never faced it.
- **What breaks it:** Ukraine DID reckon — 1997 reconciliation declaration, 2023 Zelensky mourned at Lutsk — then un-reckoned: never called it genocide, recast 1943 as a two-sided war, wrote these men into law as heroes in 2015.
- **Now believes:** The same state mourned the victims, then honored their killers — a *choice*, not ignorance.
- **Question it opens:** *How does a state make that reversal stick?*

### B9 — The machinery + memory war (ACT 4, ~8:30)
- **Believes:** They're honored *for* the killing (Russia's read) — or it's hidden by accident.
- **What breaks it:** Honored for anti-Moscow resistance, not Volhynia; the 2015 decommunization law codifies OUN/UPA and criminalizes "disrespect"; historians who document the crimes get branded traitors ("Goebbels' grandchild"); Russia weaponizes the *same* facts into "all Ukraine is Nazi" — yet with every Soviet archive it controls, has never produced a signed kill-order.
- **Now believes:** It's a mechanism — remember the resistance, bury the massacre, punish the reminders — and Russia's overclaim is the mirror-image abuse.
- **Question it opens:** *So where does the truth actually sit between these two weaponizations?*

### B10 — The consensus + the cycle (ACT 5, ~10:15)
- **Believes:** One side is the victim, one side the villain.
- **What breaks it:** The shared scholarly floor (real + organized, 50–60K; same men fought the Soviets; Bandera collaborator AND prisoner; "Nazi Ukraine" unsupported) + the cycle (Polish reprisals 1944; Operation Vistula 1947, ~140K Ukrainians deported; ~70K/~20K asymmetric, and it *reverses* within present-day Poland) + Poland's own 2016 Sejm resolution admitting reprisals; even the *label* (genocide vs ethnic cleansing) is genuinely contested.
- **Now believes:** Asymmetric but not one-sided; the honest picture resists both national scripts.
- **Question it opens:** *If even this is contested, what's the real lesson?*

### B11 — The mechanism close (ACT 5, ~11:15) — the thesis landed as a claim
- **Believes:** This is a Ukraine problem / a foreign quarrel that doesn't touch me.
- **What breaks it:** Every nation builds its heroes the same way — remembers the flattering half, buries the rest; the same Ukraine mourned then honored.
- **Now believes:** It's a universal mechanism of national memory, not a Ukrainian defect.
- **Question it opens:** *What does MY nation do this with?*

### B12 — The mirror turn + CTA (ACT 5, ~11:40) — the intended landing (terminal beat)
- **Believes:** This is about them, over there.
- **What breaks it:** The heroes on your own money have a chapter the textbooks skip — a war, a famine, a people cleared off their land.
- **Now believes:** I'm implicated; the honest move is to look at my *own* country's story.
- **Question it opens (terminal, viewer-directed):** *Can I actually look at it?* → CTA: go to the documents / subscribe.

## STRUCTURAL FLAGS (watch-points for the T1 read-aloud — none are breaks)

1. **B2 is the softest beat (pure frame-set, no belief-flip).** It survives the filter (it opens the document test),
   but it's the only beat that installs tension rather than flipping a belief. If ACT 1 drags on the read, **B2 is the
   compression candidate** — tighten the steelman, don't cut it (procedural fairness is load-bearing for the brigade-guard).

2. **ACT 3 is a third of the runtime (3:30–7:30) and carries the heaviest document load** (B5+B6+B7). This is the
   "document-sag, min 7–9" cliff. **Mitigant already built in:** three belief-flips inside ACT 3 (spontaneous→doctrine,
   vague→named, must-be-signed→kept-off-paper), each ~80–90s apart, plus decoder phrases /90s. Watch pacing here on the
   read; this is where trim room lives (the DURATION note agrees).

3. **B5→B6 seam runs scale *before* the order-crux.** Intentional (the Gemini/sequencing guard: the atrocity must land
   at full weight in ACT 3 before the crux and long before the ACT-5 mechanism read). Keep it — but on the read, make
   sure B6's "so who signed the order?" tail is audible, or B7 can feel like it re-opens a question B5 already asked.

4. **Double landing at the close (B11 mechanism → B12 mirror).** B11 already "completes" the argument as a claim; B12
   adds the personal implication. **Risk:** the video can feel like it ended at B11 and restarted at B12 (the "land the
   meaning once" tic). The script sequences comprehension→mirror deliberately — on the read, confirm B12 feels like the
   *point of impact*, not an epilogue. If it sags, the fix is one clause tying B12's "your own money" straight off
   B11's "who gets to decide," not a fresh paragraph.

5. **Sequencing hard-guards (already enforced, verify on read):** the acknowledgment→reversal beat (B8) stays ACT 4 —
   never ACT 1 (pre-atrocity, it reads as "but they mourned them" minimization); the atrocity gravity (B6) precedes the
   mechanism (B11). The belief-chain respects both.

**Bottom line:** structure is sound and retention-optimized — the Turn lands in the 15–25% window (B3 at ~18%), the
crux is a genuine both-extremes-break beat, and the chain never loses the viewer's next question. The only real risks
are pacing (ACT 3 length) and the close's double-landing — both are read-aloud watch-points, not rewrites.
