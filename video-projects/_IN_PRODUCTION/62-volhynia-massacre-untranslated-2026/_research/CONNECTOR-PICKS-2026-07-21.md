# CONNECTOR PICKS — the 18 em-dashes that are doing a word's job

**Why this exists.** Your unscripted gold runs `so` at 11.8 per 1,000 words and `but` at 9.2 — **21.0 combined.** The script runs **7.7**, and the em-dash runs at 21.8. The dash has taken over your causal spine. This is measured, and it's a scripting-layer drift that also shows in #57, which you filmed and published (`CALIBRATION-CORPUS.md` 62-14).

**What's NOT here.** The canonical em-dash stays untouched — the within-sentence pivot into a verdict, and the appositive ("the UPA — the Ukrainian Insurgent Army"). Of 61 dashes in the script, 43 are those and are fine. The 18 below are the ones sitting between **two complete clauses**, where your mouth would put a word.

**How to use this.** Pick A, B or C, or write your own in the blank. "Keep the dash" is a legitimate answer — say so and it stays. Lines marked 🔒 are your own harvested words; I've listed them so you can see the tension, but the dash there may well be yours.

## Two I'd flag before you pick

**#9 (CH6, "It was a Sunday ▸ the villagers were at mass")** — this is the video's most restrained atrocity beat and the dash is arguably doing real work: the pause *is* the horror. `so` makes it causal and slightly brisk. This may be the one place the dash beats the word. Your call, but I'd listen to it twice.

**#4 (CH4)** — your own T4 dictation for this line used a comma, not a dash. So option A isn't a change, it's a restoration.

## What this is worth

If you take the `. And` / `, and` / `, so` / `. But` options across the 18, `so`+`but` rises from **7.7 toward roughly 12–14 per 1,000** and the em-dash rate drops from 21.8 to about **15**. That still sits under your natural 21.0 — the remaining gap is the kind that only closes when the words come out of your mouth on the read, not off a page.

**Word cost: zero.** Every option is a swap, not an addition.
