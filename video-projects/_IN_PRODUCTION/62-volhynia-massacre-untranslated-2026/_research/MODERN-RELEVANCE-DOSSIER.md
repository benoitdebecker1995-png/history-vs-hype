## B. WHY IS HE A HERO TODAY? — the #1 comment demand, answered (the legal mechanism = HOW>WHY)

This is the question every competitor leaves unanswered. The answer is **legal-administrative**, which is exactly the channel's subscriber trigger (systems, not slogans):

1. **22 Jan 2010** — President **Yushchenko** awards Bandera the posthumous **"Hero of Ukraine"** title. International + domestic uproar. *(Roman Shukhevych got it in 2007.)*
2. **2 Apr 2010 → 12 Jan 2011** — Ukrainian courts (Donetsk, then the Higher Administrative Court) **annul** it — on a technicality: **Bandera was never a Ukrainian citizen** (died 1959, before the 1991 independence). So the *first* rehabilitation failed on administrative grounds, not moral ones.
3. **9 Apr 2015** — post-Maidan **Verkhovna Rada passes the decommunization laws**; enacted by Poroshenko **15 May 2015**. The key instrument: **Law #2538-1, "On the legal status and honoring of fighters for Ukraine's independence in the 20th century"** — legally recognizes the **OUN and UPA as independence fighters**, and the package makes publicly showing **"disrespect"** for them an offense.
4. **Architect: Volodymyr Viatrovych**, director of the **Ukrainian Institute of National Remembrance (2014–2019)** — frames it as Ukraine's *"hundred-year war for independence."* Co-sponsor: **Yuri Shukhevych** (son of UPA commander Roman Shukhevych).

→ **That's the mechanism:** in 2015 the state *legally codified* OUN/UPA as heroes. Everything downstream — street renamings, the 2026 unit name, the Melnyk reburial, the sanction on "disrespect" — flows from that statute. *(Viatrovych is also one of our notebook authors — the Ukrainian-camp steelman speaks in his own voice on this.)*
