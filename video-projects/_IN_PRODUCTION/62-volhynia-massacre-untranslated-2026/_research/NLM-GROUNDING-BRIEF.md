### EXHIBIT 5 — Genocide vs. Ethnic Cleansing Scholarly Split

**VERDICT: ✅ VERIFIED — clear scholarly positions, all groundable**

#### McBride position
**Explicit "ethnic cleansing," not genocide.** Two stated reasons:
1. "That is the definition that its organizers used" (OUN-UPA leadership used the term *chystka*/cleansing)
2. "Scholarly consensus that this was a case of ethnic cleansing as opposed to genocide"

McBride's key definitional distinction: OUN-UPA did not intend "to kill every Pole as a goal in and of itself — they only wanted to cleanse the territory of them." Goal was territorial removal, not biological extermination.

**Quotable passage (McBride pp. 631–632):**
> "Importantly, I refer to this case specifically as one of 'ethnic cleansing' first, because that is the definition that its organizers used, and second, because of scholarly consensus that this was a case of ethnic cleansing as opposed to genocide (notably, scholarly consensus of what constitutes genocide is much less clear)."

*Source: McBride, Jared. "Peasants into Perpetrators." Slavic Review 75, no. 3 (Fall 2016): 631–632.*

Also groundable from McBride — OUN-UPA leader Lebed's own stated intention:
> "cleanse the entire revolutionary territory of the Polish population."
*McBride p. 631, fn. 6 (citing Petro Balei, Fronda Stepana Bandery, 141).*

#### Snyder position
**"Ethnic cleansing" — consistent across publications.**

Snyder's definition (the most-cited in the corpus): "violent policies aiming to clear territories of national enemies, though not to kill every man, woman and child."
*Source: Snyder, Timothy. "The Causes of Ukrainian-Polish Ethnic Cleansing 1943." Past & Present 179, no. 1 (May 2003): 197. [Cited in McBride p. 632, fn. 5.]*

Snyder's chapter title in *Reconstruction of Nations*: "**The Ethnic Cleansing of Western Ukraine (1939–1945)**."
*Source: Snyder, Timothy. The Reconstruction of Nations. Yale University Press, 2003. Chapter 8, p. 153 heading.*

#### Motyka position (important nuance)
Motyka's Polish-language work is titled **"Ludobójcza Czystka"** ("Genocidal Cleansing") — a hybrid framing suggesting the *methods* were genocidal in character even if the strategic *goal* was territorial cleansing. This is the scholarly formulation closest to the Polish Parliament's genocide designation. FLAG: this is from Motyka's Polish text, cited by Rossoliński-Liebe. The exact Polish title needs your Polish-speaker check before on-screen use.
*Source: Cited in Rossoliński-Liebe, Stepan Bandera, and in Himka, Ukrainian Nationalists and the Holocaust.*

#### Himka position
Uses "massive ethnic cleansing project" and "strategic cleansing."
*Source: Himka, Ukrainian Nationalists and the Holocaust, endnote 1537 area.*

#### Rossoliński-Liebe position
Explicitly resists the genocide label for analytical purposes. Key passage:
> "'Genocide' is a contested term and a concept that makes more sense in legal and political discourse than in historical studies. The use of the term may interfere with academic analysis, by obscuring links between different forms of mass violence conducted by the same group of perpetrators against various ethnic and political enemies."

Prefers "mass violence," "ethnic cleansing," or "crimes against humanity" depending on context. Notes the term "genocide" (*ludobójstwo*) has been instrumentalized by Polish kresowiacy (resettler) community groups "competing in suffering" with the Holocaust and Soviet crimes.
*Source: Rossoliński-Liebe, Stepan Bandera. pp. 32, 442, 545.*

**REFEREE FRAMING THAT CARRIES THE VERDICT:**
Four English-language scholars (McBride, Snyder, Himka, Rossoliński-Liebe) use "ethnic cleansing." One Polish scholar (Motyka) uses "genocidal cleansing" as a hybrid. The Polish Parliament's official term "genocide" is not supported by Anglophone scholarly consensus but reflects Motyka's Polish formulation + political factors. This is exactly the both/and the referee format handles — all positions are grounded, none requires the channel to adjudicate.

### EXHIBIT 6 — 1941 Lviv Pogrom / OUN Participation (Himka + Rossoliński-Liebe)

**VERDICT: ✅ VERIFIED — rich detail, multiple groundable passages**

**SCOPE NOTE:** This is designated a context exhibit, not spine. The findings below are grounded but should not overwhelm the Volhynia arc.

#### Mechanism — three-phase structure (Himka p. 17)

Himka distinguishes three distinct phases of OUN involvement in Jewish killing:

**Phase 1 (Summer 1941 — Militias):**
- OUN-established Ukrainian National Militia was the primary organizational force for roundups
- Militia "assembled the Jews for the Germans' violence, since they could identify Jews more easily than the invaders and knew the localities" (Himka p. 14)
- "In Lviv, it was the Ukrainian National Militia that rounded up the Jews and participated in the violence against them" (Himka p. 228)
- Scale: "accomplices in the murder of thousands of Jews and shooters in the murder of many hundreds" (Himka p. 17)
- Nachtigall battalion (linked to OUN-B, led by Roman Shukhevych, future UPA commander) also present at all three prisons as one of several perpetrator groups

**Phase 2 (1942–43 — Auxiliary Police):**
- OUN infiltrated Ukrainian Auxiliary Police; police provided "indispensable manpower for the Holocaust"
- Death toll from police roundups: "hundreds of thousands" (Himka p. 17)

**Phase 3 (1943–44 — UPA):**
- UPA killed Jews on its own initiative as part of ethnic cleansing project
- "In the winter of 1943-44, as the Red Army moved westward, UPA lured surviving Jews out of their hiding places in the forests, temporarily placed them in labor camps, and then murdered them"
- Jewish victims in this phase: "probably in the thousands" (Himka p. 17)
