## What “AI slop” means in this draft

It is not “text touched by AI.” YouTube explicitly allows AI-assisted script editing when the result delivers a unique, well-researched narrative and the creator’s authentic perspective; it objects to generic, repetitive, templated work and content without a cohesive logical progression ([YouTube monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-EN)).

Here, AI slop has observable local signatures:

1. **Connector stuffing:** transitions are made to sound conversational by inserting “so,” “but,” and “and,” rather than by making the causal relation clear.
2. **Uniform rhetorical cadence:** too many paragraphs use the same setup–pivot–verdict pattern.
3. **Over-explanation:** a conclusion is stated, paraphrased, then restated as a takeaway.
4. **Abstract glue:** phrases explain that the argument is moving instead of moving it through a fact, contradiction, or question.
5. **Evidence flattening:** a contemporary report, retrospective testimony, confession, and unsigned paper are narrated as equivalent “documents” rather than evidence with different provenance and weight.
6. **Show-announcing:** the VO says a document is being shown rather than saying what matters while the image proves presence.
7. **Voice replacement:** polished formal phrasing displaces sentences Benoit actually produced or approved.

These are project-specific findings from T1–T7, the voice corpus, and the v7.1/v7.2 comparison—not a claim that any individual word is inherently artificial.

## Creator-specific voice rules

1. **Use spoken decisions as the authority.** Preserve every explicitly chosen or approved T1–T7 line unless a factual correction requires change.
2. **Calm prosecutor, not omniscient narrator.** Present the strongest alternative, test it against the record, then give the narrowest verdict the evidence supports.
3. **Say each argument once.** Repetition is useful only when its function changes: promise → evidence → verdict. Three phrasings of the same verdict are not emphasis.
4. **Name the evidentiary weight aloud.** Explain what a source is, when it was created, what it can establish, and where its limit lies.
5. **Point; do not announce.** The VO interprets the highlighted phrase. Production shows the page, mark, signature, strike pattern, or absence.
6. **Plain language without forced chumminess.** Prefer concrete verbs, contractions, and one claim per breath. Do not add fillers to hit a conversational metric.
7. **Preserve meaningful qualification.** “We cannot prove a top-level order” is not weak writing; it is the ethical and evidentiary hinge.
8. **Let sentence length vary by function.** Short for verdicts; longer only where a causal chain or qualification must stay intact.
9. **Use discourse markers functionally.** Research on oral narrative finds that pragmatic markers organize particular coherence relations in particular narrative positions; it does not support maximizing their frequency ([González, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461445605048767)).

## Open-question-ledger method

Treat each section as a transaction with the viewer, not as a topic container.

| At every beat | Required test |
|---|---|
| **Question opened** | What specific thing does the viewer now need to know? |
| **Evidence advanced** | What new document, event, or contradiction narrows the possibilities? |
| **Answer delivered** | What can now be concluded—and no more? |
| **Next question earned** | What unresolved consequence follows naturally from that answer? |

Use three states in the margin: **O** (open), **P** (progressed), **A** (answered). A new major question should not displace the main open question unless the script makes the relationship explicit.

Reject five failure modes established in T1–T2:

- **V1 term-before-use:** a concept arrives before the viewer knows why it matters.
- **V2 answer-unasked:** the script supplies information no active question requires.
- **V3 open-unanswered:** a promised question disappears.
- **V4 re-answer-closed:** the script spends time proving a settled point again.
- **V5 break-promise:** the opening promises an evidentiary test the body does not deliver.

Why this is a reasonable method: listeners integrate each spoken word with the wider discourse extremely quickly; discourse-inconsistent words produced an N400 response beginning roughly 150–200 ms after word onset in two listening experiments ([van Berkum et al., 2003](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item59347/when-and-how-do-listeners-relate-sentence-wider-discourse-evidence-n400)). A preregistered experiment with 554 participants also found narrative messages easier to process than non-narrative messages, with processing fluency mediating persuasive outcomes ([Bullock, Shulman & Huskey, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2021.719615)). **Inference:** the best retention edit is therefore not more rhetorical energy; it is reducing the work required to understand why this fact follows that one.

Questions must also be concrete enough for the viewer to represent the missing answer. Experimental work finds that information gaps and uncertainty predict curiosity, while curiosity changes as relevant information unfolds ([Singh & Manjaly, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/20445911.2021.1908311); [Hsiung et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2301974120)). **Inference:** keep one dominant unresolved question alive through several pieces of evidence; do not manufacture a new “hook” every paragraph.

## No-new-read-aloud editing protocol

1. **Freeze the base:** current `SCRIPT.md`, not v7.2.
2. **Create a protected-line map:** all T1–T7 wording explicitly approved by Benoit is immutable unless factually wrong.
3. **Build the O/P/A ledger:** one row per paragraph or document beat; flag V1–V5.
4. **Cut before rewriting:** remove re-answers, recap verdicts, formal throat-clearing, and show-announcing.
5. **Repair only broken joints:** use phrases already spoken in T1–T7 or attested in `VOICE-CORPUS-FOR-MODEL-PASSES.md`; do not invent a new polished register.
6. **Run the evidence-weight pass:** for every document, preserve nature, date/author if relevant, intended use/recipient if relevant, probative value, and limit.
7. **Run the breath-page pass:** one claim per teleprompter unit; break long qualifications at logical boundaries without changing meaning.
8. **Run a connector audit:** flag clusters, but never edit toward a quota. A connector stays only if it names the actual relation.
9. **Reconcile timing and production cues:** highlight what the editor must point to; avoid narrating the visual action.
10. **Fact-check the locked result, then export:** factual verification precedes teleprompter lock. No additional full read-through is required by this protocol.
