## THE THREE CAMPS (steelman each — referee discipline)

### Camp 1 — Poland / Polish memory (steelman, with an affirmative defender)
- **Defender:** Polish IPN, President Nawrocki, Filar, (partly) Motyka.
- **Strongest form:** This was a top-down, organized ethnic cleansing — possibly genocide — of tens of thousands of Polish civilians, women and children included; honoring the perpetrating formation in 2026 is morally indefensible regardless of its anti-Soviet record. The Klyachkivsky order + coordinated simultaneous village attacks (11 July 1943) show planning, not spontaneous "war."

### Camp 2 — Ukraine / Ukrainian national memory (steelman)
- **Defender:** Ukrainian Institute of National Memory; majority Ukrainian public opinion post-2022.
- **Strongest form:** The UPA was a genuine anti-Soviet, anti-occupation independence movement; members do not deny killings but reject a *systematic extermination plan*; the Volhynia violence was a brutal *reciprocal* wartime conflict (Polish reprisals too) under triple occupation. Honoring independence fighters ≠ endorsing massacres.

### Camp 3 — Anglophone / international scholarship (the referee's center of gravity)
- **Defenders:** Rossoliński-Liebe, Himka, McBride, Snyder, Motyka (cross-over).
- **Strongest form:** OUN-B was a fascist movement; it collaborated in 1941 *and* was then suppressed by the Nazis; UPA perpetrated **ethnic cleansing** in Volhynia (most scholars resist "genocide" as the legal term, while affirming the atrocity); the leadership-order question is genuinely debated. Russia's "denazification" framing is propaganda that flattens this into a casus belli.

### What Russia adds (the propaganda delta — name it explicitly)
- Extends a real 1943 atrocity + 1941 collaboration into "modern Ukraine is a Nazi state," used to justify the 2022 invasion. The documents do NOT support the leap from "the UPA committed war crimes" to "2026 Ukraine is Nazi."
