## The video

**Viewer question.** Why does Ukraine officially honour the UPA when the same organisation carried out an organised campaign against Polish civilians?

**Answer the video reaches.** Ukraine commemorates the UPA as a genuine independence movement that fought Soviet rule, and its public memory separates that struggle from the organisation's responsibility for Volhynia. Poland's objection is that honouring the organisation makes that separation impossible.

**What makes it different.** Competitors give an atrocity account, a Bandera biography, or the standard two-national-memories explanation. This one starts by testing the easy assumption that Ukraine must be hiding Volhynia — finds that it isn't — and then tests three propositions against documents: the campaign was organised; the exact originating command is less certain than the campaign itself; the anti-Soviet insurgency was real. The viewer sees what the sources establish, what historians infer, and where the paper trail ends.
