## ⭐⭐ ROOT ASK 1 — the cold open's logic is backwards. State the CLAIM before the TEST.

His words: *"No, this is a bit weird. I think we should say why it's important to have this order. First state Poland's claim, then say in order to call it this we need to have this order… the main claim of Poland is that this massacre constituted a genocide. And in order to call it a genocide, according to historical consensus, we need these three things. And that's why I looked into the documents."*

The three-things test currently arrives with no stated reason to care. He wants the chain rebuilt as: **Poland says genocide → here is what that word requires → that is why I went to the documents.** The test stops being a device and becomes the consequence of a definition.

Also his rephrase, mid-read: *"if an order to commit this massacre came down from the top"* (script: "an order to do this").

⚠ **ACCURACY COLLISION — must be resolved before this is written.** The verification pass (B6) specifically removed "genocide means intent from the top" because that is **not** the legal test; the Convention's element is intent to destroy the group as such, and a plan is *evidentiary*, not an ingredient. So "in order to call it a genocide we need these three things" cannot be said as law. The honest form is closer to: *Poland says genocide. That word turns on intent — and the way you'd normally show intent is an order, reports coming back, and coordination in time.* His §CH8 note (below) already points the same way: he wants the genocide definition moved out of CH8 and earlier — which is exactly where this restructure would put it.
