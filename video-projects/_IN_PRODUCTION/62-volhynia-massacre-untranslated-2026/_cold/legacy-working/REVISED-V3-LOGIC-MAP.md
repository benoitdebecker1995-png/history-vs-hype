## Beat 1 — Honour, Polish reaction and textbook paradox

**Viewer knows**  
Zelensky gives a current special-forces unit the UPA honour. Poland withdraws its highest honour from him.

**Missing connection to supply**  
For Poland, the UPA name is inseparable from the killing of Polish civilians in Volhynia. This makes the Polish reaction understandable before the script calls it foreseeable.

**Benoit's question**  
Why does Ukraine consider the UPA honour valuable enough to create this dispute during a war with Russia?

**Surface Ukrainian answer**  
The UPA fought for an independent Ukraine.

**Tentative first thought**  
Perhaps the ugly parts of the movement's history no longer play much role in the heroic story.

**Evidence hook**  
The current textbook explicitly teaches the decision to expel Poles and the July attacks, yet later describes the anti-Soviet struggle as heroic.

**Understanding earned**  
Simple omission is not a sufficient explanation. The script must test the anti-Polish campaign and the anti-Soviet struggle, then ask why one defines the honour.

## Beat 2 — From territorial conflict to armed capacity

**Viewer's question**  
How did a movement seeking Ukrainian independence arrive at mass violence against Polish civilians?

**Causal chain**

- Ukrainian statehood attempts fail after the First World War.
- Volhynia and Eastern Galicia become part of Poland.
- OUN nationalists consider those lands necessary for a future Ukrainian state.
- Polish settlement and presence become connected, in nationalist thinking, to future Polish territorial claims.
- Interwar Polish repression contributes to radicalization.
- Qualification at its trigger: that context does not make mass killing inevitable, and most Ukrainians are not OUN nationalists.
- Pre-war removal ideas exist inside OUN thought.
- Wartime state collapse creates opportunity.
- The 1943 police desertions supply trained armed manpower to the growing UPA.

**Concrete synthesis**  
By 1943, ideas about removing the Polish population already existed within the nationalist movement, and the movement now possessed a substantial armed force. Final narration must stay within the verified evidence and not imply a single automatic causal mechanism.

**Next evidence**  
11 July attack wave, Poryck, and Volhynia casualty scale.

## Beat 3 — The competing explanation is introduced

**Viewer knows**  
UPA units attacked Polish communities across Volhynia and tens of thousands of Polish civilians were killed.

**Live interpretation to state before rebutting it**  
One possible account treats the violence primarily as a wider local Polish-Ukrainian eruption during wartime chaos—neighbours attacking neighbours, with UPA units participating—rather than as an anti-Polish campaign organized through OUN/UPA structures.

**Research boundary**  
Do not say “Jacquerie” unless a verified scholar and exact formulation are added. Current verified project material supports stating the interpretation in ordinary language.

**Question earned**  
Do the movement's own records look like reports from an organization carrying out an anti-Polish campaign, or are they more consistent with UPA units being drawn into a broader local eruption?

**Why the next beat follows**  
Attack patterns establish scale and participation, but internal and operational records are better evidence of how the movement itself understood, implemented and reported the action.

## Beat 4 — Several records, one main exhibit

**Source-convergence setup**  
Several contemporary internal and operational records matter. They include Document 149, the July Volodymyr–Horokhiv district report, and the Huta Stepańska operational report. Only Document 149 carries the narrated scene unless another source is needed for a distinct claim.

**Orientation required before quotation**

- Describe Document 149 as an internal situation/reporting document from late 1943 and specify the period it covers if verified.
- Introduce “Tretii” functionally as the pseudonym signing the report; do not add a personal identity unless independently verified.

**Main exhibit**  
“Акція нищення поляків не принесла очікуваних наслідків” — “The action of destroying the Poles did not bring the expected results.”

**Literal inference**  
The author refers to an anti-Polish “action” and evaluates whether it produced the expected results.

**Wider inference**  
Together with the other internal/operational sources and wider attack pattern, the report makes a purely spontaneous/local account difficult and supports organizational involvement in an anti-Polish campaign.

**Qualification**  
The same report documents weak higher control over local SB structures. Organizational involvement does not mean perfectly uniform execution. Exact initiating authority remains less certain. Do not reopen the full command-order debate.

**Understanding earned**  
Poland's association of the UPA name with the anti-Polish campaign is grounded in the movement's own record, not merely in later national accusation.

## Beat 5 — Test the Ukrainian rationale

**Transition**  
The internal record explains why Poland sees the UPA as it does. Ukraine honours a different part of the history: resistance to Soviet rule in the name of an independent Ukraine. Benoit's next step is to test how substantial that struggle was.

**Evidence**

- Red Army and Soviet rule return before Soviet records are introduced.
- Soviet security records document clashes, mobilization resistance, counterinsurgency, arrests, infiltration and deportations.
- Enemy-source logic: the services trying to destroy the movement independently establish that the armed resistance was real.
- Large formal UPA formations decline by the end of the 1940s.
- Smaller OUN/SB underground groups continue resistance into the 1950s.
- Coercion and killing of suspected Ukrainian collaborators prevent a clean heroic account.

**Understanding earned**  
The anti-Soviet independence struggle is historically real without being morally clean.

## Beat 6 — Return to the textbook

**Sequence**

**Bounded inference**

## Beat 8 — Poland, acknowledgment and fairness

**Polish objection**  
The UPA that fought Soviet rule and the UPA implicated by internal records in the anti-Polish campaign are the same organization.

**Why joint mourning is insufficient for Poland**  
Poland's objection is not only whether Ukraine acknowledges the victims. It is whether the UPA name itself should receive state honour.

**Fairness beat**  
Use the 2024 dispute between a Polish ministry and the IPN over official commemoration of Józef Kuraś “Ogień.” One institution treated crimes against civilians as disqualifying; the IPN argued that critically judged actions should not erase a real contribution to the fight for independence. State explicitly that the cases are not equivalent: the comparison concerns the rule for public honour.
