# Voice and Brain Model patch proposal

## Status

Proposal only. Do not update `OPERATING-MODEL.md`, the creator Voice Profile, the Voice and Brain Model, or the raw corpus until Benoit approves these changes.

## Voice Profile proposals

### V1. Mark preliminary interpretations

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model favors cleaned spoken analytic prose and meaningful qualifications.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Benoit rejects “The basic disagreement is clear” and supplies “seems obvious on the surface.” He also produces a tentative, not fully clean, initial idea that the ugly history may no longer be part of the heroic story.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “When Benoit's interpretation is preliminary, preserve the provisional status explicitly. Prefer ‘on the surface,’ ‘initially I thought,’ or ‘at first it seemed’ over narrator-certainty. Do not polish a tentative thought into a doctrine he never held.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It protects the real investigation from sounding pre-decided and preserves the exact strength of Benoit's belief.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Too many hedges would weaken conclusions that the evidence genuinely supports. Apply only before investigation or where uncertainty is material.

### V2. Repeat argument-bearing nouns

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model prefers ordinary vocabulary and meaningful connectors.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Repeated stops at “the massacre,” “the story,” “their version,” “the honour,” “the action,” “execution,” “present,” and “what is happening.”

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “When a noun carries the causal, evidentiary, or moral distinction, repeat the concrete noun after a paragraph break, topic shift, or competing referent. Referential repetition is preferable to elegant pronoun chains.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It captures a measurable feature of what Benoit can read fluently: he needs the exact object kept active.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Mechanical repetition can sound clumsy. Use it at argumentative hinges, not in adjacent simple sentences with one obvious subject.

### V3. Ban editorial organization from narration

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model removes institutional wording and artificial transitions.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
“Germany belongs in this background” triggers “I would never say something like this.”

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “Do not narrate the writer's outline: ‘X belongs in the background,’ ‘we now turn to,’ or ‘this section matters because.’ State the historical dependency or ask the question that makes X necessary.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It distinguishes a voice problem from generic informality and produces causal transitions rather than stage directions.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Benoit does use direct navigation such as “Now we can go back to the textbook.” Keep navigation when the referent and reason for returning are already secure.

### V4. Prefer fully named contrasts

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model avoids stacked abstractions and decorative symmetry.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Benoit accepts concrete Poland/Ukraine and polling contrasts but rejects “factual erasure,” “what is happening,” and “connect that action to the organization.”

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “When explaining a contrast, name both concrete sides in the sentence. Do not compress a multi-part contrast into an abstract label unless the label has already been defined and remains active.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It prevents sentences that are grammatically smooth but cognitively empty.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Some repeated contrasts may lengthen prose. Use the concrete form at first statement and at final payoff; shorter callbacks can follow when safe.

## Brain / Reasoning Model proposals

### B1. Add a question-before-evidence gate

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model values primary sources and claim → evidence → inference.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Zahrawa, Volodymyr–Horokhiv, Huta Stepańska, and Soviet records all cause stops when introduced before their precise question is established.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add a gate: “Before narrating a source, state the viewer question it answers. Then identify the source producer/function in ordinary language, show or quote the source, make the immediate inference, and add only the qualification needed for that inference. If no viewer question exists, demote the source.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It turns source-first storytelling into question-led source storytelling and prevents bibliography-as-narrative.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Not every obvious exhibit requires a spoken question. The question may be implicit if the immediately preceding sentence makes it unmistakable.

### B2. Add a functional actor-introduction rule

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
No explicit rule requires a one-clause role before a historical name.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Stops at Bandera, Stetsko, Shukhevych, Zahrawa, Tretii, and Security Service.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “Before a named actor or institution carries argumentative weight, give a one-clause functional introduction: who or what it is, where it sits, and why it matters now. If the function cannot be stated briefly, remove the name or defer it.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It preserves factual precision without asking the viewer to hold unexplained labels.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Universally familiar actors or a name used only in a visible citation may not need spoken setup.

### B3. Add an unheard-rebuttal prohibition

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model stress-tests claims and counterarguments.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Stops at “not spontaneous,” “not equal,” “not an outside accusation,” and “not invented later.”

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “Do not rebut an interpretation merely because it exists in the research. The viewer must first hear who advances it and why it changes the video's question. Otherwise remove the rebuttal and state the positive finding.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It prevents the script from answering invisible scholars and preserves focus.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
A widespread audience assumption may be addressed without naming a scholar, but the assumption must still be stated before the rebuttal.

### B4. Make causal dependency the default background test

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
Connectors must carry real logical or causal links.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
The viewer loses the chain at the two wars, German detour, police capacity, July date, and Soviet return.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Sharpen to: “For every background fact, complete: ‘This creates the condition for the next fact because…’ If the sentence cannot be completed, the fact is chronology without mechanism and should be cut, moved, or reframed.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It converts a general connector principle into an executable reasoning check.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Some orientation facts establish place or identity rather than causation. Those must still have a stated orientation job.

### B5. Make “why does this matter?” a hard narration gate

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
Repository sophistication and research should serve the active problem.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
Benoit repeatedly asks why a document, actor, or comparison appears.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “If Benoit is likely to ask ‘why are we talking about this?’, stop polishing. The item lacks a narrative job. It must advance the active causal chain, answer the active evidentiary question, or change the final interpretation.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It identifies structural failure earlier than sentence-level editing.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Human detail and atmosphere can earn runtime without changing an argument, but their emotional or orienting job must be explicit.

### B6. Split functional and technical provenance

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
Exhibits require exact provenance and locators.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
The full archive locator is dismissed as “blah, blah, blah,” while the lack of a functional explanation for Zahrawa produces confusion.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “Narration gives functional provenance: producer, purpose, date/context, and why the source can answer the question. The exhibit gives technical provenance: edition, archive, fond, inventory, file, folio, and translation note. Speak technical locators only when disputed identity is itself part of the story.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It preserves historical safety without making the listener process catalog data.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
In a document-authentication video, technical provenance may itself be the narrative.

### B7. Require evidence for belief updates

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
The model says gut discomfort should trigger inspection and approved language outranks generalized rules.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
One tentative omission hypothesis appears genuinely; the “independence struggle invented to hide Volhynia” hypothesis is explicitly rejected.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “A narrated belief update requires a recorded prior belief or question in current creator evidence. Otherwise state the result without claiming transformation. Preserve tentative live thoughts as tentative.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It prevents artificial documentary arcs while allowing real intellectual movement.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Benoit may approve a new formulation later. The rule should allow explicit creator approval to establish the prior question.

### B8. Distinguish locked macro-spine from revisable subquestions

**CURRENT RULE / SECTION**  
Approved structure is treated as authoritative.

**NEW EVIDENCE FROM READ-THROUGH**  
The macro order survives, while “spontaneous?”, “who organized it?”, and “master order?” are exposed as leftovers.

**PROPOSED CHANGE**  
Add: “A locked thought order fixes the major reasoning sequence, not every inherited viewer-facing subquestion. If a read-through shows that a subquestion answers an older premise, replace it locally while preserving the macro chain.”

**WHY THIS IMPROVES THE MODEL**  
It prevents structure lock from protecting obsolete internal scaffolding.

**RISK OF OVERGENERALIZING**  
Local changes can accumulate into redesign. Require an explicit mapping showing that every macro beat remains in the same order.

## Recommended disposition

Approve V1–V4 and B1–B8 as a single patch set only after Benoit reviews the risks. No raw examples should be deleted. The read-through should be added as a dated high-priority source, with factual-authority warnings preserved.
