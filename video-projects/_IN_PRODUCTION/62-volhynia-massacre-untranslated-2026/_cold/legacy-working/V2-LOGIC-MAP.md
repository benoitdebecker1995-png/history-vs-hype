# V2 logic map

## Governing question

Why does Ukraine consider the UPA worth honouring during a war with Russia despite the predictable Polish reaction, when the same organization is tied to the anti-Polish campaign in Volhynia?

## Beat 1 — Modern dispute and textbook paradox

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
Zelensky attached the UPA name to a current military unit; Poland reacted strongly.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
Why is this honour important enough to create a dispute with Poland during a war?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
On the surface, Ukraine remembers independence fighters and Poland remembers Polish civilians killed in Volhynia. Benoit's tentative first explanation is that the uglier history may no longer be part of Ukraine's heroic account. The 2023 textbook explicitly teaches the expulsion decision and July attacks while later describing the anti-Soviet struggle as heroic.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
It eliminates simple forgetting as a sufficient explanation and reveals that both parts can coexist inside one current educational account.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The investigation must test both histories before explaining why one receives the honour.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
The textbook's Volhynia claim must first be checked against the historical record.

## Beat 2 — How an independence movement arrived at an anti-Polish campaign

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
OUN-B/UPA wanted an independent Ukraine and the textbook says its Volhynian leadership decided to expel Poles.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
How did an independence movement come to see Polish civilians as a problem?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
Failed Ukrainian statehood; Volhynia and Eastern Galicia inside interwar Poland; OUN's objective to include those territories in a future state; Polish population connected to future Polish claims; Kolodzinskyi's removal doctrine; German-Soviet war destroys the old order; police desertions give the underground armed capacity.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
Each fact creates a condition for the next: territorial aim supplies motive, wartime collapse supplies opportunity, and trained armed recruits supply capacity.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The campaign was neither an automatic result of Polish rule nor an unexplained eruption. A territorial idea acquired a wartime opportunity and an armed force.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
The viewer now understands why July 1943 became a moment to change who lived on the land.

## Beat 3 — Poryck and scale

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
The movement had a territorial objective, an opportunity, and military capacity.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
What did that become on the ground?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
The July 1943 wave, Poryck during Mass, the wider attack pattern, and verified Volhynia casualty range.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
Poryck makes the civilian action concrete; the wider pattern establishes that it was not one local atrocity.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The anti-Polish campaign targeted communities across Volhynia and killed tens of thousands of civilians.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
The viewer needs to know whether this was merely violence around the UPA or something the movement itself treated as its action.

UPA units took part in a broad anti-Polish campaign, but the connection between those attacks and the movement's own internal understanding has not yet been shown.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
Did the violence merely happen around the UPA, or did the movement itself discuss it as an action it was carrying out?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The anti-Polish action belongs to the history of the movement being honoured. The same report's weak-control discussion shows serious problems in higher control over local Security Service structures, without proving that the whole campaign was decentralized.

## Beat 5 — Test the anti-Soviet claim

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
The movement's own records tie it to anti-Polish action. Ukraine honours the UPA as fighters for independence, not for Volhynia.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
Did the UPA and later nationalist underground genuinely fight Soviet rule for an independent Ukraine?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
The Red Army returns; Soviet rule returns; the nationalist movement continues armed resistance. Soviet enemy records document clashes, mobilization resistance, counterinsurgency, arrests, infiltration and deportations. Large UPA formations decline; smaller OUN/SB underground groups continue into the 1950s.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
Records made by the security services trying to destroy the movement independently confirm that the armed resistance was real.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The modern Ukrainian claim that these people fought Soviet rule for Ukrainian independence has a real historical basis.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
Both textbook halves have survived historical testing, so the script can return to how the textbook combines them.

## Beat 6 — Return to the textbook

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
The anti-Polish action and anti-Soviet struggle are both supported by evidence.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
How can one textbook teach both and still call the UPA struggle heroic?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
The textbook teaches the expulsion decision and July attacks inside a wider Polish-Ukrainian confrontation, while describing the later anti-Soviet struggle as heroic.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
The source reveals the hierarchy directly: the attacks are taught, but the anti-Soviet struggle supplies the heroic meaning.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The issue is not simply whether students are told about Volhynia. It is which part answers “what should the UPA mean now?”

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
The viewer needs to know how the anti-Soviet part became nationally important beyond western Ukraine.

## Beat 7 — Memory development and polling

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
The heroic emphasis falls on the real anti-Soviet struggle.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
Why did that emphasis become nationally and officially important?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
Western-Ukrainian memory after 1991; Yushchenko-era national rehabilitation; Russian aggression in 2014; 2015 recognition law; full-scale invasion in 2022; 2026 military honour. Then the two differently worded polls.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
The chronology supplies a mechanism: Russian aggression makes past resistance to Moscow more relevant, while law and commemoration give it official form. Polling shows broad support for the independence-fighter label but greater uncertainty about overall wartime activity.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
The UPA's anti-Soviet history already existed; war with Russia made that history matter more nationally. Public opinion is not one undifferentiated verdict.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
The viewer can now understand why acknowledgment of Volhynia does not settle Poland's objection.

## Beat 8 — Poland and Russia

**WHAT THE VIEWER CURRENTLY KNOWS**  
Ukraine honours a real independence struggle, while internal evidence ties anti-Polish action to the same movement.

**QUESTION NOW IN THE VIEWER'S HEAD**  
Why does Poland still object, and does admitting the history validate Russia's Nazi claim?

**NEXT FACT / SOURCE**  
Poland's concrete objection: the UPA that fought Moscow and the UPA whose structures carried out the anti-Polish action are the same organization. Then the German/ideological facts and Russia's modern inference.

**WHY THAT SOURCE ANSWERS THE QUESTION**  
It separates two conclusions: Poland's historical objection follows from the organizational record; Russia's claim that modern Ukraine is therefore Nazi does not.

**WHAT NEW UNDERSTANDING WE EARN**  
Real crimes need not be sanitized to reject propaganda built from them.

**WHY THE NEXT BEAT NOW FOLLOWS**  
Both national positions have now been understood at their strongest, allowing Benoit's conclusion.
