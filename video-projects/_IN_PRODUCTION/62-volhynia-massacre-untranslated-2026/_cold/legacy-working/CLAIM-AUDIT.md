# Claim audit — SCRIPT-V3

**Verdict:** The central argument is supportable, and the script avoids the most dangerous overclaims about Bandera, a missing signed order, casualty scope and Ukrainian unanimity. It is **not yet fact-locked for filming** because the title/open depends on an exhibit whose exact pages and translation have not yet been directly locked in the project.

## Blocking before record

1. **Textbook double claim.** The Ministry listing and recent ZN.ua comparison confirm the book, its roughly three-page Volhynia section, the regional OUN-B expulsion decision, the 11–13 July attacks, its causal ordering and its anti-genocide exercise. The exact “heroic struggle” phrase still needs direct confirmation in the PDF, with page number and checked translation. Until then, the title and open are a strong hypothesis rather than a record-ready exhibit.
2. **Contemporary internal records.** The narration refers collectively to operational reports and regional regulation of liquidation actions. The final on-screen selection must be fixed claim by claim. “Liquidation” cannot be translated as killing Polish civilians without the adjacent text establishing its object.
3. **Soviet anti-insurgency exhibits.** Select exact 1944 and 1948 pages. A collection description proves that such reporting exists, but the spoken examples must match the displayed records.

## Causal-leap check

- Interwar Polish policy explains a grievance and territorial problem; it does not cause or justify the massacre. The script says this.
- Kolodzinskyi establishes doctrine, not the 1943 operational decision. The script says this.
- Police desertion establishes capacity, not collective perpetration or policy. The script says this.
- Simultaneous attacks establish coordination, not the identity of the original authorizer. The script says this.
- Soviet counterinsurgency records establish a real insurgency, not the moral character of every UPA action. The script says this.
- The war after 2014 helps explain increased usefulness of UPA memory. It should remain an interpretation supported by the dated state actions and polling, not a single-cause claim.

## False-equivalence and generalization check

- The script acknowledges Polish violence without equalizing the two campaigns.
- It does not generalize from one textbook to all schools.
- It says Ukrainian public memory was regionally and politically contested.
- It does not use “Ukraine knows what the UPA did” as a population-wide claim.
- It distinguishes the Polish official position from every Polish person's view.

## Final claim verdict

**Conditional pass.** Historical spine: pass. Modern state-action spine: pass. Textbook premise: pass as a carefully scoped research finding, but **exhibit lock remains open** until the direct pages verify both halves of the title.
