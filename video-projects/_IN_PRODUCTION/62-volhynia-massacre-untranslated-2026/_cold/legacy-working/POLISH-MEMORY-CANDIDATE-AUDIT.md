# Polish memory-selection candidate audit

## Purpose

Prevent the script from making Poland represent complete, neutral historical memory while Ukraine alone selects a useful strand. The comparison concerns the rule for public honour, not equivalence between crimes.

## Candidate A — Romuald Rajs “Bury”

The same subject has a commemorated anti-communist strand and a documented record involving attacks on Belarusian civilians. Official commemoration exists, the IPN's own investigation assigns command responsibility, and Anna Moroz analyzes Rajs as a conflict-of-memory figure.

**Decision:** safe backup, but too much context for this video.

## Candidate B — 2024 institutional dispute over Józef Kuraś “Ogień”

In February 2024 Poland's Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy stopped official commemorative events for Kuraś and the Świętokrzyska Brigade, citing serious objections concerning violence against civilians. The IPN objected publicly. It acknowledged that killings of unarmed civilians cannot be justified, but argued that critically judged actions should not erase these formations' contribution to the struggle for a free and independent Poland.

This is unusually relevant because two Polish institutions articulate the same kind of moral dispute as this video: whether serious wrongdoing disqualifies a historical subject from public honour despite a real independence struggle.

**Decision:** approved, provided narration explicitly says the cases are not equivalent and compares the argument about honour rather than the crimes.

## Candidate C — generic “Cursed Soldiers” memory

The scholarship supports a heroic public category containing historically diverse and contested formations, but the category is less concrete and risks overgeneralization.

**Decision:** background only.

## Approved bounded claim

Polish institutions also disagree over whether serious wrongdoing should disqualify an independence fighter from official commemoration. Do not call this Polish hypocrisy or say Poland “does the same thing” as Ukraine.
