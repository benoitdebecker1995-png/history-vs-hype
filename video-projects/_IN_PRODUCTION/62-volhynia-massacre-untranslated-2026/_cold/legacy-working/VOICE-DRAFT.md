## ACT 1 — THE CONTROVERSY + THE TWO STORIES  ✅ DRILLED 2026-07-12

**[COLD OPEN — locked/drilled earlier, unchanged]**
On May 26th, 2026, Ukraine's president signed a decree naming an elite army unit after the UPA — and called them heroes.
Three weeks later, one of Ukraine's closest allies stripped Zelensky of its highest honour. Not Russia. Poland. A real
rift between two NATO neighbours, in the middle of a war. So I pulled the documents these men left behind — the orders
they signed, and the reports they filed during the Volhynia massacre, almost none of it read outside Polish and
Ukrainian archives.

**[BEAT 1 — Ukraine's case]**
The UPA — the Ukrainian Insurgent Army — was the armed wing of a nationalist movement, the OUN. What they wanted was a
Ukraine of their own, free of Moscow. They fought everyone who occupied Ukraine — the Soviets, and the Germans. For a
lot of Ukrainians, it's simple: these men stood up to the Russians — exactly what they're doing right now.

**[BEAT 2 — Poland's case]**
But Poland remembers these same men very differently. Between 1943 and '45, they massacred tens of thousands of Poles.
And for years, Ukraine blocked Poland from even digging up the bodies. So when Ukraine turned these men into heroes,
Poland heard something else entirely. For Poland, it was as if Germany had started honoring the SS.

**[BEAT 3 — referee pivot]**
So instead of picking a side, let's see what claims the evidence supports. And none of this is an attack on Ukraine, or
the people fighting for it right now — it's about what these particular men did, and how a country chooses to remember it.

## ACT 2 — THE 1941 TURN  ✅ DRILLED 2026-07-12

**[THE REVEAL]**
The first, most important source is from 1941. On June 30th, in Lviv, they declared independence — and promised to work
together with the Germans. So in 1941, they weren't fighting the Nazis. They were working with them. Why? To break free
of Moscow — the Soviets had occupied western Ukraine two years earlier and ruled it hard, mass deportations, and as they
fled in '41 the secret police shot thousands of prisoners in the cells. To a lot of Ukrainians, Germany looked like the
way out.

**[THE SNAP + BANDERA JAILED]**
But Germany had no interest in a free Ukraine. Within days, the same Germans he'd courted arrested him. Bandera was sent
to Sachsenhausen — but not to the death camp; to a special wing for prisoners they wanted kept alive and useful. They saw
him as a card worth holding, not a threat worth killing. His two brothers weren't so lucky — both were killed in Auschwitz.

**[THE BOTH/AND PAYOFF]**
So he was a collaborator and a victim at the same time — and that's what makes this whole thing so hard, because it means
neither side gets to keep the clean version it likes.
