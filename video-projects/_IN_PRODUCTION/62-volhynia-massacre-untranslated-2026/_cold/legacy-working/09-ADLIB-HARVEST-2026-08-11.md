## Raw claims corrected through research

These statements were not carried into the script:

- `UPA` does not mean “Ukrainian Patriotic Action”; it means Ukrainian Insurgent Army.
- Volhynia and Galicia were western Ukrainian lands assigned to interwar Poland; the raw east/west account was confused.
- The OUN's interwar activity in Volhynia and Galicia cannot be described as resistance under Soviet occupation; those regions were under Polish rule.
- Kolodzinskyi wrote about removing Poles from western Ukrainian lands, not eastern Ukraine.
- OUN-B cooperation with Nazi Germany cannot be reduced to opportunism. The tactical goal and the ideological affinity both belong in the explanation.
- Sachsenhausen was a concentration camp, but Bandera was held in its special-prisoners wing with privileges unavailable to ordinary inmates.
- A single specific `29 March 1942 order` should not be spoken unless the actual document is shown. The broader infiltration policy and the spring 1943 police desertions are verified.
- The 11 July death toll is an estimate range, and some attacks targeted churches or worshippers at Mass; not every attack did.
- Article 6 of the 2015 law declared denial unlawful and referred contempt cases to existing law; it did not itself state a new fine or prison term.
- Puźniki concerns UPA killings in February 1945 and must not be presented as a July 1943 Volhynia grave.

## Process correction

The earlier pass treated uncertainty in the creator's ad-lib as a reason to exclude material. That was backwards. The correct sequence is: extract the claim → research it → correct it → then decide whether it earns runtime. The creator's uncertainty is not evidence, but it is often a pointer to the part of the story he finds most useful.
