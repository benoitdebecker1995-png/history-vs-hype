# Strategy memo — Volhynia / UPA rebuild

**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Decision:** Use the textbook paradox as the entry point, but make the dispute over organizational commemoration—not “Ukrainian hypocrisy”—the actual answer.

## Core viewer question

Why does Ukraine officially honour the UPA when the same organization carried out an organized campaign against Polish civilians?

## One-sentence answer

Ukraine commemorates the UPA as a genuine independence movement that fought Soviet rule, while its public memory separates that struggle from the organization's responsibility for Volhynia; Poland's objection is that honouring the organization makes that separation impossible.

## What is different about this video

Most close competitors provide an atrocity account, a perpetrator biography, or the standard “two national memories” explanation. This video begins by testing the easy assumption that Ukraine must omit Volhynia. A Ministry-listed 2023 textbook instead acknowledges an OUN-B decision to expel the Polish population and describes UPA's later struggle against Soviet security forces as heroic. The video then shows why both statements can coexist by testing three propositions against documents: the anti-Polish campaign was organized; the exact originating command remains less certain than the campaign itself; and the anti-Soviet insurgency was real.

The differentiation is the method: viewers see what the sources establish, what historians infer, and where the paper trail ends.

**Selected title:** *Ukraine Teaches the Massacre. So Why Call the UPA Heroes?* It contains the surprising verified discovery and the exact unresolved question. The opening must scope the claim within 30 seconds.

### Thumbnail concepts

1. **Textbook / HEROES?** A real textbook page on the left, the 2026 decree on the right, Benoit between them. Two-word text: `UPA HEROES?`
2. **One movement, two records.** UPA photograph against a split background: Polish victim registry and Soviet security report. Text: `WHAT DO WE HONOUR?`
3. **Poland / Ukraine rupture.** Zelensky's returned medal beside the “Heroes of the UPA” decree. Text: `WHY RISK POLAND?`

Concept 1 wins because it embodies the unique premise and can be assembled from documents already available or readily captured. Avoid gore, swastikas, and a Bandera-only face; those promise a different video.

## Opening 30-second promise

Ukraine's own textbook tells students that the OUN-B leadership in Volhynia decided to expel the Polish population and that UPA units attacked Polish settlements. The same textbook calls the UPA's later fight against Soviet security forces heroic. I expected the first part to be missing. It wasn't. So the question is harder: if the massacre can remain in the story, how does the organization responsible for it still become a national symbol?

## Essential exhibits

1. The 2023 Vlasov/Kulchytsky/Panarin textbook: whole-page identity, anti-Polish passage, and “heroic struggle” passage, with checked translation and exact pages.
2. A restrained Volhynia map plus Poryck and the Siemaszko named-victim registry.
3. Contemporary operational/command evidence independent of Soviet interrogations; use only items whose original page and translation are verified.
4. Stelmashchuk's 1945 statement, visibly labelled `COPY, TYPESCRIPT` and `SOVIET CUSTODY`, to show both its value and limit.
5. Soviet/party security reports documenting sustained operations against UPA/OUN underground forces.
6. The 2015 recognition law and 2026 decree, showing the modern state-memory mechanism.

## Leave out

- A full Bandera biography, the OUN split beyond one clarifying sentence, and a Holocaust chapter.
- A scholar-by-scholar classification debate.
- Graphic descriptions beyond one human case.
- The Melnyk/Yad Vashem dispute unless the textbook/decree hook fails in read-aloud.
- The exhumation chronology unless one short line is needed to show the dispute's material consequence.
- Belgium, AK/Sahryń, or another national-memory comparison in the close. The central disagreement already supplies an earned ending.

## Retention structure

1. **0:00–0:45 — belief update:** the textbook does not support the easy erasure claim.
2. **0:45–3:05 — necessary origin:** why Polish settlement became a territorial problem for OUN nationalists; idea becomes armed capacity.
3. **3:05–6:30 — evidentiary test:** Poryck, campaign scale, independent command material, Soviet-custody evidence, and the exact ceiling.
4. **6:30–8:15 — second update:** UPA's sustained anti-Soviet insurgency was real.
5. **8:15–10:30 — memory mechanism:** regional rehabilitation, 2014, 2015 law, 2022 shift, 2026 decree.
6. **10:30–12:00 — disagreement:** textbook acknowledgment does not settle whether the organization can be honoured separately from its crime.

## Top retention risks

1. Too many initials, names and borders before a human consequence. Introduce each actor only when it changes the answer.
2. Reaching “organized campaign” twice. The first evidence should raise the command question; the document sequence should answer it.
3. Turning the final third into memory-studies exposition. Use dates and state actions; scholars interpret the sequence in production notes rather than speaking through the script.

## Competitor test

After France 24/AP-style coverage, the viewer still needs the historical answer. After Historia Bez Cenzury or OllieKay, the viewer still needs to see why acknowledgment and honour can coexist in Ukrainian state memory. After a Bandera biography, the viewer still needs the distinction between Bandera's symbolism, regional UPA command, organizational responsibility, and a missing authenticated master order. This video supplies all three without requiring a 30–55 minute runtime.
