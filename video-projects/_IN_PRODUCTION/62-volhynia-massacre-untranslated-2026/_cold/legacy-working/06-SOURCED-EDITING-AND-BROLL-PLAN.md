## Primary-source-first visual hierarchy

1. **Lead with showable primary records:** Ukraine Decree 440/2026, the Polish president's revocation statement, Law 314-VIII, Klym Savur Order No.11, the Stelmashchuk protocol copy, and the Sejm resolution once manually retrieved.
2. **Use reproduced primaries second:** Kolodzinskyi manuscript facsimile and the Stetsko Act once their acquisition/rights lines are complete. Label the reproducing edition and never imply direct archive custody.
3. **Use testimony with a permanent status label:** Stelmashchuk and Redesha remain primary-via-testimony, not clean administrative records.
4. **Use secondary scholarship only where the primary is unavailable or the claim is inherently interpretive:** the Klymchak report quote through McBride, Snyder's estimates, and scholar classification cards.
5. **Never replace a missing primary with an archival-looking reconstruction.** If the original is unavailable, show the evidence gap honestly.

## Historical-claim and ethics guardrails

| Claim class | On-screen treatment |
|---|---|
| Documented fact | UPA after-action reporting, the 2015 law, 2026 decree, Polish revocation, signed Order No.11: show the actual object and its custody/licence label. |
| Estimate | 50–60k, ~4k on 11 July, Snyder 70k/20k, Siemaszko ~36k names: always show `estimated`, `around`, `registry contains`, or the named scholar. Never animate false precision. |
| Testimony | Redesha and Stelmashchuk: label testimony, interrogator, date, duress/custody. Testimony can support an inference; it is not the missing order. |
| Scholarly interpretation | Genocide / ethnic cleansing / genocidal ethnic cleansing: bind each label to a scholar or institution and date. No anonymous “historians say.” |
| Contested claim | The signed kill-order: visually separate what is alleged, what survives, and what is unavailable. Never fabricate an archival-looking proxy. |
| Ethical exclusion | No bodies, remains, massacre reenactments, AI historical scenes, fire overlays, weapons montage, screaming SFX, or Holocaust footage used as generic evil shorthand. |
