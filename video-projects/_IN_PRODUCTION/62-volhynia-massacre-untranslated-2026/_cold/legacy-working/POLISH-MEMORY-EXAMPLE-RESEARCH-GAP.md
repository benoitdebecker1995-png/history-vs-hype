# Polish memory-selection example — research gap

## Status

**Resolved by targeted research. Retained as the record of the original gap.**

The verified project research was searched for a specific Polish example in which a real historical figure, movement or event is publicly remembered through a useful or heroic strand while an uncomfortable strand is subordinated. No sufficiently verified example is currently present.

## What the project does contain

### Verified Polish crimes or coercive actions

- The killing of Ukrainian civilians at Sahryń in 1944.
- Polish reprisals against Ukrainian villages.
- Operation Vistula, which forcibly resettled about 141,000 Ukrainians in 1947.
- A Polish Sejm acknowledgment of “acts of Polish revenge on Ukrainian villages.”

These facts matter to the wider history, but they do not by themselves prove how Poland publicly selects or organizes national memory.

### Verified memory asymmetry

Portnov's *Poland and Ukraine: Entangled Histories, Asymmetric Memories* supports the narrower synthesis that Poland mainly remembers what the UPA did to Poles, while Ukrainian public memory gives much greater attention to Russia, Soviet rule and what the UPA did against the Soviets.

This establishes different subjects of memory. It does not supply the required Polish mirror case of a publicly honoured Polish figure or movement whose uncomfortable actions are subordinated.

### Generic comparison

The project has previously considered Belgium and Leopold II as a general example of national-memory selection. That can illustrate the universal mechanism, but it cannot fully repair a script that directly contrasts Polish and Ukrainian memory.

## What kind of example is needed

A usable example should contain all of the following:

1. A recognizable Polish figure, organization, armed formation or national event that receives official or broadly public honour.
2. A clearly documented heroic or useful strand that supplies the public meaning.
3. A clearly documented uncomfortable strand involving the same figure, organization or event—not merely an unrelated Polish crime.
4. Evidence that public commemoration, education, law, museums, monuments or political rhetoric gives the useful strand greater weight or leaves the uncomfortable strand peripheral.
5. Enough scholarly or primary-source support to state the comparison without false equivalence.

## Claim the example must support

The example should support only this bounded claim:

> Selective national memory is not uniquely Ukrainian; Poland also has cases in which public honour gives one real part of a complicated history more weight than another.

It must not be used to claim:

- that Polish and Ukrainian crimes were equal;
- that Poland has no legitimate objection to UPA honour;
- that every nation remembers history in exactly the same way;
- or that one country's selection cancels another country's responsibility.

## Where it would sit

The comparison belongs after the script explains Poland's unresolved objection to the UPA name and before the Russia/conclusion beat.

Its job is to prevent a false structure in which Poland represents complete historical memory and Ukraine alone represents selective memory. It should be concise and should not become a second historical investigation.

## Evidence required before insertion

- At least one strong scholarly source on the figure/event's historical record.
- At least one reliable source showing the form of Polish public commemoration or official memory.
- Exact identification of the uncomfortable strand and whether historians contest it.
- Evidence that the comparison is current enough for any claim about present public memory.
- A script guard explaining the limits of the analogy.

## Research candidates

No candidate is approved. Possible search directions—not conclusions—include Polish state memory around the “Cursed Soldiers,” selected Home Army formations, or another officially honoured resistance figure with a documented record affecting non-Polish civilians. Each carries a serious false-equivalence and collective-attribution risk and must be researched before use.

## Stop condition

Targeted research identified a bounded, verified solution: the 2024 dispute between a Polish ministry and the IPN over commemoration of Józef Kuraś “Ogień.” See `POLISH-MEMORY-CANDIDATE-AUDIT.md` and `FAIRNESS-BEAT-DECISION.md`. The comparison concerns competing standards for public honour and must explicitly reject equivalence between the cases.
