# Read-through 2 diagnosis

## Status

This diagnosis treats the second creator read-through as the strongest current evidence for Benoit's voice and reasoning. It supersedes conflicting conclusions in the predicted-stumble audit, the earlier voice audit, and phrase-level approvals. It is not historical evidence.

The broad spine remains locked:

modern dispute → textbook paradox → anti-Polish history → anti-Soviet test → textbook return → memory → polling → Poland → Russia → conclusion.

The failure is local. V2 often contains the right evidence and even the right question, but asks the viewer to supply the premise that makes the next sentence necessary.

## Central correction to the first diagnosis

The organized-versus-local/spontaneous question should not have been removed. Benoit's second read-through reveals that he was objecting to its missing setup, not to the investigation itself.

The live competing account is that the violence may have developed substantially from a wider local Polish-Ukrainian eruption during wartime collapse, with neighbours attacking neighbours and some UPA units joining, rather than from an anti-Polish campaign organized through OUN/UPA structures.

V2 had already told the viewer that UPA units attacked Polish settlements. It then asked whether the campaign was “tied to” the UPA, which merely repeated an established fact. Document 149 consequently appeared to prove something the script had already asserted.

The repaired sequence must be:

1. Establish the attack pattern and scale.
2. Introduce the local-eruption interpretation in ordinary language.
3. Ask whether that interpretation can account for the record, or whether OUN/UPA structures were organizing and reporting an anti-Polish campaign.
4. State that several internal and operational records bear on the question.
5. Use Document 149 as the main visible exhibit.
6. Conclude that a purely local/spontaneous account is difficult to sustain, while leaving the exact initiating authority less certain.

Do not use “Jacquerie” in narration. The current project material verifies the substance of the dispute but does not yet pin that label to a specific scholar and exact formulation.

## Interruption diagnosis

### 1. “Predictable” arrives before its cause

**V2 wording:** Poland's reaction is called completely predictable before the audience knows what Poland associates with the UPA.

**Creator interruption:** “Predictable is not being explained yet.”

**Failure type:** causal order / missing referent.

**Repair:** state the honour, Poland's reaction, and the Polish association with Volhynia. Only then ask why Ukraine considers the honour valuable enough to accept that foreseeable dispute during a war.

### 2. “Both stories” has no stable referent

**V2 wording:** “check both stories properly.”

**Creator interruption:** “Which stories? We have not described the stories.”

**Failure type:** vague backward reference.

**Repair:** name the two claims: the UPA's role in the anti-Polish campaign and its fight against Soviet rule.

### 3. The anti-inevitability qualification is jammed into chronology

**V2 wording:** “Most Ukrainians did not join the OUN, and none of this made mass killing inevitable.”

**Creator interruption:** the sentence feels inserted without introduction or flow.

**Failure type:** untriggered negative qualification.

**Repair:** first explain how interwar Polish repression contributed to nationalist radicalization. Then qualify that causal claim directly: repression helps explain the context, but cannot make the later choice inevitable, and most Ukrainians were not OUN nationalists.

### 4. The 1943 synthesis becomes abstract

**V2 wording:** “the movement had a reason to change who would control the territory when the war ended.”

**Creator interruption:** “Doesn't make any sense… comes out of nowhere.”

**Failure type:** abstraction replaces the causal object.

**Repair:** connect the two established facts concretely. Ideas about removing the Polish population already existed inside the movement; by 1943 it also had a substantial armed force. Phrase only what the verified research supports.

### 5. The Volhynia question repeats an answered question

**V2 wording:** “Is this anti-Polish campaign actually tied to the organization being honoured today?”

**Creator interruption:** the script has already said UPA units attacked settlements.

**Failure type:** false uncertainty.

**Repair:** introduce the actual interpretive dispute: broader local eruption with UPA participation versus a campaign organized and reported through OUN/UPA structures.

### 6. One document appears to carry the entire case

**V2 wording:** “This is where one internal document matters.”

**Creator interruption:** “Only one? Is there not like a bunch of them together?”

**Failure type:** source-convergence failure.

**Repair:** say several internal and operational records matter, then make Document 149 the clearest visible example. Corroboration can remain visual or in source notes.

### 7. Zahrawa is named but not structurally located

**V2 wording:** Zahrawa is called a regional structure; its Security Service is called an internal security arm.

**Creator interruption:** “This needs more explanation of what exactly this is.”

**Failure type:** functional identity remains incomplete.

**Repair:** explain where the Zahrawa military district sat in the regional OUN/UPA apparatus, what the SB did, what kind of report Document 149 is, the period it covers, and that “Tretii” is the signing pseudonym of the responsible SB official. Verify each detail before narration.

### 8. The exhibit appears before the viewer knows what it is

**V2 wording:** “And this is the line I want to show you.”

**Creator interruption:** “What exactly is it we are looking at? What is the document? Where does it come from?”

**Failure type:** exhibit orientation.

**Repair:** give the human-readable document identity and reason for using it before the quotation. Keep catalogue numbers on screen.

### 9. Literal wording is converted into an unsupported purpose claim

**V2 wording:** the report “described its purpose as destroying the Poles.”

**Creator interruption:** asks whether “destroying” means killing, expulsion over the river, or something else.

**Failure type:** source overreach.

**Repair:** state only that the author calls it “the action of destroying the Poles” and evaluates its results. Establish territorial removal from the wider evidence base, not from this sentence alone.

### 10. “Organized action” appears before organization has been argued

**V2 wording:** “So organized action did not mean perfect control…”

**Creator interruption:** “We have never made this claim before.”

**Failure type:** conclusion precedes evidence.

**Repair:** establish convergence across internal/operational sources first. Then qualify what organization means: involvement, reporting and implementation through structures do not imply perfect local control.

### 11. The master-order qualification has no trigger

**V2 wording:** “We still do not have a signed master order…”

**Creator interruption:** “Again, you are jamming this in here for no apparent reason.”

**Failure type:** inherited historiography / untriggered rebuttal.

**Repair:** do not lead with the missing order. After evidence supports organizational involvement, give one narrow qualification that the exact initiating authority remains uncertain. Mention a signed order only if the preceding claim makes that evidentiary distinction necessary.

### 12. “So did they?” is not Benoit's syntax

**Creator interruption:** “I would never talk like this.”

**Failure type:** artificial punch question.

**Repair:** use a complete reasoning transition: Document 149 explains Poland's view; Ukraine honours a different part of the history; Benoit next checks how substantial the anti-Soviet struggle actually was.

### 13. The post-war distinction is followed by an unnecessary warning

**V2 wording:** “it would be misleading to imagine intact UPA armies…”

**Creator interruption:** the scale change was already explained.

**Failure type:** negative qualification without a live mistaken image.

**Repair:** say directly that large formal UPA formations declined and resistance continued through smaller OUN/SB underground groups.

### 14. The textbook return works

The creator reads through the evidence-supported two-part return with little resistance. Preserve the concrete progression: attacks supported; Soviet fight supported; both taught; only one described as heroic. Do not generalize one textbook into all Ukrainian memory.

### 15. The polling works until the methodology slogan

**Accepted:** exact 2022 and 2025 questions and the main responses.

**Rejected:** spoken 5% “don't know” and “These are not two points on a trendline.”

**Failure type:** unnecessary detail plus model-written formulation.

**Repair:** state simply that the questions differ, explain how, and say the responses are not directly comparable.

### 16. Acknowledgment lacks the reason it is insufficient

**V2 wording:** joint mourning “does not by itself answer that objection.”

**Creator interruption:** “Why not?”

**Failure type:** missing inference.

**Repair:** Poland's objection concerns not only acknowledgment of victims but whether the UPA name itself should receive state honour.

### 17. The comparison grants Poland neutral memory

**Creator interruption:** the script makes Poland look as if it remembers real history while Ukraine constructs fairy tales; “every country does the same.”

**Failure type:** structural fairness / missing comparison.

**Repair:** find a verified Polish memory-selection example. Polish crimes alone do not prove selective Polish commemoration. Until such an example is verified, the fairness beat cannot be responsibly scripted.

### 18. The previously protected closing bridge is now rejected

**V2 wording:** “I think that's the whole fight.”

**Creator interruption:** “I never speak like this… another AI sentence.”

**Failure type:** stale phrase-level approval overriding current full-context evidence.

**Repair:** delete it. Rebuild the lead-in after the Polish fairness beat is solved. The underlying final question may survive if it remains natural in the new context.
