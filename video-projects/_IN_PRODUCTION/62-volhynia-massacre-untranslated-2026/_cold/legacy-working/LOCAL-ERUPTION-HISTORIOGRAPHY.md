# Local-eruption historiography

## Status: VERIFIED WITH ATTRIBUTION

The “peasant revolt” or spontaneous-revenge interpretation is a real historiographical position, not an opponent invented for the script.

Grzegorz Motyka's 2016 article, *Zbrodnia wołyńska 1943 roku i mit buntu ludowego*, explicitly identifies a hypothesis explaining the anti-Polish outbreaks as spontaneous revenge by local Ukrainian peasants for discrimination in interwar Poland. Motyka rejects it. He argues that OUN-B and UPA organized the mass campaign and mobilized peasants, sometimes coercively. His abstract says the manner of killing could create the appearance of a local “Jacquerie.”

Bohdan Hud and Oleh Muravskyi give greater weight to long-term social conflict, discriminatory Polish policies, competing propaganda, occupation, collapse of order, banditry and local violence. This is a multicausal account, not a claim that UPA was absent.

Jared McBride describes the campaign as Ukrainian-nationalist-led while showing that ordinary peasants participated for different reasons. Organization and local agency can therefore coexist.

## Narrator-safe conclusion

There is a real disagreement over how much explanatory weight belongs to local social conflict and wartime collapse, and how much belongs to organization through OUN/UPA structures. Do not say the alternative position claims that UPA merely happened to be present. Do not use “Jacquerie” without attributing the term to Motyka's description of the appearance or hypothesis he is rebutting.
