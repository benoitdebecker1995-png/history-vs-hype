# Final targeted refinement audit

**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**Script assessed:** `SCRIPT-V3.md`  
**Verdict:** Ready after targeted correction. The historical spine is defensible and the main primary-source scene works. The remaining problem is not missing research in general; it is that the opening delays the textbook discovery, the anti-Soviet case lacks a comparably concrete movement source, the polling comparison can be cleaner, and the ending settles Benoit's ethical judgment more firmly than his read-through supports.

## 1. Current-script verdict

Keep the structure and most of the wording. Do not rebuild the Volhynia investigation. Document 149 remains the main internal exhibit. The script already demonstrates both halves of the history and answers why the UPA has become more nationally useful in Ukrainian memory.

The specific weaknesses are:

- the textbook paradox arrives after the opening has already explained Benoit's initial theory;
- “historical weight” and “organizational direction” sound like editor language rather than Benoit thinking aloud;
- the anti-Soviet section proves resistance from Soviet records but does not first show what the UPA itself said it was fighting for;
- the 2022/2025 polls separate two questions, but the different years make the comparison harder to explain than necessary;
- the final paragraph turns a still-open personal question into a clean rule for state commemoration.

## 2. Creator-intent check

The actual video question is: **why does Ukraine honour the UPA now, despite the conflict this creates with Poland?**

The intended answer is not that Ukraine forgot Volhynia. It is that Ukraine can acknowledge the anti-Polish campaign while allowing the anti-Soviet independence struggle to define the organization in public memory. Russian aggression after 2014, and especially after 2022, made that already-existing memory nationally relevant. The unresolved question is whether a real and honourable contribution can justify honouring the organizational name when another organized campaign belongs to the same organization.

V3 mostly serves that intent. Its final judgment goes one step beyond Benoit's current position.

## 3. YouTube audit

The hook has a live political event, a clear disagreement and an immediate question. It does not need a new cold open. Moving the textbook discovery into the first 30–45 seconds improves the promise because the viewer learns immediately that the easy answer—Ukraine simply leaves Volhynia out—does not work.

The middle remains coherent if each investigation answers one question:

1. Is the anti-Polish campaign tied to the organization being honoured?
2. Did that organization and its underground really fight for independence against Soviet rule?
3. Why has the second part become more important in Ukrainian public memory now?

The public-versus-internal contrast adds value only as a short turn into Document 149. Expanding it into a second historiographical dispute would slow the strongest scene. The polling should be the payoff to the memory section, not a new sociology section.

## 4. Voice-and-brain audit

The revised script follows the creator model's preferred thought movement: surface explanation → inconvenient evidence → narrower question → source test → qualified conclusion. It preserves moderately long sentences where they carry causation and uses short sentences only for genuine turns.

The main voice corrections are concrete:

- “how much historical weight each part should carry” becomes a direct question about why one part defines the public honour;
- “organizational direction was not a secondary detail” becomes a plain statement that the campaign was being handled through movement structures;
- the anti-Soviet turn becomes “So did they?” rather than an abstract test of a later heroic image;
- the final position is expressed as Benoit's actual uncertainty, not a general “public-honour rule.”

The final script does not copy raw disfluency. It preserves the raw read-through's causal scaffolding, self-correction and willingness to hold two facts at once.

## 5. Better-evidence audit

### MUST USE

**Two 2022 polling questions.** Rating Group asked on 27 April 2022 whether OUN-UPA should be recognized as participants in the struggle for Ukrainian independence: 81% supported, 10% opposed. KIIS asked on 7–13 September 2022 how respondents assessed OUN-UPA activity during the Second World War: 43% positive, 8% negative, 37% difficult to assess, with the remainder answering that they did not know or refusing. These are different samples, organizations and questions, so they are not a trend line. Their value is precisely that they separate recognition of the independence struggle from judgment of the whole wartime record.

**The official commemoration chain.** The 2010 presidential decree explicitly included OUN and UPA participants among twentieth-century independence fighters; the 2015 law separately listed OUN and UPA and made honouring that struggle state policy; Decree 440/2026 gave the special-forces unit the “Heroes of the UPA” name to restore national military traditions while defending Ukraine's independence. These documents demonstrate the state's stated rationale instead of making the script infer it.

### WORTH USING

**UPA leaflet, “What Does the Ukrainian Insurgent Army Fight For?”** The Central State Archive of Public Associations and Ukrainica displays the leaflet and gives the locator TsDAHOU f. 1, op. 70, spr. 999, ark. 129–130, dated 1942–43. The text states that the UPA fights for an independent united Ukrainian state and against Russian-Bolshevik and German occupiers. This is useful as the movement's stated purpose, paired with hostile Soviet records showing that anti-Soviet resistance occurred. The project should not call it a July 1943 General Command document unless that tighter date and issuer are independently established.

**October 1943 OUN communiqué.** Scholarly literature identifies a public communiqué that condemned “mutual mass murders” and framed the violence through Polish policy, reciprocal conflict and outside powers. It is useful as a brief public framing before Document 149. The original-language page and full archival provenance have not been secured in the project, so it should not become an on-screen quotation or carry more than the narrow claim that the public explanation differed from the internal report. The stronger claim that it explicitly denied OUN responsibility is not used.

### CURRENT EVIDENCE BETTER

**Document 149 remains the main Volhynia exhibit.** It is closer to internal operational assessment than the public communiqué and has verified wording and provenance. The public source should sharpen the contrast, not displace it.

**The existing Soviet-record convergence remains better than forcing one dramatic operation.** The archive exhibition supplies a February 1944 Soviet appeal to UPA members, a January 1945 NKVD results report and a June 1945 Khrushchev memorandum to Stalin. Together with the project's existing research, they establish sustained Soviet counterinsurgency. No newly located single document is clear enough in translation and context to replace that convergence as the narrated proof.

### DO NOT ADD

- Do not use July 1943 as the leaflet's date without a verified edition or original carrying that date.
- Do not turn the October communiqué into proof that the movement merely described all violence as spontaneous.
- Do not claim the modern textbook repeats the 1943 public defense; it explicitly acknowledges OUN-B involvement.
- Do not add another command-order debate, genocide terminology section, German investigation or Soviet-operation detour.

## 6. Revised reasoning sequence

1. Zelensky honours “Heroes of the UPA”; Poland reacts.
2. The current Ukrainian textbook already teaches both the decision to expel Poles and a “heroic” anti-Soviet struggle. So omission cannot by itself explain the honour.
3. Ask the actual question: why does the second part define the public honour now?
4. Give only the background needed to understand why Poles became connected to territory and a future border.
5. Show that ideas of removal existed before 1943 and that the movement acquired the capacity to act.
6. Establish the scale and character of the Volhynia attacks.
7. Briefly contrast the OUN's public presentation of reciprocal conflict with Document 149's internal evaluation of an “action of destroying the Poles.”
8. Explain the safe conclusion: an organized anti-Polish campaign was handled through OUN/UPA structures, but execution and higher control were uneven.
9. Turn to Ukraine's explicit rationale: it honours the movement as part of the independence struggle. So did it really fight for that?
10. Use the UPA leaflet to establish stated purpose and hostile Soviet records to establish actual resistance; distinguish later underground from formal UPA units.
11. Return to the textbook: both halves are present, but only one receives the heroic description.
12. Trace the state-memory chain from an older western Ukrainian memory through 2010 and 2015 to its increased national relevance after Russian aggression and the 2026 decree.
13. Use the two 2022 polls to show that recognizing the independence struggle and judging the whole wartime record are different questions.
14. Explain Poland's objection, keep Kuraś as a narrow commemoration comparison, and dismiss Russia's overreach concisely.
15. End with firm historical findings, better understanding of Ukraine and Poland, and a genuinely unresolved question about honouring the UPA organizational name.
