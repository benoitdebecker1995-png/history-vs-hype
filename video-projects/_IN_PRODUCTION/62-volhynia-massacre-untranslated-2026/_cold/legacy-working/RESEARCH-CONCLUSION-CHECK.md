# Research conclusion check

## 1. What I thought before re-checking

The basic explanation was already present in Benoit's raw discussion: Ukraine remembers an independence movement; Poland remembers the civilians it killed. The question was why Ukraine would choose this honour now and why Poland regarded it as serious enough to damage relations during a war.

## 2. What the research strongly confirms

- The anti-Polish campaign was organized and cannot be reduced to spontaneous village warfare.
- UPA/OUN-B regional command carried responsibility; Kliachkivsky's position is central.
- Polish violence was real but does not make Volhynia 1943 symmetrical.
- UPA's anti-Soviet insurgency was real, sustained and important enough to ground a genuine independence memory.
- Heroic memory moved from regional contestation into state policy through identifiable stages, especially 2014, the 2015 law and the post-2022 war.
- Poland's objection concerns the honour given to the organization itself.

## 3. What the research weakens

- A script organized around a missing master order. The exact command question matters, but cleaner contemporary/internal evidence establishes organization without making archival absence the dramatic centre.
- Any claim that UPA became a straightforward anti-Nazi resistance movement.
- Any claim that public support follows one simple upward polling line.
- The title “Ukraine Teaches the Massacre...” until both textbook passages are directly locked.

## 4. What remains uncertain

- Who made the original early-1943 decision and whether the Central Provid authorized it beforehand.
- The exact wording/form of the originating directive.
- The evidentiary weight of particular Soviet interrogation records.
- The scope of the textbook paradox beyond specific editions.

## 5. Previous claims that were too strong

- “Ukraine teaches the massacre” as a national-system generalization.
- Implying the same verified textbook page already proves the “heroic struggle” half.
- Describing all auxiliary-police deserters as Holocaust-trained perpetrators.
- Letting the signed-order question stand in for the entire organization question.

## 6. Previous claims that were unnecessarily cautious

- The organized nature of the campaign. Contemporary/internal evidence makes this firmer than a script centred on Stelmashchuk suggested.
- The reality of the anti-Soviet insurgency. Soviet documentation makes the basic claim very strong, while leaving the movement's civilian coercion intact.

## 7. Does the working conclusion survive?

**Yes, because it survived attempts to separate its components.** Ukraine honours a historically real independence and anti-Soviet tradition. Poland refuses to separate that tradition from the same organization's anti-Polish campaign. Some Ukrainian educational/state narratives can mention Volhynia while still placing UPA inside a positive liberation story. The dispute is therefore not only about whether the crime happened; it is about what an organizational honour asks the public to treat as defining.

This is an interpretation built from the factual sequence. It is not proof that all Ukrainians or Poles think alike.
