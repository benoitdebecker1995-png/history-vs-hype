# Rebuild failure diagnosis

## Verdict

`SCRIPT-REBUILT.md` was not a genuine rebuild. It was a cleaner, somewhat better-sourced version of the inherited script architecture. It improved individual sentences and added useful qualifications, but it did not allow the new research or the new premise to determine the video's thought order.

The central failure was conceptual: the draft treated the research as material to insert into an existing historical march rather than as evidence that should change the question the video is answering.

## What the rebuild was supposed to become

The strongest new premise is not simply:

> Ukraine remembers the UPA as freedom fighters; Poland remembers Volhynia.

That is true, but familiar and static. The sharper problem is:

> What if Volhynia is acknowledged, yet the UPA can still remain heroic because the massacre is contained inside a different explanatory frame?

The 2023 textbook evidence makes that question visible. The book does not simply erase the violence. It reportedly names the regional OUN-B expulsion decision and the July 1943 attacks, while placing them inside a longer Polish-Ukrainian confrontation and inviting consideration of an argument against the genocide classification. The intellectual problem is therefore not only denial. It is narrative integration: which facts define the organization, which facts become context, and which facts are allowed to determine the honour.

The failed rebuild stated part of this near the beginning and returned to it near the end, but the middle of the video did not investigate that mechanism. It reverted to the old job of proving what the UPA was, what happened in Volhynia, and whether the campaign was organized.

## The inherited architecture that remained in place

The old script's macro-sequence was:

1. 2026 diplomatic controversy.
2. OUN origins and interwar borders.
3. Germany, the 1941 proclamation, and auxiliary police.
4. Volhynia, Poryck, victim totals, and the Siemaszko registry.
5. An evidence ladder culminating in Stelmashchuk and the missing order.
6. Classification and Polish violence.
7. Anti-Soviet resistance.
8. Post-1991 memory policy.
9. A brief return to modern commemoration.
10. Moral conclusion.

The rebuilt script's macro-sequence was:

1. 2026 diplomatic controversy.
2. A short textbook preface.
3. OUN origins and interwar borders.
4. Germany, the 1941 proclamation, and auxiliary police.
5. Volhynia, Poryck, victim totals, and the Siemaszko registry.
6. Public statements, internal reports, Stelmashchuk, and the missing order.
7. Anti-Soviet resistance.
8. Post-1991 memory policy.
9. Return to textbook and official acknowledgments.
10. Moral conclusion.

That is substantially the same historical march. The textbook became a bracket around the old video, not the engine of a new one.

## Where the old spine overruled the new evidence

### 1. The textbook contradiction was announced, then abandoned

The rebuilt draft correctly noticed that simple denial does not explain the evidence. But after stating this, it spent most of its running time reconstructing OUN history, military capacity, the massacre, and the command problem. The viewer was not shown how education, official memory, and wartime utility can acknowledge a crime while preventing it from defining the honoured organization.

### 2. The command question remained the dramatic centre

The old script built toward Stelmashchuk and the absent written order. The rebuilt script replaced part of that ladder with better public-versus-internal evidence, but it still made the command-chain problem the major investigative payoff. That is a useful supporting question—especially for establishing organization and evidentiary limits—but it is not the new video's governing question.

### 3. New memory research was reduced to a timeline

The new research on regional memory, Yushchenko, 2014, decommunization, the 2015 law, UINP framing, the full-scale invasion, and differentiated polling should explain a mechanism: why one part of UPA history became nationally useful and how the inconvenient part was narratively contained. In the failed rebuild these findings became a short chronological list. The scholars and their competing explanations did not shape the reasoning.

### 4. The anti-Soviet case was asserted rather than investigated

The draft said Soviet records independently confirm a real insurgency. That is an important correction to a simplistic Polish or Russian story. But it was delivered as reassurance, not as a source-led scene with a named document, a bounded claim, and an explanation of what it changes. The strongest inconvenient fact for the video's critical case therefore felt like a concession paragraph rather than a discovery.

### 5. Competitive differentiation was not structural

The premise that both national stories contain truth appeared in the opening. But the draft still resembled a conventional explainer: origins, wartime chronology, atrocity, command responsibility, memory politics. It did not organize the entire video around the more distinctive discovery that acknowledgment and glorification can coexist because they operate at different levels of the story.

### 6. Surface fluency was mistaken for internal consistency

The rewrite improved sentence-level flow and used more of Benoit's ordinary vocabulary. That did not solve the deeper issue. Benoit's spontaneous reasoning proceeds by asking a question, discovering evidence that complicates his first assumption, and then asking the next necessary question. The draft instead followed sections inherited from a research dossier. Its connectors often moved the chronology forward, but did not always show why Benoit's mind had to move to the next problem.

### 7. The source scenes remained partly generic

The public-versus-internal section named an operational report and Security Service material, but the script had not yet locked every original page, exact quotation, checked translation, and provenance statement. The textbook scene also depended on a distinction that was not fully resolved: the broad contradiction is supportable, but the phrase “heroic struggle” has not been securely located in the exact 2023 Vlasov textbook. It is verified in other official Ukrainian educational and memory materials. Those are not interchangeable objects.

## Important research the failed rebuild underused or omitted

- The exact educational framing of Volhynia: acknowledged violence, conflict framing, sequencing, and the genocide-classification exercise.
- The distinction between the exact 2023 textbook and separate official educational/UINP language describing UPA struggle as heroic.
- UINP's role in connecting UPA memory to military and state-building traditions and to present defenders.
- The strongest memory scholarship and its disagreements: regional inheritance, state sponsorship, mnemonic security after 2014, institutional canonization, and the limits of national polling.
- The fact that survey results depend on the proposition asked: “fighters for independence” is not equivalent to approval of all wartime conduct.
- The difference between Polish demands for factual acknowledgment and Poland's deeper objection to honouring the organization itself.
- The public OUN account of spontaneous or externally driven conflict versus internal operational records.
- The July 1944 high-level retrospective acceptance/knowledge evidence, carefully separated from proof of original authorization.
- The strongest counterweight: anti-Soviet armed resistance was real and substantial, while OUN/UPA/SB coercion and violence against Ukrainian civilians were also real.

## What should survive from the failed rebuild

Some findings remain useful, but they should be subordinate to the new argument:

- Both national memories contain a factual core.
- The Volhynia campaign was organized, even though the exact initiating order remains unresolved.
- Bandera's personal operational responsibility for the 1943 campaign is not established by the current evidence.
- Polish violence was real but does not erase the asymmetry in chronology, objective, geography, and scale in Volhynia.
- The anti-Soviet insurgency was real and cannot be reduced to propaganda.
- Acknowledging victims is not the same as accepting that an honoured organization should be defined by the crime.
- Russia's use of real OUN/UPA crimes does not establish that modern Ukraine is Nazi or justify invasion.

## Rebuild rule going forward

No new script should begin until the thought order is approved. The future script must be generated from the causal question chain in `BENOIT-THOUGHT-ORDER.md`, not by revising the headings or paragraphs of either existing script.

The stopping test is simple: if the middle can still be summarized mainly as “first the OUN, then Germany, then Volhynia, then the command evidence, then memory,” the rebuild has failed again.
