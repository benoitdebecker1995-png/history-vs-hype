# Project 62 — thumbnail specification

23 August 2026. Two treatments to build and compare. Written against the locked title, the live news
shelf, and the project's own exhibit rules.

**Locked title:** *Zelensky Honored These WWII 'Heroes.' Poland Calls It Genocide.*

---

## Why the selected concept should be retired

Concept A from 10 August — Zelensky photograph dominant, cropped decree card, `FACT CHECKED` overlay —
has two problems, and the second is the serious one.

**`FACT CHECKED` describes your method, not the conflict.** It promises a process. Nobody clicks a
process. It also repeats what the title already implies rather than adding a second hook.

**A Zelensky headshot collides with the shelf it will appear next to.** The live competition on this
subject is news coverage: a DW Short at 25,399 views, Hindustan Times at 6.4K, APT at 1K. Every one of
those is a politician's face against a flag. Putting a Zelensky headshot in that row makes you the
sixth identical result, and you are the one nobody has heard of. Your only structural advantage in
that row is that you are not a news channel.

Nobody on that shelf is showing a document. That is the gap, and documents are the channel's whole
visual identity.

## The division of labour

The title carries the accusation and the two named parties. The thumbnail must not repeat any of that.
It has one job: promise the thing the news coverage does not have.

---

## Treatment A — The textbook *(recommended)*

**What it tests:** that the real discovery is the hook. Ukraine's own schoolbook says it.

**Frame.** One object, filling roughly 60–70% of the frame: an open Ukrainian school textbook, shot or
composited at a slight angle so it reads as a physical book rather than a screenshot. Page visible,
Cyrillic visible, **not** intended to be readable. A single horizontal highlight band across one
paragraph — colour only, no arrow, no callout box, no translated overlay.

**Text.** `THEIR TEXTBOOK` — two words, upper case, heavy weight, bottom-left or bottom-right, never
across the page itself.

**Why the highlight and not a translation.** At feed size nobody reads Ukrainian and nobody reads a
translation either. The highlight does the whole job: it says *someone found the specific passage*.
That is the promise.

**Colour.** Keep the book warm and paper-coloured against a dark, desaturated ground. The highlight is
the only saturated element in the frame.

**What kills it.** Making the Cyrillic readable — it reads as foreign and dense and people scroll. A
second object in frame. Any face. Any flag.

## Treatment B — The medal

**What it tests:** the rupture, told through an object instead of a face.

**Frame.** The Order of the White Eagle — the sash and star, which are genuinely striking, white and
red and gold — dominant and sharply lit against a dark ground. The 2026 decree page behind it,
heavily out of focus, present only as texture and enough shape to read as *an official document*.

**Text.** `TAKEN BACK` — two words.

**Why this one might beat A.** It has real colour and a shape no other thumbnail on that shelf has, and
it works with zero knowledge of Ukrainian. It also states a concrete event rather than an abstraction.

**Risk.** A viewer who does not recognise the medal sees a pretty object with no meaning. That is the
thing the test would tell you.

**What kills it.** Showing it broken, cut or crossed out — the honour was withdrawn, not destroyed, and
a fake-damage image is the kind of small dishonesty this channel exists to avoid.

---

## Rules for both

Carried from the project's own packaging lock and evidence rules. These are not style preferences.

- **One dominant object.** A collage of book, medal, map and portrait reads as nothing at feed size.
- **Two words maximum.** No body text, no sentence, no question mark stacked on a phrase.
- **No invented patch, insignia or unit marking.**
- **No fabricated highlighted sentence** from Order No. 11 or any UPA report. Treatment A highlights a
  real passage in a real book or it does not ship.
- **No gore, no swastikas, no Bandera-only portrait.** Each promises a different video and would draw
  an audience the video does not serve.
- **Do not use** the retired `READ THE ORDER / WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY SAY?` dossier render.

## How to judge them

Not by looking at them full size on your monitor, which is where every thumbnail looks fine.

1. Export both at 1280×720, then view at **320 px wide**. If the dominant object is not identifiable
   at that size, it has already failed.
2. Put each one **in the actual row**: open YouTube, search the subject, screenshot the results grid,
   and paste yours into it. You are choosing the image that looks least like its neighbours, not the
   one that looks best alone.
3. Read the title beside it. If the thumbnail restates the title, one of the two is wasted.

## Running it as a test that teaches you something

The catalogue currently contains five recorded package swaps and every one is uninformative, because
four changed title and thumbnail together and none got enough impressions after the change.

So: **title stays fixed.** Only the thumbnail varies. Record which treatment went live, on what date,
with a hash of the file, before publication. If you swap mid-run, record that too, and give the second
version a real serve before concluding anything. If impressions after a swap are under a few thousand,
the honest verdict is *uninformative* — write that down rather than inventing a lesson.

## Prediction to record before upload

Fill this in and put it in `PROJECT.md` before the video goes live, not after:

- Which treatment is going up, and why you expect it to work.
- Expected traffic mode — is this a search video, a suggested video, or a news-window video?
- Expected first-week views, as a range you would actually bet on.
- The single thing most likely to make it fail.

That record is what makes the 7-day and 28-day reviews mean anything. Without it, whatever happens is
just a number.
