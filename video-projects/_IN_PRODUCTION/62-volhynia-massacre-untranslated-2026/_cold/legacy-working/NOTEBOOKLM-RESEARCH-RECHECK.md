# NotebookLM research re-check

**Fresh pass:** 2026-08-17, Volhynia notebook, 58 selected sources. NotebookLM answers were treated as retrieval leads and checked against the project's verified research and genealogy where load-bearing.

## A–B. Movement, Germany and capacity

- OUN/OUN-B sought an independent, united Ukrainian state. Polish settlement mattered because it strengthened a future Polish territorial claim. Kolodzinskyi's doctrine shows removal thinking before 1943; it is ideology, not the operational order for Volhynia.
- OUN-B tried to use Germany in 1941 and its proclamation promised cooperation with Hitler's Germany. Germany rejected an independent Ukrainian state and detained Bandera and Stetsko. Bandera's 1943 operational connection is unsupported; detention does not prove ignorance or opposition.
- Around 5,000 Ukrainian auxiliary policemen deserted in spring 1943. They did not all follow one path, and police service cannot be converted into collective guilt for the future UPA. Some personnel nevertheless brought weapons, training and experience acquired within a force implicated in Holocaust policing.

## C. Six separate command verdicts

1. **Organized anti-Polish campaign:** strongly established by synchronized attacks, identified units, operational reporting and internal organizational records.
2. **Kliachkivsky's regional responsibility:** strongly supported. He commanded UPA-North; evidence ties regional policy and commanders to his authority.
3. **Exact original decision / Central Provid authorization:** unresolved. Serious reconstructions divide between a higher-level decision and a Volhynian initiative later accepted by central leaders.
4. **Authenticated signed master extermination order:** not established in the accessible record.
5. **Bandera's personal operational tie:** not established.
6. **Historiographical overlap:** organization and OUN-B/UPA responsibility are much less disputed than exact command genesis or the genocide label.

### Best evidence scene

- A 27 July 1943 operational report connected to the Huta Stepańska/Lytvynchuk operations describes an identified UPA action against a Polish settlement. This directly demonstrates unit participation and reporting, not merely an inferred pattern.
- Zahrawa Security Service material describes Lytvynchuk as an initiator/organizer of killings and burnings. Exact quotation and original page still require exhibit lock.
- Public OUN statements spoke of peaceful coexistence or a spontaneous two-sided conflict. Internal language and later leadership records describe organized anti-Polish action in a different register.
- The July 1944 UHVR minutes show high-level knowledge and retrospective acceptance; they do not prove who made the original early-1943 decision.
- Stelmashchuk remains useful supporting evidence, with mandatory Soviet-custody, copy and accessibility caveats.
- Order 1/43 is logistical/command evidence, not an extermination order.

## D. Polish violence

Polish formations killed Ukrainian civilians, including at Sahryń in 1944. Operation Vistula forcibly resettled about 141,000 Ukrainians in 1947. These crimes and coercive policies belong in the history. They do not make Volhynia 1943 symmetrical: sequence, territorial objective, geography and scale differ.

## E–F. Germany and the Soviet Union

- Narrator-safe Germany summary: OUN-B cooperated with Germany in 1941; Germany then repressed it when it pursued an independent state; UPA later fought limited anti-German actions, while some units also made tactical arrangements with Germans as the Soviet front returned. It was neither simply a Nazi puppet nor a major anti-Nazi resistance equivalent to its anti-Soviet struggle.
- The anti-Soviet insurgency was real and sustained. Soviet records themselves describe armed operations and a large counterinsurgency response, including deportations. Organized resistance declined after 1950, while underground activity persisted into the 1950s.
- OUN/UPA/SB also coerced civilians and killed suspected collaborators. A real independence struggle is not the same as a morally clean one.

## G. Heroic memory

UPA memory was initially regional and contested after 1991. Yushchenko elevated it within state policy; 2014 made resistance to Moscow more nationally useful; the 2015 law legally recognized OUN/UPA members as independence fighters; decommunization removed Soviet symbols and created replacement space; the full-scale invasion accelerated positive identification with anti-Soviet resistance. This is a political development, not the vague claim that “Ukraine needed heroes.”

## I–K. Polling, Poland and Russia

- Polls ask different questions. Recognition as independence fighters is not the same as approval of wartime activity. Percentages cannot be placed in one trend line without displaying wording, date and sample.
- Poland's strongest objection is supported: it objects to honouring OUN/UPA as organizations associated with the campaign, not merely to denial of deaths.
- Russia exploits real collaboration, ideology and crimes to make a separate modern claim. Historical OUN/UPA crimes do not establish that present-day Ukraine is Nazi or justify invasion.
