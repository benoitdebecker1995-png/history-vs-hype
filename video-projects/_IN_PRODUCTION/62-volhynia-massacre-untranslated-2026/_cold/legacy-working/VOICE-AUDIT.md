# Voice audit — SCRIPT-V3

**Compared against:** creator operating model, T8 Volhynia ad-lib, approved current-project phrasing, and the mechanical voice lint. The raw voice corpus was not loaded wholesale.

## Verdict

**Pass with read-aloud required.** The draft now follows Benoit's characteristic reasoning rather than the polished answer/counteranswer rhythm of the pasted alternative. It starts with an apparent explanation, discovers contrary evidence, tests both sides, separates what the evidence proves from what it does not, and ends with a narrower conclusion.

Mechanical lint: **0 hard, 0 warning** findings. Its remaining advisory flags are the two opening research questions and section-heading transitions; neither is automatically a voice failure.

## Lines closest to Benoit's voice

- “So my first explanation was too simple.”
- “That gives us an idea, but it does not yet explain how an armed campaign became possible.”
- “Again, that establishes capacity. It does not by itself establish a policy against civilians.”
- “Those facts belong in the history. But they do not make the campaigns equivalent.”
- “I could not find an authenticated signed extermination order in the project sources.”
- “For that, I wanted to test the heroic part of the story as well.”
- “So I ended up changing one part of my view.”

These lines name the current evidentiary problem and use connectors to carry an actual inference. They resemble the T8 ad-lib's recurring forms: “what we have evidence for,” “what we can prove,” “that doesn't mean,” and “to get to the more complicated part.”

## Remaining written-prose pressure points

1. **“The removal doctrine, attack pattern, military reporting, command structure and testimony...”** is a dense five-item summary. It is logically useful but may need a breath or one natural restart during read-aloud.
2. **“The Polish objection is stronger than...”** is accurate, but the following sentence is highly compressed. Listen for whether Benoit naturally adds “because” and restates the actor.
3. **The opening's paired questions** are earned research questions and immediately answered by the structure. If they sound staged aloud, merge them: “I wanted to test how organized the campaign was and whether the later independence struggle was substantial enough to explain the memory.”
4. **“A public denial cannot settle the question...”** risks sounding like an essay verdict. It should survive only if it feels natural in the read-aloud; otherwise use “So I don't think the public denial settles this, because...”

## AI-pattern check

- No repeated `not X but Y` construction.
- No “two things can be true,” “here's the twist,” cinematic anthropomorphism, decorative metaphors or section-ending slogans.
- Short fragments are rare and functional rather than a dominant cadence.
- Scholars remain mostly in production notes; the narration speaks through evidence.
- First person is singular throughout. There is no channel “we.”

## Internal-consistency check

The script has one continuous causal chain:

`textbook contradiction → territorial doctrine → armed capacity → coordinated campaign → command-evidence ceiling → real anti-Soviet insurgency → post-1991 rehabilitation → organizational commemoration dispute`

Each section resolves the question opened by the previous one. The close returns specifically to the textbook discovery rather than adding a new universal moral.

## Read-aloud instruction

Do not polish the prose again before Benoit reads it. Mark only lines where he naturally changes the causal order, has to inhale in the middle of a list, or replaces an institutional verb with an ordinary one. Those spontaneous corrections outrank this audit.
