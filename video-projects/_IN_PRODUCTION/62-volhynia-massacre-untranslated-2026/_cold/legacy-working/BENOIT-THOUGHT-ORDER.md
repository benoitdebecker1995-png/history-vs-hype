# Benoit thought order

## Purpose

This is the reasoning order, not narration and not a script outline. It records the questions that actually move the argument forward. No script should be written until Benoit approves this chain.

## 1. Why would Ukraine honour the UPA now, and why did Poland react so strongly?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
When I saw the 2026 dispute, I wanted to understand why Ukraine would honour the UPA now, while it is fighting Russia, and why Poland considered that serious enough to confront an ally.

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
Both countries tell a different story about the same organization, and each story has a grain of truth. I want to see what version the evidence actually supports and why the two countries end up emphasizing different parts.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
The Ukrainian honour connects the UPA name to a present military unit. The Polish response connects the same name to the killing of Polish civilians. Then I find the paradox that gives me a way into the investigation: one current Ukrainian textbook explicitly says the Volhynian OUN-B leadership decided to expel the Polish population and describes the July 1943 attacks, while the same textbook describes the UPA's later struggle against the NKVD as “heroic.”

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
The textbook does not answer the question yet. It tells me I have to investigate both halves properly: first the anti-Polish campaign, then the anti-Soviet struggle. Only after that can I return to why one part gets emphasized more than the other.

## 2. Was Volhynia an organized UPA campaign or a wider conflict that got out of control?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
Before discussing memory, I need to know whether the killing of Polish civilians was organized by UPA structures or whether it can reasonably be described as spontaneous violence inside a broader Polish-Ukrainian conflict.

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
The attack pattern and death toll show that something large happened, but they do not by themselves tell me how the movement understood or directed it. I need to compare what it said publicly with what appears inside its own records.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
UPA units attacked Polish communities across Volhynia. The attacks had a territorial purpose and were heavily asymmetric in their sequence, geography, and civilian scale. Polish forces also committed real crimes against Ukrainian civilians, but the major anti-Polish campaign in Volhynia came first.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
Polish violence has to be included, but it does not adequately explain Volhynia as an equal or spontaneous conflict. The decisive test is now the public-versus-internal record.

## 3. What did the movement say publicly, and what do its internal reports show?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
Did the OUN describe the anti-Polish action internally in the same way it explained the violence publicly?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
If the public explanation presents a Polish-Ukrainian conflict or spontaneous local violence, while internal reports evaluate how an “action” was carried out district by district, that contrast tells me whether there was an organized campaign without requiring a signed master order.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
Public OUN statements blamed Polish actions and outside powers and presented the violence as part of a wider conflict. The main internal document is different. Document 149 in *Litopys UPA*, New Series, volume 2, is a Zahrawa Security Service report signed “Tretii.” It states: “Акція нищення поляків не принесла очікуваних наслідків” — “The action of destroying the Poles did not bring the expected results.” The edition gives its provenance as TsDAVO of Ukraine, fond 3838, inventory 1, file 55, folios 1–5, copy. The same report discusses weak Security Service control. That matters because it shows an organized policy being evaluated inside the organization while also showing that control and execution on the ground were uneven rather than perfectly centralized.

The July 1943 Volodymyr–Horokhiv district report corroborates that conclusion by evaluating the action locality by locality and recording places where it worked badly or was not carried out. The 27 July 1943 Huta Stepańska operational report separately shows an identified unit receiving and reporting an anti-Polish operation. These reports support the Zahrawa document underneath the main scene rather than replacing it.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
“You cannot call that spontaneous. Then the question becomes: who organized it?” The Zahrawa report uses the organization's own language for an action of destroying the Poles and judges its results. At the same time, its discussion of weak Security Service control stops me from turning organized policy into a picture of perfectly centralized execution. It supports an organized regional campaign, but it does not settle whether the first order came from the regional command or the central leadership. Stelmashchuk remains secondary and is not needed to make the main contrast.

## 4. Does the UPA's anti-Polish campaign mean the Ukrainian freedom-fighter story is false?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
After seeing the anti-Polish evidence, I need to test the other side properly. Did the UPA really carry out the anti-Soviet struggle for which Ukraine honours it, or is that mostly a later heroic story?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
I should test that claim using Soviet records, not only UPA memoirs or modern Ukrainian commemorations.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
Soviet records document armed clashes, underground activity, resistance to mobilization, security operations, and deportations intended to break the movement and its support. The underground survived into the 1950s. The movement also coerced and killed Ukrainians it considered enemies or collaborators.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
The Ukrainian side also rests on something real. The UPA did fight Soviet rule and did fight for an independent Ukraine. That does not remove Volhynia, but it explains why the organization cannot be understood only through Volhynia.

## 5. If both parts are real, how does one of them become the main story?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
Now I can return to the textbook. The same organization was involved in an organized campaign against Polish civilians and a real fight against Soviet rule. So which part gets to define what the UPA means in Ukraine?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
The important question is not only whether Volhynia appears in the book. I need to see what role it plays beside the independence story.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
The textbook acknowledges the OUN-B decision to expel Poles and the July attacks, but places them inside a wider Polish-Ukrainian confrontation, begins that account with Polish violence, and presents the genocide classification as something students should debate. In the same textbook's wider account of the UPA, the later fight against the NKVD is described as a “heroic struggle.”

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
The important issue is not necessarily factual erasure. Volhynia can be acknowledged while the fight for independence remains the answer to the larger question, “Who were the UPA?” The massacre is present, but it does not get to define the organization.

## 6. How did that part of UPA history become more important across Ukraine?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
The anti-Soviet memory already existed, so why did it become much more relevant nationally and in state commemoration?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
A list of presidents, laws, and dates is not enough. I want to know what changed around that existing memory.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
Positive UPA memory was initially strongest in western Ukraine, and Yushchenko pushed it further into state memory. Russia's seizure of Crimea and the war in Donbas after 2014 made resistance to Moscow more relevant across the country. The 2015 law officially recognized OUN and UPA members as fighters for independence. The full-scale invasion in 2022 made the connection between the old anti-Soviet struggle and Ukraine's present defense against Russia even more immediate.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
The anti-Soviet memory was not created by the current war. Russian aggression made that existing part of UPA history increasingly important nationally and increasingly useful for state commemoration.

## 7. Do public attitudes show agreement about the UPA, or does the answer depend on the question?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
Official honour does not automatically tell me what Ukrainians think. Do people support the UPA as a whole, or do they respond differently depending on which part they are asked to judge?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
The exact wording matters. A question about recognizing independence fighters is not the same as a question about everything OUN-UPA did during the Second World War.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
On 27 April 2022, Rating Group asked about recognizing OUN-UPA as participants in the struggle for Ukraine's state independence. Among 1,000 adults surveyed by telephone outside occupied Crimea and Donbas, 81% supported recognition and 10% opposed it. In 2025, KIIS instead asked, “How do you, in general, assess the activities of the OUN-UPA during World War II?” In that survey, 37% answered positively, 8% negatively, 50% chose “hard to say for sure, negatively or positively,” and 5% said they did not know.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
These figures cannot be put on one line as rising or falling support. The contrast is the point: the independence-fighter description receives very broad support, while a broader question about wartime activity produces much more uncertainty.

## 8. Why does acknowledgment still not satisfy Poland?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
If Ukrainian material can acknowledge the attacks, and the anti-Soviet struggle was real, why does Poland still reject the honour?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
I need to distinguish an argument about whether the facts are mentioned from an argument about whether the organization should be honoured.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
Polish objections connect the UPA name itself to the campaign against Polish civilians. Joint mourning, reconciliation statements, and the inclusion of Volhynia in Ukrainian material have not settled that objection.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
Poland is not only asking Ukraine to admit that Polish civilians were killed. It is saying that the fight for independence and the organization that carried out the anti-Polish campaign cannot be separated when the state honours the organization itself. “That's the main point of contention.”

## 9. Where do I end up?

**MY QUESTION / THOUGHT**  
Can I understand why Ukraine honours the UPA without pretending that the honour resolves the other part of the organization's history?

**WHY I ASK THE NEXT QUESTION**  
This is the point where the evidence should sharpen my judgment rather than lead into another history section.

**EVIDENCE I ENCOUNTER**  
The anti-Soviet independence struggle was real. The organized anti-Polish campaign was also real. Ukrainian national memory gives the first part more weight in defining the organization, while Poland insists that the second part must affect whether the same organization is publicly honoured.

**HOW MY VIEW CHANGES**  
I understand much better now why Ukrainians honour the independence struggle, especially during another war against Russia. The unresolved issue is whether an organization can be publicly honoured for that real struggle while separating the honour from another organized campaign carried out by the same organization. “And that, I think, is the whole fight. Can you glorify people for one thing they stood for, when they also did other terrible things?”
