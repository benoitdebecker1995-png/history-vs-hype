THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Could one person edit this script cleanly without the production becoming exhausting?

Judge the current script, not an imagined high-budget version.

For every spoken paragraph or natural beat, determine whether it can be covered by:

- Talking head only
- Talking head plus simple overlay
- Existing document or photograph
- Existing finished asset
- Simple map or timeline

1. VISUAL AVAILABILITY

Check:

- Does the required visual already exist in the ZIP?
- Is it a finished usable asset, a raw source, a template or only a written proposal?
- Is the exact source file or exhibit named?
- Does the script ask for a document that has not actually been acquired?
- Does a visual depend on unresolved translation, licensing, authentication or archival retrieval?
- Can it be made from existing material in under thirty minutes?
- Does an existing asset still match the current script, or was it built for an older version?

Do not count an asset as READY until you have inspected the actual file or a reliable visual preview of it.

2. EDITING WORKLOAD

Flag beats requiring:

- several different assets in quick succession;
- a custom historical map;
- synchronised multi-stage animation;
- readable document text at mobile size;
- comparison of multiple documents;
- recreating a location or massacre visually;
- sensitive imagery that may be difficult or inappropriate to show;
- unusually frequent B-roll changes;
- visuals whose production time is out of proportion to their narrative value.

3. EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Separate:

- MANDATORY EVIDENCE: the document or source must appear because the spoken claim depends on it.
- HELPFUL EVIDENCE: it improves comprehension, but talking head could carry the claim.
- DECORATIVE B-ROLL: it adds atmosphere but no argumentative value.

Never recommend cutting mandatory evidence merely to make editing easier.

For every mandatory document, check:

- whether the exact file is present;
- which page or passage must be shown;
- whether an English side-card is needed;
- which phrase must be highlighted;
- how long it must remain readable;
- whether cropping could distort its meaning;
- whether provenance, translation, custody or copy/original warnings must remain visible.

4. TALKING-HEAD VIABILITY

Identify paragraphs that should deliberately remain on camera because:

- the distinction is easier to understand through direct explanation;
- no useful visual exists;
- B-roll would distract from a qualification;
- the personal investigative voice is the point;
- the moral or analytical conclusion should land face-to-camera.

Do not assume every sentence needs visual coverage.

5. PACING

At 163 spoken words per minute, estimate where each section falls in the runtime.

Check:

- whether a visual must appear before the viewer has time to understand it;
- whether document-heavy sections need pauses or longer holds;
- whether any one-minute stretch demands too many unique assets;
- whether there are long talking-head stretches that genuinely need a pattern interrupt;
- whether the editing plan becomes most complex exactly where the argument is already hardest to follow.

6. CURRENT ON-SCREEN INSTRUCTIONS

Audit every [ON SCREEN / ANIMATE] block in SCRIPT.md.

Classify each one:

- READY: clear, feasible and supported by assets in the ZIP.
- NEEDS SIMPLIFICATION: useful idea, but currently overbuilt.
- NEEDS SPECIFICATION: feasible, but missing an exact asset, page, wording or visual sequence.
- BLOCKED: depends on an unavailable asset or unresolved verification.
- OPTIONAL: can safely be replaced by talking head.

If simplifying, propose the smallest production-friendly version that preserves the argument.

WORKLOAD SCALE

Classify each required asset:

- EASY: already available; under 10 minutes to prepare.
- MODERATE: simple map, document card or composite; approximately 10–30 minutes.
- HARD: custom animation, substantial sourcing or complex reconstruction; over 30 minutes.
- BLOCKED: missing source, unresolved provenance, translation, rights or factual decision.

Do not give false precision. Use realistic time ranges.
