# E01 resolved — the textbook locator

Project 62. 24 August 2026. This was the one thing blocking upload.

**Short version: the claim holds, both halves are in the same book and the same section, and I have
the exact Ukrainian. Two things you have to confirm yourself from the PDF before it goes on screen.
And there is a complication about that book that changes what the narration is allowed to say.**

---

## The book

Власов В. С., Кульчицький С. В., Панарін О. Є., **Історія України (рівень стандарту), 10 клас**,
Літера ЛТД, **2023** (повторне видання).

The section is **§ 32. Розгортання руху Опору в Україні** — "The unfolding of the Resistance movement
in Ukraine". Both quotes are inside it.

Note the renumbering: in the 2018 edition this was **§ 33**. The text is essentially unchanged
between the two. Say "§ 32" and "2023 edition" on screen; if a critic checks a 2018 copy and finds
§ 33, that is not an error, but you should know it before it is put to you.

## Quote 1 — the acknowledgement (§ 32, subsection 3, *Суть українсько-польського протистояння часів Другої світової війни*)

> Одним із найболісніших епізодів українсько-польського конфлікту була Волинська трагедія 1943 р.
> Навесні 1943 р. Крайовий провід ОУН(б) на Волині ухвалив рішення про вигнання з Волині місцевих
> поляків. На той час українці становили до 80 % населення Волині, поляки — 15 %. Обґрунтуванням
> такого рішення стала потреба завадити планам польських політичних сил повернути повоєнній Польщі
> Західну Україну.

**Checked translation:**

> One of the most painful episodes of the Ukrainian-Polish conflict was the Volhynia tragedy of 1943.
> In the spring of 1943 the Regional Leadership of the OUN(b) in Volhynia adopted a decision on the
> **expulsion** of the local Poles from Volhynia. At that time Ukrainians made up as much as 80% of
> the population of Volhynia, Poles 15%. The justification for this decision was the need to thwart
> the plans of Polish political forces to return Western Ukraine to post-war Poland.

**Translation limits, and they matter on this channel:**

- **вигнання = "expulsion" / "driving out".** Not "extermination", not "removal", not "cleansing".
  Same discipline as `нищення` → "destroying" in Document 149. If the narration says the textbook
  admits to an extermination decision, that is a distortion of the source, and it is the exact
  distortion this channel exists to catch.
- **Крайовий провід ОУН(б) на Волині** = the *Regional* Leadership in Volhynia, not the central OUN-B
  leadership. This is precisely the point the video makes about the originating command being less
  certain than the campaign — the textbook itself attributes it regionally. Do not upgrade it.
- **Волинська трагедія** = "the Volhynia tragedy". That is the Ukrainian state term. Poland says
  *rzeź wołyńska*, "the Volhynia massacre", and treats "tragedy" as itself a minimisation. If the
  page is on screen, that word is on screen. Do not translate it as "massacre" in your subtitle.

And the sentence that follows in the same paragraph — **11-13 липня 1943 р. стався виступ проти
місцевих поляків. Загони УПА майже одночасно атакували десятки польських поселень** ("on 11–13 July
1943 an action against the local Poles took place. UPA detachments attacked dozens of Polish
settlements almost simultaneously") — is the July 1943 attacks, on the same page. You get both
halves of the acknowledgement in one shot.

## Quote 2 — the "heroic struggle" wording (§ 32, subsection 2)

> Коли територію України було звільнено від німецьких, румунських та угорських окупантів,
> партизанські загони НКВС розформували й приєднали до складу Червоної армії. УПА продовжувала вести
> **героїчну боротьбу** проти НКВС на території України аж до середини 50-х років XX ст.

**Checked translation:**

> When the territory of Ukraine was liberated from the German, Romanian and Hungarian occupiers, the
> NKVD partisan detachments were disbanded and incorporated into the Red Army. The UPA **continued to
> wage a heroic struggle** against the NKVD on the territory of Ukraine right up to the mid-1950s.

**"героїчну боротьбу" is unambiguous. "Heroic struggle" is the correct translation and there is no
softer reading available.** A state-approved school textbook, in its own voice, calls it heroic.

---

## The single sharpest thing on this page, and the one you can get wrong on camera

**The "heroic struggle" sentence is not about Volhynia.** It is about the UPA's post-liberation
insurgency against the NKVD, 1944 to the mid-1950s. It sits in a different subsection from the
Volhynia paragraph.

That is *still* the video's point, and it is a better point than the sloppy version: the same book, a
few paragraphs apart, records an expulsion decision and dozens of attacked villages, and calls the
organisation's other campaign heroic. **The separation is the finding.** Ukrainian public memory
divides the anti-Soviet insurgency from Volhynia, and here is the state curriculum performing that
division on a single spread.

What the narration must not do is compress those two paragraphs into "Ukraine's textbook calls the
Volhynia perpetrators heroes." The book does not say that. If that is what is on the recording, it
has to be fixed in the edit, and it is fixable with narration over the same exhibit — the honest
version is stronger anyway, and it is the version that survives a Ukrainian viewer reading the page.

## Page number: p. 256, and how I got there

I could not open the 30 MB PDF, so I did not read the printed page myself. But the number arrived
from a direction that is better than a page-count estimate.

On **16 July 2025** Poland's Minister of Education, **Barbara Nowacka**, wrote to Ukraine's Minister
of Education, **Oksen Lisovyi**, formally objecting to how Ukrainian textbooks present Volhynia. Her
letter cites **"Historia Ukrainy", grade 10, 2023, page 256**, and quotes the passage beginning *the
UPA acted mainly in Volhynia and Galicia; in 1943 Roman Shukhevych took command* and running on
through the Polish-Ukrainian conflict.

That passage is § 32. It is the same section, the same book, the same year.

**So: § 32 begins at or immediately before p. 256, and the Volhynia paragraph is on or within a page
or two of it.**

### What you still have to do yourself — 5 minutes, and it must be done before this ships

Open `utek.uz.ua/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/istor_ukr_10_Vlasov_2023.pdf`, go to p. 256, and:

1. **Confirm the printed page number for each of the two quotes.** They may be on different pages.
   Whatever number goes on the exhibit card must be the one you saw.
2. **Confirm "героїчну боротьбу" is running body text, not an image caption.** This is the one real
   risk. The web transcription I worked from runs captions into the body, and in that transcription
   the sentence is followed by a dangling clause — *«жертовна боротьба українських націоналістів
   тривала впродовж десяти повоєнних років»* — that reads exactly like a caption under a photograph.
   If "героїчну боротьбу" turns out to be a caption, the exhibit is materially weaker and the card
   has to say "caption" on it. If it is body text, it is as strong as the video needs.
3. **Screenshot the page at full resolution.** You need the image regardless.

I have deliberately not guessed at either. An exhibit card with a page number nobody verified is the
failure mode this whole project is built to avoid.

## The complication: this is the book Poland formally protested

The plan asked whether the July 2025 Polish protest concerned the same textbook. **It does.** Same
book, same year, same grade, and the cited page is the page you are putting on screen.

Polish coverage ran under headlines like *"Ukraińskie podręczniki negują rzeź wołyńską"* — Ukrainian
textbooks negate the Volhynia massacre. Nowacka's objection is that the passage presents the events
as a symmetric conflict, against a record of roughly 100,000 Polish civilians killed by the UPA and
Ukrainian militias versus 10,000–12,000 Ukrainian deaths in Polish retaliation. She asked for an
immediate review of Ukrainian history textbooks and proposed a joint Polish-Ukrainian textbook.

A separate 2026 study by *ZN.ua* comparing Ukrainian and Polish school treatments examined this same
Vlasov textbook and found it introduces **«масові вбивства українців, здійснені Армією Крайовою»**
(mass killings of Ukrainians carried out by the Home Army) as the *cause* of the tension, placing
Polish violence chronologically **before** the Volhynia events — which frames the UPA's actions as
responsive. That framing is the substance of the Polish objection.

### What this costs you, and what it buys you

**Costs:** you cannot put this page up as clean evidence that *Ukraine openly acknowledges Volhynia*
and leave it there. A viewer who knows the story — and on this subject a lot of them will — knows
that Warsaw reads the same page as minimisation. Presenting only half of that is the move the channel
exists to criticise.

**Buys:** it is a far better exhibit than the one you thought you had. The page is not just a page.
It is a page a foreign government formally objected to, by number, thirteen months ago. And the
argument gets a second floor: the textbook does acknowledge the decision and the attacks — that part
is real and it defeats the lazy "Ukraine hides it" claim — *and* the surrounding framing is contested
enough that Poland's education ministry wrote a letter about it. Both things are true. That is
exactly the shape of argument this channel is supposed to make.

**One line of narration handles it.** Something to the effect of: *this is the page — and it is the
page Poland's education minister objected to by number in July 2025, which tells you the
acknowledgement and the framing are two different questions.* Then the video keeps going.

**Do not** let this reopen the script. It is a card and a line, not a restructure.
