# V2 predicted-stumble audit

## Method

Each spoken paragraph in `SCRIPT-V2-READTHROUGH-REBUILD.md` was tested against the questions Benoit actually asked during the read-through: “who?”, “which war?”, “why are we here?”, “what does this prove?”, “execution of what?”, and “who are these people?” The test is about spoken comprehension and reasoning continuity. It is not a fact-check, and the read-through is not used as factual authority.

## Fixes made because of this audit

- Removed the V1 claim that Zelensky sent the medal back.
- Defined every person or structure before asking it to carry evidence.
- Put the Soviet return before the Soviet records.
- Removed Shukhevych and the unnecessary German mini-investigation.
- Replaced the unintroduced “outside accusation” construction in the Document 149 inference.
- Kept the archive locator on screen rather than in narration.
- Kept the corroborating local reports entirely below narration.
- Stated exactly what the two polls asked and explicitly blocked a trend-line reading.

## Remaining recording risks

1. **Opening proper nouns.** “Order of the White Eagle” is factually necessary but slightly dense. Put the medal image and label on screen as Benoit says it.
2. **Kolodzinskyi.** The name may slow delivery. The sentence still needs him because the claim is attributed; phonetic help should sit in the teleprompter, not enter the narration.
3. **Document 149 quotation.** Pause before and after the line. The viewer must read the original and translation before Benoit explains it.
4. **Formal UPA versus later underground.** This paragraph carries a subtle correction. Keep it intact and do not cover the distinction with fast montage.
5. **Polling figures.** Use two visually separate cards labelled with the exact questions. Any shared chart would invite the trend-line interpretation the narration rejects.

## Final prediction

The V2 chain is internally continuous: each source is introduced by a question already in the viewer's head, and every named actor has a function before the name appears. The highest remaining stumble risk is factual density, not missing reasoning. The Document 149, resistance-form and polling paragraphs need deliberate delivery, but none now requires Benoit to invent a missing premise while reading.
