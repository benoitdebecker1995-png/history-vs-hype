# Read-through 2 — proposed Voice/Brain patch

**Status:** proposal only. Do not merge into the master Voice/Brain model yet.

## A. Current full read-through overrides phrase-level approval

**Previous assumption**  
A phrase identified as native or approved can be protected across later drafts.

**New evidence**  
“I think that's the whole fight” had previously been treated as approved. In the second complete read-through, Benoit immediately identified it as an inserted AI sentence.

**Proposed rule**  
A phrase is never permanently protected. A later full read-through in the phrase's actual logical context outranks earlier approval, isolated raw wording, an audit prediction, and a generalized voice rule.

**Why it matters**  
Voice is contextual. A sentence Benoit might say in one thought sequence can feel artificial when used as a bridge the reasoning has not earned.

**Risk if overapplied**  
Do not treat every stumble as permanent rejection of the words themselves. Record what function failed in that context.

## B. A valid question fails when its opponent is invisible

**Previous assumption**  
The repeated objection to spontaneous-versus-organized language meant the distinction was no longer part of Benoit's reasoning.

**New evidence**  
In read-through 2, Benoit supplies the missing opponent himself: a local, neighbour-against-neighbour eruption that may have drawn in UPA units rather than a campaign organized through movement structures.

**Proposed rule**  
Before answering a disputed interpretation, state that interpretation in ordinary language and explain why the evidence already presented leaves it open. Do not make Benoit rebut an opponent the viewer has never met.

**Diagnostic test**  
Immediately before a question, ask: could the viewer name the two live explanations without seeing the next paragraph?

**Risk if overapplied**  
Do not manufacture a formal debate for every qualification. The opponent must exist in the evidence or in a serious audience interpretation.

## C. Source convergence and source display are different decisions

**Previous assumption**  
Making Document 149 the main scene permits narration to sound as though that one document establishes the entire organizational conclusion.

**New evidence**  
Benoit asks, “Only one?” but also previously rejected narrating each corroborating report.

**Proposed rule**  
Briefly tell the viewer when several independent or complementary records support a conclusion. Select one source for the visible scene. Keep corroborating sources on screen, in notes, or under the narration unless one adds a logically distinct fact.

**Preferred pattern**  
“Several internal reports matter here. One of the clearest is…”

**Risk if overapplied**  
“Several sources show” cannot become a vague authority claim. The research notes must identify what each source contributes.

## D. Literal source wording must be separated from interpretive purpose

**Previous failure**  
The script turned “The action of destroying the Poles did not bring the expected results” into the claim that the document described the action's precise purpose as destroying the Poles.

**Proposed rule**  
For a primary-source quotation, separate three layers:

1. the exact words and verified translation;
2. the narrow inference those words directly permit;
3. any wider claim supported by the broader evidence base.

For Document 149, the sentence directly shows that the author calls something an anti-Polish “action” and evaluates its results. It does not alone settle whether “destroying” meant killing, forced flight, territorial removal, or some combination.

**Risk if overapplied**  
Do not become so cautious that the source loses meaning. State the direct inference clearly, then identify where the wider interpretation comes from.

## E. Negative qualifications need a trigger

**Previous failures**  
“None of this made mass killing inevitable,” “it would be misleading to imagine intact UPA armies,” and the missing-master-order discussion appeared before the script had created the overinterpretation they qualified.

**Proposed rule**  
A negative qualification belongs immediately after a live inference that a reasonable viewer might overextend. If the script has not created that inference and no active public claim requires it, state the positive fact and move on.

**Diagnostic test**  
Complete the sentence: “A viewer might now conclude ___ because I just said ___.” If the blanks cannot be filled concretely, the qualification is probably jammed in.

## F. Fairness in memory politics requires a supported comparison

**New evidence**  
Benoit notices that the script gives Ukraine a selective national memory but allows Poland to occupy the position of complete, neutral remembrance.

**Proposed rule**  
When a script analyzes memory selection in one country while using another country as its moral interlocutor, check whether the comparison silently treats the second country's memory as complete. If a comparison is needed, use a verified case of selection in commemoration—not merely a crime committed by that country.

**Guardrails**

- Do not create false equivalence in severity, sequence, or responsibility.
- A Polish crime is not automatically evidence of Polish memory selection.
- A Belgium/Leopold II analogy can establish the general mechanism but cannot by itself repair a specifically Poland-versus-Ukraine imbalance.
- If no verified comparison exists, record a research gap rather than improvising one.

## G. Concrete nouns must survive transitions

**New evidence**  
“Both stories,” “reason to change who would control the territory,” and “that objection” all force Benoit to reconstruct missing nouns.

**Proposed rule**  
At major transitions, repeat the concrete object even when elegant prose would prefer a pronoun or abstraction. Name the two historical claims, the population being removed, the institution being honoured, or the precise Polish objection.

## H. Creator questions are evidence of missing setup, not copy suggestions by default

**New evidence**  
Benoit improvises possible repairs—“battalions turned into guerrilla smaller groups,” “Jacquerie,” and “an army capable of enforcing its ideas”—while diagnosing the break.

**Proposed rule**  
Treat improvised read-through language first as evidence of the missing thought. Verify factual content and rebuild in his syntax; do not automatically paste the improvised wording into narration.

## Patch decision

These rules should remain provisional through the V3 read-through. The master model should be updated only after observing whether the repaired script removes the corresponding interruptions.
