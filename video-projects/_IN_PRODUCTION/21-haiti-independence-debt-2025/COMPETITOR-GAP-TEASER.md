## Section 7: Agent Assessment

### What ALL competitors get wrong or oversimplify

**The passive framing.** Every competitor treats the 1825 event as something that happened TO Haiti, rather than a documented coercion with named actors, sealed orders, deliberate council manipulation, and a paper trail. "France demanded, Haiti agreed" is the passive-voice version of a story that has a bagman (Mackau), an inside man (Boyer), a mechanism (the double debt), and an American sequel (Farnham, Butler).

**The figure mismatch.** The standard narrative celebrates Toussaint Louverture as the heroic figure — who died in 1803, before the ransom. The actual architect of Haiti's acceptance of the ransom (Boyer) and the people who resisted it (Darfour, later Estimé) are almost completely absent from competitor content. This is like telling the story of Versailles but stopping before anyone signs anything.

**The "it's paid off" illusion.** Several sources imply the debt ended in 1883 when the direct payments stopped. The fact that the final payment to banks didn't clear until 1947 — and that this triggered the withdrawal of an American financial controller — never appears in competitor content. The 1947 date reframes the entire story from ancient grievance to living memory.

**The Eiffel Tower connection.** The Crédit Industriel et Commercial used Haiti debt income to help finance the Eiffel Tower. No competitor mentions this. It's the most visceral illustration of where the money went — and it's documented.

**The British comparison.** Britain abolished slavery in 1833 and its taxpayers paid the slaveholders. Haiti is the only case in history where the enslaved paid the enslavers. This makes it structurally unique in world history, not just morally wrong. Competitors do not make this comparative point.

### The #1 question viewers have that nobody answers

**"Wait — so who actually made the decision to accept the ransom, and could they have said no?"**

Every competitor reports that Haiti "agreed" to the ransom. None answer: who specifically agreed? Did they have a choice? What happened at that meeting? Boyer's three questions to his council — where he never showed them the actual French document and framed question 3 as "can we afford to miss this opportunity?" — is the documented answer to this question. It exists in the Ardouin archives. No competitor has ever put it on screen.

### Recommended angle for History vs Hype

**"The Inside Man" framing for the teaser.**

The teaser should not try to tell the full 18-minute story. It should answer the one question no competitor answers: HOW did France pull this off? The mechanism is:

1. 14 warships (the threat you can't refuse)
2. Boyer's secret council meeting where he showed them the wrong document (the inside man)
3. The double debt trap (borrowing from French banks to pay French banks)

That's a 5-7 minute story with three clean beats, a villain, an inside man, and a mechanism. It stands alone as content ("this is how France designed the trap") while creating obvious demand for the full video ("and here's how the trap worked for 122 years").

### Recommended Teaser Structure (5-8 minutes)

**COLD OPEN (0:00-0:40) — The Ransom Note on Screen**

Show the 1825 document immediately. No introduction. Narrate:

- "This is a ransom note. Dated April 17th, 1825. Delivered at gunpoint."
- Louisiana comparison (France sold 77x the land to the US for LESS money — 80M vs 150M)
- "150 million wasn't even the full demand. France valued Haiti at 1.5 billion. This was the down payment."
- "France still needed 14 warships to collect it."

This cold open is the entire competitive advantage: show the document. No competitor does this in the first minute.

**BEAT 1: The Threat (0:40-2:30)**

- July 3, 1825: 14 warships, 528 cannons, Baron de Mackau arrives
- Show Mackau's instructions: "My mission is NOT a negotiation"
- "If Haiti refused: treated as enemies of France until they submitted to the supremacy of France"
- Boyer's proclamation from 1824: "Bury yourselves under the ruins rather than submit"
- 18 months later: Boyer folded. What changed? The cannons arrived.

**BEAT 2: The Inside Man (2:30-4:30)**

- Boyer convened a secret council of senators and generals
- He never showed them the French document
- Boyer's three questions on screen (in English): what he asked vs. what he was hiding
- "Notice how Question 3 is framed: not 'should we accept extortion?' but 'can we afford to miss this opportunity?'"
- The commission that DID see the document rejected it "immediately and categorically"
- Boyer overruled them
- Journalist Félix Darfour called him out: executed

**BEAT 3: The Trap Mechanism (4:30-6:30)**

- First installment (30M francs) was due Dec 31, 1825 — more than Haiti's entire annual budget
- Haiti borrowed from a French bank: received 24M, owed 30M
- The money went from the French bank to the French treasury — a short trip across Paris
- Double debt: owe the ransom AND the loans to pay the ransom
- By 1914: 80% of Haiti's budget going to France and French banks
- This isn't a theory. It's documented in payment records.

**CLOSE + TEASE (6:30-7:30)**

- "The 1825 Ordinance isn't a metaphor. It's a ransom note. You can read it. I'll link it in the description."
- "But France's trap had more layers. An American bank eventually took over the extraction. A US Marine general later confessed: 'I helped make Haiti a decent place for the National City Bank boys to collect revenues in.' The debt wasn't cleared until 1947. And France's own lawyers said the whole thing was illegal — in 1829."
- "That's the full story. It's in another video [link]. If you want more of this — subscribe."

This ending explicitly funnels viewers to the full video without requiring them to watch it first.

## Section 10: Risk Assessment for the Teaser Strategy

### What could go wrong

**Risk 1: The topic is read, not watched.**
The Haiti debt story has been prominent in print (NYT 2022, Irish Times 2025, UN news 2025) but hasn't generated a dominant YouTube history video. There's a possibility the audience for this topic prefers text. Mitigation: the teaser IS the test. If it gets impressions and a >3% CTR, the full video is justified. If it flat-lines at <100 impressions, the algorithm isn't surfacing it and the demand may be text-native.

**Risk 2: News-cycle fatigue.**
The topic had two spikes: October 2021 (NPR) and May 2022 (NYT). A third spike came April 2025 (Macron's acknowledgment). That 2025 spike is now fading. The next trigger could be: French parliamentary action on reparations, another major earthquake, or a further deterioration of Haiti's security situation. Timing the teaser around a news hook would significantly improve its odds.

**Risk 3: Political framing trap.**
This topic attracts both "it's all France's fault" and "it's all corruption" audiences, each of whom will engage negatively if they feel the video doesn't validate their priors. The full script addresses this with the steelman section. The teaser should not try to resolve the debate — it should present the documented mechanism and explicitly say "both extremes are wrong" without adjudicating in 7 minutes.

### What works in the teaser's favor

- "Why is Haiti so poor?" = 4,299/mo search volume. That's a real number.
- No dominant competitor holds this search query on YouTube.
- The document-on-screen approach is genuinely differentiated.
- The "inside man" framing (Boyer) is novel — nobody has framed Boyer this way on YouTube.
- The 2025 Macron acknowledgment is recent enough that news-triggered searchers may still be arriving.

## Recommendation

**Make the teaser first. Use it as a measurement instrument.**

The teaser is not a "shorter version of the full video." It is a specific answer to a specific question: **"HOW did France pull this off?"** The three-beat structure (threat + inside man + trap mechanism) answers that question completely in 6-7 minutes, stands alone as watchable content, and creates an explicit hook for the full video at the end.

**Success metrics at 72 hours:**
- Impressions > 2,000: The algorithm is surfacing it. Proceed with full video.
- CTR > 3.5%: The title/thumbnail is converting. Proceed with full video.
- Watch time > 50%: The content is retaining. Proceed with full video.
- Comments asking "what happened next?": The teaser funnel is working.

**If all four metrics are green:** Film the full 18-19 minute video immediately.
**If impressions are <500 at 72h:** The topic is not getting algorithmic traction. Revisit packaging (try backup title and thumbnail before abandoning).
**If CTR is <2%:** The title/thumbnail isn't converting despite impressions. Swap title at 48h per SWAP-PROTOCOL.md.

**Primary source strategy for the teaser:** Show the 1825 Ordinance document on screen in the first 60 seconds. This is the single most differentiating thing the teaser can do. It signals immediately that this is not the Wikipedia summary — it's the actual paper. That signal is worth more than any SEO optimization.
