## The "Concession + Distinction" anchor — locked

**What is technically TRUE in the viral claim:**
1. Slavery was widespread in Africa before 1441 — Thornton confirms.
2. Slaves were the only form of private revenue-producing property recognized in African law (because land was corporately owned). Thornton's core insight.
3. Trans-Saharan / Red Sea / Indian Ocean trades had already moved ~6-10 million people between 800 and 1600. Lovejoy / Manning.
4. African elites were active "equal partners" in the Atlantic trade — Thornton + Heywood estimate ~90% of those shipped to the New World were enslaved by Africans and sold to European traders.

**What Europeans BUILT that did not exist before 1441 (the distinction):**
1. **Scale shift** — from kinship-edge "social" institution to "mode of production." Pieter de Marees (Dutch, c.1602) explicitly: on the Gold Coast "they also enslave one another, but not in the same manner as in Angola or Conge [Kongo], for it would not be possible to load ships here with blacks... they do not have at their disposal a multitude of Captives." This is the buyer's own contemporary admitting the European trade *created* the multitudes.
2. **Race as enslavability principle** — invented during this era. Curtin / Howard French. African slavery was outsider-based but not racial; Atlantic slavery became exclusively racial.
3. **Chattel framework** — derived from Roman law / Siete Partidas, externalized people as transferable commodities. African slavery preserved partial human rights and assimilation pathways.
4. **Capital integration** — turned enslaved people into "walking currency" convertible into international credit. Inikori / French / Davis.
5. **Gun-slave cycle** — Europeans flooded West Africa with firearms to incentivize captive-taking; locked African societies into a self-perpetuating cycle of political violence and indebtedness.

## Seven candidate exhibits — ranked

For each: date, document, the verbatim quote that lands on camera, why it is "the buyer's own document."

### TIER 1 (strongest single-exhibit anchors)

#### A. *Romanus Pontifex* (Jan 8, 1455) — Pope Nicholas V
- **Verbatim Latin (Davenport Vol. 1):** *"illorumque personas in perpetuam servitutem redigendi"* — "...and to reduce their persons to perpetual servitude."
- **Full context:** "[We grant] full and free faculty to the aforesaid King Alfonso — to invade, search out, capture, vanquish, and subdue all Saracens and pagans whatsoever, and other enemies of Christ wheresoever placed... and to reduce their persons to perpetual slavery."
- **Authority:** Pope Nicholas V, "fullness of apostolic power," "terrestrial as well as spiritual ruler of all the world."
- **Why it's the buyer's own document:** Not a critic. Issued at Alfonso's request to provide "legal or quasi-legal cover" for the Portuguese trading monopoly.
- **Strength:** Highest-authority European document. Predates major scale. Pre-emptively grants what becomes the system.
- **Weakness:** Defends religious-war framing; apologist response is "this targets infidels, not Africans qua Africans" — needs follow-up showing Black Africans got swept into "Saracens and pagans."

#### B. The Royal Fifth scene — Zurara, *Chronicle of Guinea*, **Ch XXV, p. 82** (Lagos, Portugal — August 8, 1444 auction; chapter/page re-grounded via NotebookLM 2026-05-16)
- **Verbatim:** "Henry, mounted on a horse, supervised the proceedings, taking for himself as the royal fifth some forty-six of the best slaves... mothers clasped their other children in their arms and lay face downwards on the ground, accepting wounds with contempt for the suffering of their flesh rather than let their children be torn from them."
- **Note:** This is the Lagos *Portugal* auction scene (Ch XXV), NOT the West African Arguin Bank raid (Ch XIX p. 66). Both are dated 1444 but two weeks apart. Ch XIX = raid (Beat 1 anchor); Ch XXV = auction in Portugal (cold-open hook + synth beat anchor).
- **Authority:** Zurara is Henry the Navigator's "unstinting hagiographer."
- **Why it's the buyer's own document:** Zurara recorded the screaming and family separation NOT to condemn but to celebrate Henry's "delight... in the salvation of those souls which before were lost."
- **Strength:** Most cinematically devastating. The court's own chronicler reporting trauma as minor footnote to glory. Single dated scene.
- **Weakness:** Apologist will pivot to "this was raid violence not the system" — needs scale-shift bridge (de Marees).

#### C. **The 4,000-slave Asiento** — Charles V to Lorenzo de Gomenot, August 18, 1518
- **Verbatim (Donnan Vol. 1):** "I... do give [permission] to Lorenzo de Gorrevod... whereby he... may proceed to take to the Indies... four thousand negro slaves both male and female, provided they be Christians... and this my cedula shall be written down in the books of that house [of trade]."
- **Authority:** King Charles I of Spain (later Emperor Charles V), by order of the King via Secretary Francisco de los Covos. Done in Saragossa.
- **Why it's the buyer's own document:** Direct royal contract. Genoese resold the privilege for 25,000 ducats. Locked-in quotas per island. Proves the European state was engineering a licensing system to funnel quotas, not "buying from Africans."
- **Strength:** Documents the *engineered* nature of demand. A bureaucratic instrument with a specific number on it. Excellent for Format C.
- **Weakness:** 1518 is later than the "origin" framing — better as Beat 2 of a longer video, or as the anchor for a *demand-side* video.

### TIER 2 (powerful supporting exhibits, weaker standalone)

#### D. The "Books of the Dead" (*Livros dos Mortos*) — Manuel Bautista Pérez ledgers, Lima Inquisition, 1610s-1630s
- **Verbatim (Toby Green, *Fistful of Shells*):** Captives assessed "solely on the basis of their potential economic impact, describing infirmities such as 'swollen sides', cataracts and burns as 'losses [daños]'." Branding marks of the owners "written clearly in the margins, alongside the simple names of the dead."
- **Why it's powerful:** Reduces human life to an economic equation. The branded body and the spreadsheet entry are the same document.
- **Best use:** Mid-video pivot exhibit, not opener. Demands extensive setup.

#### E. The Royal African Company "filler" memo — c. early 1700s
- **Verbatim (Smallwood, *Saltwater Slavery*):** Company to Cape Coast agents: "We like well yo.r method of putting some small boys & girls of 10 years of age and upwards abd. our own ships over & above ye complement we intended them for."
- **Why it's powerful:** Children as cargo "filler." The internal corporate memo language. Exposes "tight packing" as official policy, not deviance.
- **Weakness:** English not Portuguese — pulls timeline forward 250 years from the origin question. Better suited to a different video.

#### F. *Las Siete Partidas* (13th C. Castile, applied to Atlantic trade) — Alfonso X
- **Verbatim:** "slaves behave like desperate men because of the state of servitude in which they find themselves. Therefore everyone should expect that they will readily tell lies and conceal the truth unless force is applied to them."
- **Why it's powerful:** The legal manual Iberian courts used. Codifies torture as standard procedure. Proves Europe had the chattel-law architecture *ready* before contact with West Africa.
- **Weakness:** Pre-1441, so "Europeans built the system" framing has to work harder. Better as supporting exhibit in pre-1441 setup.

#### G. The *peça da Índia* formula — found across asientos and customs registers
- **Verbatim (Russell-Wood / Hugh Thomas):** "A peça represented one healthy, male slave in the prime of life. Older men, boys, women and children were liable to be broken up into groups irrespective of any family relationships to make up what the auctioneer considered to be the equivalent of one peça."
- **Why it's powerful:** Bureaucratic dehumanization made visual. Three children + two elderly people = one "piece." A spreadsheet ghost over a crowd of people.
- **Best use:** Synthesis exhibit, not opener.

## Bear-traps surfaced by the corpus

When this video drops, the comment section will be a war zone. Scholarly responses to the four most likely commenter attacks:

1. **"The numbers are made up."** Response: Elbl, Curtin, and the Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database use tax farms, royal quittances, and double-entry ledgers to build minimalist floors. If anything the real numbers are higher than commonly cited, not lower.
2. **"Africans were equally to blame."** Response (Thornton + Heywood admit 90% sold by Africans, BUT) — economic historians argue growth was driven by European/American demand. The *scale* and *system* were engineered by demand, not supply.
3. **"Slavery in Africa was the same."** Response: Kopytoff/Miers — African slavery was institution-of-marginality (assimilation pathways, partial rights). European chattel framework = permanent transferable commodity. Different category.
4. **"It wasn't important to Europe's wealth."** Response (the Williams thesis debate): If slavery was marginal, why did every European power spend so much blood and treasure for so long controlling the supply and the colonies? French and Inikori's "no smoke without fire" rebuttal.
