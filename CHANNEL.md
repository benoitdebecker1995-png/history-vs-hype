# History vs Hype — channel state

Updated: 22 August 2026

## What this channel is

History vs Hype translates serious historical research and source criticism for ordinary viewers. It
should help people see how claims are built, tested, simplified, romanticised or distorted, without
pretending that uncertainty, interpretation and historical disagreement have disappeared.

## Objectives

- Find a repeatable growth path without abandoning academic honesty or becoming rage bait.
- Reach roughly EUR 2,000 per month, the stated near-term viability line for Benoit and his wife.
  This is full-time work — 35+ hours a week — so the target is a floor, not an aspiration.
- Become a trusted public reference point for evidence-led historical claims.
- Publish often enough that YouTube treats the channel as active.

## What has already been tested and does not work

**Territorial and sovereignty disputes are not an undiscovered lane.** Twenty-one have been
published. Median views of the twenty non-Belize videos: about 127. Their combined total is roughly
12,000, about a third of what Belize did alone. The direct Belize sequel, same subject and same
method five weeks later, fell 81%. Do not propose "find another live territorial dispute" as a growth
strategy. It has been run twenty-one times.

Belize is explicable without a channel law: ICJ oral arguments were live, so demand existed at that
moment, and almost nobody makes history videos about Belize, so supply was near zero. Bir Tawil,
Nigeria–Cameroon and Chagos had the second condition without the first and returned 85, 20 and 29
views.

## The current working reading

What separates the videos that moved is not the format. It is whether an audience for the subject
already existed before the video did, and whether anyone else was answering it.

The move that is distinctly Benoit's, and that the smaller successes share, is **claim provenance**:
take something everyone repeats, find where it entered the record, and show it growing. The Flat
Earth myth invented in 1828. Sol Invictus. The Piri Reis map citing Columbus. The $24 Manhattan sale
with no deed. America's founding secular quote absent from the original text. These reached hundreds
because the subjects were small — not because the move is weak.

The open bet is that same move aimed at subjects with mass existing recognition. The August research
session named the candidates and did the competitive work: who calculated that Mansa Musa was worth
$400 billion; where the Trojan Horse story actually comes from; when each claim in the Oak Island
legend first appears in the newspapers. That research is preserved in
`_recovered/NEXT-VIDEO-RESEARCH-RECOVERED-2026-08-22.md` and was cut off mid-validation.

Caveat this honestly. "Existing demand" is inferred from about fifty uploads, most under 300 views —
enough for a direction, not a formula. And mass recognition brings competition: the Trojan Horse pass
had already found a same-angle video published 14 August. The test is not "is this famous" but "is
this famous *and* unanswered."

### Corrected 26 August 2026 — the discriminator is the click, not the subject

The reading above survives, but it was missing the measured half. Evidence and method:
`channel-data/PACKAGING-DIAGNOSIS-2026-08-26.md`.

**Frequency is not the constraint.** 39 long-form videos published in the forty weeks to 5 July 2026.
Weekly has already been run for most of a year. Do not propose publishing more as the growth strategy.

**Writing is not the constraint.** Six videos under 120 views hold viewers *longer* than the 30,762
breakout. Treaty of Tripoli retains 51.5% on 106 views. People who press play stay.

**Packaging is the constraint.** YouTube served 159,017 impressions to 27 videos that converted 5,898
views. JD Vance took 9,969 impressions at 9.41%; Israel/Palestine took 10,925 at 1.60%. Same
distribution, four times the views, nothing but the click. CTR across the catalogue ranges 0.46% to
9.41%, and CTR also gates how much YouTube serves at all.

**So demand is necessary but not sufficient.** Berlin Conference was served 9,485 times and converted
3.66%. The audience was found; the title did not earn the click.

**An UNTESTED hypothesis — do not treat this as a rule:** winners name someone doing something with a
stake; losers name a topic, a myth, or a method. It convicts the provenance titles specifically —
Manhattan 0.46%, Sol Invictus 2.48%, Flat Earth 3.65% — while "JD Vance Claims Christians Found Child
Sacrifice", which *is* a provenance video packaged as a live conflict, took 9.41%.

**Why the warning is this loud.** On 27 August the `title_scorer` composite was measured against this
same catalogue and found non-predictive — its perfect scores average 2.67% CTR against a 3.05%
channel mean (ADR-0029). It had guided title choices for about a year without anyone checking it.
This hypothesis was derived the same way, off the same 47 rows, and has the same standing until
tested on data it was not drawn from.

**There is currently no validated title instrument on this channel**, and no fast way to get one.
The back-catalogue retitle test that was meant to settle this is **retired** (ADR-0030): the
candidates receive 29–149 impressions a month against the ~750 per arm the measurement needs, and no
video is both served enough to test and converting badly enough to be worth fixing. Retitling a dead
video does not summon impressions YouTube is not offering.

The hypothesis can only be tested **prospectively, on new uploads** — a season-long question, not a
month-long one. Volhynia is the first entry; its prediction is recorded.
