# Topic scout

Nearly a year of topic selection produced one video that worked out of about fifty. This skill exists to stop repeating the specific mistakes that produced the other forty-nine.

## The two levers are separate

The failure underneath most of the catalogue is treating "unique and rigorous" and "someone wants to watch this" as one decision. They are two.

**The subject supplies recognition.** Whether anybody has heard of the thing. This is where audience comes from and Benoit has almost never optimised it.

**The investigation supplies uniqueness and integrity.** What question gets asked, which sources get opened, what he refuses to overclaim. This is where his values live and he has never compromised it.

Bir Tawil failed because both were chosen for obscurity: 85 views. Guatemala vs Belize worked because the subject was mass-recognisable in the moment — a country might disappear, a court is deciding — while the investigation was pure him: Article 7 of an 1859 treaty and a road Britain never built. He did not choose that split; the ICJ calendar did.

Choose it deliberately. Borrow the audience from the subject, keep the integrity in the investigation.

## What has already been tested and does not work

**Do not propose territorial or sovereignty disputes as a growth idea.** Twenty-one have been published. The twenty non-Belize videos have a median of about 127 views; combined they total roughly 12,000, about a third of Belize alone. The direct sequel on the identical subject five weeks later fell 81%. Bir Tawil 85, Honduras islands 57, Preah Vihear 33, Chagos 29, Nigeria–Cameroon 20, Mexico's vanished island 17. This lane is closed. If he raises one himself that is his call, but do not put one forward as the answer to growth.

**Do not propose a subject on the grounds that nobody has covered it.** That criterion selects for obscurity every time, because undone work is most abundant where attention is thinnest. It is how the twenty-one happened.

**Do not use composite scores.** No opportunity score, viral score, breakout score, or weighted ranking. They manufacture false precision and they are quarantined from decisions. Recommend in prose with the decisive evidence, the counterargument, and what would reverse the recommendation.

## What to look for instead

Fame produces retellers, not archivists. On a famous subject the work that gets done is retelling — explainers, complete histories, the shallow "actually that's a myth" correction. What almost nobody does is go back and find where a specific sentence, number, or image first entered the record. That is slow archival work with no trend value, and the audience for famous subjects is served by people who will not do it.

Which means **provenance work is systematically undone at the top of the recognition curve, not despite the fame but because of it.** That is the cell to hunt in: mass recognition and an untraced claim in the same object.

His catalogue already demonstrates the move — the Flat Earth myth invented in 1828, Sol Invictus, the Piri Reis map citing Columbus in its own legend, the $24 Manhattan sale with no surviving deed, the founding secular quote absent from the original text. Those reached hundreds because the subjects were small. The move is not weak; it was pointed at small things.

Strong candidates usually have: a person, event, object, or image with existing mass recognition; a specific claim whose origin has never been followed backwards; evidence that can be put on screen and interrogated; several discoveries rather than one correction stretched thin; and a payoff about how historical knowledge gets made and transmitted.

Weak candidates: complete-history explainers, mystery recaps that end in "nobody knows", debunks whose entire payoff is "this is false", academic distinctions that will not fit in a title, anything requiring physical archive travel before the premise can even be established, and anything where a successful English creator has already done the exact investigation well.

## The kill test — run it before recommending anything

This is where the last search wobbled: the Trojan Horse angle turned out to have a same-angle English video published days earlier. Finding that late nearly wasted a production cycle.

Search the *exact* angle, not the subject. Use `vidiq_youtube_search` with the specific question, `vidiq_similar_videos` on the closest match, and `vidiq_outliers` to see what is actually performing in the space. Then report honestly:

- Who has attempted this exact investigation, and how did it perform?
- Is the gap "nobody has thought of this" or "nobody has done it properly"? Both are legitimate, but they are different bets and Benoit should know which one he is taking.
- Where a competitor exists, name what is actually wrong or thin in their treatment. If the answer is "nothing much", say so and drop the candidate.

Also run `vidiq_keyword_research` on the plain-language question to check demand is real rather than assumed, and confirm the demand is for long-form, not Shorts traffic.

## Research burden

Deep reading, source tracing, and translation checking are all acceptable. The boundary is reachability: the decisive evidence has to be gettable through digitised documents, books, articles, databases, or accessible experts. A topic becomes unattractive when untranslated archival material is indispensable just to establish the title promise.

## Output

Not a ranked table of twenty. Two or three candidates, each with:

- The actual question the video asks, in one sentence.
- The central revelation — what the viewer does not currently know.
- What goes on screen, concretely.
- The two or three strongest "yes, but" turns, since repeated turns are what make it his rather than a debunk.
- The competitive picture with named videos and real numbers.
- Why it might fail.

Then a single recommendation with a reason, not a menu. Say plainly when the evidence does not support picking one.

## When he chooses, stop

Once he commits, the search is over. Do not surface marginally better alternatives, do not reopen because something interesting appeared. Switching cost rises steeply as a video advances, and a project he owns has more claim on his commitment than a suggestion you generated. Move to the next unresolved problem.

## Before publishing, write the prediction down

For a consequential topic choice, record the recommendation, the reasoning, the mechanism you expect to produce views, and what you expect to observe — before the outcome is known. Attach the real numbers afterwards. One noisy result updates confidence; it does not become a new channel law. This is the only way the channel accumulates knowledge instead of anecdotes, and its absence is why a year of data produced no usable pattern.
