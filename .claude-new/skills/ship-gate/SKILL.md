# Ship gate

Volhynia was filmed on 23 July. The script locked on 17 August. It had still not published on 22 August. That month went into reopening the script, re-researching, and operating the tooling — and the channel went dormant, with suggested traffic falling to 830 views a quarter.

That pattern is the single most expensive thing happening to this channel, more expensive than any topic choice. This skill exists to interrupt it.

## The core distinction

Some things genuinely block publication. Most things that feel unfinished do not. Sort every open item into one of two piles and say which pile it is in.

**Genuine blockers**

- A hinge claim is unverified or has turned out to be wrong.
- A load-bearing exhibit cannot be shown honestly — the object is not what it is claimed to be, the translation is unchecked, the provenance cannot be stated.
- Script and evidence contradict each other: the video asserts something the sources do not carry.
- A structural failure the rough cut exposed — the argument does not actually land, not merely that a section is slow.

**Not blockers**

- Mediocre or missing B-roll. This is a normal imperfection. A strong spoken argument survives weak visuals; the reverse is not true.
- Polish: a paragraph that could be tighter, a transition that could be smoother.
- A book, paper, or archive that has not been read, when there is no concrete reason it could change a hinge claim.
- The feeling that a better version exists. There is always a better version.
- Packaging not being final. That can be decided after the edit.

## How to run it

Read the project's current state — the script, the status file, and whatever the last few working documents were. Then:

1. **List what is actually open.** Not what could be improved. What is unresolved.
2. **Sort each item.** Blocker or not, with a one-line reason.
3. **If nothing is a blocker, say so directly and recommend shipping.** Do not soften it into "you could ship, but you might also want to…". He is asking because he cannot tell, and a hedged answer leaves him exactly where he was.
4. **If something is a blocker, name the smallest action that clears it** — a specific page to open, a specific sentence to cut, a specific pickup shot to record. Not a workstream.

## On reopening the script

Reopen the core script when editing has exposed a genuine structural failure. Do not reopen it because a section could be better, because a new source turned up that is interesting rather than decisive, or because a read-through felt uneven.

Volhynia has been rebuilt more than once. Each rebuild produced a new version and a new set of audit documents and did not produce a published video. If a rebuild is genuinely warranted, say what specifically fails and what the rebuild must fix — and say what it will cost, because the cost is now measured in months of channel dormancy rather than in hours.

Switching and reopening costs rise steeply as a video advances. Past the filming lock, the bar for reopening is that the video is wrong, not that it could be better.

## On research that will not close

If research is the stated blocker, ask what the missing source could actually change. If the answer is "it might add context", it is not a blocker. If the answer is "it could show the directive was never signed", it is.

State the stopping condition explicitly, then hold to it: the major distinct authoritative literature is covered, the strongest counterargument is understood, the origin of the disputed claim is clear, and remaining unread material is unlikely to change the argument. Bibliography completeness is not a stopping condition.

## Reducing startup friction

When he is stuck rather than blocked, the useful move is one small concrete action, not a plan. Open the file to the exact line. Draft the one paragraph. Set up the one shot. Momentum comes from a started thing, not from a clearer roadmap.

## What this skill must not do

Do not open new work. Do not propose improvements he did not ask for. Do not audit the script for quality — that is `claim-check` and `voice-pass`, and invoking them here is how a "is this ready?" question turns into another week.

The output is a verdict and, at most, a shortest path. If the honest verdict is "ship it", that is the whole answer.
