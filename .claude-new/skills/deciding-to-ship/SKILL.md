# Deciding to ship

The most expensive thing that happens to this channel is not topic choice. It is a finished video sitting unpublished while the script gets reopened, the research gets extended, and the tooling gets adjusted — while the channel goes quiet and its recommended traffic decays.

This procedure interrupts that. Check `CHANNEL.md` for the current unresolved channel problem and how long the active project has been sitting, since that context determines how much a further delay actually costs.

## The core distinction

Some things genuinely block publication. Most things that feel unfinished do not. Sort every open item into one of two piles and say which pile it is in.

**Genuine blockers**

- A hinge claim is unverified or has turned out to be wrong.
- A load-bearing exhibit cannot be shown honestly — the object is not what it is claimed to be, the translation is unchecked, the provenance cannot be stated.
- Script and evidence contradict each other: the video asserts something the sources do not carry.
- A structural failure the rough cut exposed — the argument does not land, as distinct from a section being slow.

**Not blockers**

- Mediocre or missing B-roll. A strong spoken argument survives weak visuals; the reverse is not true.
- Polish: a paragraph that could be tighter, a transition that could be smoother.
- A book, paper, or archive not yet read, with no concrete reason it could change a hinge claim.
- The feeling that a better version exists. There is always a better version.
- Packaging not being final. That is decided after the edit.

## How to run it

Read the project's current state — the script, the status file, the last few working documents. Then:

1. **List what is actually open.** Not what could be improved. What is unresolved.
2. **Sort each item**, with a one-line reason.
3. **If nothing is a blocker, say so directly and recommend shipping.** Do not soften it into "you could ship, but you might also want to…". He is asking because he cannot tell, and a hedged answer leaves him where he started.
4. **If something is a blocker, name the smallest action that clears it** — a specific page to open, a specific sentence to cut, a specific pickup shot. Not a workstream.

## On reopening the script

Reopen the core script when editing has exposed a genuine structural failure. Not because a section could be better, not because an interesting rather than decisive source appeared, not because a read-through felt uneven.

Scripts on this channel have been rebuilt more than once on a single video, each rebuild producing a new version and a new set of audit documents and no published video. If a rebuild is genuinely warranted, say what specifically fails, what the rebuild must fix, and what it costs — and the cost is now measured in weeks of channel dormancy, not hours of writing.

Past a filming lock, the bar for reopening is that the video is wrong, not that it could be better.

## On research that will not close

If research is the stated blocker, ask what the missing source could actually change. "It might add context" is not a blocker. "It could show the directive was never signed" is.

State the stopping condition explicitly and hold to it: the major distinct authoritative literature is covered, the strongest counterargument is understood, the origin of the disputed claim is clear, and remaining unread material is unlikely to change the argument. Bibliography completeness is not a stopping condition.

## When he is stuck rather than blocked

The useful move is one small concrete action, not a plan. Open the file to the exact line. Draft the one paragraph. Set up the one shot. Momentum comes from a started thing, not a clearer roadmap.

## What this must not do

Do not open new work. Do not propose improvements he did not ask for. Do not audit the script for quality — that is `verifying-claims` and `repairing-script-voice`, and reaching for them here is how "is this ready?" turns into another week.

The output is a verdict and, at most, a shortest path. If the honest verdict is "ship it", that is the whole answer.
