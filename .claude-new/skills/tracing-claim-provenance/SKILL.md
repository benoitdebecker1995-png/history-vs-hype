# Tracing claim provenance

This is the move the channel is actually for. Not "is this true" — that produces a debunk, and debunks are saturated. The question is **where did this specific sentence come from, and what did each retelling add to it.**

It works because fame produces retellers, not archivists. On a well-known subject, the available work is explanation and correction; almost nobody goes back and follows a particular claim to its first appearance. That is slow archival work with no trend value, which is why the gap sits open at exactly the subjects most people already care about.

The channel's own worked examples show the shape: the Flat Earth myth traced to an 1828 biography and then to two nineteenth-century polemicists; the Sol Invictus story; the Piri Reis map that cites Columbus in its own legend while being sold as twelve thousand years old; the $24 Manhattan sale with no surviving deed. Each is a real thing that acquired a false shape in transmission.

## Start with the exact wording

Write down the claim in its most-repeated form, verbatim. The wording is the object being traced, not the general idea — "Mansa Musa was worth $400 billion" traces differently from "Mansa Musa was the richest man in history", and the difference is usually where the story is.

Then find the current top of the chain: who is repeating it now, in what words, to how many people. This does double duty — it establishes that the claim is live, and it is usually the video's opening.

## Walk backwards, link by link

Each retelling either cites something or cites nothing. Both are informative; an uncited assertion in a widely-read source is often the exact moment a claim became a fact.

For every link record four things: the date, the source, what it actually says, and — the important one — **what this step added that the previous step did not contain.** Claims almost never travel unchanged. They accrete:

- a number where there was a vague quantity
- a certainty where there was a hedge
- a motive where there was only an action
- a named individual where there was a group
- a date where there was "around this time"
- a causal link where there was only sequence

Those accretions are the content of the video. A chain where nothing was added is a claim that is simply true, and there is no video in it.

## Find the transition point

Somewhere the claim changes character — from observation to fact, from local to universal, from possibility to certainty. Usually a specific author, edition, or year, and usually one with a reason: a polemic being fought, a book being sold, a nation being built, a website generating traffic.

Naming the transition point and the incentive behind it is what turns a genealogy into an explanation.

## Read the earliest reachable source properly

At the bottom of the chain, stop repeating and read. In the original language. What is the genre — chronicle, testimony, ledger, letter, propaganda? What is it actually evidence of? A source can be entirely genuine and still not support what it is cited for.

Then separate, explicitly: the factual core, the inference drawn from it, the interpretation layered on, the moral judgement attached, and the propaganda conclusion. Those are five different things and the collapse between them is usually the whole story.

## The verdict is rarely "false"

The satisfying and honest outcomes are almost always shaped like:

- a real thing that acquired invented precision
- a real event with a transplanted motive
- a genuine document read as evidence of something it cannot establish
- several separate events compressed into one
- a later reconstruction mistaken for a contemporary account

Concede the strongest inconvenient fact first, then say exactly what the evidence does and does not prove. "This popular fact is false" is the weak version of this work and it is not what the channel does.

## Showability is part of the method, not an afterthought

The chain is the visual structure of the video. At each link, ask what goes on screen: the page, the passage, the highlighted wording, the labelled translation. A link that cannot be shown weakens the sequence even when it is true, so find out early rather than at the edit — that is what makes this format expensive to discover late and cheap to plan for.

Run the exhibit tests in `verifying-claims` on anything load-bearing before committing to it.

## Stopping condition

Stop when reaching a source that either predates the claim's distinctive features or is demonstrably where they originate, and when the strongest alternative account of the claim's origin has been considered and addressed.

State it before starting, and hold to it. Bibliography completeness is not a stopping condition.

## The trap

The earliest source *found* is not necessarily the earliest that *exists*. Say plainly what was reachable and what was not, and whether an unreached source could plausibly move the origin point. A genealogy presented as complete when it is merely as far as digitisation goes is the same overclaiming the channel exists to expose.

## Output

A chain, oldest to newest, each link carrying date, source, what it says, and what it added. Then: the transition point and the incentive behind it; what the earliest source actually establishes; what the popular version adds that no source supports; and the strongest counterargument to the whole genealogy.

That document is the research spine. The script is written from it, not alongside it.
