# History vs Hype — Video Content Domain

This context captures the canonical terms used when developing video content for the History vs Hype channel. Terms here define how concepts are named in scripts, research, and production decisions — not in code.

## Language

### Veil / Hijab terminology

**Hijab (Quranic)**:
A physical curtain or spatial barrier. In the Quran, the word *hijab* refers to the screen drawn between the Prophet's household and male visitors (Q. 33:53). It is never addressed to women as a dress requirement.
_Avoid_: Using "hijab" interchangeably with "headscarf" in research or scripts without flagging the semantic drift.

**Khimar**:
A pre-Islamic head wrap worn by men and women for protection. The Quranic innovation (Q. 24:31) was to instruct women to draw their khimars over their chests — not to introduce a new garment.
_Avoid_: Treating khimar as a new Islamic invention.

**Jilbab**:
An outer cloak. Q. 33:59 instructs free Muslim women to wear it so they are "known and not abused" — explicitly a legal identification marker, not a spiritual modesty requirement.
_Avoid_: Conflating jilbab with modern abaya or niqab.

**Modern headscarf**:
The pinned, form-fitting style common today. A 1970s development, emerging as a political identity marker in Egypt and Turkey during the Islamist revival. Not a continuation of classical Islamic dress.
_Avoid_: Treating the modern headscarf as the historical default.

### Atlantic slave trade terms (Video #56)

**Chattel slavery**:
Slavery in which the enslaved person is a transferable commodity — permanently owned, heritable, and legally property. The form institutionalised by the Atlantic trade. Distinct from dependency/marginality slavery (assimilable status, not heritable, not racialized) that existed across many pre-Atlantic cultures.
_Avoid_: Using "slavery" without qualification when the distinction between chattel and other forms is the argument. The Sowell-concede beat turns on this distinction.

**Document stack**:
The four-beat script structure for Video #56: one primary document per Asmongold claim (Zurara Ch XIX → Afonso I letter → de Marees → Romanus Pontifex), stacked visually in the synth beat. Each document originates inside the European or African-royal apparatus — none are abolitionist secondary sources.
_Avoid_: Treating the document stack as a series format until retention data validates it.

**The turn beat**:
The 15–25% runtime slot where the thesis forms for the viewer. For Video #56: Afonso I of Kongo's July 6, 1526 letter to João III. The viewer shifts from "yes there was kidnapping" to "an African king was begging Europe to stop." This is where "Europe made it worse" lands emotionally.
_Avoid_: Moving the turn beat — Afonso is the turn beat, period.

### Mechanism terms

**Class marker**:
The pre-Islamic function of the veil (c. 1300 BCE onward). Assyrian law mandated veiling for noble/free women and explicitly forbade it for enslaved women and sex workers. The veil signalled legal status and class privilege — not modesty.
_Avoid_: "Modesty garment," "religious symbol" when describing the pre-Islamic function.

**Legal extension**:
The jurisprudential move by named male scholars (8th–11th c. CE) that converted rules explicitly addressed to the Prophet's wives into universal obligations for all Muslim women. The three moves: (1) al-Tha'labi and al-Biqa'i apply Q. 33:33 ("you are not like other women") to all believers; (2) Ibn al-Jawzi overrides Q. 24:60's explicit exception for older women; (3) the slave/free distinction of Q. 33:59 is dropped, making the obligation class-blind and universal.
_Avoid_: "Interpretation," "scholarly consensus" — these terms obscure the specific procedural nature of the moves.

**Universal obligation**:
The result of legal extension: hijab as *fard* (religiously mandatory) for all Muslim women regardless of status. This is the endpoint of the mechanism — the thing the video explains how we got to. The Quran never states it.
_Avoid_: Treating universal obligation as the starting point or as Quranic in origin.

**Named scholars**:
The specific historical actors who performed the legal extension. Canonical names for this video: al-Tha'labi, al-Biqa'i, Ibn al-Jawzi. Named because the mechanism requires individual agency — it was not anonymous consensus.
_Avoid_: "Islamic scholars," "medieval exegetes" (too vague — loses the auditor's edge).

### Production terms

**Post-publish report**:
The per-video markdown artifact written after publication, recording observed performance (views, retention curve, CTR, subscribers gained), drop-off points, and human-authored lessons. Lives at `video-projects/_ARCHIVED/<slug>/POST-PUBLISH-ANALYSIS.md` or `channel-data/analyses/POST-PUBLISH-ANALYSIS-<video_id>.md`. The canonical input to `/patterns`, `/analyze`, `/retitle`, `/news-hooks`, the dashboard, and the reconcile loop — i.e., the **lessons** loop that turns raw analytics into channel learnings.
_Avoid_: "feedback file," "analysis file," "POST-PUBLISH" used as a free-floating noun. Always say "post-publish report" when the artifact is the referent.

**Auditor's edge**:
The channel's core competitive advantage: showing the primary source document on screen with page numbers and exact quotes. No competitor does this for the hijab topic.
_Avoid_: "Research" (too generic — the edge is in the *display*, not just the finding).

**Untranslated Evidence (series)**:
Channel sub-series built on close-reading primary documents that exist in non-English originals and have no faithful English translation. Episodes #37 Vichy, #49 Code Noir. Concept = untranslated *documents*, NOT untranslated *video topics*.
_Avoid_: using "Untranslated Evidence" for the topic-arbitrage method (importing topics from non-English YouTube channels) — that is a distinct, currently-unbranded sourcing method.

**Specificity bomb**:
Hook type scoring 5.4x retention lift (85-video niche corpus). Requires a named person, a named document, and a concrete surprising fact — not a general claim.

**Mechanism word**:
A title-level term naming the *type of action or distortion* the video exposes — not the parties involved. Examples: "split," "partition," "forgery," "extension," "translation gap," "court paper-over-people" (mechanism), "independence problem" (mechanism). Empirically, 2026 wins (Two Countries Split a Continent 788v, 229 Ethnic Groups 439v, Somaliland Independence Problem 396v) all carry a mechanism word. 2026 sub-50 misses (Bakassi, Honduras Bay Islands, Mexico Missing Island, Tripoli) led with named parties without a mechanism word.
_Avoid_: Party-led titles ("Nigeria vs Cameroon," "Honduras vs UK," "Treaty of Tripoli...") without a mechanism word doing the work alongside.

**Parties-with-mechanism rule**:
Title-construction rule for this channel at sub-1K subs: country/treaty names are permitted in titles ONLY when the rest of the title contains a **mechanism word** that a non-specialist can recognize as the universal distortion. "Somaliland's Legal Independence *Problem*" works (mechanism: independence problem). "Nigeria vs Cameroon. The Court Chose Paper Over People" fails (mechanism is poetic, not categorical). Audience doesn't search the parties — they search the mechanism.
_Avoid_: Bare party-vs-party titles without mechanism word.

**Five-civilization pattern**:
The hook frame for video #52. Across Assyrian (c. 1200 BCE), Athenian (5th-4th c. BCE), Jewish/Talmudic (3rd-6th c. CE), Byzantine (5th-6th c. CE), and Sasanian Persian (224-640 CE) law — the rule was identical: free women must wear the veil; enslaved women are forbidden. Islam entered a world where this system was already universal. The pattern, not a single case, is the opening argument.
_Avoid_: "The oldest depiction of the veil" — earlier artistic representations exist. Say "the oldest law governing who could wear the veil" when citing Assyria specifically.

**Single mechanism**:
The structural constraint for this video: one jurisprudential conversion move (class marker → universal obligation), traced from origin to codification. Not a panoramic sweep.
_Avoid_: "Full history of the veil," "comprehensive overview."

**Thesis (locked):** Women's dress has always been a state control mechanism — before Islam, inside Islam, and in secular states that ban Islam. Focus: the Islamic codification mechanism specifically.

**Opening frame (locked):** Two title-promise sentences front-loaded so all three packaging variants are coherent. Sentence 1 delivers the Grenade title's promise (1200 BCE / slaves / forbidden / flogging). Sentence 2 delivers Doorway B's promise (mandatory hijab not in Quran, three medieval scholars added it). Sentence 3 pivots to three-state contradiction (Afghanistan / France / Iran). Thesis statement with "free woman" anchor closes the opening at ~67s. Doorway A's promise (status symbol before Islam) is implicit in sentence 1's "first law ever written" + slave/free binary.
_Avoid_: Opening on the modern chyrons before the title-promise sentences — that's the thumbnail-mismatch failure pattern (16.7% median drop at 2% mark, packaging notebook). The "free woman" anchor is internal scaffolding only; titles and thumbnails center on slave/forbidden, scholar-mechanism, or pre-Islamic-privilege frames.

**Packaging variants (3 — for native A/B rotation):**
- **Doorway A — "Once a Privilege":** Modern hijab portrait + small ancient inset, overlay "ONCE A PRIVILEGE", title *"The Hijab Was a Status Symbol Before Islam Adopted It."* Medium-intensity invitation, audience: curious-and-patient.
- **Doorway B — "Not in the Quran":** Medieval Arabic manuscript photograph + small modern hijab silhouette, overlay "NOT IN THE QURAN", title *"The Hijab Wasn't Made Mandatory by the Quran. Three Medieval Scholars Did."* Forensic invitation, audience: mechanism-curious.
- **Grenade — "FORBIDDEN":** Middle Assyrian Law tablet, dramatic chiaroscuro, red "FORBIDDEN" stamp angled across cuneiform, title *"The Veil Started as a Privilege Slaves Were Forbidden to Wear. **Title (locked):** *"The Veil Was Forbidden for Slaves 2,000 Years Before Islam Made It Mandatory."*
**Title rationale:** "veil history" = 50,361 searches/mo, competition 10.80, overall score 77.79 (VidIQ 2026-05-14). Primary search anchor. All other hijab/veil terms tested returned zero volume except "origin of hijab" (5,013/mo). Previous title had no keyword anchor.

**Video structure (locked)**:
- Opening (60s): Three-state contradiction (Afghanistan / France / Iran). Pivot: "It isn't a new conviction."
- Hook (90s): Five-civilization pattern — Assyrian, Greek, Jewish/Talmudic, Byzantine, Sasanian. Same rule across all: free women must wear it, enslaved women forbidden. One sentence per civilization. Assyrian law quote on screen (Ahmed p. 14). Pattern lands in repetition, not depth.
- Mechanism: Three beats, unequal time. (1) Q. 33:59 as legal class shield — 1 beat. (2) al-Tha'labi/al-Biqa'i extend wives-of-prophet rules to all women — 1 beat. (3) Ibn al-Jawzi overrides Q. 24:60 exemption using Babylonian class logic (free/slave visual boundary, structurally identical to b. Ketubot 72a-b per Bauer p. 162-163) — 2x time. Talmud bridge and "logic collapsed when slavery ended" payoff live here. On-screen clarification (one line, not a disclaimer): "Ibn al-Jawzi doesn't cite the Talmud. What Bauer shows is that he's using the same class logic — because both systems inherited it from the same Babylonian legal environment."
- Climax: Ibn al-Jawzi + "logic collapsed when slavery ended, rule didn't." Brief bridge (2-3 sentences): the ruling was codified into fiqh and survived the caliphate's end. Colonialism added new players to an already-political garment — Lord Cromer (Ahmed p. 153: founding member and one-time president of the Men's League for Opposing Women's Suffrage) used the veil to argue Egypt needed saving; secular and Islamist states followed with bans and mandates. Same move, different uniforms. _Avoid_: "political football" — implies it was ever apolitical. Frame as: always a state instrument, colonialism just added Western states to the list.
- Close: France/Iran return. Walk-away (one sentence): "The jurisprudential ruling is 800 years old. The states enforcing it — in either direction — are just the latest ones."
- Thesis focus: Islam specifically. France/Iran are bookends, not part of the mechanism.
- Modern stakes: endpoint of the colonial bridge, not a separate beat. Afghanistan "Law on Virtue" August 2024 (voice declared awrah) + Iran "Noor Plan" AI surveillance 2024 → France → walk-away. One continuous motion.

## Relationships

- A **class marker** was the pre-Islamic function of the **jilbab** and veil
- **Legal extension** converted the **class marker** function into a **universal obligation**
- **Named scholars** performed the **legal extension** through three specific procedural moves
- **Hijab (Quranic)** ≠ **modern headscarf** — the word's referent drifted across 14 centuries
- **Khimar** and **jilbab** are the actual Quranic garment terms; **hijab** is not

## Example dialogue

> **Researcher:** "Should we say the hijab is mentioned in the Quran?"
> **Domain expert:** "Yes — but only as a curtain, not a headscarf. Use 'hijab (Quranic)' to mean the barrier, and 'modern headscarf' or 'universal obligation' to mean the dressed practice. Never conflate them without flagging the drift."

> **Scriptwriter:** "Can we call al-Tha'labi an 'interpreter'?"
> **Domain expert:** "No — 'interpreter' implies he was reading what was there. He performed a **legal extension**: taking rules the Quran addressed to the Prophet's wives and applying them universally. That's an act, not a reading."

## Flagged ambiguities

- "Veil" is used colloquially to mean everything from a face covering to a headscarf to a room divider. In scripts: define the specific garment or function on first use. Never use "veil" as a catch-all without a qualifier.
- "Hijab" in modern Arabic = headscarf; in classical Arabic/Quran = curtain/barrier. Scripts must flag this on first use — it is the central linguistic turn of the video.
- "Modesty" is disputed: the Quran links garments to legal status and safety (Q. 33:59), not spiritual modesty. Avoid "modesty" as the primary frame unless quoting a source that uses it.
- "Untranslated" was used for two distinct concepts in 2026-05-07 weekend-test planning: (1) the existing **Untranslated Evidence** document series, (2) a topic-sourcing method that imports high-performing topics from non-English-language history YouTube channels. Resolved: keep Untranslated Evidence pure (documents only); the sourcing method is unbranded until retention justifies a series slot. Working titles "Foreign Desk" / "The Import" — DO NOT use in production yet.
- "Pilot" is overloaded: (a) the channel's **Pilot Episode template** (8-10 min rapid-test format from `CONTENT-TIMELINE-2026.md`), and (b) the colloquial sense ("pilot a new format" — testing anything experimental). When ambiguous, qualify as "Pilot Episode template" for (a) or "format pilot" for (b).
